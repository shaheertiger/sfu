You've just hit 1,000 views on a video and you want to know what it's worth. YouTube doesn't pay a fixed amount per 1,000 views. What you earn is your **RPM** (revenue per 1,000 views): your share of ad revenue and other income, spread across all your views. If your RPM were $3, 1,000 views would earn about $3. If it were $0.50, the same views would earn about 50 cents.

![How much YouTube pays for 1,000 views: views divided by 1,000, times your RPM, with 55% of ad revenue on long-form and 45% on Shorts](/images/blog/how-much-does-youtube-pay-for-1000-views-hero-1200x675.webp)

You also need to be in the YouTube Partner Program (YPP) with ads turned on. Views on a channel that isn't monetized earn nothing, which is why anyone quoting a single "YouTube rate" is guessing about your channel.

This guide covers the formula, the difference between CPM and RPM, how YouTube splits revenue on videos and Shorts, the five things that move your RPM, worked scenarios, and how to find your real number in YouTube Studio.

## How Does YouTube Pay Creators? (It's Not Per View)

![How YouTube pays creators: a share of ad revenue and other income, not a fixed payment per view](/images/blog/how-much-does-youtube-pay-for-1000-views-model-1200x675.webp)

YouTube pays creators a share of the ad revenue and other revenue their content generates. Views matter because more views usually mean more ads shown, but no view has a fixed price.

The simple formula is:

**Estimated earnings = (views ÷ 1,000) × your RPM**

Because RPM varies so much from channel to channel, the table below uses hypothetical RPMs to show how the math works. These are not averages or predictions.

| Hypothetical RPM | 1,000 views | 10,000 views | 100,000 views |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0.50 | $0.50 | $5 | $50 |
| $2 | $2 | $20 | $200 |
| $5 | $5 | $50 | $500 |
| $10 | $10 | $100 | $1,000 |

To use it for your own channel, swap in the RPM shown in your YouTube Studio Revenue tab (steps below).

### What Most People Don't Know

Some YPP income doesn't depend on views at all: channel memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers, Super Thanks and shopping features (where available). That income is counted in your RPM too, so a channel with a loyal community can have a higher RPM than its ad rates alone would suggest.

> **Key point:** Your RPM is the only number that turns views into money for your channel. Every chart of "YouTube pay per 1,000 views" is someone else's RPM.

## What Is CPM vs RPM on YouTube?

![CPM is what advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions; RPM is what you earn per 1,000 views after YouTube's share](/images/blog/how-much-does-youtube-pay-for-1000-views-cpm-rpm-1200x675.webp)

Creators often mix these up. They measure different things, and only one reflects money you actually receive.

### CPM: What Advertisers Pay

CPM (cost per mille) is what advertisers pay for 1,000 ad impressions. It's measured **before** YouTube takes its share, and it only counts views where an ad was shown. A video with a healthy CPM can still have a much lower RPM.

### RPM: What You Earn

RPM is your revenue per 1,000 views **after** YouTube's share. It counts every view, including ones where no ad played, and it includes other revenue such as YouTube Premium revenue, memberships and Super Chat. That makes RPM the number to use when estimating income.

| | CPM | RPM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who it describes | Advertisers' cost | Your earnings |
| Revenue share taken out? | No | Yes |
| Which views count | Monetized playbacks only | All views |
| Includes non-ad income? | No | Yes |

### Why RPM Is Always Lower Than CPM

Several things pull RPM below CPM: YouTube's revenue share, viewers who don't see an ad (ad blockers, very short sessions, videos with limited ads), and views from places with fewer advertisers.

## How Much of the Ad Money Do You Keep? 55% vs 45%

The revenue share depends on the format. These are YouTube's terms at the time of writing; check [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube) for the current version, because they can change.

| Format | Your share | How it's calculated |
|---|---|---|
| Long-form videos | 55% of net ad revenue | Ads shown on or around your videos |
| Shorts | 45% of your allocated revenue | Pooled Shorts feed revenue, part set aside for music licensing, the rest split by share of eligible Shorts views |
| Memberships, Super Chat, Super Thanks | Not view-based | Paid by your viewers directly, under YouTube's terms |

### Long-Form Videos: 55% to the Creator

For regular videos, creators in YPP receive 55% of the net ad revenue from ads shown on or around their videos. YouTube keeps the rest.

### Shorts: 45% of Allocated Revenue

Shorts work differently, because ads play between Shorts in the feed rather than on one specific video. YouTube pools the ad revenue from the Shorts feed, sets aside a portion to help pay for music licensing, and allocates the rest to creators based on their share of eligible Shorts views. Creators keep 45% of their allocated amount.

Because of this pooled model, many creators see a very different RPM on Shorts than on long-form videos. Compare the two in your own analytics rather than assuming. Our guide to [posting YouTube Shorts](/blog/post-youtube-shorts) covers how Shorts are classified.

## What Affects How Much YouTube Pays per 1,000 Views? 5 Factors

![Five factors that change how much YouTube pays for 1,000 views: viewer location, niche, time of year, video length and ad suitability](/images/blog/how-much-does-youtube-pay-for-1000-views-factors-1200x675.webp)

Two channels with identical view counts can earn very different amounts. These are the main reasons.

### 1. Where Do Your Viewers Live?

Advertisers pay different amounts in different countries, so your audience's location matters more than your own. Views from markets with strong advertiser demand usually earn more than views from markets where ad prices are lower. For a regional example, see [how much YouTube pays for 1 million views in India](/blog/how-much-youtube-pays-for-1-million-views-in-india).

### 2. Which Niches Pay the Most?

Topics that attract advertisers with valuable products, such as personal finance, software or business tools, tend to command higher ad prices than topics with less competition. That doesn't mean you should switch niches; a topic you can't sustain won't earn much either.

### 3. Does the Time of Year Matter?

Yes. Ad prices move with advertiser budgets. Many advertisers spend more in the run-up to the end-of-year holidays and less in the weeks after, so the same views can earn noticeably different amounts in different months.

### 4. Does Video Length Change Pay?

Videos that are 8 minutes or longer can carry mid-roll ads at the time of writing, which gives more chances for an ad to play. Longer videos only help if people keep watching, so don't pad videos just to reach a length.

### 5. Is Your Video Advertiser-Friendly?

Videos that don't meet YouTube's advertiser-friendly guidelines may get limited or no ads (a yellow icon in YouTube Studio). Strong language, sensitive topics and some types of footage can reduce how many advertisers are willing to appear.

> **Pro tip:** If your RPM suddenly drops, check the yellow icons first. One limited-ads video with lots of views can drag down the whole month.

Divide $100 by your RPM and multiply by 1,000. At an RPM of $2, that's about 50,000 views; at $5, about 20,000.

## How to Find Your Own RPM in YouTube Studio (Free, 2 Minutes)

Your real numbers are in YouTube Studio once you're in YPP. Labels change from time to time, so look for the closest match.

1. Open **YouTube Studio** and select **Analytics**.
2. Open the **Revenue** tab to see estimated revenue and RPM for the period.
3. Click **Advanced mode** to break revenue and RPM down by video, content type (videos, Shorts, live) or country.
4. Change the date range to compare months and spot seasonal swings.

Studio shows **estimated** revenue. The final amount can be adjusted when YouTube finalizes the month, for example after removing invalid traffic.

## YouTube Money Calculators and "Earnings Estimator" Sites (Use With Caution)

![What YouTube earnings calculator sites can and can't tell you compared with your YouTube Studio Revenue tab](/images/blog/how-much-does-youtube-pay-for-1000-views-calculators-1200x675.webp)

Search "YouTube money calculator" and you'll find sites that estimate any channel's earnings from its public view count. They're fun, but they can't see anyone's RPM.

### The Real Risks

- **They're guesses.** They multiply public views by an assumed range, which can be far off for any one channel.
- **Some ask you to sign in with Google.** A calculator doesn't need access to your account. Never grant it.
- **"Guaranteed monetization" services are scams.** Buying views or watch hours breaks YouTube's rules; purchased views are filtered as invalid traffic, earn nothing and can put your YPP status at risk.

### If You Use One Anyway

- Treat the number as a rough range, not a forecast.
- Never connect your YouTube or Google account to it.
- Check your real RPM in the Revenue tab instead.

> **Bottom line:** Your Revenue tab is the only place your real RPM lives. Everything else multiplies someone else's guess.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does YouTube pay per view?

No. YouTube pays creators a share of the ad revenue and other revenue their content generates. More views usually mean more ads shown, but there's no fixed payment per view.

### How much does YouTube pay for 1,000 Shorts views?

It depends on your Shorts RPM, which comes from YouTube's pooled Shorts revenue model. If your Shorts RPM were $0.05, 1,000 Shorts views would earn about 5 cents. Filter the Revenue tab by Shorts for your real figure.

### Do I get paid for views from before I joined the Partner Program?

No. You only earn from ads shown after your channel is accepted into YPP and monetization is turned on. See [when YouTube starts paying you](/blog/when-does-youtube-start-paying-you) for the full timeline.

### Why did my RPM drop this month?

Common reasons are seasonal drops in ad spending, a shift in where your viewers are, more Shorts views in the mix, or videos with limited ads. Compare RPM by content type and by country in Advanced mode.

### How many views do I need to make $100 on YouTube?

Divide $100 by your RPM and multiply by 1,000. At an RPM of $2 you'd need about 50,000 views; at $5, about 20,000.

### What's the difference between CPM and RPM?

CPM is what advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions, before YouTube's share. RPM is what you earn per 1,000 views, after YouTube's share and including non-ad income.

### What percentage does YouTube take?

At the time of writing, creators keep 55% of net ad revenue on long-form videos, and 45% of the revenue allocated to them from the Shorts pool.

### Do longer videos earn more?

They can, because videos of 8 minutes or more can carry mid-roll ads at the time of writing. That only helps if viewers keep watching.
