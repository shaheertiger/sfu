YouTube doesn't pay a fixed amount per 1,000 views. What you earn depends on your **RPM** (revenue per 1,000 views), which is your share of ad revenue and other income divided across all your views. If your RPM were $3, 1,000 views would earn you about $3. If it were $0.50, the same views would earn about 50 cents.

You also need to be in the YouTube Partner Program (YPP) with ads turned on. Views on a channel that isn't monetized earn nothing, and anyone quoting a single "YouTube rate" is guessing about your channel.

## Quick answer: how to estimate what 1,000 views earn

The simple formula is:

**Estimated earnings = (views ÷ 1,000) × your RPM**

Because RPM varies so much from channel to channel, the table below uses hypothetical RPMs to show how the math works. These are not averages or predictions.

| Hypothetical RPM | 1,000 views | 10,000 views | 100,000 views |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0.50 | $0.50 | $5 | $50 |
| $2 | $2 | $20 | $200 |
| $5 | $5 | $50 | $500 |
| $10 | $10 | $100 | $1,000 |

To use it for your own channel, swap in the RPM shown in your YouTube Studio Revenue tab (more on that below).

## RPM vs. CPM: the two numbers that matter

Creators often mix these up. They measure different things, and only one of them reflects money you actually receive.

### CPM: what advertisers pay

CPM (cost per mille) is what advertisers pay for 1,000 ad impressions. It's measured **before** YouTube takes its share, and it only counts views where an ad was shown. A video with a healthy CPM can still have a much lower RPM.

### RPM: what you earn

RPM is your revenue per 1,000 views **after** YouTube's share. It counts every view, including ones where no ad played, and it includes other revenue sources such as YouTube Premium revenue, channel memberships and Super Chat. That makes RPM the number to use when estimating your income.

### Why RPM is always lower than CPM

| | CPM | RPM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who it describes | Advertisers' cost | Your earnings |
| Revenue share taken out? | No | Yes |
| Which views count | Monetized playbacks only | All views |
| Includes non-ad income? | No | Yes |

Several things pull RPM below CPM: YouTube's revenue share, viewers who don't see an ad (ad blockers, very short watch sessions, videos with limited ads), and views from places with fewer advertisers.

## How YouTube splits ad revenue

The revenue share depends on the format. These are YouTube's terms at the time of writing; check [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube) for the current version, because they can change.

### Long-form videos: 55% to the creator

For regular videos, creators in YPP receive 55% of the net ad revenue from ads shown on or around their videos. YouTube keeps the rest.

### Shorts: 45% of allocated revenue

Shorts work differently, because ads play between Shorts in the feed rather than on one specific video. YouTube pools the ad revenue from the Shorts feed, sets aside a portion to help pay for music licensing, and allocates the rest to creators based on their share of eligible Shorts views. Creators keep 45% of their allocated amount.

Because of this pooled model, many creators see a very different RPM on Shorts than on long-form videos. Compare the two in your own analytics rather than assuming. If you publish both, our guide to [posting YouTube Shorts](/blog/post-youtube-shorts) covers how Shorts are classified.

### Income that isn't tied to views

Some YPP income doesn't depend on view counts at all: channel memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers, Super Thanks and shopping features (where available). A channel with a loyal community can earn more from these than from ads, even with modest views.

## What changes your earnings per 1,000 views

Two channels with identical view counts can earn very different amounts. These are the main reasons.

### Where your viewers are

Advertisers pay different amounts in different countries, so the location of your audience matters more than your own location. Views from markets with strong advertiser demand usually earn more than views from markets where ad prices are lower. For a regional example, see [how much YouTube pays for 1 million views in India](/blog/how-much-youtube-pays-for-1-million-views-in-india).

### Your niche

Topics that attract advertisers with valuable products, such as personal finance, software or business tools, tend to command higher ad prices than topics with less advertiser competition. That doesn't mean you should switch niches; a topic you can't sustain won't earn much either.

### Time of year

Ad prices move with advertiser budgets. Many advertisers spend more in the run-up to the end-of-year holidays and less in the weeks after, so the same views can earn noticeably different amounts in different months.

### Video length and ad formats

Videos that are 8 minutes or longer can carry mid-roll ads at the time of writing, which gives more chances for an ad to play. Longer videos only help if people actually keep watching, though, so don't pad videos just to reach a length.

### Ad suitability

Videos that don't meet YouTube's advertiser-friendly content guidelines may get limited or no ads (shown as a yellow icon in YouTube Studio). Strong language, sensitive topics and some types of footage can reduce how many advertisers are willing to appear.

## Hypothetical worked examples

These examples are invented to show the math. They aren't benchmarks for any niche or country.

- **A long-form tutorial channel**: if a video got 40,000 views in a month and the channel's RPM were $4, that video would earn about 40 × $4 = **$160**.
- **A Shorts-first channel**: if a channel got 2,000,000 Shorts views and its Shorts RPM were $0.06, it would earn about 2,000 × $0.06 = **$120**.
- **A mixed channel**: if the same creator also earned $50 from channel memberships that month, that income would be added on top and would also raise the channel's overall RPM.

## How to find your own RPM

Your real numbers are in YouTube Studio once you're in YPP:

1. Open **YouTube Studio** and select **Analytics**.
2. Open the **Revenue** tab to see estimated revenue and RPM for the selected period.
3. Click **Advanced mode** to break revenue and RPM down by video, by content type (videos, Shorts, live) or by country.
4. Change the date range to compare months and spot seasonal swings.

Keep in mind that Studio shows **estimated** revenue. The final amount can be adjusted when YouTube finalizes the month, for example after removing invalid traffic.

## How to raise earnings per view, the legitimate way

- **Make videos people watch to the end.** Better retention means more ad opportunities and more recommendations. A clear, specific title helps the right viewers click; the [YouTube title checker](/tools/youtube-title-checker) gives you a quick checklist.
- **Keep ads on and enable mid-rolls** on longer videos, placed at natural breaks.
- **Stay advertiser-friendly** when it doesn't compromise your content.
- **Build non-ad income** such as memberships once you're eligible.
- **Publish consistently.** Schedule For You can upload and schedule your YouTube videos alongside posts for your other channels, so a steady release schedule doesn't depend on you being online at the right moment. It doesn't show revenue or analytics; use YouTube Studio for that.
- **Never buy views.** Purchased or automated views are filtered as invalid traffic, don't earn money, and can put your YPP status at risk.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does YouTube pay per view?

No. YouTube pays creators a share of the ad revenue and other revenue their content generates. Views matter because more views usually mean more ads shown, but there's no fixed payment per view.

### How much does YouTube pay for 1,000 Shorts views?

It depends on your Shorts RPM, which comes from YouTube's pooled Shorts revenue model. If your Shorts RPM were $0.05, 1,000 Shorts views would earn about 5 cents. Check the Revenue tab in YouTube Studio, filtered by Shorts, for your real figure.

### Do I get paid for views from before I joined the Partner Program?

No. You only earn from ads shown after your channel is accepted into YPP and monetization is turned on. See [when YouTube starts paying you](/blog/when-does-youtube-start-paying-you) for the full timeline.

### Why did my RPM drop this month?

Common reasons include seasonal drops in ad spending, a shift in where your viewers are, more Shorts views in the mix, or videos that received limited ads. Compare RPM by content type and by country in Advanced mode to find the cause.

### How many views do I need to make $100 on YouTube?

Divide $100 by your RPM and multiply by 1,000. If your RPM were $2, you'd need about 50,000 views; at $5, about 20,000 views. Your own RPM is the only reliable input.
