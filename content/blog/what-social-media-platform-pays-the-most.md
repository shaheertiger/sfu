There's no single platform that pays every creator the most. YouTube has the longest-running and most clearly published ad revenue share, which is why many long-form creators earn most of their platform income there. But TikTok, Facebook, Instagram and X each pay in different ways, and the one that pays you the most depends on the format you make, where your audience lives, your niche, and how much you earn off the platform through brand deals and your own products.

Below is a comparison of how each platform pays, followed by how to work out which is best for you.

## How each platform pays creators

This table compares payout **models**, not rates. Rates vary widely and change often, so we don't list them here. Eligibility rules are summarized at the time of writing; check each platform's help center for the current details in your country.

| Platform | Main platform payout | How it's calculated | Eligibility (summary) | Other built-in income |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube | Ad revenue share (YouTube Partner Program) | 55% of net ad revenue on long-form videos; 45% of the revenue allocated to you from the Shorts pool | 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months, or 10 million public Shorts views in 90 days | Memberships, Super Thanks, Super Chat, Super Stickers, Shopping |
| TikTok | Creator Rewards Program | Based on qualified views of original videos over one minute | 18+, 10,000 followers, 100,000 video views in the last 30 days, eligible countries | LIVE gifts, Subscriptions, TikTok Shop affiliate |
| Facebook | Performance-based payouts and in-stream ads | Based on content performance and ads shown, for eligible creators | Varies by program and country; shown in Professional Dashboard | Stars, Subscriptions, branded content |
| Instagram | Mostly fan support and brand content | Direct payments from fans; Meta has also run invite-only bonus programs | Varies by feature and country | Subscriptions, gifts, badges in Live, branded content |
| X | Creator revenue sharing | A share of revenue tied to engagement on your posts | Requires a paid X subscription plus engagement and follower thresholds set by X | Subscriptions |
| Pinterest | No broad view-based payout | Creators mostly earn off-platform through traffic | n/a | Affiliate links, sending traffic to your site or shop |
| LinkedIn | No view-based payout | Creators earn through clients, jobs and services | n/a | Newsletters and services that lead to work |

YouTube also has a lower tier that opens fan funding features (such as memberships and Super Thanks) at 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in 90 days, and either 3,000 watch hours in 12 months or 3 million Shorts views in 90 days, in available countries. See our guide on [when YouTube starts paying you](/blog/when-does-youtube-start-paying-you) for the steps.

## Platform by platform

### YouTube

YouTube's split is published: at the time of writing, creators receive 55% of net ad revenue from ads shown on their long-form videos, and 45% of the Shorts revenue allocated to them. That transparency, plus how long people watch long videos, is why YouTube is often the first platform creators think of for ad income. Long videos can also carry several ads, which helps. Our breakdown of [how much YouTube pays for 1,000 views](/blog/how-much-does-youtube-pay-for-1000-views) explains RPM and CPM in detail.

### TikTok

TikTok pays through its Creator Rewards Program, which focuses on original videos longer than one minute and counts only qualified views. Short videos under a minute don't earn from the program, so creators who mainly make quick clips often earn more through LIVE gifts, brand deals or TikTok Shop. See [how much TikTok pays for 1 million views](/blog/how-much-money-do-you-get-on-tiktok-for-1-million-views) for worked examples.

### Facebook

Meta pays eligible Facebook creators through performance-based payouts and in-stream ads, plus Stars and Subscriptions. Eligibility and payouts vary by country, and the Professional Dashboard shows what's available to you. More in [how much Facebook Reels pay per 1,000 views](/blog/how-much-does-facebook-reels-pay-per-1000-views).

### Instagram

Instagram has fewer direct view-based payouts. Meta has run bonus programs for some creators, but these have typically been invite-only and have changed over time. Most Instagram creators earn through brand partnerships, Subscriptions, gifts and badges, and by sending followers to their own products.

### X

X shares revenue with eligible creators, based on engagement with their posts. To qualify you need a paid X subscription and to meet engagement and follower thresholds that X sets and has changed several times, so check X's help center for the current criteria. Payouts depend heavily on how much engagement your posts get.


## Why "pays the most" depends on you

### Format

Long-form video, short-form video, livestreams and text posts are each paid differently. A creator making 15-minute tutorials and a creator making 15-second comedy clips are in different payout systems, even on the same platform. On YouTube, long-form and Shorts are paid from different pools. On TikTok, videos under a minute aren't eligible for Creator Rewards.

### Audience location

Advertisers pay more to reach viewers in some countries than others. A channel with most of its viewers in a market with high advertiser demand will usually earn more per view than a similar channel whose viewers are elsewhere, on every ad-funded platform.

### Niche

Topics like personal finance, software and business attract advertisers willing to pay more per ad than topics like general entertainment. Your niche can matter as much as your platform.

### Your mix of income

For many creators, platform payouts are the smaller part. Brand deals, affiliate commissions, subscriptions and your own products often depend more on the trust and attention of your audience than on which platform they found you on.

### A hypothetical comparison

The numbers below are **hypothetical**, purely to show how format and audience change the math. They are not real or average rates.

Imagine two creators, each with 1 million views in a month:

- **Creator A** makes long tutorials. Suppose 70% of views are monetized and the effective rate is $4 per 1,000 monetized views. That's 700 × $4 = **$2,800**.
- **Creator B** makes short entertainment clips. Suppose only 30% of views qualify for payouts and the effective rate is $0.50. That's 300 × $0.50 = **$150**.

Same view count, very different results, and Creator B might still out-earn Creator A through brand deals. The lesson: compare your own RPM on each platform, not the headline numbers you see online.

## How to find the platform that pays you the most

1. **Start with your format.** If you naturally make long videos, YouTube's long-form program is worth prioritizing. If you make short clips, look at each short-form program's rules and at off-platform income.
2. **Check eligibility on each platform.** Read the official requirements and see which you already meet.
3. **Post the same content on several platforms for a few months.** Reformat as needed and write captions for each.
4. **Compare your own numbers.** Look at earnings per 1,000 views and, just as important, which platform brings brand inquiries, email sign-ups or sales.
5. **Double down on what works.** Keep a presence elsewhere, but put your best effort where your audience and income actually grow.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which social media platform pays the most per view?

No platform pays a fixed amount per view, and rates differ by format, audience location, niche and season. YouTube's long-form ad revenue share is the most established program, but your own results on each platform are the only reliable comparison.

### Does Instagram pay creators for views?

Instagram has run invite-only bonus programs at times, but most Instagram creators earn through brand partnerships, Subscriptions, gifts, badges and their own products rather than a standard per-view payout. Check your professional dashboard to see what's available to you.

### Do you get paid on X?

Eligible creators can earn through X's creator revenue sharing and Subscriptions. You need a paid X subscription and to meet X's engagement and follower thresholds, which have changed over time, so check X's help center for the current rules.

### Is YouTube Shorts or TikTok better for making money?

It depends. YouTube Shorts pays 45% of the revenue allocated to you from the Shorts pool once you're in the Partner Program, while TikTok's Creator Rewards Program only pays for videos over one minute. Many creators post vertical videos to both and compare their own results.

### Can you make money on Pinterest or LinkedIn?

Neither currently offers a broad payout per view comparable to YouTube's. Creators earn on Pinterest mostly by driving traffic to their own sites, shops and affiliate links, and on LinkedIn through clients, jobs and services their posts attract.
