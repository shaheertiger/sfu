You're deciding where to put your effort, and you want to know which platform actually pays. There's no single answer that holds for every creator, but **YouTube has the longest-running and most clearly published ad revenue share**: at the time of writing, creators get 55% of net ad revenue on long-form videos and 45% of the revenue allocated to them from the Shorts pool. That's why many long-form creators earn most of their platform income there.

![Which social media platform pays the most in 2026: YouTube's published ad share leads, then TikTok and Facebook programs, with Instagram mostly brand deals](/images/blog/what-social-media-platform-pays-the-most-hero-1200x675.webp)

TikTok, Facebook, Instagram and X each pay in different ways, though, and the one that pays you the most depends on the format you make, where your audience lives, your niche, and how much you earn off the platform through brand deals and your own products.

This guide compares how each platform pays, explains what changes the answer, works through a hypothetical example, and shows how to test which platform pays best for you.

## Why There's No Single Answer (How Each Platform Pays)

This table compares payout **models**, not rates. Rates vary widely and change often, so we don't list them here. Eligibility rules are summarized at the time of writing; check each platform's help center for the current details in your country.

| Platform | Main platform payout | How it's calculated | Eligibility (summary) | Other built-in income |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube | Ad revenue share (YouTube Partner Program) | 55% of net ad revenue on long-form videos; 45% of the revenue allocated to you from the Shorts pool | 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months, or 10 million public Shorts views in 90 days | Memberships, Super Thanks, Super Chat, Super Stickers, Shopping |
| TikTok | Creator Rewards Program | Based on qualified views of original videos over one minute | 18+, 10,000 followers, 100,000 video views in the last 30 days, eligible countries | LIVE gifts, Subscriptions, TikTok Shop affiliate |
| Facebook | Performance-based payouts and in-stream ads | Based on content performance and ads shown, for eligible creators | Varies by program and country; shown in Professional Dashboard | Stars, Subscriptions, branded content |

See [when YouTube starts paying you](/blog/when-does-youtube-start-paying-you).

### What Most People Don't Know

For many creators, platform payouts are the smaller part of their income. Brand deals, affiliate commissions, subscriptions and your own products often depend more on the trust and attention of your audience than on which platform they found you on.

> **Key point:** "Which platform pays the most" is really "which platform pays the most for my format, my audience and my niche." Only your own numbers answer that.

## How Each Platform Pays Creators, One by One

### YouTube: The Most Transparent Split

YouTube's split is published: 55% of net ad revenue from ads on long-form videos, and 45% of the Shorts revenue allocated to you. That transparency, plus how long people watch long videos, is why YouTube is often the first platform creators think of for ad income. Long videos can also carry several ads. Our breakdown of [how much YouTube pays for 1,000 views](/blog/how-much-does-youtube-pay-for-1000-views) explains RPM and CPM.

### TikTok: Only Videos Over One Minute Earn From Rewards

TikTok pays through its Creator Rewards Program, which focuses on original videos longer than one minute and counts only qualified views. Short videos under a minute don't earn from the program, so creators who mainly make quick clips often earn more through LIVE gifts, brand deals or TikTok Shop. See [how much TikTok pays for 1 million views](/blog/how-much-money-do-you-get-on-tiktok-for-1-million-views).

### Facebook: Performance Payouts That Vary by Country

Meta pays eligible Facebook creators through performance-based payouts and in-stream ads, plus Stars and Subscriptions. Eligibility and payouts vary by country, and the Professional Dashboard shows what's available to you. More in [how much Facebook Reels pay per 1,000 views](/blog/how-much-does-facebook-reels-pay-per-1000-views).

### Instagram: Brand Deals, Not View Payouts

Instagram has fewer direct view-based payouts. Meta has run bonus programs for some creators, but these have typically been invite-only and have changed over time. Most Instagram creators earn through brand partnerships, Subscriptions, gifts and badges, and by sending followers to their own products.

### X: Revenue Sharing Behind a Paid Subscription

X shares revenue with eligible creators, based on engagement with their posts. You need a paid X subscription and to meet engagement and follower thresholds that X sets and has changed several times, so check X's help center for the current criteria.

## 4 Things That Decide Which Platform Pays You Most

![What decides which platform pays you most: your format, your audience and niche, and your mix of income](/images/blog/what-social-media-platform-pays-the-most-factors-1200x675.webp)

### 1. What Format Do You Make?

Long-form video, short-form video, livestreams and text posts are each paid differently. On YouTube, long-form and Shorts are paid from different pools. On TikTok, videos under a minute aren't eligible for Creator Rewards.

### 2. Where Does Your Audience Live?

Advertisers pay more to reach viewers in some countries than others. A channel with most of its viewers in a market with high advertiser demand will usually earn more per view, on every ad-funded platform.

### 3. Which Niche Are You In?

Topics like personal finance, software and business attract advertisers willing to pay more per ad than general entertainment. Your niche can matter as much as your platform.

### 4. What's Your Mix of Income?

Brand deals, affiliate commissions, subscriptions and your own products are often the larger part, and they travel with your audience.

Same view count, very different results, and Creator B might still out-earn Creator A through brand deals. Compare your own RPM on each platform, not the headline numbers you see online.

## How to Find the Highest-Paying Platform for You (5 Steps)

1. **Start with your format.** If you naturally make long videos, YouTube's long-form program is worth prioritizing. If you make short clips, look at each short-form program's rules and at off-platform income.
2. **Check eligibility on each platform.** Read the official requirements and see which you already meet.
3. **Post the same content on several platforms for a few months.** Reformat as needed and write captions for each.
4. **Compare your own numbers.** Look at earnings per 1,000 views and, just as important, which platform brings brand inquiries, email sign-ups or sales.
5. **Double down on what works.** Keep a presence elsewhere, but put your best effort where your audience and income actually grow.

> **Pro tip:** Step 3 is where people give up: posting everywhere for months is a lot of work. Batch your videos and schedule them, so the test runs long enough to mean something.

## Buying Followers or Views to Hit Thresholds (Use With Caution)

![Safe ways to reach monetization thresholds, like steady posting, next to risky shortcuts like bought followers and views](/images/blog/what-social-media-platform-pays-the-most-safety-1200x675.webp)

Every program has thresholds, and services sell followers, views and watch hours to get you there faster.

### The Real Risks

- **Platforms check for fake engagement**, and can reject applications, remove views or restrict accounts.
- **Bought views don't earn.** Ad revenue and qualified views come from real viewers.
- **Brands notice.** Fake followers with no engagement make your account less attractive for deals.

> **Bottom line:** Thresholds are a filter for real audiences. Meet them the slow way.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which social media platform pays the most per view?

No platform pays a fixed amount per view, and rates differ by format, audience location, niche and season. YouTube's long-form ad revenue share is the most established program, but your own results on each platform are the only reliable comparison.

### Does Instagram pay creators for views?

Not as a standard per-view payout. Instagram has run invite-only bonus programs at times, but most Instagram creators earn through brand partnerships, Subscriptions, gifts, badges and their own products.

### Do you get paid on X?

Yes, if you're eligible. Creators can earn through X's creator revenue sharing and Subscriptions. You need a paid X subscription and to meet X's engagement and follower thresholds, which have changed over time.

### Is YouTube Shorts or TikTok better for making money?

It depends. YouTube Shorts pays 45% of the revenue allocated to you from the Shorts pool once you're in the Partner Program, while TikTok's Creator Rewards Program only pays for videos over one minute. Many creators post vertical videos to both and compare.

### Can you make money on Pinterest or LinkedIn?

Not through a broad payout per view. Creators earn on Pinterest mostly by driving traffic to their own sites, shops and affiliate links, and on LinkedIn through clients, jobs and services their posts attract.

### How many followers do you need to get paid on TikTok?

10,000 followers for the Creator Rewards Program at the time of writing, plus being 18+, 100,000 video views in the last 30 days and living in an eligible country.

### What are the YouTube Partner Program requirements?

1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months, or 10 million public Shorts views in 90 days, at the time of writing.
