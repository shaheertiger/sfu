You've joined Bluesky, posted a few times, and you're still talking to the same handful of people. To get followers on Bluesky, get into the places people find new accounts: **starter packs**, **custom feeds** for your topic, and the **replies** under well-followed posts. Fill in your profile, follow the people you want to be in conversation with, post a few times a day and reply generously.

What makes Bluesky different is that there's no single algorithm deciding who sees you. Growth comes from being picked up by feeds, packs and reposts in your niche, so tactics that work on Instagram or TikTok often don't apply.

This guide covers where new followers come from, five ways to get in front of them, who to follow, what to avoid, and how to keep a posting rhythm without living in the app.

## Why Growing on Bluesky Works Differently (No Single Algorithm)

![Where new Bluesky followers come from: starter packs, custom feeds, replies, reposts and search](/images/blog/how-to-get-followers-on-bluesky-sources-1200x675.webp)

On most platforms, one recommendation system decides who sees your posts. On Bluesky, your **Following** feed is chronological, and discovery happens through many smaller doors.

| Source | How it works | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starter packs | Curated lists of accounts and feeds that people follow in one tap | Get added to packs in your niche, or make your own |
| Custom feeds | Topic feeds built by the community from keywords, hashtags or member lists | Post on-topic with the words and tags those feeds pick up |
| Discover feed | Bluesky's own feed of popular and relevant posts | Post things that earn replies and reposts early |
| Replies | People read the replies under big posts | Add something useful under posts in your field |
| Reposts and quotes | Your post reaches the reposter's followers | Write posts worth sharing; credit others when you quote |
| Search | Bluesky search covers people and posts | Use clear words in your bio and posts |

### What Most People Don't Know

Starter packs are the biggest lever most new accounts never pull. New users often follow a whole pack at once, so being in one, or making one, can put you in front of hundreds of people who are actively looking for accounts like yours. Research on Bluesky's 2024 growth reported that accounts included in starter packs gained far more followers than similar accounts that weren't.

> **Key point:** On Bluesky, you grow by being findable in your niche, not by gaming a feed. Packs, feeds and replies all reward the same thing: being clearly about something.

## How to Get Followers on Bluesky: 5 Ways That Work

![Five ways to get followers on Bluesky, from joining starter packs to posting on a steady rhythm](/images/blog/how-to-get-followers-on-bluesky-five-ways-1200x675.webp)

### 1. Get Into Starter Packs (or Make Your Own)

A starter pack is a shared list of up to 150 accounts plus a few feeds. Search for packs in your field, and if you're active and on-topic, ask the curator politely whether they'd add you.

Better still, make a pack yourself: gather the 30 to 100 accounts worth following in your niche, include yourself, and share it. People who follow it see your posts from day one.

> **Pro tip:** A pack about a narrow topic ("UK bakers", "Rust developers") gets followed by the right people. A pack of "cool accounts" gets followed by nobody in particular.

### 2. Post Into Custom Feeds

Custom feeds are how niche communities find each other on Bluesky: there are feeds for science, art, gardening, cities, sports teams, programming languages and more. Find them under **Feeds** or in **Discover**, read each feed's description, and note what it picks up. Some include any post with a keyword or hashtag, others only members of a list. Our guide on [whether Bluesky uses hashtags](/blog/does-bluesky-use-hashtags) explains how tags feed into them.

Don't stuff tags onto unrelated posts. Feed owners remove accounts that spam.

### 3. Reply More Than You Post

The fastest way to get noticed is a good reply under a popular post in your field. Add a fact, a tip, a question or a counterpoint, not "great post". Readers click through to profiles of people with interesting replies, which is why a clear bio matters.

### 4. Make Your Profile Easy to Follow

- A real photo or logo and a banner.
- A bio that says what you post about, in words people search for.
- A pinned post that shows your best work or explains the account.
- A [domain handle](/blog/how-to-change-bluesky-handle) if you have a website. It proves who you are and looks trustworthy.

### 5. Post on a Steady Rhythm

A few posts a day, every day, beats ten posts once a week. Bluesky's audience is most active on weekday mornings and evenings in the US and Europe, so post when your followers are online and stay around to answer early replies.

| Tactic | Effort | How fast it works | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Join or make a starter pack | One-off | Fast, if the pack is shared | Any niche with an active community |
| Post into custom feeds | A minute per post | Steady | Clear topics with feeds |
| Thoughtful replies | A little every day | Steady | Everyone, especially new accounts |
| Profile clean-up | One-off | Helps every other tactic | Everyone |
| Daily posting rhythm | Ongoing | Slow, then compounding | Accounts in it for months |

## Who to Follow on Bluesky (Build Your Own List)

Lists of "best accounts" go stale quickly, so build your own:

- **Follow starter packs** in your interests, and unfollow anyone who doesn't fit after a week.
- **Check who your favorite accounts follow** and repost.
- **Search by topic** and look at the people behind the posts you like.
- **Subscribe to two or three feeds** in your field. The accounts that show up there repeatedly are the ones to follow.

Following 100 to 200 relevant accounts also tells feeds and other users what your account is about. You don't need to follow everyone back: your Following feed is chronological, so following everyone fills it with noise.

## How Long It Takes to Grow on Bluesky (Realistic Scenarios)

| Situation | What you do | What to expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand-new account, no pack | Daily posts and replies | Slow for the first few weeks; real momentum after about 60 days |
| Added to a popular starter pack | Keep posting on-topic | Faster growth, sometimes in days |
| Moving an audience from another platform | Share your handle there | Depends on how many follow you over |
| Posting off-topic, no replies | Occasional posts | Little growth |

These are rough patterns, not promises. Your niche and how active its community is matter more than any tactic.

## Bought Followers and Follow-for-Follow Schemes (Use With Caution)

![What grows a Bluesky account, compared with shortcuts that make it look less trustworthy](/images/blog/how-to-get-followers-on-bluesky-shortcuts-1200x675.webp)

When growth is slow, shortcuts look tempting: bought followers, "follow train" threads, mass-following in the hope of follow-backs.

### The Real Risks

- **Bought followers don't read or reply.** They pad the number and nothing else.
- **They make your account look less trustworthy** to the real people deciding whether to follow you.
- **Mass-following and spammy tagging get you dropped** from feeds and packs, whose owners curate them by hand.
- **A Following feed full of strangers** is no use to you, since it's chronological.

### Safer Ways to Speed Things Up

- Make a starter pack for your niche and share it.
- Reply to five good posts in your field every day.
- Cross-promote your Bluesky handle where your audience already follows you.

> **Bottom line:** Bluesky rewards being useful in a niche. Buying a number skips the part that actually brings followers.

## Keeping a Daily Rhythm When You Don't Live on Bluesky

The tactic that takes the most discipline is the daily rhythm. Take Ana, an illustrator who also posts on Instagram and Threads. She wants two posts a day on Bluesky: a work-in-progress image in the morning and a tip in the evening. That's 14 posts a week, and posting each live meant opening Bluesky at 8 AM and 7 PM every day, then getting pulled into the feed.

Now she writes the week's 14 posts on Sunday in about 40 minutes, adjusts each one for Bluesky's 300-character limit, and spends her daily Bluesky time on replies, which is what actually grows the account.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I get followers on Bluesky fast?

Get into a starter pack in your niche, or make one and share it. That's the quickest route, because new users often follow a whole pack at once.

### How long does it take to get followers on Bluesky?

Most new accounts grow slowly for the first few weeks. Expect real momentum after about 60 days of daily posting and replying, sooner if you're added to a popular starter pack.

### Do hashtags help you get followers on Bluesky?

They help when a custom feed or search uses them. Use one or two relevant tags; they count toward the 300-character limit.

### Should I follow back everyone on Bluesky?

No. Follow accounts whose posts you want to see. Your Following feed is chronological, so following everyone back fills it with noise.

### Is it worth buying followers on Bluesky?

No. Bought followers don't read or reply, and they make your account look less trustworthy. Our guide on [whether Bluesky is worth it](/blog/is-bluesky-worth-it) covers how to judge results by replies and clicks instead.

### How many accounts can a starter pack have?

Up to 150 accounts, plus a few feeds. Packs of 30 to 100 well-chosen accounts are easier for people to trust.

### What should I post on Bluesky to get followers?

Posts that are clearly about your topic and invite a reply: tips, questions, work in progress and useful links. Off-topic posts make it hard for feeds and people to place you.

### Does a custom domain handle help on Bluesky?

It helps people trust that you are who you say you are, especially for brands and creators with a website. It doesn't change how feeds rank your posts.
