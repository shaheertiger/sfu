You've found a great clip on Bluesky, or you want a copy of one you posted last month, and there's no download option in the menu. Bluesky doesn't have a built-in download button for videos at the time of writing, for your own posts or anyone else's. The simplest way to have a copy of your own video is to **keep the original file you uploaded**.

![How to download videos from Bluesky: keep your originals, bookmark others, and ask creators before reusing](/images/blog/how-to-download-bluesky-videos-hero-1200x675.webp)

To save someone else's video for later, **bookmark the post or copy its link**. If you want the actual file to use or repost, **ask the creator** for it. Third-party downloaders exist, but they come with risks.

This guide covers why there's no button, five ways to keep or save videos, how downloaders work, and how to post your own video on other networks without downloading anything.

## Why Bluesky Has No Download Button (and What That Means for Quality)

Bluesky compresses video when you upload it and streams it as HLS: a playlist of short segments that play one after another. There's no single video file sitting behind the post for a button to hand you.

![Why a downloaded Bluesky video looks worse than the original file](/images/blog/how-to-download-bluesky-videos-why-1200x675.webp)

### What Most People Don't Know

The copy on Bluesky is never better than your original. Bluesky compresses on upload and may stream a lower-quality version on slow connections, so any downloader or screen recording captures that compressed version. If you posted the video, the best copy you'll ever have is the one already on your phone or computer.

> **Key point:** For your own videos, "downloading from Bluesky" is almost always the wrong move. Keep the original file and you'll never need to.

## How to Save Bluesky Videos: 5 Ways That Work

Bluesky's menu labels change between app versions, so if a name doesn't match exactly, look for the closest one.

![Five ways to keep or save Bluesky videos, from your original file to asking the creator](/images/blog/how-to-download-bluesky-videos-ways-1200x675.webp)

| What you want | Method | Gives you a file? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A video you posted | Keep the original upload, or re-export it from your editor | Yes |
| A copy of all your posts | Settings > Account > Export my data | Posts only; keep media separately |
| Someone else's video, to watch later | Bookmark the post or copy its link | No |
| Someone else's video, to show your followers | Repost or quote it | No |
| Someone else's video, to use in your own content | Ask the creator for the file and permission | Yes, with permission |
| A quick personal copy | Your phone's screen recorder | Yes, lower quality |

### Way 1: Keep the Original File (Your Own Videos)

Keep the source file in your camera roll, cloud drive or editing project. If you've deleted it, re-export it from your editor. If you post the same clip to several networks, upload it once into a scheduler's media library and it's stored there too.

### Way 2: Export Your Data (a Backup, Not a Video Folder)

Bluesky's **Export my data** option (under Settings, then Account) downloads your account repository, a single file of your posts, likes and follows. It's useful as a backup but isn't a simple folder of videos, so don't rely on it to get media back.

> **Pro tip:** The step people get wrong is treating Export my data as a video backup. Make a folder called "Posted" in your cloud drive and drop each final video in it the day you post. That's your real archive.

### Way 3: Bookmark or Copy the Link (Other People's Videos)

**Bookmark** the post so you can find it again from your saved posts, or **copy the link** from the share menu and keep it in your notes. Bookmarks don't notify the creator.

### Way 4: Repost or Quote It

If you want your followers to see a video, repost or quote it. The creator keeps the views and credit, and they'll see it in their notifications.

### Way 5: Ask the Creator for the File

If you want to use someone's video in your own content, message them and ask. Most creators will send the original, which is better quality than any download, and you'll have their permission in writing.

### A Quick Personal Copy: Screen Recording

On a phone, the screen recorder (Control Center on iPhone, Quick Settings on Android) captures a video as it plays. The result is lower quality and picks up anything on screen, so it's fine for a personal reminder and nothing more.

## Third-Party Bluesky Video Downloaders (Use With Caution)

Search for "Bluesky video downloader" and you'll find websites, browser extensions and command-line tools. They take the HLS segments Bluesky streams and stitch them back into an MP4.

![What keeps you safe when using a Bluesky video downloader, and what puts your account at risk](/images/blog/how-to-download-bluesky-videos-downloaders-1200x675.webp)

### The Real Risks

- **Ads and malware.** Downloader sites are often covered in pop-ups and fake download buttons. Browser extensions can read every page you visit, so only install one you trust.
- **Password requests.** Public videos don't need a login. If a tool asks for one, use an app password at most, and delete it afterwards.
- **Copyright.** A downloaded file is someone else's work. Reposting it, editing it into your own video or using it in an ad needs the creator's permission, and posting without it can get your account reported.

### Safety Checklist

- Never enter your main Bluesky password into a downloader.
- Skip extensions you don't need, and remove ones you've stopped using.
- Click only the real download link, not the ads around it.
- Ask the creator before you reuse anything.

> **Bottom line:** A downloader gives you a compressed copy and some risk. For your own videos, the original is better; for someone else's, their permission matters more than the file.

## What to Do Next: 3 Scenarios

- **It's your video.** Find the original on your phone or drive. If it's gone, re-export it from your editor, and start a "Posted" folder for next time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you download your own videos from Bluesky?

Not with a button in the app at the time of writing. Keep the original file you uploaded; that's also the best-quality copy.

### Does Bluesky notify someone when you save their video?

Bluesky doesn't notify anyone about bookmarks or screen recordings. Reposts and quotes do show up in the creator's notifications.

### Why is the video quality worse after downloading?

Bluesky compresses video on upload and may stream a lower-quality version on slow connections. Downloaders and screen recordings capture that compressed version.

### Is it legal to download Bluesky videos?

Saving a public video for personal viewing is usually low-risk, but the creator owns the copyright. Reusing or reposting it without permission can break copyright law and Bluesky's rules.

### Does Export my data include my videos?

Not as a simple folder of videos. It downloads your account repository, a single file of your posts, likes and follows, so keep your media files separately.

### Do Bluesky video downloaders need my password?

No. Public videos don't need a login. If a tool asks for one, don't use your main password; an app password at most, deleted afterwards.

### How do I save a Bluesky video to watch later?

Bookmark the post, or copy its link from the share menu. Both keep it easy to find without downloading anything.

### Can I post my Bluesky video on TikTok or Instagram?

Yes, if it's yours. Upload the original file rather than a copy downloaded from Bluesky, so it isn't compressed twice.
