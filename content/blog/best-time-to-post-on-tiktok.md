The best time to post on TikTok for most accounts is **early evening, around 6 to 9 PM in your audience's time zone**, with a second window in the **morning (7 to 9 AM) on weekdays** and **late morning on weekends**. Those are the hours people usually have time to scroll. Your exact best time is shown in TikTok's analytics under your followers' most active times, and this guide shows you how to read it.

Treat the times below as starting points. TikTok shows videos to people based on what they watch, not just when you post, so a good video can find viewers days later. Timing mostly affects the first batch of viewers, and that first batch decides how far a video goes next.

## Best time to post on TikTok by day

These windows are in your **audience's local time**. They're based on typical daily routines (commutes, lunch breaks, evenings at home), not on a study of TikTok data, so use them to choose what to test first.

| Monday | 7 PM | 8 AM | Evenings are free after the first workday; morning phone check |
| Tuesday | 7 PM | 8 AM | Routine settles in; evening scrolling at home |
| Wednesday | 7 PM | 12 PM | Mid-week lunch break and evening downtime |
| Thursday | 7 PM | 8 AM | Evening viewing picks up before the weekend |
| Friday | 5 PM | 9 PM | People scroll before going out and late in the evening |
| Saturday | 11 AM | 8 PM | Slow mornings and evenings at home |
| Sunday | 7 PM | 12 PM | The longest evening scroll of the week for many people |

If your audience is students, shift workers, parents of young kids or people in another region, skip the table and use your analytics (below).

### Best time to post on TikTok on Monday
Start with **7 PM**, when people are home after the first workday of the week. Test **8 AM** as a second slot for quick, easy-to-watch videos that suit a morning scroll.

### Best time to post on TikTok on Tuesday
Start with **7 PM** and test **8 AM**. By Tuesday most people have settled into their weekly routine, so evening viewing is usually steady.

### Best time to post on TikTok on Wednesday
Start with **7 PM** and test **12 PM**. A mid-week lunch break is worth trying for short, useful videos such as tips, quick recipes and how-tos.

### Best time to post on TikTok on Thursday
Start with **7 PM** and test **8 AM**. Thursday evenings often run a little later as the weekend gets close, so if 7 PM works, try 8 or 9 PM too.

### Best time to post on TikTok on Friday
Start with **5 PM**, before people head out, and test **9 PM** for late-evening scrolling. Friday nights are less predictable than other weeknights, so give this day a few weeks of testing.

### Best time to post on TikTok on Saturday
Start with **11 AM** and test **8 PM**. Saturday mornings start later, and people scroll between plans. Early mornings are usually quiet.

### Best time to post on TikTok on Sunday
Start with **7 PM** and test **12 PM**. Sunday evening is a common time to relax at home before the week starts, which suits longer videos and storytimes.

## The best time to post on TikTok today

To find the best time today, open your analytics (steps below), look at today's busiest hour among your followers, and post **one to two hours before it**. That gives the video time to collect its first views and likes before most of your followers come online. Without analytics yet, use today's row in the table above.

## Best time to post on TikTok in EST, CST and PST

The windows above are in your audience's local time. If most of your followers are in one US time zone, post in that zone's local time. If they're spread across the country, choose a time that falls in the evening on both coasts.

| Goal | Eastern (EST/EDT) | Central (CST/CDT) | Mountain | Pacific (PST/PDT) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Evening on both coasts | 9 PM | 8 PM | 7 PM | 6 PM |
| East Coast evening | 7 PM | 6 PM | 5 PM | 4 PM |
| West Coast evening | 10 PM | 9 PM | 8 PM | 7 PM |
| Weekday morning, East Coast | 8 AM | 7 AM | 6 AM | 5 AM |

A post at **9 PM Eastern (6 PM Pacific)** lands in the evening for almost the whole US, which makes it a good default for a nationwide audience. If most of your followers are on one coast, post for that coast.

## How to find your best time in TikTok analytics

TikTok shows when your followers are online. Menu names change between app versions, but at the time of writing the path is:

1. Open your profile and tap the menu (three lines), then **TikTok Studio**.
2. Tap **Analytics**.
3. Open the **Followers** tab.
4. Scroll to **Most active times**. It shows follower activity by hour, and you can switch between days.

On the web, go to TikTok Studio, open **Analytics**, then **Followers**.

### How to read the chart
- **Check the time zone.** The chart has its own time zone label, which may not match where your followers live. Convert to your audience's time before scheduling.
- **Post before the peak, not at it.** A video posted an hour before the busiest hour is already being watched and liked as activity rises.
- **Compare days.** If one or two days are clearly busier, post your strongest videos then.
- **Check your best videos.** In **Content**, sort recent posts by views and note when each one went out. If your top videos cluster in one window, that's your answer.

Follower activity only appears once you have some followers. New accounts should use the table above and their own video results instead.

## Worst times to post on TikTok

There's no universally bad day, but some hours are weak for most audiences:
- **Overnight (roughly 1 to 5 AM local time):** few of your followers are awake to give the video its first views.
- **Mid-morning on weekdays (about 10 AM to 12 PM):** many people are at work or school.
- **Times that suit you but not your audience:** a 9 PM post from London lands at 4 PM in New York.

A great video posted at a weak time can still take off later. Timing affects the start; the video decides the rest.

## How often to post on TikTok

Consistency matters more than the perfect hour. There's no required number of posts, and **a schedule you can keep**, even three or four videos a week, beats a daily plan you abandon after two weeks. If you post more than once a day, space videos a few hours apart so each gets its own first batch of viewers.

## A simple four-week test

1. **Pick two or three windows** from the table or your analytics.
2. **Post similar videos in each window** so you're testing timing, not content.
3. **Rotate windows for four weeks.**
4. **Compare after the same time**, for example 48 hours, using views, average watch time and follows.
5. **Keep the winners** and retest every few months, or when your audience changes.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best time to post on TikTok?
For most accounts, early evening (6 to 9 PM) in your audience's time zone, plus 7 to 9 AM on weekdays and late morning on weekends. Your followers' most active times in TikTok Studio analytics give you the exact hours for your account.

### What is the best day to post on TikTok?
It depends on your audience. Thursday to Sunday are good days to test first, since entertainment viewing often picks up toward the weekend, but check the day-by-day view of your followers' activity in TikTok Studio, then compare how your own videos did on each day.

### What is the best time to post on TikTok in EST?
For an East Coast audience, start with 7 PM EST on weekdays and 11 AM on Saturday. For a nationwide US audience, 9 PM EST is a good default because it's 6 PM on the West Coast.

### What is the best time to post on TikTok in PST?
For a West Coast audience, start with 7 PM PST. To reach both coasts, post at 6 PM PST, which is 9 PM on the East Coast.

### Does posting time matter on TikTok?
Yes, but less than the video itself. Posting when your followers are active helps a video get its first views quickly, and those first views affect how widely TikTok shows it. A strong video can still find viewers days later.

### Should I post on TikTok at the same time every day?
A consistent slot helps you build a habit and lets you compare results fairly. Start with one reliable time per day, then adjust individual days once your analytics show differences.
