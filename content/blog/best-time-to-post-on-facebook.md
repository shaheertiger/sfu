You've written a good Facebook post and you don't want it to vanish into a quiet feed. For most Pages, the best time to post on Facebook is **weekday mornings, roughly 7 to 10 AM in your audience's time zone**, and the best days are usually **Tuesday to Thursday**. Afternoons tend to be weaker, and **Saturday is usually the weakest day**.

![Best time to post on Facebook: weekday mornings from 7 to 10 AM, with Tuesday to Thursday strongest and Saturday weakest](/images/blog/best-time-to-post-on-facebook-hero-1200x675.webp)

Those times are a starting point, not a rule. Studies published by social media tools disagree on exact hours, and your own audience may keep different habits from the average.

Our [hour-by-hour Facebook heatmap](/best-time-to-post/facebook) often peaks somewhere else, such as an evening or the weekend. The two answers measure different things: the heatmap tracks when people are active on Facebook, while the weekday-morning advice comes from engagement studies, which also reflect how crowded the feed is. Test one slot from each and keep the one that wins for your Page.

This guide gives you a time for every day of the week, the slots to avoid, a simple four-week test to find your own best time in Insights, and a way to post at those times without setting an alarm.

## Why Weekday Mornings Win on Facebook (and When They Don't)

![Weekday mornings compared with weekday afternoons and Saturdays as times to post on Facebook](/images/blog/best-time-to-post-on-facebook-why-1200x675.webp)

Facebook is a morning habit for many people. They check in on friends, family and local news before work or at the start of the workday. A post that goes out just before that check-in has the whole day to collect comments and shares.

Most published studies agree on the general pattern, even when they disagree on the exact hour:

- **Mornings beat afternoons** on weekdays.
- **Mid-week beats the weekend**, with Saturday usually the weakest day.
- **Late night reaches few people**, unless your audience keeps late hours.

### What Most People Don't Know

The best time isn't just about when people are online. It's about what happens in the first hours after you post. Facebook can keep showing a post that gets comments and shares well after it goes live, so a post that starts strong in the morning keeps working into the afternoon. A weak slot doesn't make a good post fail, and a perfect slot can't rescue a dull one.

> **Key point:** All the times in this guide are in your audience's local time, not yours. If your followers live three time zones away, 8 AM means 8 AM on their clock.

### Monday: 9 AM, Then Test 8 PM

Post at **9 AM**, after people have dealt with the first emails of the week. Monday mornings are busy, so for some audiences the evening slot does better. Try both for a few weeks.

### Tuesday: 8 AM for Important Posts

Start with **8 AM** and test **7 PM**. Tuesday is part of the strong mid-week stretch, so it's a good day for posts you care about.

### Wednesday: 8 AM, Often the Best Slot of the Week

Start with **8 AM** and test **6 PM**. Wednesday is often the best day on Facebook, so put announcements and offers here.

### Thursday: 9 AM (or 8 AM)

Start with **9 AM** and test **8 AM**. Thursday mornings are among the strongest slots of the week for many Pages.

### Friday: Get Out Early at 8 AM

Start with **8 AM** and test **7 AM**. By the afternoon many people are thinking about the weekend.

### Saturday: 10 AM Only If You Have Something Good

Start with **10 AM** and test **9 PM**. Saturday is usually the weakest day, so post here only if it suits your business, such as events, restaurants or weekend offers.

### Sunday: 10 AM for Longer Posts

Start with **10 AM** and test **9 AM**. Sunday mornings are relaxed, which suits longer posts, photo sets and community updates.

> **Pro tip:** If you only post three times a week, use Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday mornings. That covers the strongest stretch with the least effort.

## Best Day vs Worst Day to Post on Facebook

For most Pages, the best days are **Wednesday, Thursday and Tuesday**. Mid-week posts tend to outperform the start and end of the week. The **worst day is usually Saturday**, with Sunday a little better.

| Slot | Usually | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weekday mornings, 7–10 AM | Strongest | People check Facebook before or at the start of work |
| Weekday afternoons, about 12–5 PM | Weaker | Attention goes to work and errands |
| Weekday evenings | Worth testing | More time to watch video and read |
| Late night, about 11 PM–5 AM | Weakest | Few people online, unless your audience keeps late hours |
| Saturday | Weakest day | Plans, errands and time away from screens |

There are exceptions. A restaurant, venue or local event organizer may get its best results on Friday and Saturday, because that's when its audience is making plans. If your Page is like that, trust your own numbers over the general pattern. ## How to Find Your Own Best Time on Facebook (Free, 4-Week Test)

![Four steps to find your own best time to post on Facebook: pick slots, rotate, compare and keep the winners](/images/blog/best-time-to-post-on-facebook-test-1200x675.webp)

The table above is a guess about the average Page. Your Insights show what works for yours.

### Step 1: Open Insights in Meta Business Suite

Open **Meta Business Suite** (or your Page's **Professional dashboard**) and go to **Insights**. Meta changes these menus often, so labels may be different on your Page.

### Step 2: Sort Your Recent Posts by Reach

Open **Content** to see your recent posts with their reach and engagement. Sort by reach or engagement and note the **day and hour** each of your top posts went out.

### Step 3: Check Where Your Audience Lives

Check **Audience** for your top cities and countries, so you know which time zone to schedule for. If your Insights include a chart of when your audience is online, post shortly before the busiest hour.

### Step 4: Run the Test, Then Keep the Winners

1. Choose **two or three slots** from the table.
2. **Rotate them for three to four weeks**, posting similar types of content in each.
3. **Compare after the same time**, for example 48 hours, using reach, reactions, comments and shares.
4. **Keep the slots that win** and drop the rest. Retest every few months.

> **Pro tip:** Compare like with like. A Reel at 8 PM against a photo post at 8 AM tells you about formats, not times.

## Time Zones: EST, CST, PST and Audiences Abroad

A 9 AM post for a Chicago audience is 9 AM Central. If your followers are spread across the US, Eastern time is often the best anchor, because a large share of the US population lives there. A post at **10 AM Eastern** is 7 AM Pacific, which still catches West Coast morning scrolling.

| Your audience | Schedule for | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One city or region | That local time | 8 AM Central for a Chicago bakery |
| Spread across the US | Eastern time | 10 AM ET reaches 7 AM PT |
| Mostly in another country | Their clock, not yours | 8 AM in London while you sleep in New York |

## Best Time to Post Reels and Marketplace Listings on Facebook

Reels can be recommended to people who don't follow your Page, so they depend less on your followers' active hours than regular posts. Start Reels in the same morning slots, then test evenings, when people have more time to watch video. Compare Reels with other Reels, not with photo posts.

Marketplace works differently. Buyers browse when they have time to shop, so **evenings (around 6 to 9 PM) and weekend mornings** are good starting points to test. Listings are posted from Facebook itself; Schedule For You doesn't post to Marketplace.

Timing gets your post seen, but whether people react depends on the post. Ask a real question instead of "like if you agree" bait, which Facebook downranks. Use photos and short videos, and reply to comments quickly, especially in the first hour. ## What to Do Next: Your First Week of Facebook Posting

![What helps Facebook posts reach people at the right time, next to habits that waste a good slot](/images/blog/best-time-to-post-on-facebook-next-1200x675.webp)

1. **Pick three slots** from the table: Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday mornings are the safest.
2. **Check your audience's time zone** in Insights before you schedule anything.
3. **Schedule a week of posts** so you hit every slot, even on busy mornings.
4. **Reply to comments in the first hour** after each post goes live.
5. **Compare after four weeks**, keep the winners and test one new slot.

What helps:

- ✓ Posting in your audience's time zone
- ✓ Real questions people want to answer
- ✓ Replying to comments quickly

What wastes a good slot:

- ✗ Engagement bait such as "like if you agree"
- ✗ Changing the time every week before you have results
- ✗ Comparing Reels with photo posts

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best time to post on Facebook?

Weekday mornings, roughly 7 to 10 AM in your audience's time zone, are the best starting point for most Pages. Wednesday and Thursday mornings are often the strongest slots of the week.

### What is the best day to post on Facebook?

Wednesday is often the best day, followed by Thursday and Tuesday. Saturday is usually the weakest, but local businesses and event Pages can see different patterns, so check your own Insights.

### What is the worst time to post on Facebook?

Weekday afternoons (around 12 to 5 PM), late nights and Saturdays tend to get the least engagement for most Pages.

### What is the best time to post on Facebook for likes?

Post when your audience is online and has a moment to react, which is weekday mornings for most Pages. Content matters more than timing for likes, so pair a good slot with a post people want to respond to.

### Do these times work in any time zone?

Yes. They're in your audience's local time, so 8 AM means 8 AM where your followers live. If your audience is in another time zone, schedule for their clock.

### What is the best time to post on Facebook on Wednesday?

Start with 8 AM and test 6 PM in your audience's time zone. Wednesday is often the best day of the week on Facebook.

### What is the best time to post Reels on Facebook?

Start with the same weekday morning slots, then test evenings. Reels can reach people who don't follow your Page, so they depend less on your followers' active hours.

### How long should I test a posting time?

Three to four weeks per set of slots is a good minimum. Compare each post after the same amount of time, for example 48 hours, and only compare similar types of post.
