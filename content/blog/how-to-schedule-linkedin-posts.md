You've written a strong LinkedIn post at 10 p.m. and you'd rather it went out at 8 tomorrow morning. To schedule a LinkedIn post, click **Start a post**, write it, click the **clock icon** next to the Post button, pick a date and time, click **Next** and then **Schedule**. It works on your profile and any company page you manage, up to about three months ahead at the time of writing.

![How to schedule a LinkedIn post: start a post, click the clock icon, pick a date and time, then schedule](/images/blog/how-to-schedule-linkedin-posts-hero-1200x675.webp)

It's free and good enough for one person posting to one account. It gets awkward when several people's profiles have to go out around the same launch, or when the same post also goes to X, Bluesky or Threads.

This guide covers the native method for profiles and pages, what the scheduler can't do, where it breaks for teams, and how to schedule several profiles from one calendar.

## Why LinkedIn's Scheduler Works for One Person but Not a Team

![LinkedIn's built-in scheduler compared: what it handles for one person and where it stops for a team](/images/blog/how-to-schedule-linkedin-posts-limits-1200x675.webp)

LinkedIn's scheduler is built around one login. Every scheduled post lives in that account's own list, and nobody else can see it, review it or move it, unless it's a company page with several admins.

| Question | Answer at the time of writing |
| --- | --- |
| How far ahead? | Up to about three months |
| Profile and company page? | Both, each from its own composer |
| Text, images and video? | Yes |
| Polls and document (PDF) posts? | Varies by account and version; check whether the clock icon appears |
| Articles and newsletters? | Not through the post scheduler |
| Several profiles in one place? | No. Each person schedules from their own login |
| Other networks in the same calendar? | No |
| Approval before a post goes out? | No |

### What Most People Don't Know

A scheduled post doesn't appear on your profile until it publishes, and there's no obvious "scheduled" tab. To check, edit or cancel it later, you open the composer again, click the clock icon and choose **View all scheduled posts**. Our guide to [seeing scheduled posts on LinkedIn](/blog/how-to-see-scheduled-posts-on-linkedin) covers editing, rescheduling and deleting.

> **Key point:** LinkedIn's scheduler is free and reliable for one account. The cost appears when several people need to see, review or move each other's posts.

## How to Schedule a Post on Your LinkedIn Profile (5 Steps, Free)

![Five steps to schedule a LinkedIn profile post: start a post, write it, click the clock icon, pick a time, schedule](/images/blog/how-to-schedule-linkedin-posts-profile-1200x675.webp)

LinkedIn changes the composer often, so labels and the icon's position may differ slightly in your version.

### Step 1: Click Start a Post

On your LinkedIn home page, click **Start a post**.

### Step 2: Write the Post and Add Media

Add any images, video or a document.

### Step 3: Click the Clock Icon, Not Post

The **clock icon** sits next to **Post**. On some versions it's at the bottom of the composer, on others at the top. This is the step people miss, clicking Post out of habit.

### Step 4: Choose a Date and Time

Pick both, then click **Next**.

### Step 5: Check the Preview and Click Schedule

In the mobile app the route is the same: tap **Post**, write it, tap the clock icon, pick the date and time, then tap **Next** and **Schedule**.

> **Pro tip:** Read the first two lines in the preview before you click Schedule. They're all most people see before "see more", and they're much harder to fix once the post is live.

## How to Schedule a Post on a LinkedIn Company Page (Admins Only)

You need an admin role on the page (super admin or content admin).

1. Open your page's **admin view** (from **For Business** or the page link under your profile picture).
2. Click **Create** or **Start a post** so the composer opens as the page, not as you. The page's logo should show at the top.
3. Write the post, then click the **clock icon**.
4. Pick a date and time, click **Next**, then **Schedule**.

Page posts and profile posts are scheduled separately. A post scheduled as the page only shows in the page's scheduled list, and other admins of the page can see, reschedule or delete it too.

## Where the Native Scheduler Breaks: a Launch Week With 3 Profiles

![A launch week with three LinkedIn profiles: three logins, no review step and edits that mean chasing people](/images/blog/how-to-schedule-linkedin-posts-launch-1200x675.webp)

Picture a small company launching a product on Tuesday. The founder posts the announcement from her profile at 8 a.m. Her head of product follows at 11 with the story behind the feature, and the sales lead posts a customer quote on Wednesday. The founder wants to read both teammates' posts first, and the announcement should also go to X and Bluesky.

With LinkedIn's own scheduler:

- **Three logins.** Each person schedules their own post while signed in to their own account. Nobody sees the full plan, so two posts can land at the same time.
- **No review step.** The founder only sees her teammates' posts once they're live, unless they paste drafts into a chat first.
- **Edits mean chasing people.** If launch moves to Thursday, each person reschedules from their own list.
- **LinkedIn only.** The X and Bluesky versions need another tool or more reminders.

None of this matters for one person posting once a week. It adds up when a team coordinates around a date.

## Sharing LinkedIn Logins With an Assistant or Ghostwriter (Use With Caution)

The usual workaround is for one person, often an assistant or ghostwriter, to log in to everyone's LinkedIn and schedule from each account in turn.

### The Real Risks

- **A shared password is full access.** Messages, connections and settings come with it, not just posting.
- **Logins from new devices can trigger security checks** that lock the account owner out at the worst moment.
- **Nobody knows who changed what.** With one shared login, there's no record of who edited or rescheduled a post.

### A Safer Setup

- Each person signs in to LinkedIn themselves to grant posting access, then never shares the password.
- Use two-factor authentication on every profile.
- Keep a single plan that everyone can see, with a review step before posts go out.

> **Bottom line:** Share the plan, not the password.

## How the Launch Week Works in Schedule For You

**1. Each person connects their own profile once.** On Studio, invite teammates to the workspace from **Settings → Team**. Each one opens **Social accounts**, clicks **Connect LinkedIn** and signs in with their own LinkedIn account, which is how LinkedIn grants access to post. After that, the team can schedule to that profile from the workspace. LinkedIn's sign-in lasts about two months, and owners get an email before it lapses so posts don't fail.

![The Social accounts screen with Bluesky, X, Instagram, LinkedIn and Threads accounts connected, each with its status and number of queued posts](/images/blog/social-accounts-1440x900.webp)

**2. Write each post once, then tailor it.** Open **Create post**, pick the founder's LinkedIn profile plus the company's X and Bluesky accounts, and write the announcement. The composer has a tab for each platform, so the LinkedIn version can run to several paragraphs while the X version stays under 280 characters and Bluesky under 300. Attach up to nine images or one video to the LinkedIn version, and put the link in the **first comment**, which publishes right after the post.

![The Schedule For You composer with the LinkedIn tab open and a character counter for each platform under the caption](/images/blog/composer-linkedin-1440x900.webp)

**3. Stagger the times and ask for sign-off.** Schedule the founder's post for Tuesday 8:00, the head of product's for 11:00 and the sales lead's for Wednesday. Under **Advanced options**, mark the teammates' posts as needing approval. They wait under **Needs approval** in the queue until the founder approves them, and you can also send a preview link to someone outside the workspace. Approvals and preview links are part of the paid plans.

**4. See the whole week, and move it in one place.** The calendar shows every person's posts next to the X and Bluesky versions. If launch slips to Thursday, drag each post to the new day instead of messaging three people.

**5. Know it went out.** Turn on reminder emails in **Settings** to get a heads-up before posts go out (an hour before, by default), so someone is around to answer early comments. If LinkedIn rejects a post, it shows under **Failed** with the reason and a retry button.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you schedule LinkedIn posts for free?

Yes. LinkedIn's built-in scheduler is free for personal profiles and company pages. Use the clock icon next to the Post button.

### How far in advance can you schedule a LinkedIn post?

Up to about three months ahead with LinkedIn's own scheduler, at the time of writing.

### Where do I find my scheduled LinkedIn posts?

Open the composer, click the clock icon and choose View all scheduled posts. They don't appear on your profile until they publish.

### Can I schedule posts for multiple LinkedIn accounts?

Natively, only by signing in to each account in turn; company pages are scheduled from each page's admin view. In Schedule For You, each person connects their own profile once, and the team schedules all of them from one queue and calendar.

### Can Schedule For You post to a LinkedIn company page?

No. It publishes to personal profiles only. Schedule company page posts with LinkedIn's built-in scheduler.

### Can I schedule a LinkedIn carousel or poll?

It depends on your account and LinkedIn version, so check whether the clock icon appears once you've added the poll or document. Schedule For You publishes text posts, posts with up to nine images and posts with one video, not polls or PDF documents.

### Can I schedule LinkedIn articles or newsletters?

Not through the post scheduler at the time of writing.

### Do scheduled LinkedIn posts get less reach?

There's no sign that LinkedIn ranks scheduled posts lower. Timing, the first lines and early replies matter more; see [do scheduled posts get fewer views](/blog/do-scheduled-posts-get-less-views).
