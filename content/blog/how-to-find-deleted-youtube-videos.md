In most cases, YouTube can't bring back a video once its owner has deleted it, and there's no hidden "trash" folder in YouTube Studio. But many "deleted" videos aren't actually deleted. They're private, saved as drafts, filtered out of your Content list, or removed by YouTube, which is a different situation with its own appeal route. And even when a video is truly gone, a copy often survives on a phone, hard drive or old backup.

Here's how to work out which situation you're in and where to look.

## First, work out what happened

The message you see when you open the link tells you a lot. The exact wording changes over time, but it usually falls into one of these groups.

| What you see | What it usually means | What you can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Private video" | The owner set it to private | If it's yours, change visibility in Studio. If not, only the owner can share it |
| "Video unavailable" with a note that the uploader removed it | The owner deleted it | Owner: look for a copy (below). Others: ask the owner |
| Removed for violating YouTube's policies | YouTube took it down | Owner: check Studio and your email for an appeal option |
| Unavailable due to a copyright claim | A rights holder's claim or takedown | Owner: check the Copyright section in Studio |
| The account was terminated | The whole channel is gone | Owner: use YouTube's appeal process |
| Not available in your country | Region restrictions | Nothing is deleted; it's blocked where you are |

If the video is yours, the most useful distinction is between **deleted** (you or someone with access to your channel deleted it) and **removed** (YouTube or a copyright owner took it down). Removed videos often still appear in YouTube Studio with a restriction label, and you may be able to appeal.

## How to find your own "missing" videos in YouTube Studio

Before assuming the worst, check that the video isn't just hidden.

### Check every tab and filter

1. Open YouTube Studio and select **Content**.
2. Look across the tabs at the top: **Videos**, **Shorts**, **Live**, **Posts**, **Playlists** and **Podcasts** (the exact set depends on your channel). A vertical upload may have landed under Shorts, and a past stream lives under Live.
3. Clear any filter in the filter bar. Then add a **Visibility** filter and check **Private**, **Unlisted** and **Draft** one at a time.
4. Sort by date and scroll to the time you remember uploading.

Drafts are a common culprit. If an upload was interrupted or you never finished the details screen, the video can sit as a draft that nobody else can see.

### Check the right channel

If you manage more than one channel, or your channel is a Brand Account, you may simply be signed in to the wrong one. Select your profile picture, choose **Switch account**, and check each channel. Also confirm you're using the same Google account you uploaded with. If you can't get into that account at all, see our guide on [how to recover a YouTube account](/blog/how-to-recover-youtube-account).

### Check for restrictions and copyright issues

In the Content list, look at the **Restrictions** column. A video that YouTube removed or blocked may show a label such as "Copyright" or "Community Guidelines" there. Select the label to see the details and any options to dispute or appeal. YouTube also emails the channel owner when it removes a video, so search your inbox for messages from YouTube around that date.

## If you really did delete it: where to find a copy

YouTube's help pages say that deleted videos can't be recovered, and you shouldn't count on support being able to make an exception. Your best bet is a copy outside YouTube. Work through these places:

1. **Your phone's camera roll and its "Recently deleted" album.** If you filmed on a phone, the original may still be there, or in the recently deleted album for a limited time.
2. **Cloud photo and file backups.** Google Photos, iCloud Photos, Google Drive, Dropbox or OneDrive may have synced the file automatically.
3. **Your editing software.** Editing apps keep project files and often a render or export folder. Even if the export is gone, the project and raw footage may let you re-export.
4. **Old computers, external drives and SD cards.** Check the camera's memory card before reusing it.
5. **Anyone you worked with.** Editors, collaborators, clients or the person who filmed it may still have the final file.
6. **A previous Google Takeout export.** Google Takeout (takeout.google.com) lets you export your YouTube data, including videos you've uploaded. It only exports what still exists, so it can't help after deletion, but if you ran an export before you deleted the video, that archive contains it.
7. **Other platforms.** If you also posted the video to TikTok, Instagram or Facebook, you can download your own upload from your account there.

### Find the video's details even without the file

If you don't have the file but want to remember what it was:

- **Watch history.** Your Google activity at myactivity.google.com may list videos you've watched, even if they've since been removed. Some entries show only that the video is no longer available.
- **Playlists.** Deleted videos can stay in playlists as "[Deleted video]" or "[Private video]". Open a playlist's three-dot menu and choose **Show unavailable videos** if the option appears.
- **Old links.** Emails, chat messages, your website and descriptions of your other videos may contain the URL. The 11-character video ID at the end is the key piece of information.
- **The Wayback Machine.** The Internet Archive's Wayback Machine (web.archive.org) sometimes saved a copy of a video's watch page. Paste the full URL and look for snapshots. You'll usually find the title, description and thumbnail, but rarely the video itself.

### Re-uploading a recovered video

If you find the file, you can upload it again. It will be a new video with a new URL, and the old views, likes and comments don't come back. Update any links that pointed to the old video, such as cards, end screens, playlists and links in other descriptions. If the recovered video is long, you can also cut fresh highlights from it; our guide on [how to clip a YouTube video](/blog/how-to-clip-a-youtube-video) covers trimming and turning moments into Shorts.

Going forward, keep a backup of every master file before it goes to YouTube. That applies even if you upload through a scheduler: Schedule For You publishes the file you give it to YouTube, but it isn't an archive, so your own drive or cloud storage should hold the original.

## Finding a video someone else deleted

When another creator deletes a video, it's their decision, and YouTube won't restore it on request from viewers. Here's what you can reasonably do:

- **Check whether it's still on the channel.** It may have been made unlisted or moved to a members-only section rather than deleted.
- **Search YouTube for the title.** The creator may have re-uploaded an edited version, or included it in a compilation.
- **Ask the creator.** A polite comment on a recent video or a message through their listed business contact sometimes gets an answer, especially for educational content.
- **Look at their other platforms.** Many creators post the same content to a podcast feed, a website or another social network.
- **Use the Wayback Machine for the details.** As above, an archived watch page can confirm the title, description and upload date.

Avoid sites that claim to "recover any deleted YouTube video." They rarely work, they're often loaded with misleading ads and downloads, and copies they host are usually unauthorized re-uploads. Downloading or redistributing someone else's video without permission can break YouTube's Terms of Service and copyright law, and the creator may have deleted it for a good reason, such as privacy.

## How to avoid losing videos in the future

- **Keep masters in two places,** for example an external drive and cloud storage.
- **Run a Google Takeout export of your YouTube data occasionally,** so you have an off-platform copy of what's live.
- **Use private or unlisted instead of delete** when you only want to hide a video. You can bring it back any time.
- **Limit channel permissions.** In YouTube Studio settings, give editors and managers only the access they need, and review the list now and then.
- **Turn on 2-Step Verification** for the Google account that owns the channel, so nobody else can delete your work.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can YouTube restore a video I deleted?

Generally, no. YouTube's help documentation says deleted videos can't be recovered, and you shouldn't expect support to make an exception. Check that the video isn't private, a draft or on another channel first, then look for a copy on your devices or backups.

### Where do deleted YouTube videos go?

There's no recycle bin in YouTube Studio. Once a deletion is processed, the video is gone from your channel. Videos that YouTube removed for policy or copyright reasons are different: they may still appear in Studio with a restriction label.

### Does Google Takeout include deleted videos?

No. Google Takeout exports the YouTube videos that exist on your channel when you run the export. It can only help if you made an export before the video was deleted.

### Can I see the title of a deleted YouTube video?

Often, yes. Look in your watch history, in playlists with unavailable videos shown, in old links that include the video ID, or in the Wayback Machine, which may have archived the watch page.

### Is it legal to download a video before someone deletes it?

Downloading other people's YouTube videos with third-party tools is against YouTube's Terms of Service, and re-sharing them can infringe copyright. Use official options like saving to a playlist or, where available, YouTube Premium's offline downloads, which stay inside the YouTube app.
