You open an old link and the video is gone, or you can't find one of your own uploads in YouTube Studio. In most cases, YouTube can't bring back a video once its owner has deleted it, and there's no hidden "trash" folder in Studio. But many "deleted" videos aren't deleted at all.

![How to find deleted YouTube videos: check whether it's hidden or removed, then look for a copy](/images/blog/how-to-find-deleted-youtube-videos-hero-1200x675.webp)

They're private, saved as drafts, filtered out of your Content list, or removed by YouTube, which is a different situation with its own appeal route. And even when a video is truly gone, a copy often survives on a phone, a hard drive or an old backup.

This guide covers how to tell which situation you're in, where to look for your own videos, seven places a copy can survive, and what's realistic for videos from other channels.

## Deleted, Private or Removed? What the Error Message Tells You

Before you try to find a deleted video, read the message you see when you open the link. The exact wording changes over time, but it usually falls into one of these groups.

![A deleted YouTube video compared with one that is private, a draft or removed by YouTube](/images/blog/how-to-find-deleted-youtube-videos-deleted-vs-hidden-1200x675.webp)

| What you see | What it usually means | What you can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Private video" | The owner set it to private | If it's yours, change visibility in Studio. If not, only the owner can share it |
| "Video unavailable" with a note that the uploader removed it | The owner deleted it | Owner: look for a copy (below). Others: ask the owner |
| Removed for violating YouTube's policies | YouTube took it down | Owner: check Studio and your email for an appeal option |
| Unavailable due to a copyright claim | A rights holder's claim or takedown | Owner: check the Copyright section in Studio |
| The account was terminated | The whole channel is gone | Owner: use YouTube's appeal process |
| Not available in your country | Region restrictions | Nothing is deleted; it's blocked where you are |

### What Most People Don't Know

If the video is yours, the most useful distinction is between **deleted** (you or someone with access to your channel deleted it) and **removed** (YouTube or a copyright owner took it down). Removed videos often still appear in YouTube Studio with a restriction label, and you may be able to appeal. Deleted ones don't come back.

> **Key point:** There's no recycle bin on YouTube. If the video was deleted, your only real hope is a copy outside YouTube, so start looking before you clear any phones, cards or drives.

## How to Find Your Own "Missing" Videos in YouTube Studio (4 Checks)

Before assuming the worst, check that the video isn't just hidden. Menu labels in Studio change between versions.

![Four checks in YouTube Studio for a missing video: tabs, filters, the right channel and restrictions](/images/blog/how-to-find-deleted-youtube-videos-studio-1200x675.webp)

### Check 1: Look Across Every Content Tab

Open YouTube Studio and select **Content**. Look across the tabs at the top: **Videos**, **Shorts**, **Live**, **Posts**, **Playlists** and **Podcasts** (the set depends on your channel). A vertical upload may have landed under Shorts, and a past stream lives under Live.

### Check 2: Clear Filters, Then Filter by Visibility

Clear any filter in the filter bar. Then add a **Visibility** filter and check **Private**, **Unlisted** and **Draft** one at a time. Sort by date and scroll to when you remember uploading.

> **Pro tip:** Drafts are the most common culprit. If an upload was interrupted or you never finished the details screen, the video can sit as a draft nobody else can see.

### Check 3: Make Sure You're on the Right Channel

If you manage more than one channel, or your channel is a Brand Account, you may be signed in to the wrong one. Select your profile picture, choose **Switch account** and check each channel. Also confirm you're using the Google account you uploaded with. If you can't get into that account, see [how to recover a YouTube account](/blog/how-to-recover-youtube-account).

### Check 4: Look for Restrictions and Copyright Labels

In the Content list, look at the **Restrictions** column. A video YouTube removed or blocked may show a label such as "Copyright" or "Community Guidelines". Select it to see the details and any dispute or appeal options. YouTube also emails the channel owner when it removes a video, so search your inbox around that date.

## If You Really Did Delete It: 7 Places a Copy Survives

YouTube's help pages say deleted videos can't be recovered, and you shouldn't count on support making an exception. Work through these places instead.

![Seven places a copy of a deleted YouTube video can survive, from your camera roll to old exports](/images/blog/how-to-find-deleted-youtube-videos-copies-1200x675.webp)

| Where to look | What you might find | Time limit? |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Phone camera roll and "Recently deleted" | The original recording | Recently deleted only keeps files for a limited time |
| 2. Cloud backups (Google Photos, iCloud Photos, Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive) | An auto-synced copy | Depends on the service |
| 3. Your editing software | Project files, raw footage, render folders | Until you clear them |
| 4. Old computers, external drives and SD cards | Raw clips or exports | Check before reusing the card |
| 5. People you worked with | Editors, collaborators, clients may have the final file | — |
| 6. A previous Google Takeout export | Your uploads, if you exported before deleting | Takeout only exports what still exists |
| 7. Other platforms | Your own upload on TikTok, Instagram or Facebook | While it's still posted |

Google Takeout (takeout.google.com) exports your YouTube data, including your uploaded videos. It can't help after deletion, but if you ran an export before, that archive contains the video.

### Find the Video's Details Even Without the File

- **Watch history.** Your Google activity at myactivity.google.com may list videos you watched, even if they've since been removed. Some entries only say the video is no longer available.
- **Playlists.** Deleted videos can stay in playlists as "[Deleted video]" or "[Private video]". Open the playlist's three-dot menu and choose **Show unavailable videos** if the option appears.
- **Old links.** Emails, chats, your website and your other video descriptions may contain the URL. The 11-character video ID at the end is the key detail.
- **The Wayback Machine.** The Internet Archive's Wayback Machine (web.archive.org) sometimes saved the watch page. You'll usually find the title, description and thumbnail, but rarely the video itself.

### Re-Uploading a Recovered Video

A re-upload is a new video with a new URL. The old views, likes and comments don't come back. Update anything that pointed at the old video: cards, end screens, playlists and links in other descriptions. If it's long, you can also cut fresh highlights; see [how to clip a YouTube video](/blog/how-to-clip-a-youtube-video).

## How to Find Deleted YouTube Videos From Other Channels (5 Options)

When another creator deletes a video, it's their decision, and YouTube won't restore it on request from viewers.

1. **Check whether it's still on the channel.** It may be unlisted or members-only rather than deleted.
2. **Search YouTube for the title.** The creator may have re-uploaded an edited version or put it in a compilation.
3. **Ask the creator.** A polite comment on a recent video, or a message through their listed business contact, sometimes gets an answer.
4. **Look at their other platforms.** Many creators post the same content to a podcast feed, a website or another network.
5. **Use the Wayback Machine** to confirm the title, description and upload date.

## "Deleted Video Recovery" Sites (Use With Caution)

Search for this topic and you'll find sites that claim to "recover any deleted YouTube video". They can't reach YouTube's systems any more than you can.

### The Real Risks

- **They rarely work.** At best they show an archived page you could find yourself on the Wayback Machine.
- **Misleading ads and downloads.** Many push installers, browser extensions or "premium" unlocks.
- **Unauthorized copies.** Videos they host are usually re-uploads without the creator's permission. Downloading or redistributing someone else's video can break YouTube's Terms of Service and copyright law, and the creator may have deleted it for a good reason, such as privacy.

### If You Look Anyway

- ✓ Use the Wayback Machine, not a "recovery" site.
- ✗ Don't install software or extensions to view a video.
- ✗ Don't enter your Google password anywhere except Google's own sign-in.

> **Bottom line:** Nobody outside YouTube can undelete a video. The only copies that matter are the ones you or the creator kept.

## Keep a Copy Everywhere You Publish

Take Rosa, who runs a cooking channel and uploads two videos a week. Last year she deleted a video while tidying her channel and found the only master had been on a laptop she'd since wiped. The 30-second clip she'd posted to Instagram was the only piece left.

Now she keeps masters on an external drive and in cloud storage, and she schedules every YouTube video alongside a short cut for TikTok and Instagram. Those copies aren't a backup system, but they're one more place the work survives.

## How to Never Lose a Video Again: 5 Habits

- ✓ **Keep masters in two places,** for example an external drive and cloud storage.
- ✓ **Run a Google Takeout export now and then,** so you have an off-platform copy of what's live.
- ✓ **Use private or unlisted instead of delete** when you only want to hide a video. You can bring it back any time.
- ✓ **Limit channel permissions.** In Studio settings, give editors and managers only the access they need.
- ✓ **Turn on 2-Step Verification** for the Google account that owns the channel.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can YouTube restore a video I deleted?

Generally, no. YouTube's help documentation says deleted videos can't be recovered, so check that the video isn't private, a draft or on another channel, then look for a copy on your devices or backups.

### Where do deleted YouTube videos go?

Nowhere you can reach: there's no recycle bin in YouTube Studio. Videos YouTube removed for policy or copyright reasons are different and may still appear in Studio with a restriction label.

### Does Google Takeout include deleted videos?

No. Takeout exports the videos that exist on your channel when you run it, so it only helps if you exported before the video was deleted.

### Can I see the title of a deleted YouTube video?

Often, yes. Look in your watch history, in playlists with unavailable videos shown, in old links that include the video ID, or in the Wayback Machine.

### Is it legal to download a video before someone deletes it?

Downloading other people's YouTube videos with third-party tools is against YouTube's Terms of Service, and re-sharing them can infringe copyright. Use official options like playlists or, where available, YouTube Premium's offline downloads, which stay inside the YouTube app.

### Why did my video disappear without me deleting it?

Usually it's a draft, a filter, the wrong channel, or a removal by YouTube. Check the Restrictions column in Studio and your email for a notice.

### Can I get my old views back if I re-upload?

No. A re-upload is a new video with a new URL, and views, likes and comments start from zero.
