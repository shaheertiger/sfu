You're thinking about joining Bluesky, or letting your teen join, and want to know what you're signing up for. Bluesky is safe to use in the way most mainstream social networks are: it has a moderation team, content labels, strong blocking and muting tools, and adult content is hidden unless an adult turns it on.

![Is Bluesky safe: strong blocking and moderation, but posts, likes, follows and blocks are public and DMs aren't end-to-end encrypted](/images/blog/is-bluesky-safe-hero-1200x675.webp)

Two things catch people out. **Almost everything on Bluesky is public**, including your posts, likes, follows and blocks. And **Bluesky's built-in DMs are not end-to-end encrypted** at the time of writing. Treat it like a public stage rather than a private group chat, and it's a comfortable place to be.

This guide covers what's public, how moderation and blocking work, whether DMs are private, how to secure your account, the scams to watch for, and whether Bluesky suits teens.

## Why So Much of Bluesky Is Public (and What Isn't)

![What's public on Bluesky, like posts, likes, follows and blocks, next to what stays private, like your email, password and mutes](/images/blog/is-bluesky-safe-public-1200x675.webp)

Bluesky runs on the AT Protocol, an open network where account data is published so other apps can read it. That's what makes custom feeds and third-party apps possible, and it's also why there are no private accounts.

| What | Public? |
| --- | --- |
| Posts and replies | Yes, to anyone, including people without an account (unless you limit logged-out viewing) |
| Likes | Yes, others can see who liked a post |
| Follows and followers | Yes |
| Blocks | Yes, block records are public data |
| Lists you create | Yes |
| DMs | No, but stored by Bluesky without end-to-end encryption |
| Email address, password, muted accounts | No |

### What Most People Don't Know

Deleting a post removes it from Bluesky, but copies may already exist in other apps or archives that read the network. And blocks, which feel private on most apps, are public records on Bluesky.

> **Key point:** Post as if anything public could be kept. Our guides on [making a Bluesky account private](/blog/how-to-make-bluesky-account-private) and [whether likes are public](/blog/are-likes-public-on-bluesky) cover the settings that help.

## How to Make Bluesky Safer in 5 Steps (Moderation and Blocking)

![Bluesky safety tools: private mutes, strong but public blocks, and per-post reply controls](/images/blog/is-bluesky-safe-tools-1200x675.webp)

Bluesky has its own moderation team and community guidelines, and adds a layer most networks don't: **stackable moderation**. You can subscribe to extra labeling services run by other groups and to shared block or mute lists. Menu labels change between app versions, so look for the closest match.

### Step 1: Block Anyone Who Bothers You

The person can't see your posts while logged in, reply to you or mention you. Blocks are strong on Bluesky: a block also hides the thread between you and stops quote posts.

### Step 2: Mute Instead When You Want It Quiet

Muting hides someone from your feeds without them knowing. Mutes are private.

### Step 3: Mute Words and Tags

Hide posts containing specific words or hashtags.

### Step 4: Set Reply Controls and Detach Quotes

Choose who can reply to each post, hide replies on your threads, and detach your post from someone else's quote post.

### Step 5: Report

Every post, account and list has a report option.

> **Pro tip:** Mute when you want peace, block when you want distance. The step people get wrong is blocking to stay quiet, then being surprised it's visible: block records are public, mutes aren't.

See [who blocked you on Bluesky](/blog/who-blocked-me-on-bluesky) for how blocks show up.

## Are Bluesky DMs Private? (Not End-to-End Encrypted)

Only you and the other person can read a DM in the app, but Bluesky's standard DMs aren't end-to-end encrypted, so they're stored on Bluesky's servers in a form the company could access, for example during a moderation review.

- **Don't send** passwords, ID documents or anything you'd regret leaking.
- **For sensitive chats**, use an encrypted app. Bluesky has said it plans to support encrypted messaging, and a third-party app called Germ offers end-to-end encrypted chat linked to Bluesky profiles.
- **Choose who can message you.** See [how to DM on Bluesky](/blog/how-to-dm-on-bluesky).

## How to Secure Your Bluesky Account

| Setting | Where | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strong, unique password | Settings | Stops reused-password takeovers |
| Two-factor authentication | Settings, Account (sign-in code by email) | Blocks logins without your inbox |
| App passwords for third-party apps | Settings, Privacy and security, App passwords | Apps never see your main password, and each can be revoked |
| Your own domain as your handle | Settings, handle | Proves you are who you say you are |

Bluesky also gives a blue check to some notable accounts and trusted verifiers. To set a domain handle, see [how to change your Bluesky handle](/blog/how-to-change-bluesky-handle).

## Scams and Impersonation: Signs to Watch For

Bluesky has the usual social media scams: fake giveaways, crypto "investment" pitches, romance scams in DMs, and impersonation accounts copying a well-known name and avatar.

| Sign | What it usually means |
| --- | --- |
| A handle that's slightly off | An impersonation account |
| A default `.bsky.social` handle for an organization that should have its own domain | Possibly not the real organization |
| A brand-new account with few posts asking you to move to WhatsApp or Telegram | A scam moving you off-platform |
| A link asking you to "log in to Bluesky" on a site that isn't bsky.app | A phishing page |

Bluesky never asks for your password by DM.

## Is Bluesky Safe for Kids and Teens?

Bluesky's terms set a minimum age (13 in most places at the time of writing). Adult content is off by default and can only be turned on by accounts that say they're 18 or older.

In some places, including the UK, Bluesky requires age verification before an account can see adult content or use some features such as DMs, and it blocked access in Mississippi rather than comply with that state's age law.

Parents should know that Bluesky has no parental control dashboard, and posts are public, so a teen account is visible to everyone. See [how adult content settings work on Bluesky](/blog/how-to-see-adult-content-on-bluesky).

## Third-Party Bluesky Apps That Want Your Main Password (Use With Caution)

![Safe ways to connect Bluesky apps, like app passwords you can revoke, next to risky ones like typing your main password into an app](/images/blog/is-bluesky-safe-apps-1200x675.webp)

The open network means plenty of third-party clients, feeds and tools. Most are fine. The risk is in how you log in.

### The Real Risks

- **Your main password gives full control**, including changing your email and password.
- **You can't revoke it per app.** Changing your main password logs out everything at once.
- **Fake "log in to Bluesky" pages** collect passwords for takeovers.

### A Quick Safety Checklist

- Give apps an **app password**, never your main password.
- Name each app password after the app, so you know what to revoke.
- Delete app passwords you no longer use.

> **Bottom line:** App passwords make third-party Bluesky apps safe to try. Your main password stays with you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Bluesky safe to use?

Yes, about as safe as other mainstream social apps, with strong blocking and moderation tools. Just remember that almost everything you do is public and DMs aren't end-to-end encrypted.

### Can people see what I like on Bluesky?

Yes. Likes are public records. Anyone can open a post's likes and see who liked it, and third-party tools can list an account's likes.

### Does Bluesky sell my data?

Bluesky says it doesn't sell user data, and it has no ad business at the time of writing. Public posts can still be read by anyone on the open network, including apps and researchers.

### Is Bluesky safer than X?

It depends on what you mean. Bluesky has stronger blocking and more moderation choices, and no ads tracking you. Like X, it's public by default, and its DMs aren't end-to-end encrypted.

### Can Bluesky see my DMs?

Bluesky's built-in DMs aren't end-to-end encrypted, so the company could access them, for example to investigate a report. Use an encrypted app for anything sensitive.

### Are blocks private on Bluesky?

No. Block records are public data. Mutes are private.

### Is Bluesky safe for 13-year-olds?

It's allowed from 13 in most places, and adult content is off by default, but there's no parental control dashboard and posts are public. Talk it through and check settings together.

### What's a Bluesky app password?

A separate password you create for one third-party app, in Settings, Privacy and security, App passwords. The app never sees your main password, and you can revoke each one.
