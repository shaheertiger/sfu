## Why Google Business Profile Posting Fails (Before You Click Anything)

![What posting to a Google Business Profile needs compared with what makes it fail](/images/blog/connect-google-business-profile-requirements-1200x675.webp)

Most problems come from the Google side. Google checks two things before anyone can post: whether the profile is verified, and whether the Google account you sign in with has the right role on it.

| Requirement | Details |
|---|---|
| A verified Business Profile | Unverified or pending profiles can't publish posts |
| Owner or manager role | Set in Google Business Profile's people and access settings |
| The right Google account | The account that holds the owner or manager role, not your personal Gmail by default |

### What Most People Don't Know

Business Profiles have three roles: primary owner, owner and manager. Owners and managers can both create posts, so you don't need to be the primary owner. But an invitation you haven't accepted doesn't count. If someone added you as a manager, accept the invitation with the same Google account you'll post from.

Google's help center at [support.google.com/business](https://support.google.com/business) explains the verification options for each type of business.

> **Key point:** If you're an owner or manager of a verified profile and you sign in with that exact Google account, posting almost always works first time. The same will be true when connecting a scheduler.

## How to Post a Google Business Profile Update Now: 5 Steps

Labels in Google's screens change from time to time, so look for the closest match if something is worded differently.

### Step 1: Pick the Google Account That Manages the Business (the Step People Get Wrong)

Google often uses whichever account you're already signed in to in your browser. If that's your personal account and not the one with the owner or manager role, switch accounts first.

> **Pro tip:** Not sure which account has access? Open Google Business Profile, go to the people and access settings, and note the email listed next to your name before you start.

### Step 2: Open Your Profile

Search for your business name on Google while signed in, or open the Business Profile manager if you look after several locations.

### Step 3: Choose Add Update

Look for an option such as **Add update** or **Add post**. Pick a standard update unless you're announcing an offer or event.

### Step 4: Add Text, One Photo and a Button

Write the update, attach a photo and, if you want, add a button that links to your menu, booking page or offer.

### Step 5: Publish and Check It

Publish the update, then look at your profile on Search or Maps to confirm it shows. Google reviews posts, so a new one can take a little while to appear.

## What You Can Post to Google Business Profile

![What a Google Business Profile update can include: update text up to 1,500 characters, one image and an optional button link](/images/blog/connect-google-business-profile-what-to-post-1200x675.webp)

Google posts appear on your Business Profile in Google Search and Maps, which makes them a useful way to reach people already searching for you.

| What you can add | Limit | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Update text | Up to 1,500 characters | Put the useful part first, since previews cut it short |
| Image | A photo | A clear photo of your product, team or space |
| Button | Optional link, such as "Learn more" | Point it at the page that matches the post |

Google also offers offer and event posts, with their own dates and coupon fields. Our [Google Business Profile post character limit](/blog/google-business-profile-post-character-limit) guide explains how to use the space.

### 4 Tips for Google Updates That Get Read

- **Lead with the useful part.** Only the beginning of your post shows in the preview on Search and Maps.
- **Use a real photo.** A well-lit image of your product, team or space tends to work better than a text-heavy graphic.
- **Keep it current.** Old updates make a profile look neglected. A steady schedule helps.
- **Follow Google's content policies.** Google reviews posts and can reject ones that break its rules, such as misleading content or restricted products. Edit a rejected post in line with the policy and post again.

## Can't Post on Google Business Profile? 6 Fixes

| Problem | Fix | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Your business doesn't show up for you | Switch to the account that holds the owner or manager role | The account you used doesn't manage any profile |
| The post option is missing | Check the profile is verified | Unverified profiles can't publish |
| Google signed in with the wrong account | Switch accounts, or use a private browser window | Google uses your browser's current account |
| You were invited but still can't post | Accept the invitation with the invited account | Pending invitations don't grant a role |
| Your role was removed or ownership changed | Ask the owner to restore access, then accept | Posting rights follow the role |
| A post was rejected | Edit it to follow Google's content policies and post again | Google reviews every post |

### My Profile Lost Verification

Profiles can lose verification, for example after major edits to the business name or address, or if Google asks for reverification. Posts can't publish until it's verified again.

### I Manage Many Locations for Clients

If your Google account manages several locations, possibly in business groups, each one is listed separately in the Business Profile manager. Post to each location from there for now.

## Sharing Your Google Login With an Agency (Use With Caution)

![Safe ways to give someone access to a Google Business Profile compared with sharing your password](/images/blog/connect-google-business-profile-access-1200x675.webp)

When a marketer or agency offers to "just log in as you" to post for you, it's tempting. It's faster than setting up roles. It's also the riskiest way to do it.

### The Real Risks

- **Your whole Google account is exposed.** The same login usually opens your email, files and payment details, not just the Business Profile.
- **You can lose the profile.** Someone with your login can change ownership or remove you.
- **Two-step prompts break.** Their sign-ins trigger security checks on your phone at awkward times.
- **Offboarding is messy.** When you stop working together, you have to change passwords everywhere instead of removing one role.

### A Safer Checklist

- Add the agency's own Google account as a **manager**, not an owner, from the people and access settings.
- Keep the primary owner role yourself.
- Have them post with their own account.
- When the work ends, remove their role and check [myaccount.google.com/permissions](https://myaccount.google.com/permissions) for apps you no longer use.

> **Bottom line:** Give people a role, not your password. A manager can post for you, and you can remove them in seconds.

## How to Remove Someone's Access to Your Profile

To remove a manager or owner, open Google Business Profile's people and access settings and remove their role.

To check which apps can use your Google account:

1. Go to [myaccount.google.com/permissions](https://myaccount.google.com/permissions) while signed in to the Google account that manages the business.
2. Review the list of third-party apps with access.
3. Remove any you no longer use.
