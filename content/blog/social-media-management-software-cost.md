Social media management software costs anywhere from **nothing to several hundred dollars per user per month**. Free plans and native tools cover the basics for one or two channels. Scheduling tools for solo users and small businesses typically cost **about $5 to $30 a month**. Tools built for teams usually land around **$50 to $150 a month**. All-in-one suites with inboxes, listening and advanced reporting start around **$80 to $100 per user per month** and climb past **$200** quickly, with enterprise plans priced on request.

The headline price rarely tells you what you'll pay, though. The number of accounts you connect, the number of people who need a login, and add-ons like AI credits change the total. This guide covers typical tiers, real entry prices at the time of writing, what drives cost, hidden fees, and how to choose as a small business.

## Social media management pricing tiers at a glance

| Tier | Typical cost | Who it suits | What you usually get |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free and native tools | $0 | One or two channels, low volume | Basic scheduling with limits on accounts or posts |
| Solo and small business schedulers | About $5–30/month | Owners, creators, freelancers | Scheduling, calendar, a set number of accounts, one user |
| Team schedulers | About $50–150/month | Small teams, agencies | More accounts, team members, approvals |
| All-in-one suites | About $80–400+ per user/month | Marketing teams | Publishing plus inbox, reporting, listening |
| Enterprise | Custom quote | Large brands | Security, admin controls, dedicated support |

These ranges are drawn from the vendor pricing pages below. They're broad on purpose: plans change, and many tools sit between tiers.

## What drives the cost of social media management software

### Accounts and channels

The biggest variable for most small businesses. Check how a tool counts accounts: is a Facebook Page and its linked Instagram one account or two? Do two Instagram profiles count twice? Count every profile you post to today, plus any you plan to add this year.

### Users and seats

Per-seat pricing multiplies the bill for every person who needs a login. If a client or owner only needs to approve posts, look for tools that allow approvals without a paid seat.

### Analytics and reporting

Deeper analytics, longer data history and branded reports push tools into higher tiers. If you only need to know which posts did well, the free insights inside each platform may be enough.

### Inbox, listening and customer care

A unified inbox for comments and messages, and social listening for brand mentions, are what separate all-in-one suites from schedulers. They're valuable for teams that answer customers all day, and expensive if you don't use them.

### Approvals and client features

Approval workflows, client preview links and separate workspaces per brand matter to agencies and anyone posting on someone else's behalf. Some tools reserve them for team plans.

### AI add-ons

AI writing and image tools are increasingly sold as credits or add-ons. Sprout Social, for example, lists its Trellis Plus add-on at $28 per user per month with yearly billing at the time of writing, and Later sells extra AI credits on top of its plans. Factor these in if you'd use them, and skip them if you wouldn't.

## Hidden costs to watch for

- **Prices shown with yearly billing.** Many pricing pages display the yearly rate by default. Monthly billing usually costs more per month.
- **Post caps.** Some plans limit scheduled posts per profile or per month, which can force an upgrade even with few accounts.
- **Extra users and accounts.** Adding one more profile or person can push you into the next plan.
- **Taxes.** Several vendors quote prices without VAT or sales tax.
- **Refund terms.** Annual plans aren't always refundable. Postiz, for example, says annual plans aren't refundable on its pricing page.
- **Your time.** A cheap tool that's slow to use, or that doesn't support a network you need, costs you hours every week.

## How to choose social media management software for a small business

1. **List every account** you post to, including Google Business Profile, and any you'll add soon.
2. **List the people** who need to write, schedule or approve.
3. **Mark must-haves**: platforms, post types (Reels, carousels, videos), approvals, analytics, inbox.
4. **Price your exact setup** on two or three tools, at monthly and yearly rates.
5. **Trial with real posts.** Schedule a week of content and check each one publishes correctly.

## DIY vs hiring an agency or freelancer

Software is usually the smaller cost. Managing social media yourself costs your time: planning, creating, posting and replying. Hiring a freelancer or agency costs far more than any tool, because you're paying for people's time and skills, and fees vary widely by scope, location and experience. Many small businesses land in the middle: they create content themselves or with a part-time helper, and use an affordable scheduler to keep posting consistent. If you do hire help, check whether their fee includes the software or whether you'll pay for it separately.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does social media management software cost for a small business?

Most small businesses can expect to pay roughly $5 to $30 a month for a solo scheduler, or about $50 to $150 a month once several people need access. All-in-one suites with inboxes and reporting usually cost $80 or more per user per month. Price your exact number of accounts and users, because that's where tools differ most.

### What is the cheapest social media management tool?

Native tools like Meta Business Suite and YouTube Studio are free, and some schedulers offer free plans with limits on accounts or posts. Among paid tools, per-channel plans can be cheapest for one or two accounts, while bundle plans often work out cheaper as you add accounts.

### Why is Hootsuite or Sprout Social more expensive than other tools?

They're priced per user and include more than scheduling: a shared inbox, reporting and, on higher plans, listening. If you mainly need to publish posts, a scheduler usually costs much less.

### Is yearly billing worth it?

If you're sure about a tool after the trial, yearly billing usually saves money. Many vendors offer around two months free or a percentage discount. Check the refund terms first, since some annual plans aren't refundable.

### Does Schedule For You have a free plan?

Yes, with limits: 3 connected accounts, up to 10 scheduled posts at a time, and a short link back to Schedule For You on each post. No card is needed. Bulk scheduling, approvals, the API, RSS and webhooks are on the paid plans: Solo at $19 a month and Studio at $59 a month, each with a 7-day free trial that requires a card.
