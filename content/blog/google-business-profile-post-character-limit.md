You've written an update for your Google Business Profile and you're not sure how long it can be. A Google Business Profile post (an "update") can hold **1,500 characters** of text, one image, and an optional button such as Learn more, Book or Call. But Google shows only roughly the first 100 characters on your profile before someone opens the post.

![Google Business Profile post character limit: 1,500 characters, with about the first 100 shown before the reader opens the post](/images/blog/google-business-profile-post-character-limit-hero-1200x675.webp)

So the real limit that matters is closer to 100 characters. That's what a customer sees while deciding whether to call, book or keep scrolling. And Google's own interface publishes immediately, with no scheduler at the time of writing.

This guide covers every post limit in one table, the three post types, why Google rejects posts, what to write in those first 100 characters, and how to schedule updates so your profile stays active.

## Why Only the First 100 Characters Matter (What Customers Actually See)

People find your Business Profile in Google Search and Maps, usually when they're about to choose somewhere to go. They glance at your latest update, read a line, and decide. Very few open the full post.

![What customers see on a Google Business Profile post compared with what's hidden until they tap](/images/blog/google-business-profile-post-character-limit-preview-1200x675.webp)

### What Most People Don't Know

Google hasn't named posts as a ranking factor. Posts don't push you up the results. What they do is add fresh content and photos to the profile, which helps people choose you once they've already found you. That changes what a good post looks like: it's written for a customer deciding right now, not for search engines.

> **Key point:** Write the first 100 characters as if they're the whole post. The other 1,400 are for the few people who tap to read more.

## Google Business Profile Post Limits: 7 Numbers to Know

| What | Limit or recommendation |
|---|---|
| Post text | 1,500 characters |
| Shown before "Learn more" | About 100 characters |
| Image | One per post, JPG or PNG, 10 KB to 5 MB |
| Image size | At least 400×300; 1200×900 (4:3) recommended |
| Video | Not supported in updates at the time of writing |
| Button | Optional: Book, Order online, Buy, Learn more, Sign up, Call |
| Lifetime | Updates stay listed; older ones scroll down the profile |

![Google Business Profile post limits: 1,500 characters, one image at 1200 by 900, and an optional button](/images/blog/google-business-profile-post-character-limit-limits-1200x675.webp)

Google changes these from time to time, so if a post won't publish, check Google's Business Profile help pages for the current rules.

## 3 Post Types: Update vs Offer vs Event

| Post type | What it adds | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Update | Text, an image and a button | News, this week's hours, a new product, a tip |
| Offer | A title, start and end dates, and an optional coupon code or terms | Discounts and promotions with an end date |
| Event | A title and dates | Workshops, tastings, launches, open days |

Products and services have their own sections on the profile and aren't posts.

## How to Write a Google Business Profile Post in 5 Steps

Menu labels change as Google updates the Business Profile interface, so if a button has moved, search for your business name in Google and look for the option to add an update.

![Four steps to write a Google Business Profile post that gets clicks: add an update, write the first 100 characters, add a real photo, match the button](/images/blog/google-business-profile-post-character-limit-steps-1200x675.webp)

### Step 1: Open Your Profile and Choose "Add Update"

Search for your business name in Google while signed in as an owner or manager, then choose the option to add an update, offer or event.

### Step 2: Write the First 100 Characters First

Lead with the specific thing: "New autumn menu from Friday: pumpkin ravioli and three new desserts." Not "We're excited to share some news with you all!"

### Step 3: Add One Real Photo

A real photo taken at your location tends to work better than a designed graphic. Use 1200×900 (4:3) if you can, in JPG or PNG between 10 KB and 5 MB.

### Step 4: Pick a Button That Matches the Post

Book for an appointment post, Call for a question, Order online for a menu item. A mismatched button wastes the click.

### Step 5: Check for Rejection Triggers, Then Publish

This is the step people skip, and it's why posts get rejected. Remove phone numbers from the text (use the Call button instead) and check the link doesn't redirect.

> **Pro tip:** Write your post in a notes app first and check the length. The first sentence should make sense on its own if it's cut off mid-way.

## Why Google Rejects a Post (and the Fix)

Google reviews posts automatically. These are the common rejections:

![What gets Google Business Profile posts approved compared with what gets them rejected](/images/blog/google-business-profile-post-character-limit-rejected-1200x675.webp)

| Problem | Fix | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| A phone number in the text | Remove it and use the Call button | Phone numbers in post text are a common rejection trigger |
| Words read as regulated content (alcohol, gambling, some health claims) | Rephrase or remove the flagged phrase | Automated review is cautious with regulated topics |
| A stock photo with a text overlay | Use a real photo without text | Text-heavy images get rejected more often |
| A link that redirects | Link straight to the final page | Redirects can look like spam |

If a post is rejected, fix the cause and resubmit.

## Which Business Profile Posts Get Engagement

Posts that answer the question someone searching for your business has right now:

- ✓ This week's hours, especially around holidays
- ✓ A new menu item or product, with a photo
- ✓ A seasonal offer with an end date
- ✓ A short "what to expect" for first-time visitors

What tends to fall flat:

- ✗ Generic "thanks for your support" posts
- ✗ Designed graphics full of text
- ✗ A button that doesn't match the post

## Giving Agencies or Tools Access to Your Profile (Use With Caution)

Many businesses hand their Business Profile to an agency or a posting tool. That can save time, but access is the part to get right.

### The Real Risks

- **Ownership.** Whoever is the primary owner controls the profile. If an agency holds it and you part ways, getting it back can be slow.
- **Password sharing.** Sharing your Google password gives access to far more than your Business Profile.
- **Forgotten access.** People and tools you no longer work with can still post if you never remove them.

### Safety Checklist

- Keep primary ownership yourself, and add agencies as managers.
- Connect tools with Google's own sign-in, never by sharing your password.
- Review who has access every few months and remove anyone you don't recognize.

> **Bottom line:** Give the least access that gets the job done, through Google's own sign-in, and keep ownership in your own hands.

1. **Pick one posting day**, a day or two before your busiest days.
2. **Take five real photos** this week: food, products, your team, the space.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the character limit for Google Business Profile posts?

The limit is 1,500 characters. Around the first 100 show on the profile before the reader opens the post.

### What image size should a Google Business Profile post be?

Use at least 400×300 pixels; 1200×900 (a 4:3 ratio) is the recommended size, in JPG or PNG between 10 KB and 5 MB.

### Can you add a video to a Google Business Profile post?

No, not to updates at the time of writing. Each post takes one image.

### Can you schedule Google Business Profile posts?

Not in Google's own interface, which publishes immediately. A scheduler that connects through the Business Profile API, such as Schedule For You, can schedule them.

### Why can't I put a phone number in a Google Business Profile post?

Phone numbers in the post text are a common reason Google rejects posts. Use the Call button instead, which puts your number one tap away.

### Do Google Business Profile posts help ranking?

Google hasn't named posts as a ranking factor. They add fresh content and photos to the profile, which helps people choose you once they've found you.

### Do old Google Business Profile posts disappear?

Updates stay listed, but older ones scroll down the profile as you add new ones, so your latest post is the one most people see.

### How often should I post on Google Business Profile?

Once a week is a sensible rhythm for most local businesses, timed a day or two before your busiest days. Consistency matters more than volume.
