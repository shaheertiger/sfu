You're looking at the two numbers under your profile picture and wondering what the difference really is. On Instagram, **Followers** are the people who follow you: your posts can appear in their feed. **Following** is the accounts you follow: their posts can appear in yours. The two lists are independent, so someone can be on one, both or neither. Tap either number on your profile to see the full list.

![Followers are the people who follow you, Following is the accounts you follow, and the two lists don't have to match](/images/blog/followers-vs-following-on-instagram-hero-1200x675.webp)

Where it gets confusing is the ratio between the two numbers. Plenty of advice treats it as a score. It isn't, and there's no official "good" ratio.

This guide explains what each list means, where to find them on the app and desktop, how private accounts change things, what your ratio actually signals, and how to tidy your Following list without tripping Instagram's limits.

## What Followers and Following Mean on Instagram (and Why It Matters)

![Followers are people who see your posts and can be removed by you, Following is accounts whose posts you see and you can unfollow](/images/blog/followers-vs-following-on-instagram-lists-1200x675.webp)

Following on Instagram is one-way. You can follow anyone with a public account without asking, and they don't have to follow you back. That's why the two lists almost never match.

| | Followers | Following |
|---|---|---|
| Who's on it | People who follow you | Accounts you follow |
| What it affects | Who can see your posts in their feed | Whose posts you see in your feed |
| How it grows | Other people choose to follow you | You choose to follow others |
| How you shrink it | Remove a follower, or block them | Unfollow |
| Does anyone get notified? | Not when you remove them | Not when you unfollow |

When both of you follow each other, you're mutual followers. Our guide to [what follow back means on Instagram](/blog/what-does-follow-back-mean-on-instagram) explains the Follow Back button and the Follows you label.

It matters because the two numbers are the first thing anyone sees on your profile, and because your Following list shapes your own feed. Following 2,000 accounts you don't care about buries the 50 you do.

### What Most People Don't Know

Your follower count can drop without anyone choosing to unfollow you. Followers disappear when they deactivate or delete their account, block you, or get removed by Instagram in a spam cleanup. Your following count can drop for the same reasons. If you want to know who actually left, our guide to [who unfollowed me on Instagram](/blog/who-unfollowed-me-on-instagram) shows how to compare two exports.

> **Key point:** Your Followers list is your audience. Your Following list is your feed. Grow the first with good posts and curate the second for yourself, not for the ratio.

### Public vs Private Accounts: Who Can See Your Lists

| | Public account | Private account |
|---|---|---|
| Who can follow you | Anyone, instantly | Only people you approve, after a request |
| Who can see your Followers and Following lists | Anyone who visits your profile | Only your approved followers |
| Who sees your posts | Anyone | Only your approved followers |
| Can you remove a follower? | Yes | Yes |

Professional accounts (Business and Creator) are public. At the time of writing, Instagram doesn't offer a setting that hides your lists on a public account, so switching to private is the main way to limit who sees them. Settings change, so check yours in the app.

### Followers vs Following Ratio: What's "Good"? (No Magic Number)

The ratio is just followers divided by following. Instagram doesn't publish a target and doesn't show it anywhere. What visitors notice is the pattern:

| Pattern | Often looks like | Fine if… |
|---|---|---|
| Far more followers than following | A brand, creator or public figure | You post things people want to follow |
| Roughly equal | A personal account among friends | Always fine |
| Far more following than followers | A new account, or one chasing follow-backs | You're new, or you follow accounts for your own interest |

A low ratio on a new account is normal. It becomes a problem only when it comes from mass-following strangers in the hope they follow back, which looks spammy and can trigger limits.

## How to See Your Followers and Following Lists (Free, App and Desktop)

![Three steps to see your Instagram lists: open your profile, tap Followers or Following, then search or sort the list](/images/blog/followers-vs-following-on-instagram-where-1200x675.webp)

Menu labels change between app versions, so look for the closest match if something is worded differently.

### On the App

1. Tap your profile picture in the bottom corner to open your profile.
2. Tap the **Followers** number to see who follows you, or the **Following** number to see who you follow.
3. Use the search bar at the top of either list to find one account.

### On Desktop

1. Go to instagram.com in a browser and sign in.
2. Open your profile.
3. Click **followers** or **following** under your name.

### Sort and Filter Your Following List (the Feature Most People Miss)

Your Following list usually has sorting and category options. Depending on your version, you can sort by the date you followed accounts (earliest first is handy for cleanups) and see categories such as accounts you've interacted with least. These are the quickest way to find accounts you followed years ago and forgot about.

> **Pro tip:** Sort your Following list by earliest first. The oldest follows are the ones most likely to be dormant, renamed or no longer relevant.

To check whether one person follows you, open their profile and look for the **Follows you** label, or search their name in your Followers list.

## How to Compare Your Full Followers and Following Lists (Data Download Method)

Scrolling two lists side by side doesn't work past a few hundred accounts. To see exactly which accounts are on one list but not the other, use Instagram's **Download your information** export.

### Step 1: Request Your Lists

1. Open Instagram and go to your profile.
2. Tap the menu icon in the top corner, then **Accounts Center**.
3. Tap **Your information and permissions**, then **Download your information**.
4. Choose to download or transfer information and select your Instagram account.
5. Choose **Some of your information**.
6. Under connections, tick **Followers and following**. Leave everything else unticked.
7. Choose to download to your device, set the date range to **All time**, and pick **JSON** as the format.
8. Submit the request.

Instagram tells you when it's ready. There's no fixed time, but a small request like this is often quick. Our guide to [how long an Instagram data export takes](/blog/how-long-does-instagram-data-export-take) covers what to do if it isn't.

### Step 2: Compare the Two Files

Unzip the download, find the followers file (usually something like `followers_1.json`) and the following file, and choose both in our free [Instagram unfollowers checker](/tools/instagram-unfollowers-checker). It shows:

- **Don't follow you back:** on your Following list but not your Followers list.
- **You don't follow back:** on your Followers list but not your Following list.
- **Mutual follows:** how many accounts are on both.

The files are read in your browser and nothing is uploaded. Our guide to [who doesn't follow me back on Instagram](/blog/who-doesnt-follow-me-back-on-instagram) covers reading the results.

## Follower Apps and Mass-Unfollow Tools (Use With Caution)

![Bar chart of account safety: the app's own lists and the data download highest, mass-unfollow apps that need your password lowest](/images/blog/followers-vs-following-on-instagram-risk-1200x675.webp)

If your Following list is in the thousands, apps that promise to "fix your ratio" in one tap are tempting. They show followers and following side by side, then offer to unfollow everyone who doesn't follow back. Most need your Instagram password to do it.

### The Real Risks

- **Full account access.** With your password, an app can post, message, follow and unfollow as you.
- **Action blocks.** Unfollowing hundreds of accounts in minutes is exactly the pattern Instagram limits.
- **Restricted or disabled accounts** in serious cases. Our guide to the [disabled account appeal](/blog/instagram-disabled-account-appeal) covers what to do if it happens.
- **Broken terms.** Instagram prohibits accessing accounts through unauthorized automated means, and its rules prohibit artificially collecting followers.
- **Bought followers don't last.** Services selling followers inflate your count with accounts Instagram often removes later.

### If You Still Want to Use One

- Never type your Instagram password into an app's own login form.
- Turn off any bulk-unfollow or auto-follow features.
- Review the apps connected to your account in Instagram's security settings afterwards and remove anything you don't recognize.
- If you've already used one, change your password and turn on two-factor authentication.

> **Bottom line:** A better ratio isn't worth a blocked account. Tidy your Following list by hand, slowly, and grow your followers with posts.

## How to Tidy Your Following List Without an Action Block

1. **Start with the oldest.** Sort your Following list by earliest first and go through accounts you followed long ago.
2. **Use the least-interacted category**, if your version has it. These are accounts you never engage with.
3. **Check who doesn't follow back**, with the data download and our [checker](/tools/instagram-unfollowers-checker), but keep brands, creators and friends you enjoy.
4. **Unfollow in small batches over several days.** Instagram doesn't publish limits, and sudden mass unfollowing is a common trigger for action blocks.
5. **Remove followers sparingly.** Remove spam and bot accounts from your Followers list, and block anyone who bothers you.
6. **Repeat every few months**, not every day.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between followers and following on Instagram?

Followers are the people who follow you and can see your posts in their feed. Following is the accounts you follow, whose posts appear in your feed.

### Is it better to have more followers or more following?

Neither is better by rule. Brands and creators usually have more followers than following, and personal accounts often have a similar number of both. Grow followers with good posts rather than by trimming your Following list.

### What is a good followers to following ratio on Instagram?

There isn't an official one. Instagram doesn't publish or display a ratio. What matters is that your followers are real people who engage with your posts.

### Can people see who I follow on Instagram?

On a public account, yes: anyone can open your Following list. On a private account, only approved followers can see it.

### Does Instagram notify someone when I unfollow them or remove them?

No. Neither unfollowing nor removing a follower sends a notification.

### Why did my following count go down when I didn't unfollow anyone?

Accounts you follow may have deactivated, been deleted, blocked you or been removed by Instagram. Their follow disappears with them.

### How do I see who doesn't follow me back?

Download your followers and following lists through Accounts Center and open them in our [unfollowers checker](/tools/instagram-unfollowers-checker), or search individual names in your Followers list.

### Is there a limit to how many accounts I can follow?

Instagram limits how many accounts you can follow overall and how quickly you follow or unfollow. It doesn't always publish the exact numbers, so follow at a human pace.
