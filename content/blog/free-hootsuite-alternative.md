If you want a free Hootsuite alternative, the options that are genuinely free are the platforms' own built-in schedulers (such as Meta Business Suite for Facebook and Instagram, and YouTube Studio for YouTube) and open-source schedulers you host yourself. If you want one tool for many networks without running your own server, a low-cost paid scheduler is usually the practical middle ground.

Hootsuite is a long-established tool with a broad feature set, and for some teams it's the right fit. But if you're a solo creator, a small business or a small agency, you may not need everything it offers. This guide explains the categories of alternatives, what each is good at, and what to check before you move. For Hootsuite's current plans, check the pricing page on its website, since plans and prices change.

## Quick answer: the three types of alternatives

| Type | Cost | Best for | Main trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Native schedulers (Meta Business Suite, YouTube Studio and others) | Free | One or two platforms | Separate tool for each network |
| Open-source, self-hosted schedulers | Free software, you pay for hosting | Technical users and teams with developers | You handle setup, updates and platform API access |
| Low-cost paid schedulers | Monthly subscription | Several networks in one calendar | Not free, though often far cheaper than enterprise suites |

Some paid schedulers also offer a limited free tier, while others offer a free trial only. Read the plan details carefully: a "free" tier may limit the number of accounts, scheduled posts or platforms.

## Option 1: Native schedulers built into each platform

The most reliable free option is the scheduling that platforms build in themselves. These tools use the platform's own systems, so there's nothing extra to connect and no subscription.

### Meta Business Suite (Facebook and Instagram)

Meta Business Suite lets you create and schedule posts and Stories for your Facebook Page and connected Instagram professional account. It also includes a planner view, an inbox for messages and comments, and insights. If Facebook and Instagram are your main channels, it covers a lot of ground for free.

### YouTube Studio

When you upload a video in YouTube Studio, you can set its visibility to **Schedule** and choose a date and time for it to go public. You can also schedule a Premiere. It's built for YouTube only, but it's the official way to time your uploads.

### Other platforms' built-in scheduling

Several other networks offer native scheduling in some form, including LinkedIn, Pinterest and TikTok's desktop and web upload tools. Features and availability change, and some options depend on the type of account you have or where you post from, so check each platform's help center for current details.

### When native tools are enough

- You post to one or two platforms.
- You don't need a shared calendar across networks.
- You're happy to log in to each platform separately.

### Where they fall short

- You write and schedule each post separately on each network.
- There's no single view of everything going out this week.
- Team approvals and client sign-off are limited or absent.

## Option 2: Open-source, self-hosted schedulers

Open-source social media schedulers let you download the software and run it on your own server. The software itself is free, which makes this category attractive if you have technical skills.

### What you get

- Full control over your data and setup.
- The ability to customize the tool or contribute changes.
- No per-seat or per-account licence fees for the software.

### What it really costs

"Free" software isn't free to run. Before you choose this route, plan for:

- **Hosting**: a server or cloud instance, plus storage for images and video.
- **Setup and maintenance**: installing updates, fixing problems and keeping the server secure.
- **Platform API access**: many networks require you to register your own developer app, request permissions and sometimes go through a review before you can publish. This can take time.
- **Support**: community forums rather than a support team, unless you buy a hosted or supported version.

If you or someone on your team is comfortable with servers, this can be a great fit. If not, the time cost can quickly outweigh the savings.

## Option 3: Low-cost paid schedulers

Between free native tools and large enterprise suites there's a wide range of affordable schedulers. They're paid, but many are priced for individuals and small teams rather than large organizations.

### What you typically get

- One calendar or queue for several networks.
- One place to write a post and adapt it for each platform.
- Bulk scheduling, media libraries and recurring posts.
- Basic team features such as approvals.

### Watch the pricing model

Low-cost tools price in different ways, and the cheapest headline price isn't always the cheapest for you. Check whether the plan limits:

- The number of **connected social accounts**.
- The number of **scheduled posts** per month or per account.
- The number of **team members** or seats.
- Which **platforms** you can publish to.

## What to look for in any Hootsuite alternative

Whichever category you choose, run through this checklist before switching.

1. **Platform coverage.** List every network you post to today and every one you're planning to add. Make sure the alternative publishes to all of them, including newer networks such as Threads and Bluesky if they matter to you.
2. **Account limits.** Count your connected accounts, including client accounts if you're an agency.
3. **Per-platform editing.** Character limits and formats differ. A good tool lets you tailor each version of the post and warns you when you're over the limit.
4. **Calendar and queue.** Can you see the whole week at a glance and move posts around easily?
5. **Bulk scheduling.** If you plan a month at a time, look for CSV upload or another fast way to load many posts.
6. **Approvals and client access.** If others need to sign off, check how approvals work and whether clients need their own login.
7. **Reliability and alerts.** Find out what happens when a post fails to publish and whether you get notified.
8. **Integrations.** If you use automation tools, check for webhooks, an API or connections to tools like Zapier.
9. **Analytics needs.** Some schedulers focus on publishing and leave analytics to the native platforms. Decide whether you need built-in reports or whether native insights are enough. Our guide to [content performance analysis](/blog/content-performance-analysis) can help you decide what you actually need to measure.
10. **Trial terms.** Check how long the trial lasts, whether a card is required and when you'll be charged.

## How to switch without losing posts

1. **Export or note your scheduled posts** in your current tool before you cancel anything.
2. **Connect your accounts** in the new tool and check that each one publishes correctly with a test post.
3. **Rebuild your posting schedule.** Recreate your time slots so posts still go out at your usual times. If you're not sure your current times are the right ones, our guide to the [best days to post on social media](/blog/best-days-to-post-on-social-media) explains how to test.
4. **Run both tools in parallel for a short period**, with no overlapping posts, until you're confident.
5. **Cancel your old plan** once everything is moved.

You write one post, tailor the caption for each platform with live character-limit checks, and schedule it from a queue or drag-and-drop calendar. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a completely free Hootsuite alternative?

Yes, in two forms. Native schedulers like Meta Business Suite and YouTube Studio are free but only cover their own platforms. Open-source schedulers are free software, but you pay for hosting and spend time on setup and maintenance.

### Does Hootsuite have a free plan?

Plans change, so check the pricing page on Hootsuite's website for current options. At the time of writing, Hootsuite doesn't list a free plan, though it has offered free trials.

### What's the best free way to schedule Instagram posts?

Instagram's own in-app scheduling for professional accounts and Meta Business Suite are both free and official. Meta Business Suite is the better choice if you also manage a Facebook Page, since it lets you schedule to both in one place.

### Are free tiers of paid schedulers worth it?

They can be, if your needs are small. Check the limits on connected accounts, scheduled posts and platforms. If you quickly outgrow the free tier, compare the paid plans across tools rather than assuming the one you started with is cheapest.

### Is Schedule For You free?

No. Schedule For You has a 7-day free trial that requires a card, then plans start at $19/month. There is no free plan. You can cancel during the trial and you won't be charged.
