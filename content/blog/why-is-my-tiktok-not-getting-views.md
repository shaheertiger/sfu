When your TikTok isn't getting views, the cause is usually one of two things: something is stopping the video from being shown (a privacy setting, a restriction or a guideline issue), or people are being shown it and swiping away in the first few seconds. Check the first group in settings and TikTok Studio, then improve the second with a stronger hook, a length that fits the idea, and original content.

Here's how to tell which problem you have and what to do about each.

## Quick diagnosis: why your TikTok isn't getting views

| What you're seeing | Likely cause | Where to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 views for a long time after posting | Video still under review, privacy not set to public, or a restriction | The video's privacy setting, Account status |
| Views stuck at a few hundred on every video | Viewers swipe away early, or the content isn't reaching the right people | Average watch time and retention in analytics |
| One video flagged, others fine | That video is ineligible for the For You feed | Notifications and Account status |
| Sudden drop across all videos | A guideline warning or a change in what you post | Account status, recent changes to your content |
| Most views from your profile or Following | Little For You distribution | Traffic source in video analytics |
| Good views but almost no followers | Content doesn't give people a reason to see more | Profile, bio, consistency of topic |

## Problems that stop TikTok from showing your video

Start here. No amount of editing helps if the video isn't eligible to be recommended.

### 1. The video isn't public

It sounds obvious, but it's common. Open the video, tap the three dots, and check **Privacy settings**. If it's set to **Friends** or **Only me**, only those people can see it. Also check whether your whole account is private in **Settings and privacy** → **Privacy**. A private account limits who can watch your content.

If you schedule TikToks with a tool, check the privacy chosen there as well. In Schedule For You, for example, you choose public, friends or only me for each TikTok post, so a test post set to "only me" will stay that way.

### 2. Your account has a violation

Go to **Settings and privacy** → **Account** → **Account status** (the exact path can vary by app version). This page shows guideline violations, which features are restricted, and whether any of your videos are ineligible for the For You feed. If you think a decision was wrong, you can appeal from there. Don't use third-party "unban" or "unshadowban" services; only TikTok can change your account status.

### 3. The video is ineligible for the For You feed

TikTok's Community Guidelines include standards for what it recommends. A video can stay up on your profile but not be pushed to new viewers, for example if it contains content TikTok considers unsuitable for broad recommendation, is unoriginal or low quality, or is misleading. You'll usually get a notification, and it shows in Account status.

### 4. The account is too new or too young

Very new accounts have little history for TikTok to work with, so early videos may reach few people. Age also matters: TikTok applies extra protections to younger users, including limits on recommending their content. Make sure your account's birthday is accurate.

### 5. It's still processing

New uploads can sit at zero views while they're checked. Give a video some time before you judge it, and don't delete and re-upload the same video to "reset" it. That doesn't help and repeated duplicates can look like spam.

## Problems that stop people from watching

If everything above is fine, the video is being shown but not holding attention. Open the video's analytics in TikTok Studio (or tap **More data** under the video) and look at average watch time, the retention graph, and traffic sources.

### 6. The hook is weak

The first second or two decides whether people stay. Strong openings tend to:

- Start with the payoff, problem or surprise, not a greeting or logo.
- Put a clear line of on-screen text in the first frame.
- Show movement or a change on screen straight away.
- Make a promise the rest of the video keeps.

If the retention graph drops steeply right at the start, the hook is the first thing to rework.

### 7. The length doesn't fit the idea

A video should be as long as the idea needs and no longer. If people leave halfway, cut the middle. If they watch to the end and rewatch, the idea might support a longer, more detailed version. Longer videos (over one minute) matter for TikTok's Creator Rewards Program, but only if they hold attention.

### 8. It isn't original

Reposting other people's clips, reaction videos that add little, and content with another app's watermark are all less likely to be recommended. Film your own footage, add your own voice or perspective, and export cleanly without watermarks from other platforms.

### 9. The audio isn't working

Sound matters on TikTok. Check that your voice is clear and the music isn't drowning it out. If you have a business account, you can only use sounds from TikTok's commercial library, and using unlicensed music can get audio muted, which hurts watch time. Add captions too, since many people watch with the sound off.

### 10. TikTok can't tell what it's about

TikTok is used as a search engine for many topics. Help it understand your video:

- Say the topic out loud early in the video.
- Put the main phrase in on-screen text.
- Write a caption that describes the video in plain words someone might search for.
- Use a few specific hashtags rather than a long list of generic ones.

### 11. You post inconsistently

Posting in bursts and then disappearing makes it harder to build a returning audience and to learn what works. You don't need to post every day; you need a pace you can keep. Many creators batch-film on one day, then schedule the week. Schedule For You can queue TikTok videos and photo posts alongside your other channels, but the content itself still has to earn the views.

### 12. Your topic changes every video

If one video is cooking, the next is gaming and the next is a vlog, it's hard for TikTok to find the people who'll like your next post, and hard for viewers to decide to follow. Pick two or three related themes and stay with them for a while.

## What not to do

When your TikTok is not getting views, searching for a fix turns up a lot of "hacks." Most of them are against the rules or simply don't work.

- **Don't buy views, likes or followers.** It breaks TikTok's rules and fills your audience with accounts that never watch.
- **Don't join engagement groups** where people like and comment on each other's videos for the sake of it. That counts as fake engagement.
- **Don't spam hashtags** like #fyp in place of real keywords. They don't explain what your video is about.
- **Don't keep deleting videos** that start slowly. Some videos pick up later, especially through search.
- **Don't give your login to "growth" apps.** Apps that ask for your TikTok password put your account at risk.

## A simple weekly routine

1. Review last week's videos in TikTok Studio: note the two with the best average watch time and the two with the worst.
2. Compare their first two seconds. What's different?
3. Plan the next batch around the format that held attention.
4. Film, edit and caption, checking audio, captions and keywords.
5. Schedule them at a steady pace, and check Account status if anything looks off.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does my TikTok have 0 views?

Usually the video is still being reviewed, its privacy is set to Friends or Only me, your account is private, or there's a restriction on your account. Check the video's privacy settings and your Account status page first.

### Why did my TikTok views suddenly drop?

Check Account status first, since a guideline warning or ineligible videos can cut For You distribution. If nothing is listed, look at what changed: a new topic, a different format, weaker hooks or a gap in posting. Compare retention on recent videos with older ones that did well.

### Is my TikTok account shadowbanned?

TikTok doesn't use the word, but it can make videos ineligible for the For You feed or restrict features after a guideline violation. Check **Account status** in settings, which lists violations and restrictions and lets you appeal. If nothing is listed, the cause is more likely the content itself.

### Does deleting and reposting a TikTok help?

Generally, no. Reposting the same video rarely changes how it performs, and repeated duplicate uploads can look like spam. It's better to learn from the video's analytics and make a new version with a stronger hook.

### How long does it take for a TikTok to get views?

Some videos get most of their views in the first day or two, and others pick up later through search or shares. Avoid judging a video in the first hour, and look at patterns across several posts instead.

### Do hashtags still matter on TikTok?

A few relevant hashtags can help describe your video, but they matter less than what you say, show and write. Clear spoken keywords, on-screen text and a descriptive caption help TikTok understand your video.
