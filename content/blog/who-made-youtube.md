You use YouTube every day and suddenly wonder who actually built it. YouTube was made by **Chad Hurley, Steve Chen and Jawed Karim**, three former PayPal employees, in 2005. **Google** bought it in late 2006 for **$1.65 billion** in stock, and today it's run by CEO **Neal Mohan**.

![Who made YouTube: Chad Hurley, Steve Chen and Jawed Karim in 2005, bought by Google in 2006 for $1.65 billion](/images/blog/who-made-youtube-hero-1200x675.webp)

The youtube.com domain was registered on **February 14, 2005**, the first video, "Me at the zoo", was uploaded by Karim on **April 23, 2005**, and the site launched publicly later that year. Less than two years later it belonged to Google.

This guide covers the three founders, why YouTube was needed in the first place, the first video, the Google deal, and every CEO since.

## Who Are YouTube's 3 Founders? (and Who Did What)

![YouTube's three founders and their roles: Chad Hurley as CEO and designer, Steve Chen as CTO, Jawed Karim as adviser](/images/blog/who-made-youtube-founders-1200x675.webp)

| Founder | Role at YouTube | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chad Hurley | CEO until 2010 | Designer at PayPal; designed YouTube's look and logo |
| Steve Chen | Chief technology officer | Engineer at PayPal; built much of the site's infrastructure |
| Jawed Karim | Adviser; uploaded the first video | Engineer at PayPal; left to return to university |

Hurley and Chen ran the company day to day. Karim stepped back before the Google sale and kept a smaller stake.

### What Most People Don't Know

The founders have told different versions of the origin story over the years, from a dinner party video that was hard to share to an early idea of a video dating site ("Tune In, Hook Up"). What isn't disputed is that the team quickly dropped the dating idea and opened the site to any video.

> **Key point:** YouTube wasn't built to be the world's video library. It was built to solve one annoying problem: sharing a clip with friends.

## Why YouTube Was Needed in 2005 (Video Was Hard to Share)

In early 2005, there was no easy way to share a video online. Files were too large to email, and most sites required special software to play them.

The founders' idea was simple: a site where anyone could upload a clip and get a link, or an embeddable player, that worked in any browser. No special software for the viewer, no email attachments for the sender.

| Sharing a video before YouTube | Sharing a video on YouTube |
| --- | --- |
| Files too large to email | Upload once, share a link |
| Viewers needed special software | Played in any browser |
| Hard to put on your own site | An embeddable player |

## YouTube's Story, Step by Step (2005 to Today)

![YouTube's timeline: domain registered in February 2005, first video in April 2005, Google purchase in 2006, Neal Mohan CEO in 2023](/images/blog/who-made-youtube-timeline-1200x675.webp)

### Step 1: February 14, 2005: The Domain Is Registered

The youtube.com domain was registered on February 14, 2005.

### Step 2: April 23, 2005: The First Video Goes Up

Jawed Karim uploaded "Me at the zoo" on April 23, 2005.

### Step 3: Late 2005: The Public Launch

The site launched publicly later in 2005.

### Step 4: Mid-2006: One of the Most Visited Sites on the Web

YouTube grew fast. By mid-2006 it was one of the most visited sites on the web.

### Step 5: October–November 2006: Google Buys YouTube

Google announced the deal in October 2006 for $1.65 billion in stock, and it closed in November 2006.

### Step 6: February 2023: Neal Mohan Becomes CEO

Mohan, previously YouTube's chief product officer, took over when Susan Wojcicki stepped down.

> **Pro tip:** If you need these dates for a school project or an article, the one people most often get wrong is the launch. The domain (February), the first video (April) and the public launch (late 2005) are three different dates.

## What Was the First YouTube Video? ("Me at the Zoo", 19 Seconds)

![The first YouTube video in numbers: Me at the zoo, 19 seconds long, uploaded April 23, 2005 by Jawed Karim](/images/blog/who-made-youtube-first-video-1200x675.webp)

**"Me at the zoo"** is 19 seconds long. Jawed Karim stands in front of the elephants at the San Diego Zoo and notes that they have "really, really, really long trunks". It's still on his channel, jawed, and has hundreds of millions of views at the time of writing.

## Why Did Google Buy YouTube for $1.65 Billion?

Google's own video service, Google Video, was struggling, while YouTube had become one of the most visited sites on the web within about a year of launch. Buying YouTube gave Google the leader instead of a struggling rival to it.

YouTube kept its brand and stayed in San Bruno, California, where its headquarters still is.

## YouTube Today: From a Zoo Clip to Long Videos, Shorts and Live

YouTube is now the world's largest video platform, with long videos, Shorts, live streams and music under one roof, and creators earn money through the YouTube Partner Program.

The founders' original problem, getting a video in front of people easily, has turned into a new one for creators: keeping up a regular upload schedule. Take Theo, who's starting a cooking channel.
