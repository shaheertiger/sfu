You keep seeing a competitor's ads and want to know what else they're running, or you're about to launch your own campaign and want to see how your market talks. The Meta Ad Library (still often called the Facebook Ad Library) is a **free, public, searchable database of ads running across Meta's apps**, including Facebook and Instagram. Open it at **facebook.com/ads/library**, without logging in or having an ad account.

![The Meta Ad Library: search any advertiser or keyword for free at facebook.com/ads/library and see their active Facebook and Instagram ads](/images/blog/meta-ads-library-hero-1200x675.webp)

Search by advertiser name or keyword and you'll see the ads a business is currently running. For ads about social issues, elections or politics, it also shows extra details such as spending ranges and who paid for the ad.

What it won't show is often what people want most: spend and targeting for ordinary ads. This guide covers how to search, what the filters do, the limits, and how to turn what you find into fair competitor research.

## What the Meta Ad Library Shows (and What It Hides)

![What the Meta Ad Library shows for most ads, like creative and start date, compared with what it hides, like spend and targeting](/images/blog/meta-ads-library-shows-1200x675.webp)

Meta built the library for transparency, but marketers also use it to understand their market: what competitors promote, which formats they use, and how their messaging changes over time.

| Question | Answer at the time of writing |
| --- | --- |
| Where is it? | facebook.com/ads/library |
| Does it cost anything? | No, it's free |
| Do I need an account? | No for basic searches; some features may ask you to log in |
| Which apps are covered? | Ads across Meta's technologies, such as Facebook, Instagram, Messenger and Audience Network |
| Which ads can I see? | Active ads from all advertisers; inactive ads mainly for issue, electoral and political ads |
| Does it show spend? | Only for issue, electoral and political ads, as ranges |
| Does it show targeting? | Limited details for political ads and for ads delivered in some regions, such as the EU |

### What Most People Don't Know

For most advertisers, the library only shows ads that are running right now. Once an ordinary ad stops, it usually drops out of public view. So a single visit is a snapshot; checking regularly is what reveals a competitor's campaign rhythm.

> **Key point:** The library shows the creative, platforms and start date of ordinary ads, but not their budget, results or targeting. Treat it as a window into messaging, not performance.

Meta changes the library's layout and what it shows, especially as transparency laws evolve, so check the library itself or the [Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help) for current details.

## How to Search the Meta Ad Library (6 Steps, Free)

![Steps to search the Meta Ad Library: choose a country, choose a category, search an advertiser or keyword, then open the ad details](/images/blog/meta-ads-library-steps-1200x675.webp)

Labels change as Meta updates the library, so look for the closest match.

### Step 1: Go to facebook.com/ads/library

Any browser works.

### Step 2: Choose a Country

Pick the country where the ads are shown, or "All" to search everywhere.

### Step 3: Choose an Ad Category

Select **All ads**, or **Issues, elections or politics** for political and social-issue ads. In some countries you'll also see special categories for housing, employment or financial products.

### Step 4: Type Your Search

Enter an advertiser's name (a brand or Page) or a keyword.

### Step 5: Pick a Result

As you type, the library suggests matching Pages. Selecting one shows that advertiser's ads. Pressing Enter on a keyword shows all ads that mention it. Picking the wrong Page here is the most common mistake: many brands have similarly named fan pages or regional pages, so check the name, profile picture and follower count.

> **Pro tip:** Search by advertiser when you want everything one brand runs. Search by keyword, with quotation marks for an exact phrase when available, to see how a whole category talks about a product.

### Step 6: Open the Ad's Details

Click **See ad details** (or similar) to view every version of the creative, the platforms it runs on and its start date.

## Meta Ad Library Filters Explained

Once you've searched, filters narrow the results. Options vary by country and category, but typically include:

| Filter | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Language | Ads in a particular language |
| Advertiser | Narrows keyword results to specific Pages |
| Platform | Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, Audience Network and other Meta surfaces |
| Media type | Images, videos, memes, or ads without images or video |
| Active status | Active ads, inactive ads, or both |
| Date range | Ads shown during a particular period |

You can also sort results, for example by most recent, and some views group identical ads that use several versions of the same creative.

## Active vs Inactive Ads: How Long Ads Stay Visible

- **Active ads** are being shown right now. For most advertisers, these are the only ads you can see.
- **Inactive ads** have stopped. For issues, elections or politics ads, Meta keeps them for years (seven years at the time of writing). For most other ads, inactive ones drop out of public view, with exceptions in some regions.

The library also shows when an ad **started running**. An ad that has been active for a long time may suggest the advertiser is happy with it, but it isn't proof that it performs well. Businesses keep ads running for many reasons.

## Political and Issue Ads: The Extra Transparency

Ads about social issues, elections or politics come with much more information. Advertisers complete an authorization process and add a **"Paid for by" disclaimer**. In the library these ads usually show:

- **Amount spent**, as a range.
- **Impressions**, as a range.
- **Audience breakdown**, such as age, gender and location.
- **Who paid for it**, from the disclaimer.

Meta also publishes an **Ad Library Report** with aggregated spending on these ads by advertiser and region.

### Ads Shown in the European Union

Because of EU transparency rules, ads delivered in the EU can show additional information, such as reach in EU countries and some targeting parameters, and stay viewable for a period after they stop. Details depend on the country and the rules in force.

## How to Use the Facebook Ad Library for Competitor Research (5-Step Routine)

![Checklist for fair competitor research in the Meta Ad Library: look for patterns and take notes, and avoid copying creative or scraping](/images/blog/meta-ads-library-research-1200x675.webp)

### What You Can Learn

| Signal | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Offers | Discounts, free trials, bundles, seasonal campaigns |
| Messaging angles | Which problems a competitor leads with |
| Creative formats | The mix of video, image and carousel ads |
| Landing pages | Where ads send people and what those pages ask for |
| Timing | When campaigns launch and how long they run |
| Testing | Several versions of one ad usually mean a headline or visual test |

### A Simple Monthly Routine

1. **List three to five competitors** and find their correct Pages.
2. **Review their active ads once a month** and note offers, angles and formats in a spreadsheet.
3. **Look for patterns**, not individual ads. One ad tells you little; a theme repeated across many tells you more.
4. **Compare with your own content.** Where is your message weaker? Where do you have an advantage they don't mention?
5. **Turn insights into your own ideas**, then test them.

### Research Ethically

- **Don't copy creative.** Images, videos and copy belong to the advertiser. Use them as inspiration, not templates.
- **Don't impersonate or mislead.** Never create ads or posts that could be mistaken for another brand's.
- **Be careful with personal data.** Political ad data is there for transparency, not for targeting or harassing individuals.

## Ad Spy Tools and Library Scrapers (Use With Caution)

Paid "ad spy" tools and browser extensions promise everything the library hides: estimated budgets, archives of old ads, "winning ad" lists.

### The Real Risks

- **Scraping can break Meta's terms.** Use the library's normal interface or Meta's official Ad Library API rather than unauthorized scraping tools.
- **Spend and performance figures are estimates.** Ordinary ads don't publish their budgets, so any number a tool shows is its own guess.
- **Extensions can see more than ads.** Some request broad access to everything you browse.
- **"Winning ads" invite copying**, which is exactly what gets brands into trouble.

### A Short Checklist

- Prefer the official library or the official API.
- Read an extension's permissions before installing it.
- Treat any spend estimate as a guess, not data.
- Never reuse another brand's creative.

> **Bottom line:** The free library shows what's actually running. Anything beyond that is an estimate, and some of the ways tools get it break Meta's terms.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the Meta Ad Library free to use?

Yes. It's free and public at facebook.com/ads/library, needs no ad account, and basic searches don't require logging in.

### Can I see how much a competitor spends on Meta ads?

Not for most ads. Spend, shown as ranges, is only available for ads about social issues, elections or politics. For other ads, the library shows the creative, platforms and start date, but not budgets.

### Does the Meta Ad Library include Instagram ads?

Yes. It covers ads across Meta's technologies, including Instagram, Facebook, Messenger and Audience Network, and you can filter by platform.

### Why can't I find an advertiser's old ads?

For most advertisers, the library only shows active ads. Inactive ads are kept long term mainly for issue, electoral and political ads, and in some regions with extra transparency rules.

### Can I see who an ad was targeted to?

Only in some cases. Political and issue ads show audience breakdowns, and ads shown in the EU can include some targeting details. For other ads, targeting isn't shown.

### Is it OK to use the Ad Library to research competitors?

Yes. It's a public transparency tool, and reviewing it for market research is common. Use it for insight, don't copy other brands' creative, and don't scrape it with tools that break Meta's terms.

### Does a long-running ad mean it's working?

Not necessarily. It may suggest the advertiser is happy with it, but businesses keep ads running for many reasons.

### Is there an API for the Meta Ad Library?

Yes. Meta offers an official Ad Library API, which is the right route for programmatic research instead of scraping.
