You've written a week of posts, and now you're wondering how to get them out without opening six apps at 9 AM every day. To schedule social media posts, write the post in the platform's composer, choose **Schedule** instead of **Publish**, and pick a date and time. Most networks have this built in: Instagram, Facebook (through Meta Business Suite), X, LinkedIn, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest and Threads all schedule natively, while **Bluesky and Google Business Profile don't** at the time of writing.

![How to schedule social media posts: use each platform's free scheduler, or one queue for every platform](/images/blog/how-to-schedule-social-media-posts-hero-1200x675.webp)

Native scheduling is free but works one platform at a time. If the same post goes to three or more accounts, a scheduler that publishes to all of them from one queue saves the repeat work.

This guide covers the whole process from plan to published, the native route on each platform, where it gets inconvenient, how the options compare, and the same job done in one queue.

## Why Scheduling Is the Last Step, Not the First

Scheduling is the end of a short routine. Skip the earlier steps and you end up scheduling whatever's ready, not what you planned.

![The six steps from plan to published when you schedule social media posts](/images/blog/how-to-schedule-social-media-posts-process-1200x675.webp)

1. **Plan.** Decide what goes out this week and where, on a [social media content calendar](/blog/social-media-content-calendar).
2. **Write and adapt.** Draft one core post, then adjust it per platform: X allows 280 characters, Bluesky 300, Threads 500, LinkedIn 3,000, and Instagram needs an image or video.
3. **Prepare media.** Crop and export at each platform's size before you upload.
4. **Schedule.** Pick an exact time for date-sensitive posts, or drop routine posts into fixed weekly time slots.
5. **Review.** Have someone check posts that need sign-off before they go out.
6. **Monitor.** Check that each post published, and fix and retry any that a platform rejected.

### What Most People Don't Know

Every platform's native scheduler has a different horizon, from 10 days on TikTok to 18 months on X. A third-party scheduler holds the post itself and publishes it at the time you chose, so its limit doesn't depend on the platform's window. That matters the moment you try to plan more than a week and a half ahead.

> **Key point:** Native schedulers are free and official, and they're the right answer for one or two platforms. The cost shows up when you multiply them: every extra platform is another composer, another upload and another list to check.

### Step 1: Open the Composer (on Desktop for X and TikTok)

X doesn't offer scheduling in its mobile app, and TikTok's scheduler is on its website, so use a computer for those two.

### Step 2: Write the Caption and Attach the Media

Use the version written for that platform, not a copy of another one.

### Step 3: Look for Schedule, Not Post

It's a **Schedule** option, a clock icon or a calendar icon, usually next to or instead of the Post button.

### Step 4: Choose the Date and Time, and Check the Timezone

This is the step people get wrong. The time is usually in your device's timezone, not your audience's. A 9 AM post set from London lands at 4 AM in New York.

### Step 5: Confirm

The post now sits in that platform's scheduled list until it goes out.

> **Pro tip:** To change a scheduled post later, find the platform's scheduled list (for example LinkedIn's **View all scheduled posts**, or Planner in Meta Business Suite) and edit, reschedule or delete it there. Make a note of where each list lives, because they're all in different places.

## Where Native Scheduling Gets Inconvenient: One Illustrator, Six Platforms

Native schedulers are fine for one or two networks. The work multiplies with every platform you add.

![What native scheduling handles well compared with where it breaks down across six platforms](/images/blog/how-to-schedule-social-media-posts-problem-1200x675.webp)

Take a freelance illustrator who finishes a new piece and wants it on Instagram, Threads, X, Bluesky, LinkedIn and Pinterest next Tuesday at 10:00, then goes on holiday for two weeks.

- **Six composers, six uploads.** The same image is uploaded six times, and the caption is pasted and trimmed six times: 280 characters for X, 300 for Bluesky, a longer story for LinkedIn, a keyword-rich description for Pinterest.
- **Bluesky has no scheduler.** Either someone posts it by hand on Tuesday from the beach, or it doesn't go out.
- **Different horizons.** Two weeks of posts is fine for most platforms, but a TikTok post planned for day 12 can't be scheduled before leaving, because the native window is 10 days.
- **No single list.** Checking that everything is lined up means opening six apps. If Instagram rejects a post, the only notice is in the Instagram app.
- **Changes don't travel.** Spot a typo in the caption and it's six edits.

For two weeks of three posts a week, that's well over a hundred separate steps before the trip.

## Unofficial Auto-Posting Bots and Extensions (Use With Caution)

Some tools "schedule" posts by logging in as you through a browser extension or a bot, instead of using each platform's official API.

### The Real Risks

- **Your password leaves the platform.** Anything that logs in as you can do anything you can.
- **Unusual logins get accounts locked.** Platforms watch for automated logins and can restrict accounts that use them.
- **Engagement automation.** Many bots also auto-like, auto-follow or auto-reply, which is what gets accounts limited.

### Safety Checklist

- Use tools that connect through each platform's own sign-in (OAuth), or app passwords on Bluesky.
- Never type your main password into a scheduler's site.
- Only schedule your own original posts. Skip auto-likes, auto-follows and auto-replies.

> **Bottom line:** Scheduling through official APIs is normal and allowed. Bots that pretend to be you are what get accounts limited. Our guide to [social media posting bots](/blog/social-media-posting-bots) covers the difference.

## The Same Job in Schedule For You (Step by Step)

Here's the illustrator's Tuesday post and the two weeks after it, done once.

### Step 1: Write Once, Adapt per Platform

Click **Create post**, tick the accounts, and write the caption. Each platform gets its own tab, so you can shorten the X version or expand the LinkedIn one without touching the others, and the counters show each limit as you type (Bluesky 300, X 280, LinkedIn 3,000, Instagram 2,200). Problems such as a missing image for Instagram show under **Fix before publishing** before you can schedule.

![New post in Schedule For You with Bluesky, X, Instagram and LinkedIn selected, a tab for each platform, character counters for each, and Publish now, Schedule, Add to queue and Save draft options](/images/blog/composer-four-platforms-1440x900.webp)

Then choose:

- **Schedule** for an exact date and time, in your workspace's timezone. Bluesky and Google Business Profile included.
- **Add to queue** to drop it into the next open posting slot.
- **Publish now** or **Save draft**.

### Step 2: Fill Two Weeks With Posting Slots

Set posting slots once in **Settings**, for example Monday, Wednesday and Friday at 9:00 and 17:30. Every post added to the queue fills the next open slot in order, so two weeks of work takes one sitting. On a paid plan, **Bulk scheduling** loads a whole CSV of posts into those slots at once.

![Settings in Schedule For You showing the timezone, email reminders, posting slots for weekdays at 9:00 AM, 12:30 PM and 5:30 PM, and API keys](/images/blog/settings-posting-slots-api-keys-1440x900.webp)

The TikTok post for day 12 is scheduled with the rest: Schedule For You holds it and publishes it at the time you chose.

### Step 3: Check One Queue, and Get Alerts When Something Fails

The **Queue** lists every upcoming post across accounts. If a platform rejects one (a video that's too short, a caption that's too long after link expansion), it lands in **Failed** with the reason and a **Retry** button, and with failure emails switched on in Settings you hear about it straight away.

![Queue in Schedule For You showing a countdown to the next post, three failed posts with their reasons and a Retry button, and upcoming posts with their accounts](/images/blog/queue-1440x900.webp)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you schedule posts on every social media platform?

Most of them. Instagram, Facebook Pages, X, LinkedIn, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest and Threads have native scheduling. Bluesky and Google Business Profile don't at the time of writing, so you need a third-party scheduler for those.

### What is the best free way to schedule social media posts?

Use each platform's own scheduler, and Meta Business Suite for Facebook and Instagram together. They're free and official. Our list of the [best free social media schedulers](/blog/best-free-social-media-schedulers) covers the options and their limits.

### Do scheduled posts get less reach?

Not because they're scheduled. Posts published through official APIs are treated the same as posts made in the app. Reach depends on the content and when your audience is online. More in [do scheduled posts get less views](/blog/do-scheduled-posts-get-less-views).

### Can I schedule the same post to several platforms at once?

Not with native tools, except Meta Business Suite for Facebook and Instagram. A scheduler like Schedule For You sends one post to as many connected accounts as you select, with a separate caption per platform when you need one. See [how to cross-post to multiple platforms](/blog/how-to-cross-post-to-multiple-platforms).

### How far in advance can I schedule social media posts?

It depends on the platform: TikTok allows 10 days on its website, Meta Business Suite about 29 days, Pinterest 30 days, Instagram and Threads 75 days, LinkedIn 3 months and X 18 months, at the time of writing. A third-party scheduler holds the post itself, so its limit doesn't depend on the platform's window.

### What should I set up after scheduling my first posts?

Automate the routine parts: posting slots, bulk uploads, evergreen posts and RSS auto-post. Our guide to [automating social media scheduling](/blog/how-to-automate-social-media-scheduling) covers each one, and [planning content in advance](/blog/plan-content-in-advance) gives you a weekly routine to keep the queue full.

### Can I schedule Bluesky posts?

Not in Bluesky's own app at the time of writing. Use a third-party scheduler that connects with a Bluesky app password. See [how to schedule Bluesky posts](/blog/how-to-schedule-bluesky-posts).

### What timezone do scheduled posts use?

Native schedulers usually use your device's timezone. In Schedule For You, posts use your workspace's timezone, set in Settings.
