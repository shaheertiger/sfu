To schedule social media posts, write the post in the platform's composer, choose **Schedule** instead of **Publish**, and pick a date and time. Most networks have this built in: Instagram, Facebook (through Meta Business Suite), X, LinkedIn, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest and Threads all schedule natively, while Bluesky and Google Business Profile don't at the time of writing. Native scheduling is free but works one platform at a time, so if the same post goes to three or more accounts, a scheduler that publishes to all of them from one queue saves the repeat work.

Below: the whole process from plan to published, the native route on each platform, where it gets inconvenient, how the options compare, and the same job done in Schedule For You.

## The process: from plan to published

Scheduling is the last step of a short routine. Skip the earlier ones and you end up scheduling whatever's ready, not what you planned.

1. **Plan.** Decide what goes out this week and where, on a [social media content calendar](/blog/social-media-content-calendar).
2. **Write and adapt.** Draft one core post, then adjust it per platform: X allows 280 characters, Bluesky 300, Threads 500, LinkedIn 3,000, and Instagram needs an image or video.
3. **Prepare media.** Crop and export at each platform's size before you upload.
4. **Schedule.** Pick an exact time for date-sensitive posts, or drop routine posts into fixed weekly time slots.
5. **Review.** Have someone check posts that need sign-off before they go out.
6. **Monitor.** Check that each post published, and fix and retry any that a platform rejected.

The steps are nearly identical everywhere:

1. Open the composer on the platform (on desktop for X and TikTok).
2. Write the caption and attach the media.
3. Look for **Schedule**, a clock icon or a calendar icon instead of the Post button.
4. Choose the date and time. Check the timezone, which is usually your device's.
5. Confirm. The post now sits in that platform's scheduled list until it goes out.

To change it later, find the platform's scheduled list (for example LinkedIn's **View all scheduled posts**, or Planner in Meta Business Suite) and edit, reschedule or delete it there.

## Where native scheduling gets inconvenient

Native schedulers are fine for one or two networks. The work multiplies with every platform you add.

Take a freelance illustrator who finishes a new piece and wants it on Instagram, Threads, X, Bluesky, LinkedIn and Pinterest next Tuesday at 10:00, then goes on holiday for two weeks.

- **Six composers, six uploads.** The same image is uploaded six times, and the caption is pasted and trimmed six times: 280 characters for X, 300 for Bluesky, a longer story for LinkedIn, a keyword-rich description for Pinterest.
- **Bluesky has no scheduler.** Either someone posts it by hand on Tuesday from the beach, or it doesn't go out.
- **Different horizons.** Two weeks of posts is fine for most platforms, but a TikTok post planned for day 12 can't be scheduled before leaving, because the native window is 10 days.
- **No single list.** Checking that everything is lined up means opening six apps. If Instagram rejects a post, the only notice is in the Instagram app.
- **Changes don't travel.** Spot a typo in the caption and it's six edits.

For two weeks of three posts a week, that's well over a hundred separate steps before the trip.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you schedule posts on every social media platform?

Most of them. Instagram, Facebook Pages, X, LinkedIn, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest and Threads have native scheduling. Bluesky and Google Business Profile don't at the time of writing, so you need a third-party scheduler for those.

### What is the best free way to schedule social media posts?

Use each platform's own scheduler, and Meta Business Suite for Facebook and Instagram together. They're free and official. Our list of the [best free social media schedulers](/blog/best-free-social-media-schedulers) covers the options and their limits.

### Do scheduled posts get less reach?

Not because they're scheduled. Posts published through official APIs are treated the same as posts made in the app. Reach depends on the content and when your audience is online. More in [do scheduled posts get less views](/blog/do-scheduled-posts-get-less-views).

### Can I schedule the same post to several platforms at once?

Not with native tools, except Meta Business Suite for Facebook and Instagram. A scheduler like Schedule For You sends one post to as many connected accounts as you select, with a separate caption per platform when you need one. See [how to cross-post to multiple platforms](/blog/how-to-cross-post-to-multiple-platforms).

### How far in advance can I schedule social media posts?

It depends on the platform: TikTok allows 10 days on its website, Meta Business Suite about 29 days, Pinterest 30 days, Instagram and Threads 75 days, LinkedIn 3 months and X 18 months, at the time of writing. A third-party scheduler holds the post itself, so its limit doesn't depend on the platform's window.

### What should I set up after scheduling my first posts?

Automate the routine parts: posting slots, bulk uploads, evergreen posts and RSS auto-post. Our guide to [automating social media scheduling](/blog/how-to-automate-social-media-scheduling) covers each one, and [planning content in advance](/blog/plan-content-in-advance) gives you a weekly routine to keep the queue full.
