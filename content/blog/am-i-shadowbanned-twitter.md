Your posts on X got dozens of replies last week. This week, almost nothing. To find out whether you're shadowbanned, open **Settings and privacy → Your account → Account status** first. It's the only place X confirms a restriction. Then run four quick manual tests: a logged-out search, a search-suggestion check, a reply check, and a check of your protected and sensitive-media settings.

X never uses the word "shadowban". It talks about **visibility filtering** and **limiting reach**, and it doesn't always tell you when either is applied. That's why so many free checker sites exist, and why most of their results are guesses.

This guide covers what a shadowban on X actually is, the tests that work, the triggers behind most limits, and a step-by-step recovery plan.

## What a Shadowban on X Actually Is (and What X Calls It)

"Shadowban" is a community term for a restriction you can't see from your own account. You can still post and like normally, but fewer people see what you post.

| What you notice | Normal account | Possibly limited account |
|---|---|---|
| Views per post | In line with your usual range | A sudden, sharp drop across every post |
| Search for `from:yourhandle` while logged out | Your recent posts appear | Posts missing from Latest |
| Typing your handle in search | You appear in the suggestions | You only appear after the full name, or not at all |
| Your replies in threads | Shown with other replies | Behind "Show more replies" or "Show probable spam" |
| Labels on your posts | None | A notice that the post's visibility is limited |

![Signs of a normal X account compared with signs of an account whose reach is limited](/images/blog/am-i-shadowbanned-twitter-signs-1200x675.webp)

### What Most People Don't Know

X's own rules describe the actions it can take: limiting a post's visibility (keeping it out of search, trends and recommendations), limiting an account's reach, requiring you to delete a post, putting an account in read-only mode, and suspending it. Some of these show a label or notice, and some don't. The [X Help Center](https://help.x.com) has the current list.

> **Key point:** Most "shadowbans" turn out to be something more ordinary: a protected account, media marked as sensitive, a brand-new account, or just a quieter week. Rule those out before you assume the worst.

## How to Check If You're Shadowbanned on X: 5 Tests That Actually Work

Run these in order. Menu names on X change between versions, so if you can't find a setting, use the search box in Settings.

![Five tests for an X shadowban, starting with the Account status page](/images/blog/am-i-shadowbanned-twitter-tests-1200x675.webp)

### Test 1: Open Your Account Status Page (the Only Official Answer)

Go to **Settings and privacy → Your account → Account status**, or search Settings for "account status". If it lists no restrictions, an account-wide limit is unlikely. If it lists one, it usually names the rule involved and may offer an appeal.

Also check your X notifications and the email address on your account for notices. Only trust messages from X's official domains, and never enter your password from a link in an unexpected email.

### Test 2: The Logged-Out Search Test

Open a private browser window and search for `from:yourusername`, then switch to the **Latest** tab. If X asks you to log in, ask a friend who doesn't follow you to run the same search. If your recent posts are missing while other accounts' posts appear, your search visibility may be limited.

### Test 3: The Search Suggestion Test

Ask a non-follower to start typing your handle in search. If you only show up after they type it in full and press enter, or not at all, that's the pattern people call a search suggestion ban. New accounts often take a few weeks to appear here, so don't read much into it on a brand-new account.

### Test 4: The Reply Test

Reply to a post, then ask a non-follower to open that conversation. If your reply sits behind **Show more replies** or **Show probable spam** in several different threads, your replies are being ranked down. One low-ranked reply is normal, since X orders replies by its own signals.

### Test 5: Rule Out the Boring Explanations

- **Protected account?** If "Protect your posts" is on, only followers see your posts and they won't appear in public search. See [how to make your Twitter account public](/blog/how-to-make-twitter-account-public).
- **Sensitive media?** Under **Privacy and safety → Your posts**, check whether "Mark media you post as having material that may be sensitive" is on.
- **New account?** New accounts with little activity take time to show up in search.

> **Pro tip:** Write down what each test showed and the date. If you appeal, or retest after a week, you'll know what changed.

### The 5 Tests Compared

| Test | What it checks | How reliable | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Account status page | Restrictions X has applied to your account | Highest: X's own record | 1 minute |
| Logged-out search | Whether your posts appear in search | High | 3 minutes |
| Search suggestions | Whether your handle is suggested | Medium: new accounts lag | 3 minutes |
| Reply test | Whether your replies are ranked down | Medium: needs several threads | 10 minutes |
| Settings check | Protected account, sensitive media | High | 2 minutes |

## Why X Limits Accounts: 8 Common Triggers

X doesn't always explain why reach drops, but these behaviors are commonly linked to spam detection and enforcement.

![Behaviors that get X accounts limited compared with the safe alternatives](/images/blog/am-i-shadowbanned-twitter-triggers-1200x675.webp)

| Behavior | Red flag | Safe approach |
|---|---|---|
| Duplicate posts | The same post again and again, or on several accounts you run | Write a fresh version for each post and account |
| Following | Mass following and unfollowing in a short time | Follow gradually and naturally |
| Replies and mentions | Lots of unsolicited replies, especially with links | Reply where you have something to add |
| Hashtags | Trending tags unrelated to your post | One to three relevant tags, or none |
| Links | Links to sites flagged as spam or unsafe | Links to reputable pages |
| Automation | Auto-likes, auto-follows, auto-replies | Only schedule your own original posts |
| Reports | Many blocks or reports in a short time | Stay inside X's rules on harassment and abuse |
| Policy | Posts that break X's rules | Read the rules before posting borderline content |

## Shadowbanned or Just Lower Reach? A 10-Minute Check

1. **Account status says "no restrictions"?** You probably aren't limited. Move on to step 2.
2. **Compare the last two weeks with the month before.** A drop on a few posts is normal. A sudden drop on every post, from one day to the next, is more suspicious.
3. **Run the logged-out search test.** If your posts appear, search isn't limited.

## Shadowban Checker Sites: Why Most Results Are Wrong (Use With Caution)

Search "X shadowban test" and you'll find sites that claim to scan your account in seconds. They can't see X's internal systems, so all they can do is run searches from the outside and guess, and those searches break whenever X changes how search works.

### The Real Risks

- **False alarms and false all-clears.** A site can say "banned" because its own search failed, or "clean" while your replies are hidden.
- **Some ask you to log in with X.** Never give a checker your password, and be careful which apps you allow to access your account.
- **"Shadowban removal" services are scams.** Only X can lift its own restrictions, and appeals are free.

### If You Use One Anyway

- Treat the result as a hint, then confirm it on your Account status page.
- Prefer tools that walk you through manual tests over ones that claim to see inside X.
- Review connected apps under **Security and account access → Apps and sessions** and revoke anything you don't use.

> **Bottom line:** Your Account status page and X's own notices are the only authoritative sources. Everything else is a guess.

## How to Remove a Shadowban on X: 7-Step Recovery Plan

![Four moves to recover reach on X: read what X says, fix what it names, stop the trigger, then appeal and wait](/images/blog/am-i-shadowbanned-twitter-recovery-1200x675.webp)

1. **Find out what X says.** Read your Account status page and any notices. If a post or rule is named, start there.
2. **Complete any required steps.** Verify your phone or email, or delete the named post, through X's official prompt.
3. **Remove or edit problem content.** Delete posts that clearly break the rules, and tidy up repeated or spammy posts.
4. **Stop the trigger.** Pause mass following, automated replies, repeated links and duplicate posting.
5. **Revoke apps you don't trust**, under **Security and account access → Apps and sessions**.
6. **Appeal if it's a mistake.** Use the appeal option on your Account status page or X's help center forms. Explain briefly why you think it's wrong.
7. **Post normally and give it time.** Keep posting original content at a steady pace. Many limits are temporary.

## How Long Does a Shadowban on X Last?

There's no fixed length, and X doesn't publish one. Some limits lift on their own once the spam-like activity stops. Others last until you complete a step X asks for, or until an appeal is reviewed. If nothing has changed after a couple of weeks of normal posting, check Account status again and appeal if a restriction is still listed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does X (Twitter) shadowban accounts?

X doesn't use the term, but it can limit the visibility of posts and accounts that break its rules or look like spam. That includes keeping content out of search and recommendations, which is what most people mean by a shadowban.

### How do I check if I'm shadowbanned on X?

Open your Account status page (Settings and privacy → Your account at the time of writing), and check your notifications and email for notices from X. Then run a logged-out `from:yourusername` search and confirm your account isn't protected and your media isn't marked as sensitive.

### How long does a shadowban on X last?

There's no fixed length. Some limits lift once spam-like activity stops, while others last until you complete a step or an appeal is reviewed.

### Are shadowban checker websites accurate?

Not reliably. They can't see X's internal systems and only infer restrictions from search results, which change often. Use your Account status page instead, and never share your password.

### Can scheduling posts get me shadowbanned on X?

Scheduling your own original posts through X's official API at a normal pace is allowed. Problems come from duplicate posting across accounts, automated replies or mass following.

### How do I appeal a restriction on X?

Use the appeal option on your Account status page or the relevant form in X's help center. Explain briefly why you think the action is a mistake. Response times vary.

### What is a search ban on Twitter?

A search ban is when your posts or account don't appear in X search for other people, even though your account works normally. Test it with a logged-out search for `from:yourusername` on the Latest tab.

### What is a ghost ban on Twitter?

A ghost ban is when your replies are hidden from others in a conversation, or pushed behind "Show more replies". Ask a non-follower to open a few threads you replied to and check where your replies appear.
