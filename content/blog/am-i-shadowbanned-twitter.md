If your posts on X (formerly Twitter) suddenly get far fewer views, or people say they can't find you in search, you may be under what users call a "shadowban". X itself talks about **visibility filtering** and **limits on reach** rather than shadowbans. The quickest way to check is to look at your **Account status** page in X's settings and any notices X has sent you, then rule out simpler explanations such as a protected account or a normal drop in engagement.

This guide explains what people mean by a shadowban, what X actually does, how to check, and how to recover.

## Quick answer: signs and checks

| What you notice | Possible explanation | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A label on a post saying its visibility is limited | X applied a visibility restriction to that post | The label itself and any linked explanation |
| Your account doesn't appear in search suggestions | A search-related limit, a new account, or sensitive-media settings | Account status; your media settings |
| Replies hidden behind "Show more replies" | Lower ranking of replies, or a limit on your account | Account status; recent activity |
| Sudden drop in views across all posts | A limit, or ordinary changes in reach | Account status; notifications; analytics |
| You can only read, not post | Your account is locked or in read-only mode | The notice shown when you log in |
| Followers can't see your posts at all | Your account is protected | Privacy and safety settings |

## What people mean by "shadowban" on X

"Shadowban" is a community term, not an official X label. People use it for several different things:

- **Search ban**: your account or posts don't show up when people search for you.
- **Search suggestion ban**: your handle doesn't appear in the suggestions as someone types your name.
- **Reply deboosting**: your replies are pushed down or hidden behind "Show more replies" or "Show probable spam".
- **Reduced reach**: your posts get shown to far fewer people than usual, without an obvious reason.

The common thread is that the restriction is hard to see from your side.

## What X itself says it does

X's rules describe enforcement actions that can apply to individual posts or to whole accounts. At the time of writing, these include:

- **Limiting the visibility of a post.** X may make a post that breaks its rules less discoverable, for example by keeping it out of search results, trends and recommendations. Some of these posts get a public label noting that visibility is limited.
- **Limiting an account's reach.** Accounts that break rules repeatedly, or look like spam, can have their content shown to fewer people for a period.
- **Requiring you to delete a post.** You may need to remove a post before you can use your account normally again.
- **Locking an account or putting it in read-only mode.** You can browse but can't post, like or repost until you complete a step or a time limit passes.
- **Suspending an account.** The most serious action, which X tells you about directly.

X's approach to labeling has been described publicly as limiting reach rather than removing posts in some cases. The details change, so the [X Help Center](https://help.x.com) is the place to read the current rules and enforcement options.

## How to check your account status on X

### 1. Open your Account status page

X has an **Account status** page that shows whether your account has restrictions. At the time of writing, you can usually reach it from **Settings and privacy → Your account**, though X has moved it between versions. If you can't find it, open Settings and search for "account status".

If the page says your account is in good standing and lists no restrictions, an account-wide limit is unlikely. If it lists a restriction, it typically names the rule involved and may offer a way to appeal.

### 2. Check your notifications and email

X usually notifies you when it takes action on your account, such as locking it or asking you to delete a post. Look in your X notifications and in the email inbox linked to your account. Check spam folders too, but be careful: only trust messages from X's official domains, and never enter your password from a link in an unexpected email.

### 3. Look at labels on your own posts

Open your recent posts. If X has limited a specific post, you may see a notice on it. That suggests the issue is the post itself, not your whole account.

### 4. Rule out the simple explanations

- **Is your account protected?** If "Protect your posts" is on, only followers see your posts, and they won't appear in public search. Our guide on [how to make your Twitter account public](/blog/how-to-make-twitter-account-public) shows how to switch it off.
- **Is your media marked as sensitive?** Under **Privacy and safety → Your posts** (at the time of writing), check whether "Mark media you post as having material that may be sensitive" is on. Sensitive media can be hidden from people who've chosen not to see it.
- **Is your account new?** New accounts with little activity often take time to appear in search suggestions.
- **Did engagement just drop?** Reach on X varies a lot from post to post. A quieter week isn't necessarily a restriction.

### A note on third-party "shadowban checkers"

Various websites claim to test whether you're shadowbanned by searching for your account in different ways. They can't see X's internal systems, so their results are guesses at best and can be out of date when X changes how search works. Don't treat them as authoritative, and never give any of them your X password. Your Account status page and X's own notices are the reliable sources.

## Common causes of reduced visibility

X doesn't always explain why reach drops, but these behaviors are commonly linked to spam detection and enforcement:

- **Posting the same or nearly the same content repeatedly**, or across several accounts you run.
- **Mass following and unfollowing** in a short period.
- **Lots of unsolicited replies or mentions**, especially with links, to people who don't follow you.
- **Aggressive use of hashtags or trending topics** unrelated to your post.
- **Links to sites flagged as spam or unsafe.**
- **Automation that breaks X's automation rules**, such as auto-replies, auto-likes or auto-follows.
- **Many reports or blocks** from other users in a short time.
- **Posts that break X's rules** on topics like harassment, hateful conduct or private information.

## How to recover from a shadowban on X

1. **Find out what X says.** Read your Account status page and any notices. If a specific post or rule is named, start there.
2. **Complete any required steps.** If X asks you to verify a phone number or email, or delete a post, do it through the official prompt.
3. **Remove or edit problem content.** Delete posts that clearly break the rules, and tidy up repeated or spammy posts.
4. **Stop the behavior that triggered it.** Pause mass following, automated replies, repeated links and duplicate posting.
5. **Turn off third-party apps you don't trust.** Under **Security and account access → Apps and sessions**, revoke access for apps you no longer use or don't recognize.
6. **Appeal if you think it's a mistake.** Use the appeal option on your Account status page or the forms in X's help center. Timing varies and isn't guaranteed.
7. **Post normally and give it time.** Keep posting original content at a steady pace. Many limits are temporary.

### Avoid recovery scams

If someone offers to "remove your shadowban" for a fee, or asks for your login or two-factor codes, it's a scam. Only X can lift its own restrictions, and the appeal routes are free.

### Keep posts clean going forward

Write posts that fit the limit rather than chaining sloppy threads. The free [X character counter](/tools/x-character-counter) shows how X counts characters (links count as 23, most emoji as 2), so you can check before posting.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does X (Twitter) shadowban accounts?

X doesn't use the term "shadowban", but it can limit the visibility of posts and accounts that break its rules or look like spam. That can include keeping content out of search and recommendations, which is what most people mean by a shadowban.

### How do I check if I'm shadowbanned on X?

Open your Account status page in X's settings (usually under Settings and privacy → Your account at the time of writing) and check your notifications and email for notices from X. Also confirm your account isn't protected and your media isn't marked as sensitive.

### How long does a shadowban on X last?

There's no fixed length. Some limits are temporary and lift on their own once spam-like activity stops, while others last until you complete a step or an appeal is reviewed. X doesn't publish a set timeline.

### Are shadowban checker websites accurate?

Not reliably. They can't see X's internal systems and only infer restrictions from search results, which change often. Use X's Account status page instead, and never share your password with a checker site.

### Can scheduling posts get me shadowbanned on X?

Scheduling your own original posts through X's official API at a normal pace is allowed. Problems come from behavior like posting duplicate content across accounts, automated replies or mass following, which X's automation rules restrict.

### How do I appeal a restriction on X?

Use the appeal option shown on your Account status page or the relevant form in X's help center. Explain briefly why you think the action is a mistake. Response times vary and aren't guaranteed.
