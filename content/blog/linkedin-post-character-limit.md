You've written a LinkedIn post, pasted it in, and you're not sure if it's too long. The LinkedIn post character limit is **3,000 characters** for a regular feed post, including spaces, emoji and hashtags. But the feed only shows roughly the first **210 characters on desktop** and about **140 on mobile** before the **…see more** cut.

![LinkedIn post character limit: 3,000 characters in total, about 210 visible on desktop and 140 on mobile before see more](/images/blog/linkedin-post-character-limit-hero-1200x675.webp)

So the real limit that decides whether anyone reads your post is closer to 140 characters than 3,000. The rest only matters to people who tap.

This guide covers every LinkedIn limit, where the "see more" cut falls, how long posts that get read actually are, and how to write one post for several platforms with different limits.

## Why the 3,000 Limit Matters Less Than the First 140 Characters

![The first 140 to 210 characters of a LinkedIn post compared with the rest: the hook decides whether anyone taps see more](/images/blog/linkedin-post-character-limit-cutoff-1200x675.webp)

The feed truncates posts after about three lines. In practice that's around 210 characters on desktop and 140 on mobile, depending on line breaks. Everything after the cut is invisible until someone taps.

### What Most People Don't Know

Line breaks count against the preview, not just characters. An early line break pushes text below the cut, so a post that opens with a one-word line and a blank line shows far fewer than 210 characters. Line breaks in the body are kept as you type them, but LinkedIn strips extra blank lines beyond one.

> **Key point:** Write your first line as if it's the whole post. Put the hook, the claim or the question there, and never start with "I'm excited to share".

## How to Write a LinkedIn Post That Fits (Step by Step)

![Four steps to write within the LinkedIn limit: hook first, short paragraphs, one idea, a question at the end](/images/blog/linkedin-post-character-limit-steps-1200x675.webp)

### Step 1: Write the First Line Last

Draft the post, then come back and rewrite the opening so it works on its own in under 140 characters.

### Step 2: Keep Paragraphs to One or Two Sentences

Short paragraphs read better once someone taps "see more", and LinkedIn keeps your single line breaks.

### Step 3: Stick to One Idea

A 3,000-character post is about 450 to 500 words, longer than most posts need. Cut anything that serves a second idea and save it for another post.

### Step 4: End With a Question

A specific question gives readers something to answer in the comments, where they have 1,250 characters.

> **Pro tip:** Check the preview on your phone before posting. Mobile shows the least text, so if the hook works there, it works everywhere.

## Realistic Scenarios: How Long Should a LinkedIn Post Be?

The posts that get read tend to be 800 to 1,500 characters: a one-line hook, short paragraphs, one idea, a question at the end.

| Situation | Length | What fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A quick take or announcement | Under 500 characters | Hook, two short paragraphs, a link in the comments |
| A lesson or story | 800–1,500 characters | Hook, story in short paragraphs, one takeaway, a question |
| A how-to or list | 1,500–3,000 characters | Hook, numbered steps, a short summary |
| Longer than 3,000 | Over the limit | Write an article instead (110,000 characters) |

## Unicode Bold and Italic Text on LinkedIn (Use With Caution)

LinkedIn's own composer has no bold or italic. Text formatters, including our [LinkedIn text formatter](/tools/linkedin-text-formatter), get around that with Unicode characters that look bold or italic.

### The Real Risks

- **Screen readers.** Unicode bold and italic are separate characters from the regular alphabet. Some screen readers read them letter by letter or skip them, so a fully styled sentence can become unreadable.
- **Search.** Styled words may not match when people search for them.
- **Readability.** A whole post in bold reads as shouting.

### Use It Sparingly

- Style a few words, never the whole post.
- Don't style keywords people might search for.
- Keep names, numbers and links in plain text.

> **Bottom line:** A bolded phrase can help a hook stand out. A bolded post helps no one.

## What to Do Next

1. **Post feels too long:** cut it to one idea and aim for 800–1,500 characters.
2. **Low engagement:** rewrite the first line so it works under 140 characters.
3. **Over 3,000 characters:** turn it into an article.
4. **Posting the same idea elsewhere:** write a separate version for each limit.

Do:

- ✓ Hook in the first line
- ✓ Short paragraphs with single line breaks
- ✓ Three to five relevant hashtags

Don't:

- ✗ Open with a one-word line and a blank line
- ✗ Style the whole post in Unicode bold
- ✗ Paste a LinkedIn post into X unchanged

About 450 to 500 words.
