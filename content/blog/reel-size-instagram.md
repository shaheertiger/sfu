You've edited a Reel, uploaded it, and the title on your cover is cut in half on your profile, or the captions sit under Instagram's buttons. **The best size for an Instagram Reel is 1080×1920 pixels, a vertical 9:16 aspect ratio.** Upload at that size and your Reel fills the screen in the Reels tab without black bars or awkward cropping.

![Instagram Reel size 2026: 1080×1920 at 9:16, with the cover cropped to 3:4 on the profile grid](/images/blog/reel-size-instagram-hero-1200x675.webp)

Your cover image should also be 1080×1920, but plan for it to be cropped to a **3:4 rectangle on your profile grid** at the time of writing. Instagram also places your username, caption and buttons on top of the video.

This guide covers the specs in one table, why 9:16, how the cover crops, the safe zones, export settings for sharp Reels, length limits, and how the same file works on TikTok and YouTube Shorts.

## Why 1080×1920 and 9:16 (and What Happens With Other Ratios)

![Instagram Reel ratios compared: 9:16 fills the screen, while 1:1, 4:5 and 16:9 get bars or crops](/images/blog/reel-size-instagram-ratios-1200x675.webp)

Phones are tall, and Reels are designed to fill a phone screen held upright. A 9:16 ratio means the video is 9 units wide for every 16 units tall. At 1080 pixels wide, that's 1920 pixels tall.

| Your video | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 9:16 at 1080×1920 | Fills the screen |
| Square (1:1) or 4:5 | Empty space above and below, or Instagram lets you zoom and crop |
| Horizontal (16:9) | Small in the middle with large bars; text is hard to read |
| 9:16 at 720×1280 | Right shape, but scaled up on most phones and can look soft |

### What Most People Don't Know

The cover is shown in **different shapes in different places**: tall in the Reels tab on your profile, cropped to 3:4 on the main grid, and cropped differently again in the feed and Explore. A cover designed only for the full 9:16 frame almost always loses something on the grid.

> **Key point:** Design the cover at 1080×1920, but keep the title and faces inside the center 1080×1440. That's roughly what the 3:4 grid tile shows.

![The steps to design an Instagram Reel cover that survives the 3:4 grid crop](/images/blog/reel-size-instagram-cover-1200x675.webp)

### Step 1: Create the Cover at 1080×1920

The same size as the video.

### Step 2: Mark the Center 1080×1440 Area

That's roughly what remains visible in a 3:4 grid tile.

### Step 3: Keep Title, Face and Subject Inside It

This is the step most people skip: they center the text in the full frame, which often puts it too high or too low for the grid.

### Step 4: Use the Top and Bottom Strips for Background Only

They show in the Reels tab but get cropped on the grid.

### Step 5: Preview Before Posting

When you add a cover in the Instagram app, you can adjust how it appears on your profile grid.

> **Pro tip:** Keep cover text in the same font, size and position on every Reel. A custom cover image gives you more control than a frame from the video, and your grid looks planned.

## Instagram Reel Safe Zones: Where Text and Faces Get Covered

![Instagram Reel safe zones: keep text in the center, away from the bottom caption area, right-side buttons and top header](/images/blog/reel-size-instagram-safe-zones-1200x675.webp)

| Area | What covers it | Margin to leave |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bottom | Username, caption, audio name, sometimes location or product tags | Generous: the largest overlay |
| Right side | Like, comment, share, save and menu buttons | Narrower |
| Top | The Reels header, camera icon and the phone's status bar | Small |
| Center | Nothing | Put text and faces here |

Exact overlay sizes depend on the phone, the caption length and the app version, so no pixel measurement is perfect. Check the result on your own phone before publishing. If you add on-screen captions, place them a little above the center line rather than at the very bottom, where creators often put them out of habit from horizontal video.

## Export Settings for Sharp Reels (Problem, Fix, Why)

Most blurry Reels come from export settings or heavy compression, not from the wrong dimensions.

| Setting | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Resolution | 1080×1920 |
| Format | MP4 (or MOV) with H.264 video and AAC audio |
| Frame rate | The rate you recorded at; 30 fps is a safe default, and 24, 25 and 60 generally work |
| Bitrate | Reasonably high; many editors have a "high quality" or "social media" preset |

| Problem | Fix | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soft, blurry Reel | Re-export at 1080×1920 with a high-quality preset | Detail survives Instagram's compression |
| Looks worse on mobile data | Upload over Wi-Fi | On a weak connection the app may upload at lower quality |
| Still soft after upload | Turn on the high-quality upload setting | Its name and location change between versions; look under media or data quality |
| Gets worse each time | Don't re-save the same file many times | Each export adds compression |
| Blurry repost | Use the original file, not a screen recording | Screen recordings add blur and may include someone else's work |

If you shoot on a phone, record vertically at 1080p or higher. If you edit on a computer, set your project to 1080×1920 before you start, rather than resizing a finished horizontal edit, which often cuts off heads or text. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best size for an Instagram Reel?

1080×1920 pixels at a 9:16 aspect ratio. That fills a phone screen held upright and avoids black bars or cropping in the Reels viewer.

### What size should a Reel cover be?

Make the cover 1080×1920 pixels so it matches the video. Keep the important part in the center area, because the profile grid shows a 3:4 crop of the cover at the time of writing.

### Can I post a horizontal video as a Reel?

You can, but it will appear small with large bars above and below, and any text will be hard to read. Re-edit the video in a 9:16 frame, reframing each shot so the subject stays in view.

### Why is my Reel blurry after uploading?

The most common reasons are a low-resolution source file, a low export bitrate, uploading over a weak connection or using a screen recording instead of the original file. Export at 1080×1920 with a high-quality preset and upload over Wi-Fi.

### Is the Reel size the same as the Story size?

Yes. Both use 1080×1920 pixels at 9:16. The overlays differ: Stories place your profile name at the top and the reply bar at the bottom, so check safe zones separately.

### What frame rate should Instagram Reels be?

Keep the frame rate you recorded at. 30 fps is a safe default, and 24, 25 and 60 fps generally work too.

### How long can an Instagram Reel be?

At the time of writing, most accounts can upload Reels of up to a few minutes. Limits have changed several times, so check the upload screen in the app.

### Can I use the same video for TikTok and YouTube Shorts?

Yes. All three use a vertical 9:16 frame. Export one clean 1080×1920 master without watermarks and tailor the caption for each platform.
