To post a long video on Instagram, first check whether it fits the current Reel limit (at the time of writing, most accounts can upload Reels of up to 3 minutes in the app). If your video is longer, you have three practical options: trim it to the strongest section, split it into numbered parts posted as separate Reels, or post a short teaser on Instagram and send people to the full version on YouTube.

Instagram has changed its video rules several times, so the exact limits depend on your account, your app version and how you upload. This guide explains what to expect and how to handle each case without losing quality or viewers.

## Instagram video length limits at a glance

Instagram no longer has a separate long-form video section. Videos you share to the feed are generally published as Reels, and each format has its own length rules.

| Format | Length at the time of writing | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reel (uploaded in the app) | Up to 3 minutes for most accounts | The upload screen tells you if a file is too long |
| Video inside a carousel | Shorter than a Reel; often around 60 seconds per video | Check the app when you add a video to a carousel |
| Story | Up to 60 seconds per segment | Longer clips are split into consecutive segments |
| Live | Much longer sessions (hours) | Recorded lives can be shared afterward |

Treat every number in this table as "at the time of writing". Instagram tests different limits with different accounts, and uploads from a computer or through a scheduling tool can follow slightly different rules. If you're unsure, the [Instagram Help Center](https://help.instagram.com) and the upload screen in the app are the most reliable sources.

## What happens if your video is too long

When a video is longer than the limit for the format you picked, Instagram usually does one of the following:

- **Asks you to trim it.** The editor opens with a trim bar, and you can't publish until the clip fits.
- **Rejects the upload.** On some upload paths, including some third-party tools, an over-length file simply fails with an error.
- **Splits it (Stories only).** Stories cut long clips into back-to-back segments automatically.

Either way, always watch the preview before you publish, so you know exactly where the video starts and ends.

### Why shorter often performs better anyway

Even when a longer Reel is allowed, a tight cut tends to hold attention better. Viewers swipe away quickly, and a slow middle loses people before the payoff. Ask whether the video would be stronger at 60 to 90 seconds.

## Option 1: Trim the video to fit

If most of your video is setup and the valuable part is short, trimming is the cleanest route.

1. Watch the full video and note the timestamps of the strongest section.
2. Cut a version that opens on the most interesting moment, not on an introduction.
3. Export it at 1080×1920 (9:16) so it fills the screen. Our [Instagram Reel size guide](/blog/reel-size-instagram) covers export settings and safe zones.
4. Upload it as a Reel and mention in the caption where people can find the full version, if there is one.

Your editing app gives you far more control than Instagram's trim bar.

## Option 2: Split it into parts

Splitting works well for tutorials, stories and interviews that naturally fall into chapters.

1. **Find natural break points.** End each part on a moment that makes people want the next one, such as a question, a reveal or the next step in a process.
2. **Label every part clearly.** Put "Part 1 of 3" on screen in the first second and in the caption.
3. **Make each part understandable on its own.** Someone may see Part 2 first in their feed. A one-line recap at the start helps.
4. **Post the parts close together.** Publishing them on consecutive days, or a few hours apart, keeps the series fresh in people's minds.
5. **Link the series together.** Reply to comments on Part 1 when Part 2 is live, and mention the series in your captions so viewers know where to go next.

### Pin Part 1 to your profile

Instagram lets you pin a small number of posts to the top of your profile grid (up to three at the time of writing). Pinning Part 1 gives new visitors an obvious starting point for the series.

### Scheduling a multi-part series

A series is easier to run when the parts go out on a fixed rhythm. Schedule For You publishes a single video to Instagram as a Reel, so you can upload each part once, write its caption and place them in the calendar at the times you choose. It works with Instagram Business and Creator accounts linked to a Facebook Page, and Instagram's own length limits still apply to every upload, so check each part fits before you schedule it.

## Option 3: Use a carousel with several videos

A carousel holds up to 10 items, and you can mix photos and videos. Each video in a carousel has a shorter length limit than a standalone Reel, so this option suits a set of short clips rather than one long one.

Good uses for a video carousel:

- **Step-by-step how-tos**, with one clip per step.
- **Before and after** comparisons, with a photo and a short video.
- **Event recaps**, combining highlights from different moments.
- **Product demos**, showing one feature per slide.

The upside is that everything lives in one post, so people can swipe through without hunting for Part 2. The downside is that viewers have to swipe, and some won't. Put the most compelling clip first. For more on how many items you can add and how mixed carousels behave, see our guide to the [Instagram carousel limit](/blog/instagram-carousel-limit).

## Option 4: Post a teaser and link to the full version

For videos that are genuinely long, such as a 20-minute tutorial, a podcast episode or a webinar, Instagram works best as a trailer rather than the main venue.

1. **Cut a short teaser Reel** from the most useful or surprising moment.
2. **Upload the full video to YouTube** (or another platform where you own the long-form version).
3. **Add the YouTube link to your bio.** Instagram allows several links in your bio at the time of writing, so you can keep your main link and add the new video.
4. **Point to it in the caption.** Captions don't support clickable links, so say "Full video: link in bio" instead of pasting a URL.
5. **Share it in Stories with a link sticker** on the day it goes live, so followers can tap straight through.

Our guide to [posting a YouTube video on Instagram](/blog/post-a-youtube-video-on-instagram) goes deeper into combining Reels, Stories and bio links. Only re-upload videos you own or have permission to use.

### Going live for long content

For content you'd naturally do in real time, such as a Q&A or workshop, Instagram Live allows much longer sessions than Reels. Cut highlights from the recording into short Reels afterward.

## Tips for long videos that still work on Instagram

Whichever option you choose, a few habits keep long material watchable on a short-form platform.

- **Start with the payoff.** Show the result, the answer or the best moment in the first two seconds, then explain how you got there.
- **Add on-screen captions.** Many people watch with the sound off. Text keeps them following along.
- **Shoot or edit for vertical.** A horizontal video shown in a 9:16 frame leaves large empty bars and makes text tiny.
- **Use a clear cover.** For a series, keep the same cover style and put "Part 1", "Part 2" in the same spot so your grid reads as a set.
- **Write captions that orient people.** Say what the video covers, which part it is and where the rest lives. Captions can be up to 2,200 characters, but the first line matters most.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long can an Instagram Reel be?

At the time of writing, most accounts can upload Reels of up to 3 minutes in the Instagram app. Instagram has changed this limit several times and tests different lengths with some accounts, so check the upload screen if you're unsure.

### Can I upload a 10-minute video to Instagram?

Usually not as a single Reel. You'll need to trim it, split it into parts, or post a short clip on Instagram and link to the full 10-minute version on YouTube through your bio or a Story link sticker.

### Why does Instagram cut my video into pieces?

That happens in Stories, which split longer clips into segments of up to 60 seconds each. Reels don't split automatically; Instagram asks you to trim a Reel that's over the limit instead.

### Can I put long videos in an Instagram carousel?

Not really. Videos inside a carousel have a shorter limit than standalone Reels, so carousels work best for several short clips, such as one step per slide, rather than one long video.

### Does Schedule For You post long videos to Instagram?

Schedule For You publishes a single video to Instagram as a Reel and 2 to 10 items as a carousel. Instagram's own length limits apply, so a video that's too long for a Reel in the app will also be too long when scheduled.

### Is it better to post a long video in parts or as a teaser?

Parts work well when each section stands on its own and you want the whole thing on Instagram. A teaser works better for very long content, such as webinars or full tutorials, where you'd rather send people to a platform built for long-form viewing.
