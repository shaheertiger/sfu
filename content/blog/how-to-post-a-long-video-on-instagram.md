You've got a 10-minute tutorial and Instagram keeps opening the trim bar. To post a long video on Instagram, first check whether it fits the current Reel limit: at the time of writing, most accounts can upload Reels of up to 3 minutes in the app. If your video is longer, you have four practical options: trim it, split it into numbered parts, use a carousel of short clips, or post a teaser and send people to the full version on YouTube.

![How to post a long video on Instagram: trim it, split it, use a carousel, or post a teaser and link the full video](/images/blog/how-to-post-a-long-video-on-instagram-hero-1200x675.webp)

Instagram has changed its video rules several times, so the exact limits depend on your account, your app version and how you upload. That's why advice online often contradicts itself.

This guide covers the current limits, what happens when a video is too long, each of the four options step by step, and how to run a multi-part series without posting every part by hand.

## Why Instagram Cuts Long Videos (and the Current Limits)

Instagram no longer has a separate long-form video section. Videos you share to the feed are generally published as Reels, and each format has its own length rules.

![Instagram video length limits for Reels, carousel videos, Stories and Live](/images/blog/how-to-post-a-long-video-on-instagram-limits-1200x675.webp)

| Format | Length at the time of writing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Reel (uploaded in the app) | Up to 3 minutes for most accounts | The upload screen tells you if a file is too long |
| Video inside a carousel | Shorter than a Reel; often around 60 seconds per video | Check the app when you add a video to a carousel |
| Story | Up to 60 seconds per segment | Longer clips are split into consecutive segments |
| Live | Much longer sessions (hours) | Recorded lives can be shared afterward |

Treat every number here as "at the time of writing". Instagram tests different limits with different accounts, and uploads from a computer or through a scheduling tool can follow slightly different rules. The [Instagram Help Center](https://help.instagram.com) and the upload screen in the app are the most reliable sources.

### What Most People Don't Know

Even when a longer Reel is allowed, a tight cut tends to hold attention better. Viewers swipe away quickly, and a slow middle loses people before the payoff. A 10-minute video often works better on Instagram as a strong 60-to-90-second cut plus a link to the rest.

> **Key point:** On Instagram, length is a limit and a choice. Fit the format first, then ask whether shorter would be stronger anyway.

## What Happens If Your Video Is Too Long

| What you see | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| The editor opens with a trim bar | The clip is over the Reel limit | Trim it in your own editor first for more control |
| The upload fails with an error | Some upload paths, including some third-party tools, reject over-length files | Cut the file to fit before uploading |
| Your Story is split into segments | Stories cut long clips into segments of up to 60 seconds | Expected; check where each segment starts |

Always watch the preview before you publish, so you know exactly where the video starts and ends.

## Option 1: Trim the Video to Fit (Fastest)

If most of your video is setup and the valuable part is short, trimming is the cleanest route.

![Option 1: trim the long video to its strongest section in four steps](/images/blog/how-to-post-a-long-video-on-instagram-trim-1200x675.webp)

### Step 1: Mark the Strongest Section

Watch the full video and note the timestamps of the best part.

### Step 2: Open on the Payoff, Not the Intro

This is where most trims go wrong. Start on the most interesting moment, not on "Hi everyone, today we're going to…".

### Step 3: Export at 1080×1920 (9:16)

A vertical export fills the screen. Our [Instagram Reel size guide](/blog/reel-size-instagram) covers export settings and safe zones.

### Step 4: Upload and Say Where the Rest Lives

Upload it as a Reel and mention in the caption where people can find the full version, if there is one.

> **Pro tip:** Trim in your editing app, not Instagram's trim bar. You get frame-accurate cuts, and you can add captions and a hook before you upload.

## Option 2: Split It Into Parts (Best for Tutorials and Stories)

Splitting works well for tutorials, stories and interviews that naturally fall into chapters.

![Option 2: what makes a multi-part Instagram series work compared with what loses viewers](/images/blog/how-to-post-a-long-video-on-instagram-split-1200x675.webp)

1. **Find natural break points.** End each part on a question, a reveal or the next step, so people want the next one.
2. **Label every part clearly.** Put "Part 1 of 3" on screen in the first second and in the caption.
3. **Make each part understandable on its own.** Someone may see Part 2 first. A one-line recap at the start helps.
4. **Post the parts close together**, on consecutive days or a few hours apart, so the series stays fresh.
5. **Link the series together.** Reply to comments on Part 1 when Part 2 is live, and mention the series in your captions.

Instagram lets you pin a small number of posts to the top of your profile (up to three at the time of writing). Pinning Part 1 gives new visitors an obvious place to start.

## Option 3: Use a Carousel of Short Clips

A carousel holds up to 10 items, and you can mix photos and videos. Each video in a carousel has a shorter limit than a standalone Reel, so this suits a set of short clips rather than one long one.

Good uses:

- **Step-by-step how-tos**, one clip per step.
- **Before and after** comparisons, with a photo and a short video.
- **Event recaps**, combining highlights from different moments.
- **Product demos**, one feature per slide.

Everything lives in one post, so nobody hunts for Part 2. But viewers have to swipe, and some won't, so put the most compelling clip first. See our guide to the [Instagram carousel limit](/blog/instagram-carousel-limit) for how mixed carousels behave.

## Option 4: Post a Teaser and Link the Full Version

For a 20-minute tutorial, a podcast episode or a webinar, Instagram works best as the trailer.

![Option 4: post a short teaser Reel on Instagram and put the full video on YouTube](/images/blog/how-to-post-a-long-video-on-instagram-teaser-1200x675.webp)

1. **Cut a short teaser Reel** from the most useful or surprising moment.
2. **Upload the full video to YouTube**, or another platform where you own the long-form version.
3. **Add the link to your bio.** Instagram allows several links in your bio at the time of writing.
4. **Point to it in the caption.** Captions don't support clickable links, so say "Full video: link in bio".
5. **Share it in Stories with a link sticker** on the day it goes live, so followers can tap straight through.

Our guide to [posting a YouTube video on Instagram](/blog/post-a-youtube-video-on-instagram) goes deeper. Only re-upload videos you own or have permission to use. For content you'd do in real time, like a Q&A or workshop, Instagram Live allows much longer sessions; cut highlights into Reels afterward.

## Run a Multi-Part Series on Schedule (Schedule For You)

Splitting a video is a one-off job. Posting each part on time, for days in a row, is where a series usually falls apart.

Take Hana, a yoga teacher with a 12-minute morning routine. She cuts it into 4 parts of about 3 minutes, plans one a day from Monday to Thursday at 7 a.m., and puts the full version on YouTube on Monday too. That's 5 uploads across two platforms at 7 a.m., the hour she's teaching her first class.

| | Instagram app | Other schedulers | Schedule For You |
|---|---|---|---|
| Schedule a Reel | Yes, in the app | Most support Reels | Yes |
| Schedule the full version on YouTube too | No | Varies | Yes, with its own title and description |
| See the whole series on a calendar | No | Many | Yes, drag parts to other days |
| Trim or split the video | Yes, basic trim bar | Some include editors | No, edit before you upload |
| Cost | Free | Varies | Free plan for 3 accounts and up to 10 scheduled posts at a time, no card |

Where we're not the best fit: Schedule For You doesn't trim or split video, and Instagram's own length limits apply to every upload, so a part that's too long for a Reel in the app is too long when scheduled too. Edit first, then schedule.

## What to Do Next: Tips That Keep Long Videos Watchable

- ✓ **Start with the payoff.** Show the result or the best moment in the first two seconds.
- ✓ **Add on-screen captions.** Many people watch with the sound off.
- ✓ **Edit for vertical.** A horizontal video in a 9:16 frame leaves big bars and tiny text.
- ✓ **Use a consistent cover** for a series, with "Part 1", "Part 2" in the same spot.
- ✓ **Write captions that orient people:** what it covers, which part it is, where the rest lives. Captions can be up to 2,200 characters, but the first line matters most.
- ✗ Don't open with a long introduction.
- ✗ Don't leave a series unlabelled; people will see Part 3 first.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long can an Instagram Reel be?

At the time of writing, most accounts can upload Reels of up to 3 minutes in the Instagram app. Instagram has changed this limit several times and tests different lengths with some accounts, so check the upload screen if you're unsure.

### Can I upload a 10-minute video to Instagram?

Usually not as a single Reel. Trim it, split it into parts, or post a short clip on Instagram and link to the full version on YouTube through your bio or a Story link sticker.

### Why does Instagram cut my video into pieces?

That happens in Stories, which split longer clips into segments of up to 60 seconds each. Reels don't split automatically; Instagram asks you to trim a Reel that's over the limit instead.

### Can I put long videos in an Instagram carousel?

Not really. Videos inside a carousel have a shorter limit than standalone Reels, so carousels work best for several short clips rather than one long video.

### Does Schedule For You post long videos to Instagram?

Schedule For You publishes a single video to Instagram as a Reel and up to 10 items as a carousel. Instagram's own length limits apply, so a video that's too long for a Reel in the app is also too long when scheduled.

### Is it better to post a long video in parts or as a teaser?

Parts work well when each section stands on its own and you want everything on Instagram. A teaser works better for very long content, such as webinars, where you'd rather send people to a platform built for long-form viewing.

### What size should a long video be for Instagram?

Export at 1080×1920 pixels (9:16) so it fills the screen. See our [Instagram post size guide](/blog/instagram-post-size) for every format.

### Can I go live for longer than a Reel?

Yes. Instagram Live allows much longer sessions than Reels, and you can cut highlights from the recording into short Reels afterward.
