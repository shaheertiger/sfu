There is no length of a copyrighted song that's automatically safe on TikTok. The "15 seconds" or "30 seconds" rule is a myth: copyright law has no free-use time limit. What protects you is *where the music comes from*. If you're a personal account and you pick a sound from TikTok's own sound library, TikTok's licensing deals cover that use, typically up to about 60 seconds of a track. If you're a business, or you upload a song inside your own video file, those deals usually don't cover you, and even a few seconds can get muted or claimed.

This isn't legal advice. If music is central to a campaign, ask the rights holder or a lawyer.

## The short version

| Who you are | Music from TikTok's sound picker | Music in your own uploaded file |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personal account, non-commercial video | Covered by TikTok's licenses | Risky: may be muted or removed |
| Creator doing a paid or sponsored post | Use the Commercial Music Library or get a license | Needs a license that covers commercial use |
| Business account | Commercial Music Library only | Needs a license that covers commercial use on TikTok |

## Why personal accounts get more music

TikTok signs licensing deals with labels, publishers and collecting societies. Those deals let ordinary users add popular songs to non-commercial videos from inside the app. The 60-second figure you'll see quoted comes from how those licenses are set up and what distributors report, not from copyright law, and TikTok doesn't publish a single official cap.

The cover ends when the video becomes advertising. A product review, a brand collab or a post promoting your shop can count as commercial even on a personal account.

## What business accounts can use

When you switch to a **Business account**, the sound picker shows only the **Commercial Music Library (CML)**: over a million tracks that TikTok has cleared for brand posts and ads, at no per-track cost. Popular chart songs mostly aren't in it.

Your options as a business:

1. **Use the Commercial Music Library.** Tracks are pre-cleared for organic posts and TikTok ads.
2. **Use music you've licensed yourself.** A stock-music or direct license works if it explicitly covers commercial use on TikTok. TikTok may ask you to confirm this when you upload.
3. **Use original audio.** Your voice, your own recordings, or music made for you with a written agreement.

Trending sounds created by other users can also contain copyrighted songs underneath. If a sound isn't available to your business account in the picker, that's TikTok telling you it isn't cleared.

## Rules in Germany and the EU

In Germany, music rights are managed by **GEMA**. Its agreements with TikTok, Instagram, Facebook and YouTube cover **private, non-commercial** use only. GEMA states that a business using music in a Reel, TikTok or Story is not private use, and needs a license from the rights holder (usually the publisher and the label). GEMA's repertoire search shows who holds the rights to a song.

Enforcement is real: law firms send formal warnings (Abmahnungen) with damages claims, sometimes for a single post. The same logic applies broadly across the EU, where collecting societies such as SACEM, SGAE and PRS license platforms for personal use, not for brand marketing.

Practical rule for brands in Germany and the EU: use each platform's commercial library (TikTok's CML, Meta's Sound Collection) or royalty-free music with a license that names social media and commercial use.

## Instagram is a separate library

Meta's **Sound Collection** is cleared for Facebook and Instagram. That clearance doesn't follow the video to TikTok or YouTube. If you post one video to several platforms, either use music cleared on all of them or export a version without music and add the sound in each app.

This matters if you [cross-post one video to several platforms](/blog/how-to-cross-post-to-multiple-platforms). Schedule For You uploads the file you give it, so the music in that file is the music that gets published. Use a track you have rights to on every platform you're posting to.

## What happens if you use music you don't have rights to

- **Muted audio.** The most common outcome. The video stays up silent.
- **Removed video.** For repeated or clear-cut cases.
- **Restricted sound.** Your video can't be duetted or stitched with that audio.
- **Strikes and account limits.** Repeated infringement can lead to bans.
- **Legal claims.** Outside the app, rights holders can demand fees or damages from businesses.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 15 seconds of a song fair use on TikTok?
No. There's no fixed number of seconds that counts as fair use. Fair use (in the US) depends on purpose, amount, the nature of the work and market effect, and a brand using a hit song to sell rarely qualifies.

### Why can I only use some sounds on my TikTok account?
You're likely on a Business account, which is limited to the Commercial Music Library. Switching back to a personal account gives you the full library, but commercial posts still need cleared music.

### Can I use copyrighted music in TikTok ads?
Only music from the Commercial Music Library or tracks you've licensed for advertising.

### Does crediting the artist make it legal?
No. Credit is polite, but it isn't permission. You need a license or a sound TikTok has cleared for your type of account.
