You've cut the perfect 12-second clip of a chart song for your video and you're wondering if that's short enough to be safe. There is no length of a copyrighted song that's automatically safe on TikTok. The "15 seconds" or "30 seconds" rule is a myth: copyright law has no free-use time limit. What protects you is **where the music comes from**, and **who's posting**.

![How long can a song be before copyright on TikTok? No length is safe; what matters is where the music comes from and who posts it](/images/blog/tiktok-music-copyright-hero-1200x675.webp)

If you're a personal account and you pick a sound from TikTok's own sound library, TikTok's licensing deals cover that use, typically up to about 60 seconds of a track. If you're a business, or you upload a song inside your own video file, those deals usually don't cover you, and even a few seconds can get muted or claimed.

This guide covers what personal and business accounts can use, the Commercial Music Library, the rules in Germany and the EU, cross-posting to other platforms, and what happens if you get it wrong. It isn't legal advice: if music is central to a campaign, ask the rights holder or a lawyer.

## Why Personal Accounts Get More Music Than Brands

![Music TikTok's licenses cover for personal accounts compared with what business accounts can use](/images/blog/tiktok-music-copyright-who-1200x675.webp)

TikTok signs licensing deals with labels, publishers and collecting societies. Those deals let ordinary users add popular songs to non-commercial videos from inside the app.

| Who you are | Music from TikTok's sound picker | Music in your own uploaded file |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personal account, non-commercial video | Covered by TikTok's licenses | Risky: may be muted or removed |
| Creator doing a paid or sponsored post | Use the Commercial Music Library or get a license | Needs a license that covers commercial use |
| Business account | Commercial Music Library only | Needs a license that covers commercial use on TikTok |

### What Most People Don't Know

The 60-second figure you'll see quoted comes from how those licenses are set up and what distributors report, not from copyright law, and TikTok doesn't publish a single official cap. And the cover ends when the video becomes advertising. A product review, a brand collab or a post promoting your shop can count as commercial even on a personal account.

> **Key point:** The question isn't "how many seconds?" It's "did this sound come from TikTok's picker, and is this post personal or commercial?"

## What Business Accounts Can Use: 3 Safe Options

![Three safe music options for TikTok business accounts: the Commercial Music Library, music you've licensed, and original audio](/images/blog/tiktok-music-copyright-business-1200x675.webp)

When you switch to a **Business account**, the sound picker shows only the **Commercial Music Library (CML)**: over a million tracks that TikTok has cleared for brand posts and ads, at no per-track cost. Popular chart songs mostly aren't in it.

### Option 1: Use the Commercial Music Library

Tracks are pre-cleared for organic posts and TikTok ads. Search the picker on your Business account; what you see is what's cleared.

### Option 2: Use Music You've Licensed Yourself

A stock-music or direct license works if it explicitly covers commercial use on TikTok. TikTok may ask you to confirm this when you upload. Read the license for the words "social media" and "commercial"; a license for YouTube only isn't enough.

### Option 3: Use Original Audio

Your voice, your own recordings, or music made for you with a written agreement.

> **Pro tip:** Trending sounds created by other users can contain copyrighted songs underneath. If a sound isn't available to your business account in the picker, that's TikTok telling you it isn't cleared. Don't work around it by saving and re-uploading the audio.

## TikTok Music Rules in Germany and the EU (GEMA and Others)

In Germany, music rights are managed by **GEMA**. Its agreements with TikTok, Instagram, Facebook and YouTube cover **private, non-commercial** use only. GEMA states that a business using music in a Reel, TikTok or Story is not private use, and needs a license from the rights holder (usually the publisher and the label). GEMA's repertoire search shows who holds the rights to a song.

Enforcement is real: law firms send formal warnings (Abmahnungen) with damages claims, sometimes for a single post. The same logic applies broadly across the EU, where collecting societies such as SACEM, SGAE and PRS license platforms for personal use, not for brand marketing.

| Region | Who licenses the platforms | What it covers | Brands should use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Germany | GEMA | Private, non-commercial use | The platform's commercial library, or a direct license |
| France, Spain, UK and others | SACEM, SGAE, PRS and similar | Personal use on the platforms | The same |

Practical rule for brands in Germany and the EU: use each platform's commercial library (TikTok's CML, Meta's Sound Collection) or royalty-free music with a license that names social media and commercial use.

## Cross-Posting? Instagram and YouTube Are Separate Libraries

![Music cleared on one platform doesn't carry over: TikTok's Commercial Music Library, Meta's Sound Collection and YouTube are separate](/images/blog/tiktok-music-copyright-cross-posting-1200x675.webp)

Meta's **Sound Collection** is cleared for Facebook and Instagram. That clearance doesn't follow the video to TikTok or YouTube, and TikTok's CML doesn't follow your video to Instagram.

| Your music | TikTok | Instagram and Facebook | YouTube |
|---|---|---|---|
| Added from TikTok's picker in the app | Covered (per account type) | Not covered by TikTok's deal | Not covered by TikTok's deal |
| Added from Instagram's music in the app | Not covered by Meta's deal | Covered (per account type) | Not covered by Meta's deal |
| Baked into your file, licensed for all social platforms | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Your own original audio | Yes | Yes | Yes |

If you post one video to several platforms, either use music cleared on all of them or export a version without music and add the sound in each app.

| Outcome | When it happens | What it means |

| Muted audio | The most common | The video stays up, silent |
| Removed video | Repeated or clear-cut cases | The post is gone |
| Restricted sound | Depends on the sound | Your video can't be duetted or stitched with that audio |
| Strikes and account limits | Repeated infringement | Can lead to bans |
| Legal claims | Outside the app, aimed at businesses | Rights holders can demand fees or damages outside the app |

## Sped-Up, Pitched and "No Copyright" Versions (Use With Caution)

![Safe music choices for TikTok compared with risky tricks like sped-up songs and unofficial no-copyright uploads](/images/blog/tiktok-music-copyright-tricks-1200x675.webp)

Some creators speed up a song, shift its pitch or use a "no copyright" upload they found online, hoping detection won't match it. Others assume crediting the artist is enough.

### The Real Risks

- **It's still the same song.** Changing speed or pitch doesn't change who owns it, and matching systems can still catch it.
- **"No copyright" uploads may not be what they say.** Anyone can label a track that way; the label isn't a license.
- **Credit isn't permission.** Naming the artist is polite, but it isn't a license.
- **Businesses carry the legal risk.** For brands, especially in Germany and the EU, a claim can arrive long after the post.

### A Safer Checklist

- Personal, non-commercial posts: add music from TikTok's picker inside the app.
- Business or sponsored posts: Commercial Music Library, licensed tracks or original audio.
- Keep licenses and receipts for any music you pay for.
- When in doubt, ask the rights holder.

> **Bottom line:** No trick makes a song you don't have rights to safe. Pick from the right library for your account type, or use music you own.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is 15 seconds of a song fair use on TikTok?

No. There's no fixed number of seconds that counts as fair use. Fair use (in the US) depends on purpose, amount, the nature of the work and market effect, and a brand using a hit song to sell rarely qualifies.

### Why can I only use some sounds on my TikTok account?

You're likely on a Business account, which is limited to the Commercial Music Library. Switching back to a personal account gives you the full library, but commercial posts still need cleared music.

### Can I use copyrighted music in TikTok ads?

Only music from the Commercial Music Library or tracks you've licensed for advertising.

### Does crediting the artist make it legal?

No. Credit is polite, but it isn't permission. You need a license or a sound TikTok has cleared for your type of account.

### How long can a song be on TikTok from the sound library?

Personal accounts can typically use up to about 60 seconds of a track from TikTok's picker, but TikTok doesn't publish a single official cap.

### What happens if TikTok mutes my video?

The video stays up without sound. Replace the audio with a cleared sound or your own, and post it again if the video matters.

### Can a business use trending sounds on TikTok?

Only if they're available in the Commercial Music Library on your Business account. If a sound doesn't appear in the picker, it isn't cleared for you.

### Is music from Instagram's library OK on TikTok?

No. Meta's Sound Collection is cleared for Facebook and Instagram, not TikTok.
