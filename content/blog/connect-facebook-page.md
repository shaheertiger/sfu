You've signed up, you're ready to plan a month of Facebook posts, and now you need your Page in the dashboard. To connect a Facebook Page, sign in to Schedule For You, open **Accounts**, choose **Facebook**, log in with Meta, approve access to the Pages you manage, then pick which Pages to add. With the right Page access it takes a couple of minutes.

![Connecting a Facebook Page to Schedule For You in three moves: choose Facebook, approve Pages in Meta's dialog, pick the Pages to add](/images/blog/connect-facebook-page-hero-1200x675.webp)

When it goes wrong, it's almost never the scheduler. It's Meta's permission dialog, where one unticked box or the wrong logged-in account hides the Page you wanted.

This guide covers the access you need first, the exact steps, and how to fix a Page that doesn't appear or a connection that stops working.

## Why Facebook Pages Need Full Control to Connect (Not Just Posting Access)

![Facebook access you can connect with, full control, next to access that won't work, like personal profiles, Groups and task-only access](/images/blog/connect-facebook-page-access-1200x675.webp)

Meta lets scheduling tools publish to Facebook **Pages** only. Personal profiles can't be connected, and Groups aren't supported either. If you've been posting as yourself and want to schedule, you'll need a Page for your brand, business or public presence.

| Requirement | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| A Facebook Page (not a personal profile or Group) | Meta only allows scheduling tools to publish to Pages |
| Facebook access to the Page with full control | You need enough access to grant publishing permissions |
| Logged in to the right Facebook account | The Meta dialog only shows Pages that account can manage |
| A Schedule For You account | You connect the Page to a workspace |

### What Most People Don't Know

Pages using Meta's current Page experience give people either "Facebook access" or "task access". You can often post on Facebook by hand with partial access, which is why people assume they're set up. But a scheduling tool needs Facebook access with **full control**, the equivalent of the old admin role, before Meta will let you grant publishing permissions.

To check, open the Page on Facebook, go to the Page's settings and look for the section that lists people with access. If you only have partial or task access, ask someone with full control to upgrade yours. If the Page is owned by a business portfolio in Meta Business Suite (formerly Business Manager), make sure you're assigned to the Page with full control there too.

> **Key point:** Being able to post on a Page isn't the same as being able to connect it. Check for full control before you start, and the rest of the setup is a few clicks.

## How to Connect Your Facebook Page, Step by Step (9 Steps)

![Four steps to connect a Facebook Page: open Accounts, log in with Facebook, tick every Page, pick Pages to add](/images/blog/connect-facebook-page-steps-1200x675.webp)

Menu labels on Meta's side change from time to time, so look for the closest match if a button is worded differently.

### Step 1: Sign In

Go to the [sign-in page](/login). If you don't have an account yet, [create one free](/signup). Every new account starts on the Free plan, with no card.

### Step 2: Open the Right Workspace

If you manage several brands or clients, pick the workspace this Page belongs to. Each workspace has its own connected accounts, queue and calendar.

### Step 3: Go to Accounts and Choose Facebook

Open the **Accounts** screen and select Facebook from the list of platforms.

### Step 4: Log In With the Account That Manages the Page

A Meta window opens. Log in with the Facebook account that has full control of your Page. If you're already logged in, check it's the right account before you continue.

### Step 5: Tick Every Page You'll Schedule For (the Step People Get Wrong)

Meta asks which Pages you want to give access to. Select every Page you plan to schedule for now. Pages you leave unticked won't be offered in the next steps, and that's the number one reason a Page goes "missing".

> **Pro tip:** Tick all the Pages you'll need in one pass, even ones you'll only add next month. It saves running the whole Meta flow again later.

### Step 6: Keep the Requested Permissions On

Meta lists what the app is asking for, such as managing and publishing content on your Pages. Leave these switched on. Turning them off stops posts from publishing.

### Step 7: Pick Which Pages to Add

Back in your workspace, choose which of the authorized Pages to add as connected accounts. If Meta returns several new Pages, Schedule For You asks you to choose rather than importing them all.

### Step 8: Check Your Plan Limit

Each connected Page counts as one connected account: 3 on Free, 15 on Solo and 100 on Studio. If a login returns more Pages than you have room for, the ones that fit are connected and the rest are skipped, with a note telling you how many. See [pricing](/pricing) for details.

### Step 9: Confirm and Find It in the Composer

The Page now appears in your connected accounts and is available in the composer and calendar.

## Facebook Page Not Showing Up? 5 Fixes That Work

![Troubleshooting a Facebook Page connection: re-run Meta's dialog, switch accounts, reconnect after a password change](/images/blog/connect-facebook-page-fixes-1200x675.webp)

| Problem | Fix | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| The Page doesn't appear in the list | Re-run the connection and edit your previous settings in Meta's dialog | The Page wasn't ticked the first time, so Meta never granted access |
| Wrong Pages show up | Log out of Facebook or use a private window, then connect again | Meta uses whichever account your browser is logged in to |
| Posts started failing | Reconnect from Accounts and approve the permissions again | The Meta connection expired or was invalidated |
| A teammate connected it, then left | Reconnect with an account that keeps full control | The connection is tied to the account that authorized it |
| An error says the Page is restricted | Resolve it in Meta first | Restricted Pages can be blocked from publishing through any tool |

### The Page Doesn't Appear in the List

1. Go back to the Accounts screen and start the Facebook connection again.
2. In the Meta dialog, look for an option to edit or change your previous settings.
3. Select the missing Page, and keep your other Pages selected too.
4. Approve the permissions and finish the flow.

Still missing? Check your access level on the Page and your role in Meta Business Suite. You need full control, not partial or task-only access.

### You're Logged In to the Wrong Facebook Account

The Meta window uses whichever Facebook account your browser is logged in to. Log out of Facebook in that browser, or use a private window, then run the connection again and log in with the correct account.

### Permissions Expired or Posts Started Failing

Meta connections can expire or be invalidated, for example after you change your Facebook password, after a security check, when Meta updates its permission rules, or when someone removes your access to the Page. Scheduled posts to that Page then fail, and Schedule For You sends a failed-post alert so you know.

Reconnect from the Accounts screen and approve the permissions again. Then reschedule any posts that failed while the connection was broken.

### You See an Error About the Page Being Restricted

If Facebook has restricted the Page or it doesn't meet Meta's requirements, publishing through any tool may be blocked. Check the Page's status in Meta's account quality or Page settings and resolve it there first. Meta's help center at [facebook.com/help](https://www.facebook.com/help) explains Page restrictions.

## Connecting Through Someone Else's Facebook Login (Use With Caution)

It's common for a freelancer or agency to connect a client's Page with their own Facebook account, or for a client to share their Facebook password so someone else can do it. It works on day one, which is why people do it.

### The Real Risks

- **The connection breaks when that person leaves.** It's tied to the Facebook account that authorized it. Remove their Page access and scheduled posts start failing.
- **A shared password is full access.** Whoever has it can do everything the account owner can, not just schedule posts.
- **Password changes and security checks cut the connection.** A shared login tends to get reset more often, and every reset means reconnecting.

### A Safer Setup

- Give each person their own Page access in Meta instead of sharing a password.
- Connect with an account the business owns and will keep long term.
- Use one workspace per client so each client's Pages, queue and calendar stay separate.
- Keep failed-post alerts on so you hear about a broken connection straight away.

> **Bottom line:** Connect with an account that will keep full control of the Page for years, not whoever happens to be logged in today.

## How to Disconnect a Facebook Page (Both Sides)

You can remove a Page from your workspace on the Accounts screen. That stops Schedule For You from showing it in the composer.

To fully revoke the app's access on Meta's side:

1. On Facebook, open **Settings & privacy**, then **Settings**.
2. Find **Business integrations**. It may sit under a security or apps section, depending on your version.
3. Find Schedule For You in the list and remove it, or edit which Pages it can access.

Removing the integration in Meta stops all publishing from Schedule For You to your Pages until you connect again.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I connect my personal Facebook profile?

No. Meta doesn't let scheduling tools publish to personal profiles. You can connect Facebook Pages you manage, so create a Page if you want to schedule for your brand.

### Can I schedule posts to Facebook Groups?

No. Schedule For You publishes to Facebook Pages only, not Groups.

### What Page access do I need?

Facebook access to the Page with full control, the equivalent of the old admin role. If the Page is in a business portfolio in Meta Business Suite, you also need to be assigned to it with full control there.

### Why doesn't my Page show up after I log in with Facebook?

It usually wasn't ticked in Meta's dialog. Start the connection again, edit your previous settings and select the Page.

### Why does my Page keep disconnecting?

Common causes are a changed Facebook password, a security check, removed Page access or an updated Meta permission. Reconnect from the Accounts screen with an account that will keep full control.

### Do connected Pages count toward my plan limit?

Yes. Each Page counts as one connected account: 3 on Free, 15 on Solo and 100 on Studio.

### Is connecting a Facebook Page free?

Yes. The Free plan connects up to 3 accounts with no card, and a Page counts as one of them.

### Can I connect the same Page to more than one workspace?

Workspaces are designed to separate brands and clients, so the simplest setup is one workspace per Page owner. If you need the same Page in more than one place, ask support how it affects your account limits.
