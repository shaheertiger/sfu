Your Instagram Story usually looks blurry because Instagram compressed a file that wasn't the right size, or because your app is set to upload at reduced quality to save data. Low-light or zoomed footage, HDR video, and files that were sent through a messaging app before you posted them are the other common culprits. Upload a 1080×1920 (9:16) file, turn on the highest-quality upload setting, and post straight from your camera roll, and most Stories come out sharp.

The tricky part is that several causes can stack. A slightly zoomed, low-light clip shot in HDR and forwarded through WhatsApp will look bad no matter what Instagram does. Work through the list below and you'll usually find one or two things to change.

## Quick diagnosis

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Everything is a little soft, every time | Data saver or reduced upload quality | Turn on highest-quality uploads |
| Edges cropped and image looks zoomed | Wrong aspect ratio | Resize to 1080×1920 before posting |
| Blocky patches in dark areas | Low light plus compression | Add light, avoid digital zoom |
| Video looks washed out or oddly bright | HDR footage being converted | Shoot or export in SDR |
| Only images from friends look bad | File was compressed by a messaging app | Get the original file |
| Looks fine on Wi-Fi, bad on cellular | Upload or playback on a weak connection | Upload on Wi-Fi, wait for processing |
| Text and stickers are sharp, photo isn't | The source image is low resolution | Use a larger original |

## The 8 most common causes

### 1. Instagram's compression

Every Story you post is re-encoded to keep file sizes small for fast loading. You can't turn this off. What you can control is how much work Instagram has to do. A file that's already 1080×1920 with a sensible file size goes through compression with little visible loss. A huge 4K file or a tiny screenshot gets scaled and squeezed more aggressively, and that's where softness and blocky artifacts appear.

### 2. The wrong aspect ratio or resolution

Stories are 1080×1920 pixels, a 9:16 vertical frame. When you upload a square, landscape or oddly sized image, Instagram either zooms in to fill the screen (cropping and enlarging, which softens detail) or adds a background around it. Enlarging a small image is the single fastest way to make a Story look blurry.

### 3. Low-light or zoomed footage

Phone cameras handle dim scenes by boosting brightness and applying noise reduction, which smears fine detail. Digital zoom does something similar: it crops into the sensor and upscales the result. Combine either with Instagram's compression and the softness becomes obvious. Shooting in brighter light and moving closer instead of zooming makes a bigger difference than any setting.

### 4. Data saver and upload quality settings

Instagram has settings that trade quality for data. Look in **Settings**, then the section for data usage and media quality. If a data saver option is on, media may load and upload at lower quality. There's also an option along the lines of **Upload at highest quality**; turning it on tells Instagram to prioritize quality, even if uploads take longer. Labels change between app versions, so look for the closest match.

Your phone's own low-data or low-power mode can have a similar effect, so check those too when you're posting on cellular data.

### 5. Files that went through a messaging app first

If someone sent you the photo or video over WhatsApp, Messenger, iMessage or a similar app, it was very likely compressed on the way. When you then post it to your Story, Instagram compresses it again. Two rounds of compression stack up quickly. Ask for the original file, use a "send as document" or "original quality" option if the app offers one, or share via a cloud link instead.

### 6. HDR video

Many recent phones record video in HDR by default. Instagram's handling of HDR has improved, but HDR clips can still be converted in ways that look washed out, overexposed or strangely soft, especially when viewed on a phone that doesn't display HDR. If your Stories look odd only when you post camera-roll videos, try recording in standard dynamic range for social clips. On iPhone, the HDR video toggle is in the Camera settings; on Android, it's usually in the camera app's video settings.

### 7. A weak connection during upload or viewing

A slow upload can result in a lower-quality version being processed, and a slow connection on the viewer's side means Instagram shows a lower-resolution stream first. If your Story looks blurry right after posting, wait a minute and check it again on Wi-Fi. Sometimes the sharp version simply hasn't loaded yet.

### 8. Screenshots and re-shared posts

Screenshots capture your screen's resolution and whatever compression was already on the image. Sharing a feed post to your Story also places a smaller version inside the Story frame. Neither will be as crisp as uploading the original file.

## How to fix a blurry Story, step by step

Work through these in order. Most people are sorted by step 3.

1. **Turn on the highest-quality upload setting.** In Instagram's settings, open the data usage and media quality section, turn off any data saver option and turn on highest-quality uploads.
2. **Update the app.** Upload bugs get fixed in updates, and older versions sometimes handle new camera formats poorly.
3. **Resize to 1080×1920 before posting.** Use the [Instagram photo resizer](/tools/instagram-photo-resizer), choose the story/reel preset, then pick "fill (crop)" to cover the frame or "fit (add background)" to keep the whole image with a colored background. Download as PNG or JPG and post that file.
4. **Post from your camera roll, not a forwarded copy.** Get the original file from whoever shot it.
5. **Use Wi-Fi for important Stories.** Upload on a stable connection and give the Story a moment to finish processing before judging it.
6. **Shoot in SDR for social clips.** Turn off HDR video if your clips keep looking washed out after upload.
7. **Reshoot with more light.** If the original is noisy or zoomed, no setting can add detail that isn't there.

## Tips for consistently sharp Stories

### Frame for 9:16 when you shoot

Hold the phone vertically and shoot in the Story frame from the start. You skip cropping and get the full resolution of your camera into the final image.

### Keep text inside the safe area

Instagram overlays your profile name at the top and the reply bar at the bottom. Keep important text and faces roughly in the middle of the frame so nothing gets covered. Our guide to [Instagram Reel size](/blog/reel-size-instagram) explains the safe zones in more detail, and they apply to Stories too.

### Export at sensible settings

If you edit in another app, export at 1080×1920 rather than 4K. A moderate bitrate gives Instagram less to throw away. For still images, a high-quality JPG or PNG at the exact size works well.

### Add text in Instagram, not in the image

Text and stickers added in Instagram's own editor are rendered separately and stay crisp. Text baked into a low-resolution image gets blurred along with everything else.

### Check on another device

Your Story might look fine to everyone else. View it from a second account or a friend's phone on a good connection before you start changing settings.

## When the problem is the account, not the file

If you've followed every step and only some Stories look soft, look for a pattern. Blurry only on cellular points to data settings. Blurry only for videos from one phone points to that phone's camera format. Blurry only for images from a design tool points to the export size.

For brands posting the same visual across several channels, it helps to keep one master file per format, resized for each platform, in a shared media library. That way the Story, the feed post and the TikTok version each start from a clean, correctly sized original. Schedule For You's media library and calendar make that part easier when you're planning posts for Instagram and other networks in one place, though Stories themselves are best checked in the Instagram app before and after posting.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size should an Instagram Story be?

Use 1080×1920 pixels, a 9:16 vertical ratio. Files at this size go through Instagram's compression with the least visible loss. Larger files are scaled down and smaller ones are enlarged, which is where blur creeps in.

### Why does my Story look fine in my camera roll but blurry after posting?

Instagram compresses every Story. If your file was the wrong size, very large, shot in HDR, or uploaded with data saver on, that compression is more visible. Resize to 1080×1920, turn on highest-quality uploads and post on Wi-Fi.

### Does turning on "Upload at highest quality" use more data?

Yes, uploads will be larger and can take longer on cellular data. If you're on a limited plan, keep the setting on and upload important Stories over Wi-Fi.

### Why are my Story videos blurry but photos are fine?

Video is compressed more heavily than still images, and video-specific issues like HDR, low light and high bitrates make it worse. Try recording in SDR, shooting in good light and exporting at 1080×1920 before you post.

### Can I fix a Story after it's posted?

No. You can't replace the media in a posted Story. Delete it, fix the file or your settings, and post it again.
