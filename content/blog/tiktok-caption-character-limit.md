You've written a detailed TikTok caption and want to know if it fits. The TikTok caption character limit is **4,000 characters** when you post in the app, raised from 2,200 in 2023. Hashtags, mentions and emoji all count. Viewers see roughly the first line, around **60 to 80 characters**, before **…more**.

![TikTok caption character limit: 4,000 in the app, 2,200 through scheduling tools, about one line visible before more](/images/blog/tiktok-caption-character-limit-hero-1200x675.webp)

There's a catch most guides skip: TikTok's API, which every scheduling tool uses, caps video captions at **2,200 characters**. So the number that applies depends on how you post.

This guide covers every TikTok limit, why captions got longer, where the cut falls, how to write a caption that works for search, and how the limits compare with other platforms.

## Why TikTok Raised the Caption Limit to 4,000 (Search, Not Stories)

![TikTok captions for search compared with hashtag-only captions: plain words describing the video get found more](/images/blog/tiktok-caption-character-limit-search-1200x675.webp)

TikTok leans on captions for search. Longer captions give its search engine more to index, which is why TikTok itself suggests describing the video in plain words rather than stacking hashtags. A caption that says what the video shows, in a sentence or two, tends to be found more than one that's only tags.

### What Most People Don't Know

The 4,000-character limit only applies when you post in the TikTok app. Through TikTok's API, which scheduling tools rely on, video captions are capped at 2,200. If you paste a 3,000-character caption into a scheduler, it won't fit.

> **Key point:** Write for 2,200 if there's any chance you'll schedule the post. It covers both routes, and very few captions need more.

## How to Write a TikTok Caption That Gets Read (Step by Step)

![Four steps to write a TikTok caption: hook or keyword first, describe the video, short paragraphs, three to five hashtags](/images/blog/tiktok-caption-character-limit-steps-1200x675.webp)

### Step 1: Put the Hook or Keyword First

The feed shows about one line, then **…more**. The first 60 to 80 characters have to make sense on their own.

### Step 2: Describe What the Video Shows

One or two plain sentences that say what's in the video. This is what search reads.

### Step 3: Use Short Paragraphs

Line breaks are kept, so short paragraphs read better when expanded than a wall of text.

### Step 4: Add Three to Five Relevant Hashtags

There's no separate hashtag cap; they count toward the caption. Three to five relevant tags plus a plain-language description works better than a long tag list.

> **Pro tip:** Read your first line out loud. If it doesn't tell someone what they're about to watch, or why they should keep watching, rewrite it before anything else.
