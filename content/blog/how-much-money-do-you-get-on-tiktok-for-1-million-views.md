There's no fixed amount TikTok pays for 1 million views. Through the Creator Rewards Program, TikTok pays eligible creators based on **qualified views** of eligible videos longer than one minute, and the rate per 1,000 qualified views (RPM) varies from video to video and account to account. A million views on a 20-second video earns nothing from that program, while a million views on a qualifying video could earn a modest or a meaningful amount depending on how many views qualify and the RPM.

Below is how the payout actually works, a few clearly hypothetical worked examples, and the other ways TikTok creators make money.

Program rules change, and they differ by country. Check TikTok's own help pages and the Creator Rewards Program section of the app for the current details where you live.

## How the TikTok Creator Rewards Program works

The Creator Rewards Program is TikTok's main way of paying creators for views. It replaced the older Creator Fund, and it focuses on longer, original content.

### Eligibility

At the time of writing, to join you need to:

- Be at least 18 years old.
- Have at least 10,000 followers.
- Have at least 100,000 video views in the last 30 days.
- Have an account in good standing, in a country where the program is available.

Once you're eligible, you apply from the app's creator tools. Joining doesn't mean every video earns: each video must also meet the program's criteria.

### Which videos earn

Videos need to be longer than one minute and original. TikTok also applies its content guidelines and its standards for what can be recommended in the For You feed. Reposted clips, content made mostly of other people's material, and videos that don't follow the Community Guidelines won't earn.

### What a "qualified view" is

Not every view on an eligible video counts. TikTok's criteria for qualified views have included factors such as the view coming from the For You feed, a minimum watch time, and the view not being flagged as fraudulent or invalid. That means your qualified views are usually lower than the view count shown on the video. TikTok shows qualified views in your Creator Rewards dashboard, so that's the number to use when estimating earnings.

### What affects your RPM

RPM here means rewards per 1,000 qualified views. TikTok has said that rewards depend on factors including originality, how long people watch, how much search value the video has, and how much people engage with it. Outside TikTok's own formula, the same forces that shape ad-funded payouts everywhere matter too: where your audience lives, your niche, and the time of year.

## Worked examples: 1 million views

The numbers below are **hypothetical**. They're not averages, predictions or typical rates. They exist only to show how the calculation works, so you can plug in your own figures from your dashboard.

The formula is:

**Earnings = (qualified views ÷ 1,000) × RPM**

### Example 1: a short video

You post a 40-second video and it gets 1 million views. Because it's under one minute, it isn't eligible for the Creator Rewards Program. **Program earnings: $0**, whatever the view count. It may still help you grow followers, sell products or win brand deals.

### Example 2: an eligible video with a lower RPM

You post a 90-second original video. It gets 1 million views, and suppose 600,000 of them qualify. If your RPM for that video were $0.50:

- 600,000 ÷ 1,000 = 600
- 600 × $0.50 = **$300**

### Example 3: the same views, a higher RPM

Same video, same 600,000 qualified views, but suppose the RPM were $1.00:

- 600 × $1.00 = **$600**

### Example 4: fewer qualified views

Suppose only 300,000 of your 1 million views qualify, perhaps because many came from outside the For You feed, and your RPM were $1.00:

- 300 × $1.00 = **$300**

The point is that two creators with "1 million views" can see very different payouts. The share of views that qualify and the RPM both matter as much as the headline view count.

### Estimate your own

1. Open your Creator Rewards dashboard and note a recent eligible video's qualified views and its estimated rewards.
2. Divide the rewards by (qualified views ÷ 1,000) to get your RPM for that video.
3. Repeat for several videos. Your own range is far more useful than anyone else's number.

## Other ways to earn on TikTok

For many creators, view-based rewards are only part of the income. These are the main official options. Eligibility, availability and fees vary by country and change over time, so check the requirements in the app.

### LIVE gifts

When you go LIVE, viewers can send virtual Gifts bought with TikTok Coins. Gifts convert into Diamonds, which eligible creators can cash out. You need to be an adult to receive gifts, and TikTok sets its own requirements for who can go LIVE.

### Subscriptions

Eligible creators can offer a monthly subscription. Subscribers pay for perks such as badges, custom emotes or subscriber-only content, and the creator receives a share of the revenue.

### Brand deals

Brands pay creators to feature products or services. Deals can come through direct contact, an agency, or TikTok's own marketplace for connecting brands and creators. Paid partnerships must be disclosed using TikTok's content disclosure setting, and the price is negotiated, not set by TikTok.

### TikTok Shop affiliate

Where TikTok Shop is available, creators can tag products in videos and LIVE streams and earn a commission on sales. Commission rates are set per product by sellers, and there are eligibility requirements to join.

### Off-platform income

Many creators use TikTok to grow something they own: a newsletter, a course, a product line, a service business, or a YouTube channel. A link in your bio can send people there once your account qualifies for one.

## How to put yourself in a better position

There's no setting that raises your payout per view. What helps is making the kind of video the program is built to reward and posting it consistently.

- **Make videos longer than a minute that earn the length.** Padding a 30-second idea to 61 seconds tends to hurt watch time. Plan ideas that need the extra time: tutorials, stories, breakdowns.
- **Stay original.** Film your own footage, use your own voice, and avoid stitching together other people's clips.
- **Think about search.** Say and show the words people would search for, and use them in your caption.
- **Keep your account in good standing.** Check your Account status in settings so a violation doesn't quietly block eligibility.
- **Post on a schedule you can keep.** Planning longer videos takes time, so batching and scheduling helps. Schedule For You can publish your TikTok videos at set times and lets you choose public, friends or only me for each post. It doesn't track earnings or analytics; your TikTok dashboard is the place for that.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does TikTok pay per view?

TikTok doesn't publish a fixed rate per view. Through the Creator Rewards Program, eligible creators earn based on qualified views of eligible videos, and the rate per 1,000 qualified views varies. Your Creator Rewards dashboard shows your actual figures.

### Do TikTok videos under one minute earn money?

Not from the Creator Rewards Program, which requires videos longer than one minute at the time of writing. Shorter videos can still earn indirectly through brand deals, TikTok Shop affiliate sales, or by growing an audience that joins your LIVEs or subscriptions.

### How many followers do you need to get paid on TikTok?

For the Creator Rewards Program, you need at least 10,000 followers, plus 100,000 video views in the last 30 days and to be 18 or older, in an eligible country (at the time of writing). Other features such as LIVE gifts and Subscriptions have their own requirements.

### Why are my qualified views lower than my total views?

Only some views count as qualified. TikTok's criteria have included where the view came from (such as the For You feed), how long it lasted, and whether it was genuine activity. Views from other sources, very short plays and invalid traffic don't count.

### Is 1 million views on TikTok worth more than on YouTube?

It depends on the video's format and length, how many views qualify, the audience and the niche. Each platform calculates payouts differently, so compare your own RPM on each rather than relying on general claims.
