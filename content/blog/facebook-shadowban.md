Your Facebook Page used to reach a few thousand people a post. This month it's a few hundred, and you suspect a shadow ban. Facebook doesn't officially shadowban, but it can quietly reduce how many people see your posts. The quickest check is your **Page status** (for a Page) or **Account status** (for a personal profile): Facebook lists content that "can't be recommended", removed posts and other restrictions there, with a way to request a review.

![Shadow ban on Facebook: check Page status or Account status first, then look for the cause](/images/blog/facebook-shadowban-hero-1200x675.webp)

If your status is clear, a drop in reach is more likely normal variation, a change in what you post, or more competition in Feed. That's the part most guides skip.

This guide explains what Meta actually limits, four checks that work, what happens in groups, the common causes, and a step-by-step recovery plan.

## Does Facebook Shadowban? What Meta Actually Does (Remove, Reduce, Inform)

Meta describes its approach as **remove, reduce, inform**. It removes content that breaks its Community Standards, reduces the distribution of content that's problematic but allowed, and informs people with labels and context. The "reduce" part is what feels like a shadowban.

![Signs of a normal Facebook Page compared with signs of a Page whose reach is limited](/images/blog/facebook-shadowban-signs-1200x675.webp)

| What you notice | Possible explanation | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Page status mentions content that can't be recommended | A recommendation limit on posts or the Page | Page status in your Professional dashboard |
| A notice says a post goes against Community Standards | The post was removed or limited | Notifications and Support inbox |
| A post carries a label or note adding context | Reduced distribution for misleading content | The label on the post |
| Non-followers can't find your Page in search | A restriction, a new Page or a name mismatch | Page status; ask a friend to search |
| Reach falls across many posts at once | A limit, or ordinary changes in Feed | Page status; Meta Business Suite insights |
| Group posts never get approved | Group admins or Facebook's spam filter | Group rules; ask an admin |

### Content That Can't Be Recommended

Meta publishes **Recommendation Guidelines** describing content it allows on Facebook but won't suggest to people who don't already follow you, in places like Feed recommendations, Reels and suggested groups. At the time of writing these cover things like sensational health claims, borderline sexual or violent content, content from accounts that recently broke the rules, and some posts about sensitive topics.

### Reduced Distribution for Borderline Content

Meta's **Content Distribution Guidelines** list kinds of content that get shown to fewer people in Feed, even to followers. At the time of writing these include engagement bait ("comment YES if you agree"), clickbait headlines, links to pages with a poor or misleading browsing experience, and content judged misleading. Meta said in 2025 that it would also reduce reach for accounts that mostly repost other people's content without adding anything.

Fact-checking has changed too. In 2025 Meta announced it was ending its third-party fact-checking program in the US and moving to a Community Notes system. Outside the US, fact-checking partners may still rate posts at the time of writing, and posts rated false are shown to fewer people.

### What Most People Don't Know

A recommendation limit mostly affects people who **don't** follow you. Your followers can usually still see those posts. So if your followers are still commenting but new people have stopped finding you, that pattern points to a recommendation limit. Pages, profiles and groups that break the rules repeatedly can be removed from recommendations entirely for a while, and Page status is where that shows up.

> **Key point:** Facebook usually tells you when it limits a Page. If Page status is clear and there are no notices, look at your posts and your posting pattern before you assume a shadow ban.

## How to Check for a Shadow Ban on Facebook: 4 Checks That Work

Menu names on Facebook change between app versions. If you can't find a screen, search Settings for its name.

![Four checks for a Facebook shadow ban, starting with Page status](/images/blog/facebook-shadowban-checks-1200x675.webp)

### Check 1: Open Page Status or Account Status (the Only Official Answer)

For a Page, switch into the Page and open the **Professional dashboard**, then **Page status**, at the time of writing. For a personal profile, look for **Account status** in Settings. These screens list removed content, posts that can't be recommended and restrictions on features, usually with the rule involved.

If everything shows as clear, an account-wide limit is unlikely.

### Check 2: Read Notifications and the Support Inbox

Facebook usually notifies you when a post is removed or limited. Look in your notifications and the Support inbox, plus the email on the account. Only trust messages from Facebook itself, and never enter your password from a link in an unexpected message.

### Check 3: Run a Search Test With a Friend

Ask someone who doesn't follow your Page to search its exact name. If it doesn't appear, check that the name matches and that the Page is published, then look at Page status again.

### Check 4: Compare Reach Like for Like, Not Post by Post

In Meta Business Suite insights, compare similar posts over a few weeks. One weak post is normal; a sudden drop across everything you post at the same time is worth investigating.

> **Pro tip:** The mistake people make is comparing a quiet Tuesday photo with last month's viral video. Compare the same kind of post (photo with photo, link with link) over the same number of days, or you'll see a "shadow ban" that's really just a weaker post.

## Shadowbans in Facebook Groups (Admins, Not Facebook, Are Often the Reason)

Groups have their own layer of moderation, which can feel like a shadowban when your posts don't appear.

![Reasons a Facebook group post doesn't appear, from admin approval to Facebook-level limits](/images/blog/facebook-shadowban-groups-1200x675.webp)

- **Post approval.** Many groups hold new posts for an admin to approve. Your post may be waiting, not hidden.
- **Admin tools and filters.** Admins can set rules that decline posts automatically, such as posts with links or from new members, and Facebook can send suspected spam to a separate folder for admins to review.
- **Muted or restricted members.** Admins can temporarily stop a member from posting or commenting in their group.
- **Facebook-level limits.** People who repeatedly break the rules in groups can be temporarily blocked from posting or commenting in any group, at the time of writing.

If you run a group, its admin tools include a group-level status page showing removed content and violations. If you're a member, read the group rules and message an admin politely before assuming a Facebook-wide restriction.

## 7 Common Causes of Reduced Reach on Facebook

![What keeps a Facebook Page's reach healthy compared with what gets it reduced](/images/blog/facebook-shadowban-causes-1200x675.webp)

| Cause | Red flag | Safer approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engagement bait | Asking people to like, comment, share or tag to "win" or "vote" | Ask a real question |
| Clickbait headlines | Hiding or exaggerating what a link contains | Say what the link is |
| Low-quality links | Sites heavy with ads, pop-ups or misleading pages | Link to reputable pages |
| Reposting | Other people's content with nothing added | Original posts, or add your own take |
| Group spam | The same link in many groups in a short time | Share in a few relevant groups, spaced out |
| Rule-breaking content | Violence, hate speech or adult content | Read the Community Standards |
| Bursts of contact | Friend requests or messages to strangers | Grow slowly and personally |

## "Shadowban Removal" Services and Checker Sites (Use With Caution)

Search for a Facebook shadowban fix and you'll find sites that claim to scan your Page or lift a restriction for a fee.

### The Real Risks

- **They can't see inside Facebook.** No outside site can read your Page status. At best they guess from the outside.
- **Login requests.** Anyone asking for your password or login codes can take over the Page.
- **Paid "removal" is a scam.** Only Meta can lift its own restrictions, and reviews through Facebook are free.

### Safety Checklist

- Check Page status yourself before trusting any outside verdict.
- Never share your password or login codes.
- Review apps and websites connected to your account in Settings and remove anything you don't recognize.
- Turn on two-factor authentication.

> **Bottom line:** Page status, Account status and Facebook's own notices are the only reliable sources. Anyone charging to remove a restriction is selling something they can't deliver.

## How to Recover From a Facebook Shadow Ban: 6-Step Plan

![Four moves to recover reach on Facebook: read Page status, fix what's named, stop the trigger, request a review](/images/blog/facebook-shadowban-recovery-1200x675.webp)

1. **Read Page status.** Start with whatever post or rule it names.
2. **Request a review if it's a mistake.** Page status and removal notices usually include an option such as "Request review" or "Disagree with decision". Explain briefly why the post follows the rules. Some decisions can later be appealed to the Oversight Board, which takes only a small share of cases.
3. **Delete or edit content that breaks the rules,** especially recent posts that match the causes above.
4. **Stop spam-like behavior.** Space out posts, stop posting the same link to many groups and drop engagement-bait captions.
5. **Remove apps you don't trust.** In Settings, check apps and websites connected to your account and remove anything you don't recognize.
6. **Post original content steadily and give it time.** Recommendation limits on Pages are often temporary and lift once you've gone a while without new violations.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Facebook shadowban people?

Not in name. Facebook removes content that breaks its rules and reduces the distribution of borderline content, and it can stop recommending posts, Pages and groups to non-followers. That reduced distribution is what people call a shadowban on Facebook.

### How do I know if my Facebook Page is shadowbanned?

Open Page status in your Professional dashboard. It lists content that can't be recommended and other restrictions. If it's clear, compare reach across similar posts before assuming a limit.

### How long does a shadowban on Facebook last?

Meta doesn't publish fixed durations. Recommendation limits are often lifted after a period with no new violations, and a successful review can lift a limit on a specific post sooner.

### Can scheduling posts cause a Facebook shadowban?

Scheduling your own posts to a Page through Meta's official tools or API is normal use. What causes trouble is the content and behavior, such as engagement bait, spammy links or posting the same thing to many groups.

### Can my posts be hidden in a Facebook group but not elsewhere?

Yes. Group admins can require approval, decline posts automatically or restrict members, and that only affects that group. Check the group rules and ask an admin.

### How do I request a review on Facebook?

Use the option on Page status or on the removal notice, such as "Request review" or "Disagree with decision". Explain briefly why the post follows the rules. Reviews are free.

### Are Facebook shadowban checker sites accurate?

No. They can't see Facebook's internal systems or your Page status. Use Page status and Facebook's own notices instead, and never give a site your login.

### Do links reduce reach on Facebook?

Links to low-quality or misleading sites can. Links to reputable pages are fine, especially with a caption that says clearly what the link contains.
