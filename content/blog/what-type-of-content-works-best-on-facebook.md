Your Facebook Page posts every week and reach keeps sliding. The type of content that works best on Facebook in 2026 is **original short video (Reels)**, backed by **photos and multi-photo posts that start conversations**. Meta has said plainly that Pages and profiles get the most distribution when they post mostly original content, and it has folded Facebook video into the Reels format.

![What type of content works best on Facebook in 2026: original Reels and photos first, other formats in support](/images/blog/what-type-of-content-works-best-on-facebook-hero-1200x675.webp)

Link posts, text posts, Lives, Stories and Groups all still have a job, but they work best as supporting formats around a core of original video and images. What hurts most isn't a weak format; it's reposting other people's content.

This guide covers what Meta says it rewards, the eight Facebook formats compared, a sample weekly mix, a worked example for beauty brands, how to measure your own results, and how to keep a consistent schedule.

## What Meta Says It Rewards on Facebook (3 Signals)

Third-party studies often disagree, so Meta's own public statements are a steadier guide.

![What Facebook rewards compared with what it shows less of](/images/blog/what-type-of-content-works-best-on-facebook-rewards-1200x675.webp)

### 1. Original Content Gets the Most Reach

In its July 2025 post on [combating unoriginal content](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/combating-unoriginal-content), Meta said that "Pages and profiles receive the greatest distribution when they consist primarily of original content." It also said it reduces distribution of duplicate videos, and that accounts that repeatedly repost other people's work without meaningful changes can lose reach on everything they share.

### 2. Video on Facebook Now Means Reels

In June 2025 Meta announced that [all videos posted on Facebook would be shared as reels](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/making-it-easier-to-create-videos-on-facebook), with the rollout happening gradually and Reels losing their previous length and format restrictions. So "should I post a video or a Reel?" is mostly settled: vertical, short, original video is the format Facebook is built around today.

### 3. Some Content Gets Shown Less

Meta's Transparency Center lists the kinds of content it [reduces distribution of](https://transparency.meta.com/enforcement/taking-action/lowering-distribution-of-problematic-content) in Feed, including clickbait and engagement bait, links to websites "covered with ads, slow to load or broken," and "limited originality content that is principally repurposed from other sources."

### What Most People Don't Know

Meta's creator guidance also notes that captions with no links, minimal capital letters and no more than five hashtags perform best. That doesn't mean you can't share links, only that the post itself should be worth stopping for.

> **Key point:** On Facebook, originality is the ranking signal you control most. Make it yours: your face, your product, your voice.

## Facebook Content Formats Compared: 8 Types and Their Jobs

| Format | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Reels (short video) | Reaching people beyond your followers, demos, tips, behind the scenes | Reposted or watermarked clips; slow openings |
| Photos | Products, people, places, quick announcements | Stock images that look like ads |
| Multi-photo posts | Event recaps, product ranges, before-and-after sets, step-by-steps | Too many near-identical shots |
| Link posts | Blog posts, bookings, sales, news | Clickbait headlines; slow or ad-heavy pages |
| Text posts | Questions, opinions, short updates | "Engagement bait" phrasing |
| Live video | Q&As, launches, events, tutorials | Going live with no plan or promotion |
| Stories | Daily, casual updates for existing followers | Expecting new reach from them |
| Groups | Communities, customers, members, local fans | Treating a Group like an ad channel |

![Facebook formats sorted into the core of your mix and the supporting formats](/images/blog/what-type-of-content-works-best-on-facebook-formats-1200x675.webp)

### Reels: Your Best Shot at New Reach

Reels are where Facebook is most likely to show your content to people who don't already follow you. Shoot vertically (9:16), get to the point in the first couple of seconds, add on-screen captions, and upload the original file rather than one carrying another app's watermark. For the money side, see [how much Facebook Reels pay per 1,000 views](/blog/how-much-does-facebook-reels-pay-per-1000-views).

### Photos and Multi-Photo Posts: Quick and Still Effective

Real photos of your team, customers (with permission) and products usually feel more at home in the feed than polished stock images. Several photos together suit event recaps, product ranges and step-by-step guides.

### Link Posts: When the Click Is the Point

Write the post so it makes sense even if someone doesn't click. Our guide to [adding links to a Facebook post](/blog/add-links-to-facebook-post) covers link previews and the "link in the first comment" approach, which is worth testing rather than assuming.

### Text Posts: Ask Real Questions

Short text posts work when they invite a genuine response: a real question, a choice between two options, a strong opinion relevant to your audience. Avoid phrasing that asks for reactions for their own sake.

### Lives, Stories and Groups

Lives suit Q&As, launches, workshops and events; announce them in advance and answer comments by name, and the replay keeps working afterward. Stories mostly reach people who already follow your Page, so use them for casual, day-of updates (see [scheduling Instagram and Facebook Stories](/blog/schedule-instagram-and-facebook-stories)). Groups are where people talk to each other, not just to your brand, and they take moderation time.

## Example: Top Beauty Content Types on Facebook and Instagram in 2026

Beauty is a highly visual category, so it's a useful example of how one brand might split content across Facebook and Instagram.

| Content type | Facebook | Instagram |
|---|---|---|
| Tutorials | Reels for quick looks; Lives for longer, step-by-step sessions | Reels, plus carousels with step-by-step photos |
| Before and after | Multi-photo posts showing the full process | Carousels or split-screen Reels |
| Reels | Short demos, routines, product textures | The main discovery format |
| UGC (customer content) | Reshare with permission and credit; collect in a Group | Reshare with permission and credit, or feature in Reels |
| Product launches | Countdown Stories, a launch Live, a link post to the shop | Teaser Reels, launch carousel, Stories |

- **Tutorials:** keep each one to one outcome ("a five-minute everyday look") and show the product in use, not just in the packaging.
- **Before and after:** keep it honest. Same lighting and angle for both shots, no filters that change skin texture, and follow your advertising rules and Meta's policies, especially for skincare claims.
- **UGC:** always ask permission, credit the creator, and add something of your own, such as commentary or a voiceover, if you edit their content into a Reel.
- **Launches:** teaser Reels in the days before, a Live or Story countdown on the day, then a link post or carousel with the details.

## How to Measure What Works on Your Facebook Page in 5 Steps

General advice only gets you so far. Your own numbers decide. Meta renames metrics and menus from time to time, so labels may differ on your Page.

![Five steps to measure which Facebook content formats work for your Page](/images/blog/what-type-of-content-works-best-on-facebook-measure-1200x675.webp)

### Step 1: Open Insights

Open **Meta Business Suite** (or your Page's **Professional dashboard**) and go to **Insights**.

### Step 2: Open the Content Section

You'll see recent posts with their reach, views and interactions.

### Step 3: Compare Like With Like

This is where most comparisons go wrong. Group posts by format (Reels, photos, links, text) and compare them over the same time window, for example the first 48 hours, not a week-old Reel against a photo from this morning.

### Step 4: Look Beyond Likes

Comments, shares and saves show whether people found the post worth acting on. For link posts, check link clicks.

### Step 5: Test for Four Weeks, Then Shift Your Mix

Keep posting times similar, vary the format, then shift your mix toward what wins.

> **Pro tip:** Change one thing at a time. If you switch format and posting time in the same week, you won't know which one moved the numbers. Our guide to [content performance analysis](/blog/content-performance-analysis) shows how to set up a fair comparison.

## Reposts, Watermarks and Engagement Bait (Use With Caution)

Reposting viral clips and "comment YES if you agree" posts can look like shortcuts to reach. On Facebook they're the opposite.

### The Real Risks

- **Reach on everything.** Meta has said accounts that repeatedly repost other people's work without meaningful changes can lose reach on everything they share, not just the reposts.
- **Watermarks.** Meta advises avoiding visible watermarks from other apps.
- **Engagement bait.** Posts that ask for reactions for their own sake are on Meta's list of content it shows less.

### Safety Checklist

- If you use someone else's material, add real value: commentary, a voiceover, creative editing, and permission.
- Upload original files, not downloads from other apps.
- Ask questions you actually want answered.

> **Bottom line:** One original Reel does more for your Page than five reposted ones.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What type of content works best on Facebook?

Original short video (Reels) and original photos that invite conversation tend to work best. Meta says Pages and profiles get the most distribution when they post mostly original content, with link posts, text, Lives, Stories and Groups as supporting formats.

### Are Reels better than photos on Facebook?

Reels are more likely to reach people who don't follow you, because Facebook recommends them widely. Photos still work well with your existing audience and are quicker to make, so most Pages do best with a mix.

### Do link posts get less reach on Facebook?

Meta hasn't published a simple rule against links, but it does reduce distribution of links to slow, broken or ad-heavy websites, and its creator guidance says captions without links perform best. Use links when the click matters and make the post worthwhile on its own.

### What content should beauty brands post on Facebook and Instagram?

Tutorials, honest before-and-after posts, short Reels showing products in use, customer content shared with permission and credit, and launches spread across Reels, Stories and a Live. Measure each format in Insights and adjust.

### How many hashtags should I use on Facebook?

No more than five, according to Meta's creator guidance, which also favors captions with minimal capital letters and no links.

### Should I post videos or Reels on Facebook?

Reels. Meta announced in June 2025 that all videos posted on Facebook would be shared as reels, rolling out gradually, so short vertical video is the format to plan around.

### Does reposting other people's content hurt my Page?

Yes, it can. Meta has said accounts that repeatedly repost other people's work without meaningful changes can lose reach on everything they share.

### How often should a business post on Facebook?

There's no official number. About five posts a week, as in the sample mix, is a workable starting point for a small business; consistency matters more than volume.
