The type of content that works best on Facebook in 2026 is **original short video (Reels)**, backed by **photos and multi-photo posts that start conversations**. Meta has said plainly that Pages and profiles get the most distribution when they post mostly original content, and it has folded Facebook video into the Reels format. Link posts, text posts, Lives, Stories and Groups all still have a job, but they work best as supporting formats around a core of original video and images.

No format wins for every Page, so below you'll find what Meta says it rewards, how each format fits, a sample weekly mix, an example for beauty brands and how to measure your own results.

## What Meta says it rewards on Facebook

Third-party studies often disagree, so Meta's own public statements are a steadier guide.

### Original content gets the most reach

In its July 2025 post on [combating unoriginal content](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/combating-unoriginal-content), Meta said that "Pages and profiles receive the greatest distribution when they consist primarily of original content." It also said it reduces distribution of duplicate videos and that accounts that repeatedly repost other people's work without meaningful changes can lose reach on everything they share. Meta's advice in the same post: add real value if you use someone else's material (commentary, voiceover, creative editing), and avoid visible watermarks from other apps.

### Video on Facebook now means Reels

In June 2025 Meta announced that [all videos posted on Facebook would be shared as reels](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/making-it-easier-to-create-videos-on-facebook), with the rollout happening gradually and Reels losing their previous length and format restrictions. In practice, "should I post a video or a Reel?" is mostly settled: vertical, short, original video is the format Facebook is built around today.

### What Facebook shows less of

Meta's Transparency Center lists the kinds of content it [reduces distribution of](https://transparency.meta.com/enforcement/taking-action/lowering-distribution-of-problematic-content) in Feed, including clickbait and engagement bait, links to websites "covered with ads, slow to load or broken," and "limited originality content that is principally repurposed from other sources." That's a useful checklist of what to avoid: "comment YES if you agree" posts, spammy landing pages and reposted memes.

Meta's creator guidance also notes that captions with no links, minimal capital letters and no more than five hashtags perform best. That doesn't mean you can't share links, only that the post itself should be worth stopping for.

## Facebook content formats compared

| Format | Best for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reels (short video) | Reaching people beyond your followers, demos, tips, behind the scenes | Reposted or watermarked clips; slow openings |
| Photos | Products, people, places, quick announcements | Stock images that look like ads |
| Multi-photo posts | Event recaps, product ranges, before-and-after sets, step-by-steps | Too many near-identical shots |
| Link posts | Blog posts, bookings, sales, news | Clickbait headlines; slow or ad-heavy pages |
| Text posts | Questions, opinions, short updates | "Engagement bait" phrasing |
| Live video | Q&As, launches, events, tutorials | Going live with no plan or promotion |
| Stories | Daily, casual updates for existing followers | Expecting new reach from them |
| Groups | Communities, customers, members, local fans | Treating a Group like an ad channel |

### Reels and short video

Reels are where Facebook is most likely to show your content to people who don't already follow you. Shoot vertically (9:16), get to the point in the first couple of seconds, add on-screen captions, and make it yours: your face, your product, your voice. Upload the original file rather than one carrying another app's watermark. And if you're curious about the money side, see [how much Facebook Reels pay per 1,000 views](/blog/how-much-does-facebook-reels-pay-per-1000-views).

### Photos and multi-photo posts

Photos are quick to make and still work well for local businesses and anything visual. Real photos of your team, customers (with permission) and products usually feel more at home in the feed than polished stock images. Posting several photos together suits event recaps, product ranges and step-by-step guides, and gives people a reason to tap through.

### Link posts

Link posts are how you send people to your website, booking page or shop. Use them when the click is the point, and write the post so it makes sense even if someone doesn't click. Our guide to [adding links to a Facebook post](/blog/add-links-to-facebook-post) covers link previews and the "link in the first comment" approach, which is worth testing rather than assuming.

### Text posts

Short text posts work when they invite a genuine response: a real question, a poll-style choice, a strong opinion relevant to your audience. Avoid phrasing that asks for reactions for their own sake, which Meta treats as engagement bait.

### Live video

Lives suit Q&As, product launches, workshops and events. Announce them in advance, have a plan for the first few minutes, and answer comments by name. Lives remain a separate format from Reels, and the replay can keep working after the broadcast ends.

### Stories

Facebook Stories mostly reach people who already follow your Page. They're good for casual, day-of updates: today's specials, a countdown, a quick poll. Our guide on [scheduling Instagram and Facebook Stories](/blog/schedule-instagram-and-facebook-stories) explains how to plan them in Meta Business Suite.

### Groups

Groups are where people talk to each other, not just to your brand. A customer community or members' space can build loyalty that Page posts alone don't, but it takes moderation time.

## A sample weekly Facebook content mix

Here's an example week for a small business posting about five times. Treat it as a template to adapt, not a formula.

| Day | Format | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Reel | A 20-second tip or how-to related to what you sell |
| Tuesday | Multi-photo post | New arrivals, a project, or a customer story (with permission) |
| Wednesday | Text post | A genuine question for your audience |
| Thursday | Reel | Behind the scenes: how something is made, packed or prepared |
| Friday | Link post | Your latest blog post, offer or booking link |
| Any day | Stories | Quick, casual updates for existing followers |
| Monthly | Live | A Q&A, demo or launch |

To choose the times for each post, start with our guide to the [best time to post on Facebook](/blog/best-time-to-post-on-facebook), then adjust using your own results.

## Example: top beauty content types on Facebook and Instagram in 2026

Beauty is a highly visual category, so it's a useful example of how one brand might split content across Facebook and Instagram.

| Content type | Facebook | Instagram |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tutorials | Reels for quick looks; Lives for longer, step-by-step sessions | Reels, plus carousels with step-by-step photos |
| Before and after | Multi-photo posts showing the full process | Carousels or split-screen Reels |
| Reels | Short demos, routines, product textures | The main discovery format |
| UGC (customer content) | Reshare with permission and credit; collect in a Group | Reshare with permission and credit, or feature in Reels |
| Product launches | Countdown Stories, a launch Live, a link post to the shop | Teaser Reels, launch carousel, Stories |

### Tutorials

How-to content earns saves and shares because it's useful after the scroll. Keep each tutorial to one outcome ("a five-minute everyday look," "how to apply a serum") and show the product in use, not just in the packaging.

### Before and after

Before-and-after content works because it shows a result. Keep it honest: use the same lighting and angle for both shots, don't use filters that change skin texture, and follow your advertising rules and Meta's policies, especially for skincare claims. Misleading results damage trust quickly.

### Reels

Short demos, routines and texture shots are natural Reels. Make each one original: reposted clips are exactly what Meta says it shows less of.

### UGC

Customer photos and videos are persuasive because they're not made by you. Always ask permission, credit the creator, and add something of your own, such as your commentary or a voiceover, if you edit their content into a Reel.

### Product launches

Spread a launch across formats: teaser Reels in the days before, a Live or Story countdown on the day, then a link post or carousel with full details. ## How to measure what works on your Facebook Page

General advice only gets you so far. Your own numbers decide.

1. Open **Meta Business Suite** (or your Page's **Professional dashboard**) and go to **Insights**.
2. Open the **Content** section to see recent posts with their reach, views and interactions.
3. Group posts by format (Reels, photos, links, text) and compare like with like over the same time window, for example the first 48 hours.
4. Look beyond likes: comments, shares and saves show whether people found the post worth acting on. For link posts, check link clicks.
5. Run a simple test for four weeks: keep posting times similar, vary the format, then shift your mix toward what wins.

Meta renames metrics and menus from time to time, so labels may differ on your Page. Our guide to [content performance analysis](/blog/content-performance-analysis) shows how to set up a fair comparison.

## Frequently asked questions

### What type of content works best on Facebook?

Original short video (Reels) and original photos that invite conversation tend to work best. Meta says Pages and profiles get the most distribution when they post mostly original content. Link posts, text, Lives, Stories and Groups are useful supporting formats.

### Are Reels better than photos on Facebook?

Reels are more likely to reach people who don't follow you, because Facebook recommends them widely. Photos are still effective with your existing audience and are quicker to make. Most Pages do best with a mix; compare both in your Insights.

### Do link posts get less reach on Facebook?

Meta hasn't published a simple rule against links, but it does reduce distribution of links to slow, broken or ad-heavy websites, and its creator guidance says captions without links perform best. Use links when the click matters and make the post worthwhile on its own.

### What content should beauty brands post on Facebook and Instagram?

Tutorials, honest before-and-after posts, short Reels showing products in use, customer content shared with permission and credit, and launches spread across Reels, Stories and a Live. Measure each format in Insights and adjust.
