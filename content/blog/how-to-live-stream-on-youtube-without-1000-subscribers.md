You want to go live on YouTube, but your channel is small and you've heard you need 1,000 subscribers first.

You can live stream on YouTube without 1,000 subscribers by going live from a computer, either with your webcam in the browser or with streaming software (an encoder). Desktop streaming has no subscriber minimum. You only need a verified channel, no live streaming restrictions in the last 90 days, and live streaming turned on, which can take up to 24 hours the first time.

![How to live stream on YouTube without 1,000 subscribers: go live from a computer with a webcam or an encoder](/images/blog/how-to-live-stream-on-youtube-without-1000-subscribers-hero-1200x675.webp)

The confusion comes from mobile. The subscriber requirement applies only to streaming from the YouTube mobile app, and that threshold has changed over the years. It's much lower than 1,000 at the time of writing, so check YouTube Help for the current number. This guide covers the requirements, three ways to go live, how to fix "you can't go live" errors, and how to get viewers to a stream on a small channel.

## Why People Think You Need 1,000 Subscribers to Go Live

The 1,000 figure is a leftover from YouTube's older mobile streaming rules. It never applied to streaming from a computer.

![YouTube live streaming requirements by method: webcam and encoder have no subscriber minimum, mobile does](/images/blog/how-to-live-stream-on-youtube-without-1000-subscribers-methods-1200x675.webp)

| Method | Subscriber minimum | What you need | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Webcam (browser) | None | Verified channel, computer with webcam and mic | Talking-head streams, Q&As |
| Encoder (streaming software) | None | Verified channel, encoder software, stream key | Gaming, screen sharing, multiple cameras |
| Mobile (YouTube app) | Yes, a separate threshold (lower than 1,000 at the time of writing) | Verified channel plus enough subscribers | Streaming on the go |

### What Most People Don't Know

Every method shares the same three requirements, and the subscriber count isn't one of them for desktop. What actually stops most new streamers is the 24-hour activation wait the first time they turn on live streaming. If you click Go live an hour before your planned stream, you may not make it.

> **Key point:** Turn on live streaming a day before your first stream. The wait, not your subscriber count, is what catches new channels out.

## The 4 Requirements Every YouTube Live Stream Needs

YouTube's current list of requirements is in [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube). Menu labels change between versions.

### 1. Verify Your Channel

YouTube asks you to verify your channel with a phone number before you can go live. In YouTube Studio, open **Settings → Channel → Feature eligibility** and follow the steps. You'll receive a code by text or voice call.

### 2. Have No Live Streaming Restrictions in the Last 90 Days

If your channel received a Community Guidelines strike or had live streaming restricted in the last 90 days, you can't go live until the restriction ends. Copyright issues on past streams can also block live streaming.

### 3. Enable Live Streaming and Wait up to 24 Hours

The first time you try to go live, YouTube asks you to turn on live streaming. Activation can take up to 24 hours. Once it's on, you can stream right away in future.

### 4. Check Your Account Settings

The channel's audience setting matters (videos made for kids have features like live chat turned off), as do Google account restrictions and whether you're signed in to the right channel if you manage several, including channels on Brand Accounts.

## Method 1: Go Live With Your Webcam (Fastest, No Software)

This is the quickest way to stream from a laptop or desktop.

![Steps to go live on YouTube with a webcam from a computer](/images/blog/how-to-live-stream-on-youtube-without-1000-subscribers-webcam-1200x675.webp)

1. Go to youtube.com on your computer and sign in to your channel.
2. Click **Create** (the camera icon with a plus) at the top right, then **Go live**. This opens YouTube Live Control Room.
3. If asked, turn on live streaming and wait for it to activate.
4. Choose **Webcam** (sometimes shown as **Right now → Built-in webcam**, depending on the version).
5. Allow your browser to use your camera and microphone.
6. Add a title and description, choose privacy (public, unlisted or private) and set the audience (made for kids or not).
7. Pick the camera and microphone, then click **Next**. YouTube takes a thumbnail from the camera, which you can replace.
8. Click **Go live**. Click **End stream** when you're done.

Webcam streaming is simple but limited: you can't add overlays, share your screen or switch between scenes.

> **Pro tip:** Run your first stream as **unlisted** or **private** to check audio, lighting and your connection before anyone is watching.

## Method 2: Go Live With an Encoder (Games, Screen Share, Overlays)

An encoder is streaming software or hardware that sends your video to YouTube. Free software such as OBS Studio is a common choice, and many games consoles and capture devices have built-in streaming options.

1. Set up your encoder with your camera, microphone and any screen capture.
2. In YouTube, click **Create → Go live** and choose **Stream** (or **Streaming software**).
3. Fill in the title, description, privacy and audience settings.
4. Copy the **stream key** (and stream URL if your software asks for it).
5. Paste the key into your encoder's stream settings and choose YouTube as the service.
6. Start streaming in your encoder. A preview appears in Live Control Room.
7. Click **Go live** in YouTube (or turn on auto-start in stream settings).
8. Stop the stream in your encoder, then end it in Live Control Room.

Treat your stream key like a password. Anyone who has it can stream to your channel. If it's exposed, reset it in Live Control Room.

## Method 3: Stream From Your Phone (Once You're Eligible)

Mobile streaming from the YouTube app has its own subscriber threshold. At the time of writing, YouTube's help center lists a requirement far lower than 1,000, and channels below a certain size may have some limits on their mobile streams. The simplest check is to tap **Create → Go live** in the app: it tells you if your channel isn't eligible yet. If you're not, stream from a computer instead.

## Mobile Encoder Apps (Use With Caution)

Some third-party apps let you stream from your phone to YouTube using a stream key. Because they work like desktop streaming software, they don't rely on the mobile subscriber threshold.

![Safe and risky ways to use mobile encoder apps to stream to YouTube](/images/blog/how-to-live-stream-on-youtube-without-1000-subscribers-caution-1200x675.webp)

### The Real Risks

- **Password phishing.** A legitimate encoder app only needs your stream key. An app asking for your Google password is a red flag.
- **Stream key leaks.** Anyone with your key can stream to your channel.
- **Policy changes.** Check how YouTube currently treats these apps before relying on one.

### A Short Safety Checklist

- Only use reputable apps that ask for a stream key, never your Google password.
- Reset your stream key if you stop using an app.
- Test with a private stream first.

> **Bottom line:** A mobile encoder app is a workaround, not a loophole. Give it a stream key, never your password.

## How to Get Viewers to a Stream on a Small Channel

Scheduling a stream in YouTube creates a watch page ahead of time, so viewers can find it and set a reminder.

1. In Live Control Room, click **Schedule stream** (or **Manage → Schedule stream**).
2. Add the title, description, thumbnail and date and time.
3. Copy the link to share before the stream.

Small channels benefit most from promotion before a stream, and that's where the time goes. Take Tom, who streams indie games every Thursday at 8 PM to a channel of a few hundred subscribers. Each week he wanted an announcement on X and Bluesky two days before, a short teaser on TikTok and Instagram the day before, and a "live now" post at 8 PM: six posts a week, written on the fly while he was also setting up his stream.

Schedule For You can't schedule or broadcast live streams; you still do that in YouTube Live Control Room. What Tom does is write all six promotion posts on Sunday, paste in the watch-page link, give each platform its own caption, and let them go out on their own.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many subscribers do you need to go live on YouTube?

None for webcam or encoder streaming from a computer. Mobile streaming from the YouTube app has a separate subscriber threshold, well below 1,000 at the time of writing. Check YouTube Help for the current number.

### Why can't I go live on YouTube?

The usual reasons are an unverified channel, live streaming that hasn't finished activating (it can take up to 24 hours), or a restriction on your channel in the last 90 days. Mobile streaming also needs a minimum number of subscribers.

### How long does it take to enable live streaming on YouTube?

Up to 24 hours the first time you enable it. After that, you can go live immediately.

### Can I live stream on YouTube with 0 subscribers?

Yes, from a computer. Verify your channel, enable live streaming, wait for activation and go live with your webcam or an encoder.

### Is live streaming on YouTube free?

Yes. YouTube doesn't charge to stream. Free encoder software exists too, though some creators choose paid tools or hardware for extra features.

### Do live streams stay on my channel?

Yes. The replay is saved to your channel by default and can keep getting views afterwards.

### Can I stream games on YouTube without 1,000 subscribers?

Yes. Use encoder software on a computer, or a console or capture device with built-in YouTube streaming.

### Does going live help a small channel grow?

It can, especially if you stream at a regular time and promote it beforehand. Small streams make it easy to talk to every viewer.
