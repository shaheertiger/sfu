You can live stream on YouTube without 1,000 subscribers by going live from a computer, either with your webcam in the browser or with streaming software (an encoder). Desktop streaming has no subscriber minimum. You only need a verified channel, no live streaming restrictions in the last 90 days, and live streaming turned on, which can take up to 24 hours the first time.

The subscriber requirement applies only to streaming from the YouTube mobile app. That threshold has changed over the years and is much lower than 1,000 at the time of writing, so check YouTube Help for the current number.

## Quick answer: requirements by streaming method

| Method | Subscriber minimum | What you need | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Webcam (browser) | None | Verified channel, computer with webcam and mic | Talking-head streams, Q&As |
| Encoder (streaming software) | None | Verified channel, encoder software, stream key | Gaming, screen sharing, multiple cameras |
| Mobile (YouTube app) | Yes, a separate threshold (lower than 1,000 at the time of writing) | Verified channel plus enough subscribers | Streaming on the go |

Every method also requires that your channel has had no live streaming restrictions in the past 90 days. YouTube's current list of requirements is in [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube).

## The requirements every YouTube live stream needs

### 1. Verify your channel

YouTube asks you to verify your channel with a phone number before you can go live. In YouTube Studio, open **Settings → Channel → Feature eligibility** and follow the steps to verify. You'll receive a code by text or voice call.

### 2. Have no live streaming restrictions in the last 90 days

If your channel received a Community Guidelines strike or had live streaming restricted in the last 90 days, you won't be able to go live until the restriction ends. Copyright issues on past streams can also block live streaming.

### 3. Enable live streaming and wait up to 24 hours

The first time you try to go live, YouTube asks you to turn on live streaming. Activation can take up to 24 hours. Once it's on, you can stream right away in the future. Turn it on a day before you plan to stream so the wait doesn't catch you out.

### 4. Check your account settings

A few other things can affect streaming: the channel's audience setting (videos made for kids have features like live chat turned off), Google account restrictions, and whether you're signed in to the right channel if you manage several, including channels on Brand Accounts.

## Method 1: Go live with your webcam

This is the quickest way to stream from a laptop or desktop with no extra software.

1. Go to youtube.com on your computer and sign in to your channel.
2. Click **Create** (the camera icon with a plus) at the top right, then **Go live**. This opens YouTube Live Control Room.
3. If asked, turn on live streaming and wait for it to activate.
4. Choose **Webcam** (sometimes shown as **Right now → Built-in webcam**, depending on the version).
5. Allow your browser to use your camera and microphone.
6. Add a title and description, choose privacy (public, unlisted or private) and set the audience (made for kids or not).
7. Pick the camera and microphone you want, then click **Next**. YouTube takes a thumbnail from the camera, which you can replace.
8. Click **Go live**. Click **End stream** when you're done.

Webcam streaming is simple but limited: you can't add overlays, share your screen or switch between scenes.

## Method 2: Go live with an encoder

An encoder is streaming software or hardware that sends your video to YouTube. Free software such as OBS Studio is a common choice, and many games consoles and capture devices have built-in streaming options. Encoders let you share your screen, add overlays, mix several cameras and stream games.

1. Install and set up your encoder with your camera, microphone and any screen capture.
2. In YouTube, click **Create → Go live** and choose **Stream** (or **Streaming software**).
3. Fill in the title, description, privacy and audience settings.
4. Copy the **stream key** (and stream URL if your software asks for it).
5. Paste the key into your encoder's stream settings and choose YouTube as the service.
6. Start streaming in your encoder. A preview appears in Live Control Room.
7. Click **Go live** in YouTube (or turn on auto-start in stream settings so it goes live as soon as data arrives).
8. Stop the stream in your encoder, then end it in Live Control Room.

Treat your stream key like a password. Anyone who has it can stream to your channel. If it's exposed, reset it in Live Control Room.

## Method 3: Stream from your phone once you're eligible

Mobile streaming from the YouTube app has its own subscriber threshold. At the time of writing, YouTube's help center lists a requirement far lower than 1,000, and channels below a certain size may have some limits on their mobile streams. Because YouTube has changed these numbers before, check the current figure in YouTube Help or simply tap **Create → Go live** in the app: it tells you if your channel isn't eligible yet.

If you're not eligible for mobile streaming, you still have two options:

- **Stream from a computer** with your webcam or an encoder, as above.
- **Use a mobile encoder app** that streams to YouTube with a stream key. Because it works like desktop streaming software, it doesn't rely on the mobile subscriber threshold. Only use reputable apps that ask for a stream key, never your Google password, and check how YouTube currently treats them.

## Schedule your stream in advance

Scheduling a stream creates a watch page ahead of time, so viewers can find it and set a reminder.

1. In Live Control Room, click **Schedule stream** (or **Manage → Schedule stream**).
2. Add the title, description, thumbnail and date and time.
3. Copy the link to share before the stream.

Small channels benefit most from promotion before a stream. Schedule For You can't schedule or broadcast live streams, but it can publish announcement posts and a teaser video to your other channels, such as Instagram, X and TikTok, from the same calendar. After the stream, you can upload highlight clips to YouTube and schedule them there too.

## Why you can't go live: common fixes

| Problem | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| "Live streaming isn't available right now" | Wait the full 24 hours after enabling |
| No option to enable live streaming | Verify your channel with a phone number |
| Live streaming disabled | Look for strikes or restrictions in the last 90 days |
| Mobile app says you're not eligible | Use webcam or encoder streaming instead |
| Encoder connects but nothing appears | Check the stream key, server and that you've clicked Go live |

## Tips for your first streams with a small audience

- **Pick a consistent day and time** so regular viewers know when to come back.
- **Write a clear title.** Say what will happen in the stream; the [YouTube title checker](/tools/youtube-title-checker) is a quick check for length and clarity.
- **Engage with chat by name.** On small streams, every viewer can be part of the conversation.
- **Test with an unlisted or private stream first** to check audio, lighting and your encoder settings.
- **Keep the replay.** Your stream is saved to your channel and can keep getting views afterward.

As your audience grows, you can track subscribers in YouTube Studio; see [how to see your YouTube subscribers](/blog/how-to-see-your-youtube-subscribers).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many subscribers do you need to go live on YouTube?

None for webcam or encoder streaming from a computer. Mobile streaming from the YouTube app has a separate subscriber threshold, which is well below 1,000 at the time of writing. Check YouTube Help for the current number.

### Why can't I go live on YouTube?

The usual reasons are an unverified channel, live streaming that hasn't finished activating (it can take up to 24 hours), or a restriction on your channel in the last 90 days. Mobile streaming also requires a minimum number of subscribers.

### How long does it take to enable live streaming on YouTube?

Up to 24 hours the first time you enable it. After that, you can go live immediately.

### Can I live stream on YouTube with 0 subscribers?

Yes, from a computer. Verify your channel, enable live streaming, wait for activation and go live with your webcam or an encoder.

### Is live streaming on YouTube free?

Yes. YouTube doesn't charge to stream. Free encoder software exists too, though some creators choose paid tools or hardware for extra features.
