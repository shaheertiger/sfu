You spent an hour on a LinkedIn post and it's sitting at 40 impressions. Start with the basics: check that the post is visible to **Anyone**, that it isn't leading with an external link, and that the first two lines give people a reason to click "…more". Most low-reach posts are either shown to very few people at the start, or shown and scrolled past, and each problem has different fixes.

![LinkedIn post not getting impressions? Check visibility, links, timing and your hook before anything else](/images/blog/linkedin-post-not-getting-impressions-hero-1200x675.webp)

LinkedIn doesn't publish its ranking formula, so nobody can promise a specific number of impressions. That's why so much advice is guesswork. What you can do is rule out the causes you control, one at a time.

This guide gives you a quick diagnosis table, then works through visibility, links, timing, hooks, formatting, hashtags and spam signals, with a plan to raise impressions over time.

## Why LinkedIn Shows Some Posts to Almost No One

![Two kinds of low LinkedIn impressions: shown to too few people at the start, or shown and scrolled past](/images/blog/linkedin-post-not-getting-impressions-why-1200x675.webp)

Low impressions come in two shapes. Either LinkedIn showed the post to a small group and stopped, or it showed the post and people scrolled straight past. Match what you're seeing to the most likely cause:

| What you see | Likely cause | First thing to try |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Impressions near zero, even from close connections | Visibility set to connections only or a group, or a restriction on the account | Check the post's audience setting and your account notifications |
| A few dozen impressions, then nothing | The post didn't earn early engagement, so it wasn't shown further | Rework the hook and post when your network is active |
| Link posts always do worse than text or image posts | External link in the post body | Test the same idea without a link |
| Impressions dropped across all posts recently | Posting pattern changed, spam-like behavior, or a broad feed change | Review the last few weeks for changes in frequency, tagging or tools |
| Lots of impressions, few reactions | The hook got the post shown, but the content didn't land | Tighten the body and end with a specific question |

### What Most People Don't Know

A post that gets reactions and thoughtful comments soon after it's published has a better chance of being shown to more people. So the first hour does double duty: it's when your network sees the post, and it's the signal LinkedIn uses to decide whether to show it further.

> **Key point:** Most "dead" LinkedIn posts aren't penalized. They were posted when the network was offline, or their first lines didn't earn a click.

## 1. Is Your Post Visible to the Right People?

Before you change your writing, make sure LinkedIn is allowed to show the post widely.

### Check the Post Audience

When you create a post, LinkedIn lets you choose who can see it. **Anyone** lets the post appear for people outside your network; **Connections only** limits it to your first-degree connections. To check an existing post, open it, tap the three dots and look at the visibility options. Menu labels change between app versions.

### Check Your Profile and Follow Settings

LinkedIn retired "creator mode" as a separate switch in 2024 (at the time of writing) and gave its main tools to all profiles. Look in your profile settings for options such as making **Follow** your primary button and keeping your public profile visible. A clear headline (up to 220 characters) also helps people decide to follow after they see a post.

### Look for Restrictions

If impressions fell to almost nothing overnight, check your LinkedIn notifications and email for any warning. Posts that LinkedIn believes break its Professional Community Policies can be limited or removed. Appeal through LinkedIn's Help Center if you think a decision is wrong.

## 2. Do External Links Hurt LinkedIn Reach? (Test It)

![How to test whether external links lower your LinkedIn impressions: post with and without a link and compare at 48 hours](/images/blog/linkedin-post-not-getting-impressions-links-1200x675.webp)

Many LinkedIn users find that posts with an external link in the body get less reach than similar posts without one. LinkedIn hasn't confirmed a fixed penalty, so treat it as something to test.

### Step 1: Make the Post Stand on Its Own

Put the key idea, steps or takeaway in the post itself, so it's worth reading without a click.

### Step 2: Publish Two Versions on Different Days

One with the link in the body and, on a different day, a similar post without it.

### Step 3: Compare at the Same Checkpoint

Measure impressions after the same amount of time, for example 48 hours. This is the step people skip, and it's why their comparisons mislead them.

If the version without a link wins clearly, keep links for posts where clicks really matter, and make sure the post still delivers value to people who never click.

## 3. Posting Time and the First Hour

There's no single best time for everyone. A recruiter whose network works office hours will see a different pattern from a founder with an international audience. Test a few windows that match your audience's working day, such as early morning, mid-morning and lunchtime in their time zone, and compare results across several weeks.

Plan to stay for a while after publishing. Reply to comments with real answers, not just "Thanks!" A genuine conversation in the comments tells LinkedIn the post is worth showing.

## 4. Hooks and the "…more" Cut-Off

![What makes a LinkedIn hook earn the click compared with first lines that get scrolled past](/images/blog/linkedin-post-not-getting-impressions-hooks-1200x675.webp)

On LinkedIn, the feed shows only the opening of a longer post before the "…more" link. Roughly the first 200 characters or three lines are visible, though the exact cut-off varies by device. If those lines don't earn a click, the rest of the post doesn't matter.

| Weak first line | Stronger first line | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "I've been thinking a lot about onboarding lately." | "We cut our onboarding time in half by removing one step." | Leads with the point |
| "Some thoughts on hiring." | "Our best hire last year had no degree in the field." | Specific and surprising |
| "Big news!" | "After 6 months of testing, we're dropping our free trial." | Names the change |

- **Lead with the point.**
- **Be specific.** Name the problem, the role or the result.
- **Create a reason to keep reading.** A clear tension, a surprising detail or the first step of a story.

## 5–7. Formatting, Visuals and Hashtags

Once someone clicks "…more," make the post easy to scan.

- **Short paragraphs** with white space between them.
- **A short list** when you're giving steps or options.
- **One idea per post.** Three ideas may be three posts.
- **A specific closing question** people can actually answer, not "Thoughts?"
- **Visuals.** A clear image, a document post or a short native video often stops the scroll better than text alone. See [image sizes for LinkedIn posts](/blog/image-sizes-for-linkedin-posts) for dimensions.
- **Hashtags.** They carry less weight than they once did. Use a few specific ones, or none, and put the effort into the first line.

## 8–9. Spam Signals That Quietly Limit Reach

![Habits that keep LinkedIn reach healthy compared with spam signals that quietly limit impressions](/images/blog/linkedin-post-not-getting-impressions-spam-1200x675.webp)

| Habit | Why it hurts | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement bait ("comment YES") | LinkedIn has said it aims to show this kind of post less | Ask a real question |
| Tagging lots of people who aren't involved | They don't respond, and it looks spammy | Tag only people in the post |
| The same text posted repeatedly, or identical comments everywhere | Looks automated | Write each post and comment fresh |
| Too many posts close together | They compete in your followers' feeds | Space posts out |
| Tools that log into your account to like, comment, invite or message | Automated engagement | Only schedule posts you wrote yourself |

## Engagement Pods and Automation Tools (Use With Caution)

![Safe ways to grow LinkedIn impressions compared with engagement pods and automation tools](/images/blog/linkedin-post-not-getting-impressions-pods-1200x675.webp)

When a post flops, pods (groups that agree to like and comment on each other's posts) and tools that react or comment for you promise a quick fix.

### The Real Risks

- **They may break LinkedIn's User Agreement**, which covers automation and inauthentic activity.
- **The engagement looks less authentic.** The same faces commenting "Great post!" on everything is easy to spot, for people and for LinkedIn.
- **Your account can be restricted.** Tools that log in as you and act at machine pace are the riskiest.
- **It doesn't reach the right people.** Pod members rarely match your real audience.

### If You're Tempted

- Comment genuinely on posts in your field instead; it builds the same visibility honestly.
- Never give a tool your LinkedIn password.
- Review connected apps in your LinkedIn settings and remove ones you don't use.

> **Bottom line:** Fake early engagement is a short-term number with a long-term risk. Post when your network is online, and be there to reply.

## Scheduling Without Faking Engagement: A Consultant's Week

Scheduling a post you wrote yourself is different from automating engagement. Take Dev, an HR consultant whose clients are online at 8 a.m. on weekdays. His test showed Tuesday and Thursday at 8 a.m. beat his usual 9 p.m. posts, but at 8 a.m. he's often on a school run, and posting from the car park meant typos.

Writing both posts on Monday afternoon and scheduling them for 8 a.m. takes about 40 minutes, and he's free to reply to comments once he's at his desk.

## How to Increase LinkedIn Impressions Over Time (4 Habits)

1. **Pick two or three topics** you want to be known for, and stay close to them.
2. **Post consistently** rather than in bursts, at the times your testing points to.
3. **Comment thoughtfully on other people's posts** in your field. It builds relationships and puts your name in front of their audiences.
4. **Review your post analytics** every couple of weeks: impressions, reactions, comments and reposts after the same time window.

- ✓ Visibility set to Anyone
- ✓ A hook above the "…more" line
- ✓ Being around for the first hour
- ✗ Pods, bait and automation tools
- ✗ Ten hashtags at the end

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why are my LinkedIn impressions so low all of a sudden?

A sudden drop usually comes from a change on your side or in the feed. Check the post's visibility, any account notifications, and whether you recently added external links, started tagging many people or posted more often. If nothing changed, give it a few weeks of consistent posting before drawing conclusions.

### Does LinkedIn penalize posts with external links?

LinkedIn hasn't published a rule saying so, but many people see lower reach on posts with links in the body. Test it by comparing similar posts with and without a link, measured after the same amount of time.

### Does editing a LinkedIn post reduce impressions?

There's no official evidence that a quick edit hurts reach. Fixing a typo is fine. Still, proofread and preview before posting so you aren't making large changes while the post gathers its first reactions.

### How many hashtags should I use on LinkedIn?

A few specific ones at most, or none. Hashtags matter less than they used to, and a long list at the end looks cluttered. Your first line has a much bigger effect.

### Do scheduled LinkedIn posts get fewer impressions?

There's no public evidence that posts published through LinkedIn's official tools or approved integrations are shown less. What matters is the content, the timing and whether you're around to reply after the post goes live.

### How many characters show before "…more" on LinkedIn?

Roughly the first 200 characters or three lines, depending on the device.

### Should I use engagement pods on LinkedIn?

No. They may break LinkedIn's User Agreement, the engagement looks less authentic, and pod members rarely match your real audience.
