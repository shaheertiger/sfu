If your LinkedIn post is not getting impressions, start with the basics: check that the post is visible to "Anyone," that it isn't leading with an external link, and that the first two lines give people a reason to click "…more." Most low-reach posts are either shown to very few people at the start or shown and scrolled past, and each problem has different fixes.

LinkedIn doesn't publish its ranking formula, so nobody can promise a specific number of impressions. What you can do is rule out the causes you control, one at a time.

## Quick diagnosis: why your LinkedIn post is not getting impressions

When your LinkedIn impressions are low, use this table to match what you're seeing with the most likely cause.

| What you see | Likely cause | First thing to try |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Impressions are near zero, even from close connections | Visibility set to connections only or a group, or a restriction on the account | Check the post's audience setting and your account notifications |
| A few dozen impressions, then nothing | The post didn't earn early engagement, so it wasn't shown further | Rework the hook and post when your network is active |
| Your link posts always do worse than text or image posts | External link in the post body | Test the same idea without a link, or put the link somewhere less prominent |
| Impressions dropped across all posts recently | Posting pattern changed, spam-like behavior, or a broad feed change | Review the last few weeks for changes in frequency, tagging or tools |
| Lots of impressions, few reactions | The hook got the post shown, but the content didn't land | Tighten the body and end with a specific question |

## Is your post visible to the right people?

Before you change your writing, make sure LinkedIn is allowed to show the post widely.

### Check the post audience

When you create a post, LinkedIn lets you choose who can see it. "Anyone" lets the post appear for people outside your network; "Connections only" limits it to your first-degree connections. To check an existing post, open it, tap the three dots and look at the visibility options. If it was set to connections only, the low number may simply reflect a small audience.

### Check your profile and follow settings

If you want people outside your network to see your posts, make it easy for them to follow you. LinkedIn retired "creator mode" as a separate switch in 2024 (at the time of writing) and gave its main tools to all profiles, so look in your profile settings for options such as making **Follow** your primary button and keeping your public profile visible. A profile with a clear headline (up to 220 characters) also helps people decide to follow after they see a post.

### Look for restrictions

If your impressions fell to almost nothing overnight, check your LinkedIn notifications and email for any warning about your account or a specific post. Posts that LinkedIn believes break its Professional Community Policies can be limited or removed. Appeal through LinkedIn's Help Center if you think a decision is wrong.

## External links and LinkedIn post reach

Many LinkedIn users find that posts with an external link in the body get less reach than similar posts without one. LinkedIn hasn't confirmed a fixed penalty, so treat it as something to test.

How to test this on your own account:

1. Write the post so it stands on its own: the key idea, steps or takeaway should be in the post itself.
2. Publish one version with the link in the body and, on a different day, a similar post without the link.
3. Compare impressions after the same amount of time, for example 48 hours.

If the version without a link wins clearly, keep links for posts where clicks really matter, and make sure the post still delivers value to people who never click.

## Posting time and the first hour

A post that gets reactions and thoughtful comments soon after it's published has a better chance of being shown to more people. That's why timing matters on LinkedIn: if you post when your network is asleep or offline, that early signal is weak.

### Find when your network is active

There's no single best time for everyone. A recruiter whose network works office hours will see a different pattern from a founder with an international audience. Test a few windows that match your audience's working day, such as early morning, mid-morning and lunchtime in their time zone, and compare results across several weeks.

### Be around after you post

Plan to stay for a while after publishing. Reply to comments with real answers, not just "Thanks!" A genuine conversation in the comments tells LinkedIn the post is worth showing, and it gives the people who commented a reason to come back.

### Don't fake it

Engagement pods, where groups agree to like and comment on each other's posts, and automation tools that comment or react for you may break LinkedIn's User Agreement and can make engagement look less authentic. They're not worth the risk to your account.

## Hooks and the "see more" cut-off

On LinkedIn, the feed shows only the opening of a longer post before the "…more" link. Roughly the first 200 characters or three lines are visible, though the exact cut-off varies by device. If those lines don't earn a click, the rest of the post doesn't matter.

### Write a first line that earns the click

- **Lead with the point.** "We cut our onboarding time in half by removing one step" beats "I've been thinking a lot about onboarding lately."
- **Be specific.** Name the problem, the role or the result.
- **Create a reason to keep reading.** A clear tension, a surprising detail or the first step of a story.

## Formatting that keeps people reading

Once someone clicks "…more," make the post easy to scan.

- Use short paragraphs with white space between them.
- Use a short list when you're giving steps or options.
- Keep one idea per post. If you have three ideas, you may have three posts.
- End with a specific question people can actually answer, not "Thoughts?"

Visuals can help too. A clear image, a document post or a short native video often stops the scroll better than text alone. If you use images, size them properly so they aren't cropped awkwardly; our guide to [image sizes for LinkedIn posts](/blog/image-sizes-for-linkedin-posts) covers the dimensions.

## Hashtags: help or hurt?

Hashtags carry less weight on LinkedIn than they once did. They can still help describe what a post is about, but stuffing ten of them at the end adds clutter and can look spammy. Use a few specific ones that match the topic, or none at all, and put your effort into the first line instead.

## Spam signals that can quietly limit reach

Some habits make a post look low quality even when the content is good. If your LinkedIn impressions dropped, check whether any of these crept in:

- **Engagement bait.** Asking people to "comment YES" or "like if you agree" just to boost numbers. LinkedIn has said it aims to show this kind of post less.
- **Tagging lots of people** who aren't involved and don't respond.
- **Posting the same text repeatedly** or dropping identical comments on many posts.
- **Too many posts too close together**, so they compete with each other in your followers' feeds.
- **Third-party tools that log into your account** or automate likes, comments, invitations or messages.

Scheduling a post you wrote yourself is different from automating engagement. Tools that publish through LinkedIn's official connection simply post at the time you choose.

## How to increase LinkedIn impressions over time

1. **Pick two or three topics** you want to be known for, and stay close to them.
2. **Post consistently** rather than in bursts, at the times your testing points to.
3. **Comment thoughtfully on other people's posts** in your field. It builds relationships and puts your name in front of their audiences.
4. **Review your post analytics** every couple of weeks. Look at impressions, reactions, comments and reposts after the same time window, and notice which hooks and formats work best for you.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why are my LinkedIn impressions so low all of a sudden?

A sudden drop usually comes from a change on your side or a change in the feed. Check the post's visibility, any account notifications, and whether you recently added external links, started tagging many people or posted more often. If nothing changed, give it a few weeks of consistent posting before drawing conclusions.

### Does LinkedIn penalize posts with external links?

LinkedIn hasn't published a rule saying so, but many people see lower reach on posts with links in the body. Test it on your own account by comparing similar posts with and without a link, measured after the same amount of time.

### Does editing a LinkedIn post reduce impressions?

There's no official evidence that a quick edit hurts reach. Fixing a typo is fine. Still, it's better to proofread and preview before posting so you aren't making large changes while the post is gathering its first reactions.

### How many hashtags should I use on LinkedIn?

A few specific ones at most, or none. Hashtags matter less than they used to on LinkedIn, and a long list at the end of a post looks cluttered. Your first line has a much bigger effect on whether people stop and read.

### Do scheduled LinkedIn posts get fewer impressions?

There's no public evidence that posts published through LinkedIn's official tools or approved integrations are shown less. What matters is the content, the timing and whether you're around to reply to comments after the post goes live.
