You've written the perfect post, pasted a link, and now you're wondering whether anyone can actually click it. To add a link to a Facebook post, paste the full web address, starting with `https://`, into the post text. Facebook turns it into a clickable link automatically and usually builds a preview card from the page's title and image.

![How to add a clickable link to a Facebook post: paste the full URL, let the preview load, and fix previews on your website](/images/blog/add-links-to-facebook-post-hero-1200x675.webp)

The same works in Page posts, group posts, photo captions and comments. Stories are the exception: they use a link sticker instead of pasted text, and Reel captions generally don't make links clickable.

This guide covers where links work, the steps on desktop and the app, how to fix a preview that shows the wrong image, and when putting the link in the first comment is worth testing.

## Why Some Facebook Links Aren't Clickable (Where Links Work and Where They Don't)

![Places where Facebook links are clickable, like posts and comments, next to places they aren't, like Story text and Reel captions](/images/blog/add-links-to-facebook-post-where-1200x675.webp)

Facebook treats links differently depending on where you put them. This table shows the behavior at the time of writing. Meta changes its interface often, so button names in your app may differ slightly.

| Where | Is the link clickable? | How to add it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Profile post text | Yes | Paste the full URL into the post |
| Facebook Page post text | Yes | Paste the full URL into the post |
| Group post text | Yes (admins can restrict links) | Paste the full URL into the post |
| Photo or video post caption | Yes | Paste the URL into the post text |
| Comments and replies | Yes | Paste the URL into the comment |
| Stories | Only through a link sticker | Add the link sticker, if available |
| Reels captions | Generally not, at the time of writing | Point viewers to your Page or a comment |
| Page details (About section) | Yes | Add a website in the Page's details |

### What Most People Don't Know

Facebook doesn't support custom anchor text in regular posts. You can't turn the words "read the guide" into a hyperlink the way you can on a website. People see the URL itself, or the preview card, so a short, readable address like `yoursite.com/spring-sale` does more work than you'd think.

> **Key point:** If a link isn't clickable, the cause is almost always one of three things: a missing `https://`, a space or line break inside the URL, or a place that doesn't support links, such as Story text or a Reel caption.

## How to Add a Link to a Facebook Post (Desktop and App, Step by Step)

![Four steps to add a link to a Facebook post: paste the full URL, wait for the preview, add your own words, then post](/images/blog/add-links-to-facebook-post-steps-1200x675.webp)

The steps are nearly identical on a profile, a Page and a group. Menu labels change between app versions, so look for the closest match if something is worded differently.

### Step 1: Open the Composer

On desktop, go to your profile, Page or group and click the box that says something like "What's on your mind?" In the app, tap the same composer box at the top of your profile, Page or group.

### Step 2: Paste the Full URL, Including https://

Paste the complete address into the text area. On a phone, long-press in the text field and tap **Paste**. Including `https://` is the step people skip, and it's the one that makes Facebook recognize the text as a link.

### Step 3: Wait for the Preview Card

Give it a moment. A preview card with an image and headline usually appears below the text. If nothing appears, the link still works as long as it's typed correctly. The site may simply not provide preview details.

### Step 4: Decide What to Keep

- **Keep the preview, hide the raw URL:** delete the URL text after the preview has loaded. The card stays and remains clickable.
- **Keep the URL, drop the preview:** click the small "X" on the preview card to remove it. The URL stays in your text and remains clickable.

### Step 5: Add Your Own Words and Post

Write a line above or below the link that says why someone should click, then click or tap **Post**. The same steps work in Meta Business Suite if you manage a business Page.

> **Pro tip:** Write the reason to click before the link, not after it. "Full recipe and shopping list here:" followed by the URL tells people exactly what they get.

### Adding Links to Photo and Video Posts

When you attach a photo or video, Facebook usually drops the preview card, because the media takes its place. The URL in your text is still clickable. This is useful when you'd rather show your own image than the one the website provides. Write a short line such as "Full recipe here:" followed by the URL.

## How Facebook Link Previews Work (and How to Fix a Wrong One)

![Two lists comparing what controls a Facebook link preview, the website's sharing tags, with what doesn't, the post composer](/images/blog/add-links-to-facebook-post-preview-1200x675.webp)

The preview card isn't something you design inside Facebook. It's pulled from the page you're linking to, specifically from its Open Graph tags: the title, description and image the site has declared for sharing.

### Fixing an Outdated Headline or Image

If the preview shows the wrong image or an old headline, the fix belongs on the website, not in the post:

1. Make sure the page has an Open Graph title and image set. Most site builders and SEO plugins have a "social sharing" section for this.
2. Use an image that's roughly 1.91:1, such as 1200×630, so it isn't cropped awkwardly.
3. After updating the page, use Meta's Sharing Debugger (part of Meta's developer tools) to ask Facebook to fetch the page again. Facebook caches previews, so changes don't show until it re-scrapes.

Meta restricts editing a preview's headline and image directly in the composer for most accounts, which is why fixing the source page is the reliable approach.

### Link Problems and Fixes

| Problem | Fix | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Link shows as plain text | Paste again with `https://` and no spaces | Facebook only links text it recognizes as a full address |
| Preview shows an old image | Update the page's sharing image, then re-scrape | Facebook caches previews until it fetches the page again |
| Preview image is cropped | Use a 1.91:1 image such as 1200×630 | That's the shape the card displays |
| No preview at all | Check the page has Open Graph tags | Without them, Facebook has nothing to build a card from |
| Link broke after styling the text | Remove the bold or italic font from the URL | Styled characters aren't real letters, so the address no longer matches |

## How to Add a Link to a Facebook Story (Link Sticker Only)

Stories don't make pasted text clickable. Instead, Facebook offers a link sticker on many accounts, similar to the one on Instagram. Availability has varied by account type and region, so if you don't see it, your account may not have it yet.

1. Open the Facebook app and tap **Create story**.
2. Choose a photo or video, or start a text story.
3. Tap the sticker icon and look for a sticker labeled **Link**.
4. Paste your URL and tap **Done**.
5. Drag the sticker to a spot where it doesn't cover important parts of the image, then share the story.

If there's no link sticker, use the Story to tease the content and tell viewers where to find the link, for example "Link in our latest post."

## Adding Links in Comments (Without Looking Like Spam)

Links in comments and replies are clickable, just like in posts. Paste the full URL into the comment box and Facebook may show a small preview.

Two things to watch:

- **Spam filters.** Comments that are mostly links, or the same link repeated across many posts, can be hidden or flagged as spam. Post links in comments only where they genuinely help the conversation.
- **Group rules.** Many groups ban promotional links. Admins can decline posts and remove comments that break their rules.

## The First-Comment Link Strategy: Worth Testing, Not a Rule

![Checklist of when a first-comment link fits, like story-led posts, and when it doesn't, like sales with a deadline](/images/blog/add-links-to-facebook-post-first-comment-1200x675.webp)

Many social media managers put the link in the first comment instead of the post. The thinking is that platforms prefer content that keeps people on the platform, so a post without an outbound link might reach more people. Meta hasn't published a simple rule confirming this, so treat it as something to test rather than a law.

### How to Do It

1. Write the post as a complete thought that stands on its own, with an image or video.
2. End with a pointer such as "Link in the first comment."
3. Publish the post.
4. Straight away, add a comment from the same profile or Page with the URL and a short description.

### When It Makes Sense, and When It Doesn't

| Post type | Link in the post | Link in the first comment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sale or registration deadline | Better: the click is the whole point | Loses people to the extra step |
| Story-led post with a strong image | Fine | Worth testing |
| Blog article or recipe | Fine | Worth testing |
| Post in a group with link rules | Check the rules first | Check the rules first |

The honest way to decide is to compare. Run similar posts both ways over a few weeks and look at reach and link clicks in your Page insights. Our guide to [content performance analysis](/blog/content-performance-analysis) shows how to set up a fair comparison.

### Tracking Which Links Get Clicks

Whichever placement you choose, add UTM parameters so your analytics tool can tell Facebook traffic apart, for example `?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=spring-sale`. Use different `utm_content` values for "post" and "comment" if you're testing placements.

## Link Limits and Other Edge Cases to Know

- **Link-post limits on some profiles.** Meta has tested limiting how many link posts certain profiles in professional mode can share without a Meta Verified subscription. If you see a message about a limit, that's what it refers to. Pages have generally not been affected in the same way, but check any notice Facebook shows you.
- **Your Page's own links.** Add your website in your Page details and use the Page's action button (often labeled something like "Add action button") so visitors can always find your main link. If you haven't set up your Page yet, see [how to connect a Facebook Page](/blog/connect-facebook-page).
- **Styled text around links.** Unicode bold or italic text from our [Facebook font generator](/tools/facebook-font-generator) can make a call to action stand out, but never apply it to the URL itself, or the link breaks.

## What to Do Next: A Link Checklist Before You Post

Before you hit Post, run through this:

- ✓ The URL starts with `https://` and has no spaces or line breaks
- ✓ The preview shows the right image and headline (fix it on the site if not)
- ✓ There's a one-line reason to click before the link
- ✓ UTM tags are on if you want to measure clicks
- ✗ Don't apply fancy fonts to the URL itself
- ✗ Don't paste the same link into dozens of comments
- ✗ Don't expect links in Story text or Reel captions to work

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why isn't my link clickable on Facebook?

The usual cause is a missing `https://`, a space or line break inside the URL, or a place that doesn't support clickable links, such as a Reel caption or Story text. Paste the full address again and check nothing was cut off.

### Can I hyperlink text in a Facebook post?

No, not in regular profile, Page or group posts. Facebook shows the URL itself or a preview card, but it doesn't let you hide a link behind custom words. Use a short URL or delete the URL text and let the preview card do the work.

### How do I remove the link preview but keep the link?

Click the small "X" on the preview card after it loads in the composer. The URL stays in your text and remains clickable. The button's position can vary between desktop and the app.

### How do I keep the preview but hide the URL?

Delete the URL text once the preview card has loaded. The card stays attached to the post and is still clickable.

### How do I change the image on a Facebook link preview?

Change the sharing image on the web page itself, then ask Facebook to re-fetch it with Meta's Sharing Debugger. Facebook caches previews, so the old image stays until it re-scrapes the page.

### Is it better to put the link in the first comment?

It depends on the post. Many marketers find it helps posts that are interesting on their own, but Meta hasn't confirmed it as a rule. Test both placements over several weeks and compare reach and clicks in your insights.

### Can I add a link to a Facebook Story?

Yes, on accounts that have the link sticker. Create a story, open the stickers menu, choose **Link** and paste your URL. If you don't see it, the sticker may not be available for your account or region yet.

### Can I put a clickable link in a Facebook Reel?

Generally not in the caption, at the time of writing. Point viewers to your Page, a pinned post or a comment instead.
