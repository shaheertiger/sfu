To add a link to a Facebook post, paste the full web address (starting with `https://`) into the text of your post. Facebook turns it into a clickable link automatically and usually builds a preview card from the page's title and image. The same works in Page posts, group posts, photo captions and comments, while Stories use a separate link sticker.

## Where links work on Facebook at a glance

Facebook treats links differently depending on where you put them. This table summarizes the behavior at the time of writing. Meta changes its interface often, so button names in your app may differ slightly.

| Where | Is the link clickable? | How to add it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Profile post text | Yes | Paste the full URL into the post |
| Facebook Page post text | Yes | Paste the full URL into the post |
| Group post text | Yes (admins can restrict links) | Paste the full URL into the post |
| Photo or video post caption | Yes | Paste the URL into the post text |
| Comments and replies | Yes | Paste the URL into the comment |
| Stories | Only through a link sticker | Add the link sticker, if available |
| Reels captions | Generally not, at the time of writing | Point viewers to your Page or a comment |
| Page details (About section) | Yes | Add a website in the Page's details |

Facebook doesn't support custom anchor text in regular posts, so you can't turn the words "read the guide" into a hyperlink. People see the URL itself, so keep it short and readable, like `yoursite.com/spring-sale`.

## How to add a link to a Facebook post

### On desktop

1. Go to your profile, Page or group and click the box that says something like "What's on your mind?"
2. Paste the full URL into the text area. Include `https://` so Facebook recognizes it as a link.
3. Wait a moment. A preview card with an image and headline usually appears below the text.
4. Add your own text above or below the link explaining why someone should click.
5. If you want to keep the preview but hide the raw URL, delete the URL text after the preview has loaded. The preview card stays and remains clickable.
6. If you want the URL without a preview, look for a small "X" on the preview card and click it to remove the card.
7. Click **Post**.

### On the Facebook app (iPhone and Android)

1. Tap the post composer on your profile, Page or group.
2. Long-press in the text field, tap **Paste**, and wait for the preview to load.
3. Add your text and tap **Post**.

The same steps work in Meta Business Suite if you manage a business Page.

### Adding links to photo and video posts

When you attach a photo or video, Facebook usually drops the link preview card, because the media takes its place. The URL in your text is still clickable. This is a useful option when you'd rather show your own image than the image the website provides. Write a short line such as "Full recipe here:" followed by the URL.

## How Facebook link previews work

The preview card isn't something you design inside Facebook. It's pulled from the web page you're linking to, specifically from its Open Graph tags: the page title, description and image the site has declared for sharing.

### Fixing a wrong or outdated preview

If the preview shows the wrong image or an old headline, the fix belongs on the website, not in the post:

- Make sure the page has an Open Graph title and image set. Most site builders and SEO plugins have a "social sharing" section for this.
- Use an image that's roughly 1.91:1, such as 1200×630, so it isn't cropped awkwardly.
- After updating the page, use Meta's Sharing Debugger (part of Meta's developer tools) to ask Facebook to fetch the page again. Facebook caches previews, so changes don't appear until it re-scrapes.

Meta restricts editing the headline and image of link previews directly in the composer for most accounts, which is why fixing the source page is the reliable approach.

## How to add a link to a Facebook Story

Stories don't make pasted text clickable. Instead, Facebook offers a link sticker on many accounts, similar to the one on Instagram. Availability has varied by account type and region, so if you don't see it, your account may not have it yet.

1. Open the Facebook app and tap **Create story**.
2. Choose a photo or video, or start a text story.
3. Tap the sticker icon and look for a sticker labeled **Link**.
4. Paste your URL and tap **Done**.
5. Drag the sticker to a spot where it doesn't cover important parts of the image, then share the story.

If there's no link sticker, use the Story to tease the content and tell viewers where to find the link, for example "Link in our latest post."

## Adding links in comments

Links in comments and replies are clickable, just like in posts. Paste the full URL into the comment box and Facebook may show a small preview.

A few things to watch:

- **Spam filters.** Comments that are mostly links, or the same link repeated across many posts, can be hidden or flagged as spam. Post links in comments only where they genuinely help the conversation.
- **Group rules.** Many groups ban promotional links. Admins can decline posts and remove comments that break their rules.

## The first-comment link strategy

Many social media managers put the link in the first comment instead of the post itself. The thinking is that platforms prefer content that keeps people on the platform, so a post without an outbound link might be shown to more people. Meta hasn't published a simple rule confirming this, so treat it as something to test rather than a law.

### How to do it

1. Write the post as a complete thought that stands on its own, with an image or video.
2. End with a pointer such as "Link in the first comment."
3. Publish the post.
4. Immediately add a comment from the same profile or Page containing the URL and a short description.

### When it makes sense, and when it doesn't

The first-comment approach can work well for posts where the image and text carry the story and the link is a bonus. It works less well when the click is the whole point, such as a sale or registration deadline. In those cases, an extra step loses some people, and a clear link in the post is usually better.

The honest way to decide is to compare: run similar posts both ways over a few weeks and look at reach and link clicks in your Page insights. Our guide to [content performance analysis](/blog/content-performance-analysis) walks through how to set up a fair comparison.

### Tracking which links get clicks

Whichever placement you choose, add UTM parameters to your URLs so your analytics tool can tell Facebook traffic apart, for example `?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=spring-sale`. Use different `utm_content` values for "post" and "comment" if you're testing placements.

## Link limits and other things to know

- **Link-post limits on some profiles.** Meta has tested limiting how many link posts certain profiles in professional mode can share without a Meta Verified subscription. If you see a message about a limit, that's what it refers to. Pages have generally not been affected in the same way, but check any notice Facebook shows you.
- **Your Page's own links.** Add your website in your Page details and use the Page's action button (often labeled something like "Add action button") so visitors can always find your main link without scrolling through posts. If you haven't set up your Page yet, see [how to connect a Facebook Page](/blog/connect-facebook-page).
- **Styled text around links.** Unicode bold or italic text from a tool like our [Facebook font generator](/tools/facebook-font-generator) can make a call to action stand out, but never apply it to the URL itself. Styled characters aren't real letters, so the link would break.

If you publish link posts to a Facebook Page on a regular schedule, a scheduler such as Schedule For You lets you write the post once, adjust the caption per platform, and queue it on a calendar alongside your other channels. Link previews still come from your website's Open Graph tags, so set those up first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why isn't my link clickable on Facebook?

The most common causes are a missing `https://`, a space or line break inside the URL, or placing the link somewhere that doesn't support clickable links, such as a Reel caption or Story text. Paste the full address again and check that nothing was cut off.

### Can I hyperlink text in a Facebook post?

Not in regular profile, Page or group posts. Facebook shows the URL itself or a preview card, but it doesn't let you hide a link behind custom words. Use a short, readable URL or remove the URL text and let the preview card do the work.

### How do I remove the link preview but keep the link?

After the preview loads in the composer, click the small "X" on the preview card. The URL stays in your text and remains clickable. The exact position of the button can vary between desktop and the app.

### Is it better to put the link in the first comment?

It depends on the post. Many marketers find it helps posts that are interesting on their own, but Meta hasn't confirmed it as a rule. Test both placements over several weeks and compare reach and clicks in your insights.

### Can I add a link to a Facebook Story?

Yes, on accounts that have the link sticker. Create a story, open the stickers menu, choose **Link**, and paste your URL. If you don't see the sticker, it may not be available for your account or region yet.
