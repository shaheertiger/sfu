Bluesky makes very little money today. It runs mainly on **venture funding**, about $123 million disclosed so far, plus small amounts from selling **custom domains** through a registrar partnership. Its stated plans are an **optional paid subscription** with extras such as higher-quality video and profile customization, and paid services built on its open network, rather than an ad-driven feed. Bluesky is **not publicly traded**: it's a private company, so there's no Bluesky stock or ticker to buy.

## Who owns Bluesky

Bluesky Social, PBC is a US **public benefit corporation**, a for-profit company whose charter also commits it to a public mission (in Bluesky's case, an open, decentralized social web). It started in 2019 as a project funded by Twitter and became independent in 2021. Jay Graber led it as CEO from the start; in early 2026 she moved to a Chief Innovation Officer role and Toni Schneider became interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent one. Jack Dorsey, who started the project at Twitter, left Bluesky's board in 2024.

## Where Bluesky's money comes from now

- **Investor funding.** Most of the budget: staff, servers, moderation.
- **Custom domains.** Bluesky has sold domains through a partnership with Namecheap so people can use a handle like `@yourname.com`.
- **Nothing from ads.** There's no ad platform at the time of writing. Bluesky has said it won't build its business on an engagement-maximizing, ad-driven feed, though leaders haven't ruled ads out entirely.

## What Bluesky plans to sell

- **A subscription.** In 2024 Bluesky said it was developing an optional paid tier, with features like higher-quality video uploads and profile customization, and said subscribing wouldn't boost your reach. Mockups circulated showing a "Bluesky+" plan, but Bluesky said the details weren't final, and at the time of writing it hasn't launched.
- **Services on the open network.** Because anyone can build feeds, moderation services and apps on the AT Protocol, Bluesky has talked about a marketplace for paid feeds and services, and about payments for creators.
- **Developer and business services.** Hosting, tools and infrastructure for companies building on the protocol.

None of this changes how you use Bluesky today: it's free, with no ads and no paid reach. For brands, that means growth is organic, so consistency matters more than budget. ## Is Bluesky publicly traded?

No. Bluesky Social, PBC is privately held by its founders, staff and investors. It has no stock ticker and no announced IPO. Watch out for:

- **Crypto tokens** using the Bluesky name. Bluesky has no token or coin.
- **Similarly named companies.** Other businesses called "Blue Sky" or "Bluesky" (in software, energy and finance) are unrelated.
- **"Pre-IPO" share offers.** Unsolicited offers to sell Bluesky shares are a common scam pattern.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I buy Bluesky stock?
No. Bluesky is a private company and isn't listed on any stock exchange. Any coin or token using the Bluesky name isn't from Bluesky.

### Does Bluesky have ads?
Not at the time of writing. All reach on Bluesky is organic.

### Will Bluesky charge for accounts?
Bluesky has said the core app stays free and any subscription would be optional, adding extras rather than locking basic features.

### Is Bluesky owned by Jack Dorsey?
No. Dorsey started the project at Twitter in 2019 but left Bluesky's board in 2024. Bluesky is an independent public benefit corporation owned by its team and investors.
