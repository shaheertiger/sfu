How much does FB pay for 1,000 views? There's no fixed amount. Eligible creators earn through Meta's monetization programs, which pay based on how content performs and on the ads shown with it, and the amount per 1,000 views varies widely by country, audience, content type and engagement. The only reliable number is your own: once you're enrolled, your Professional Dashboard shows what your Reels and videos have actually earned.

Here's how Facebook's creator payouts work in general terms, what makes them go up or down, and some clearly hypothetical examples so you can do the math with your own figures.

| Question | Short answer |
| --- | --- |

Meta updates its programs, names and requirements fairly often. For the current rules where you live, check the Monetization section of your Professional Dashboard and the [Facebook Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/help).

## How Facebook pays creators

Meta has several ways to pay creators on Facebook. Which ones you can use depends on your country, your account and whether you meet the program's requirements.

### Performance-based payouts

Meta has been moving toward a single, performance-based program for creators. In general terms, eligible creators are paid based on how their content performs, such as views and engagement, across formats like Reels, longer videos, photos and text posts. Access has often been by invitation or application, and the details differ by region.

### In-stream ads

Eligible creators can earn a share of revenue from ads shown in their videos. Ads can play before, during or after a video, or appear as image or banner ads alongside it, depending on the format. Earnings depend on how many ads are shown and how much advertisers pay for them.

### Ads on Reels

Facebook has also offered ad revenue for Reels, with ads appearing alongside or within Reels for eligible creators. In some regions this has been folded into the broader performance-based program.

### Fan support

Beyond ad and performance payouts, eligible creators can earn directly from fans:

- **Stars:** viewers buy Stars and send them during live videos, on Reels or on other content.
- **Subscriptions:** fans pay monthly for perks such as badges and exclusive content.

### Brand partnerships

Brands pay creators for sponsored content. Facebook requires branded content to be tagged with the paid partnership label, and the price is negotiated between you and the brand.

## What affects how much Facebook pays per 1,000 views

Even with the same number of views, payouts can be very different. These are the main reasons:

- **Audience location.** Advertisers pay more to reach some countries than others, so the same view can be worth more or less depending on where the viewer lives.
- **Niche.** Topics that attract high-value advertisers, such as finance or technology, can earn more than topics with less advertiser demand.
- **Season.** Ad budgets often rise toward the end of the year and dip at the start, which moves payouts up and down.
- **Engagement and watch time.** Performance-based programs reward content people actually watch and interact with.
- **Originality.** Meta has said it prioritizes original content and can limit monetization for accounts that mostly repost others' material.
- **Monetized vs. total views.** Not every view earns. Views on content that isn't eligible, from countries where the program doesn't pay, or with no ad shown, add to your view count but not your earnings.

## Hypothetical Facebook Reels payout examples

The numbers below are **hypothetical**, chosen only to show the math. They are not averages, typical rates or predictions for your account.

The basic formula is:

**Earnings ≈ (monetized views ÷ 1,000) × your effective rate per 1,000 monetized views**

### Example 1: 1,000 monetized views

If your effective rate were $1.00 per 1,000 monetized views, 1,000 monetized views would earn about **$1.00**.

### Example 2: 100,000 total views, partly monetized

Suppose a Reel gets 100,000 views, but only 40,000 are monetized (the rest came from non-eligible regions or had no ad). If your effective rate were $1.50:

- 40,000 ÷ 1,000 = 40
- 40 × $1.50 = **$60**

### Example 3: the same views, a different audience

Same Reel, same 40,000 monetized views, but most of the audience is in markets where advertisers pay less. If your effective rate were $0.40:

- 40 × $0.40 = **$16**

### Calculate your own rate

1. Open your Page or professional profile and go to **Professional Dashboard**.
2. Open the **Monetization** section and select a date range.
3. Note your earnings for a specific Reel or for the period, and the views shown for the same content.
4. Divide earnings by (views ÷ 1,000) to get your effective rate.
5. Do this for several Reels. Your range across many posts tells you far more than any single figure you'll see online.

## How to check if you're eligible

1. Use a Facebook Page, or switch your personal profile to **professional mode**. Monetization tools live in the Professional Dashboard.
2. Open **Professional Dashboard** and select **Monetization** (sometimes shown as an overview of monetization tools).
3. Review each program listed. Facebook shows whether you're eligible, which requirements you meet, and what's still missing.
4. Check that your account follows Meta's Partner Monetization Policies and Content Monetization Policies. Violations can make you ineligible even if you meet the other requirements.
5. Set up payouts by adding your payout account and tax information when prompted.

If the dashboard says monetization isn't available, it's usually because of your country, the age or standing of your account, or the type of content you post. Meta doesn't always publish fixed thresholds for every program, so rely on what the dashboard shows for your account.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does Facebook pay for 1,000 views on Reels?

There's no fixed rate. Payouts depend on the program you're in, how many of your views are monetized, your audience's location, your niche and engagement. Once you're enrolled, your Professional Dashboard shows your actual earnings so you can work out your own rate.

### How much does Facebook pay for 1 million views?

There's no set figure, for the same reasons. Work it out from monetized views, not total views. As a hypothetical example, if 400,000 of a million views were monetized and your effective rate were $1.00 per 1,000, you'd earn about $400.

### How many followers do you need to get paid on Facebook?

It depends on the program and your country, and Meta changes requirements over time. Open Professional Dashboard → Monetization to see which programs you're eligible for and which requirements you still need to meet.

### Does Facebook pay for Reels on personal profiles?

Eligible creators can monetize from a Page or from a personal profile in professional mode, where available. A regular personal profile without professional mode doesn't have access to the monetization tools.

### Why are my Facebook earnings so low compared to my views?

Usually because only some views are monetized. Views from regions where the program doesn't pay, on content that isn't eligible, or with no ad shown count toward your view total but don't earn. Audience location and niche also affect how much each monetized view is worth.

### When does Facebook pay creators?

Meta pays out on a regular schedule once your earnings reach the minimum payout threshold for your payout method and your payout and tax details are set up. The Monetization section of your Professional Dashboard shows your payout settings and history.
