Your Reel just passed 100,000 views and you want to know what that's worth. How much does Facebook pay for 1,000 views? There's no fixed amount. Eligible creators earn through Meta's monetization programs, which pay based on how content performs and on the ads shown with it, so the rate per 1,000 views varies widely by country, audience, niche and engagement.

![Facebook Reels pay per 1,000 views: no fixed rate, so work out your own from monetized views in the Professional Dashboard](/images/blog/how-much-does-facebook-reels-pay-per-1000-views-hero-1200x675.webp)

The only reliable number is your own: once you're enrolled, your Professional Dashboard shows what your Reels and videos actually earned. Any single "Facebook pays $X per 1K" figure online is someone else's account.

This guide explains how Facebook pays creators, why total views overstate earnings, the formula to estimate yours, and how to check eligibility.

## How Does Facebook Pay Creators for Reels? (5 Ways to Earn)

![Five ways Facebook creators earn: performance payouts, in-stream ads, ads on Reels, Stars and subscriptions, brand deals](/images/blog/how-much-does-facebook-reels-pay-per-1000-views-ways-1200x675.webp)

Meta has several ways to pay creators. Which ones you can use depends on your country, your account and whether you meet each program's requirements.

| Question | Short answer |
| --- | --- |
| Is there a set rate per 1,000 views? | No. Payouts depend on performance, ads and audience |
| Who gets paid? | Eligible creators and Pages enrolled in Meta's monetization programs |
| What content can earn? | Depending on the program, Reels, longer videos and other posts |
| Where do I check eligibility? | Professional Dashboard → Monetization |
| Where do I see earnings? | The same Monetization section, once you're enrolled |
| Does it vary by country? | Yes. Both eligibility and payouts do |

### 1. Performance-Based Payouts

Meta has been moving toward a single, performance-based program. In general terms, eligible creators are paid based on how their content performs, such as views and engagement, across Reels, longer videos, photos and text posts. Access has often been by invitation or application, and details differ by region.

### 2. In-Stream Ads

Eligible creators can earn a share of revenue from ads in their videos. Ads can play before, during or after a video, or appear as image or banner ads alongside it. Earnings depend on how many ads are shown and what advertisers pay.

### 3. Ads on Reels

Facebook has also offered ad revenue for Reels, with ads alongside or within Reels for eligible creators. In some regions this has been folded into the broader performance-based program.

### 4. Fan Support: Stars and Subscriptions

- **Stars:** viewers buy Stars and send them during live videos, on Reels or on other content.
- **Subscriptions:** fans pay monthly for perks such as badges and exclusive content.

### 5. Brand Partnerships

Brands pay creators for sponsored content. Facebook requires branded content to carry the paid partnership label, and the price is between you and the brand.

### What Most People Don't Know

Not every view earns. Views on content that isn't eligible, from countries where the program doesn't pay, or with no ad shown, add to your view count but not your earnings. That gap between total views and **monetized views** is why two Reels with the same view count can pay very different amounts.

> **Key point:** Always do the math on monetized views, not total views. Total views flatter the number; monetized views pay the bills.

Meta updates its programs, names and requirements often. For the current rules where you live, check the Monetization section of your Professional Dashboard and the [Facebook Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/help).

## How to Calculate Your Own Facebook Rate per 1,000 Views (Step by Step)

![Four steps to calculate your Facebook rate per 1,000 views: open Monetization, note earnings, note views, divide](/images/blog/how-much-does-facebook-reels-pay-per-1000-views-calculate-1200x675.webp)

The basic formula is:

**Earnings ≈ (monetized views ÷ 1,000) × your effective rate per 1,000 monetized views**

Menu labels change between app versions, so look for the closest match.

### Step 1: Open Your Professional Dashboard

Open your Page or professional profile and go to **Professional Dashboard**.

### Step 2: Open Monetization and Pick a Date Range

Select the **Monetization** section and choose a date range.

### Step 3: Note Earnings and Views for the Same Content

Write down the earnings for a specific Reel, or for the whole period, and the views shown for the same content.

### Step 4: Divide Earnings by Views ÷ 1,000

That's your effective rate. For example, if a Reel earned $12 on 8,000 views, your rate is $12 ÷ 8 = $1.50 per 1,000 for that Reel.

### Step 5: Repeat Across Several Reels

> **Pro tip:** Work out the rate for at least five or ten Reels, then look at the range. One number tells you little; your range across many posts tells you what to expect next month.

## What Affects How Much Facebook Pays per 1,000 Views? (6 Factors)

### 1. Where Is Your Audience?

Advertisers pay more to reach some countries than others, so the same view can be worth more or less depending on where the viewer lives.

### 2. Which Niche Are You In?

Topics that attract high-value advertisers, such as finance or technology, can earn more than topics with less advertiser demand.

### 3. What Time of Year Is It?

Ad budgets often rise toward the end of the year and dip at the start, which moves payouts up and down.

### 4. Do People Actually Watch?

Performance-based programs reward content people watch and interact with.

### 5. Is the Content Original?

Meta has said it prioritizes original content and can limit monetization for accounts that mostly repost others' material.

### 6. How Many Views Are Monetized?

Views from non-eligible regions, on ineligible content or with no ad shown don't earn.

## How to Check If You're Eligible for Facebook Monetization

1. Use a Facebook Page, or switch your personal profile to **professional mode**. Monetization tools live in the Professional Dashboard.
2. Open **Professional Dashboard** and select **Monetization** (sometimes an overview of monetization tools).
3. Review each program. Facebook shows whether you're eligible, which requirements you meet and what's missing.
4. Check that your account follows Meta's Partner Monetization Policies and Content Monetization Policies. Violations can make you ineligible even if you meet everything else.
5. Add your payout account and tax information when prompted.

If the dashboard says monetization isn't available, it's usually because of your country, your account's age or standing, or the type of content you post. Meta doesn't always publish fixed thresholds, so rely on what the dashboard shows for your account.

## Reposting Other People's Reels to Grow Fast (Use With Caution)

Some Pages grow by reposting viral clips from other creators. It can pull views quickly, which is why people try it.

### The Real Risks

- **Limited monetization.** Meta can limit monetization for accounts that mostly repost others' material.
- **Limited reach.** Reposted content is a common reason for reduced distribution.
- **Copyright claims.** Using someone's video without permission can get it removed, and repeated infringement can lead to restrictions.

### A Safer Version

- Film your own footage, or add your own voice and perspective.
- Get permission and credit the creator when you feature their clip.
- Share the original rather than re-uploading it.

> **Bottom line:** Views from reposted clips are the easiest to get and the least likely to pay.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does Facebook pay for 1,000 views on Reels?

There's no fixed rate. Payouts depend on the program, how many views are monetized, your audience's location, your niche and engagement. Your Professional Dashboard shows your actual earnings so you can work out your rate.

### How much does Facebook pay for 1 million views?

There's no set figure. Work it out from monetized views: as a hypothetical example, if 400,000 of a million views were monetized at an effective $1.00 per 1,000, you'd earn about $400.

### How many followers do you need to get paid on Facebook?

It depends on the program and your country, and Meta changes requirements over time. Professional Dashboard → Monetization shows what you're eligible for and what's still missing.

### Does Facebook pay for Reels on personal profiles?

Eligible creators can monetize from a Page or from a personal profile in professional mode, where available. A regular profile without professional mode doesn't have the monetization tools.

### Why are my Facebook earnings so low compared to my views?

Usually because only some views are monetized. Views from non-paying regions, on ineligible content or with no ad shown count toward your total but don't earn.

### Does reposting other people's videos earn money on Facebook?

It can limit your monetization. Meta has said it prioritizes original content and can limit accounts that mostly repost others' material.

### When does Facebook pay creators?

On a regular schedule, once your earnings reach the minimum payout threshold for your payout method and your payout and tax details are set up. Your Professional Dashboard shows payout settings and history.

### Is Facebook or YouTube better for earnings?

There's no universal answer, because neither pays a fixed rate. Compare your own effective rate on each platform over the same months.
