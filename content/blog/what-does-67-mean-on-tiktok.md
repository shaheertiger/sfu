Your kid, your students or half the comments on TikTok keep shouting "six seven", and you can't work out what it means. "67" on TikTok, said **"six seven"** and never "sixty-seven", is a meme phrase with no fixed meaning. People say it, comment it or shout it with a hand gesture, palms up and moving like a scale, whenever the numbers 6 and 7 come up, or just at random.

![What does 67 mean on TikTok? Said six seven, it's a nonsense meme with a see-saw hand gesture](/images/blog/what-does-67-mean-on-tiktok-hero-1200x675.webp)

Loosely it means "so-so" or "maybe this, maybe that", but mostly it's an in-joke: saying it shows you're in on the meme. It took off in 2025, mainly among Gen Alpha and younger teens, and Dictionary.com named it its **2025 Word of the Year**.

This guide covers where "6 7" came from, the ways people use it, whether it's still a trend, and how brands can use it without getting mocked.

## Why "67" Has No Real Meaning (and Why That's the Point)

There isn't a dictionary-style definition, and that's the joke. Dictionary.com called it effectively impossible to define. It spread because saying it is a signal, not a message: you're part of the group that gets it, and adults who ask what it means are part of the fun.

![What people think 67 means compared with what it actually is](/images/blog/what-does-67-mean-on-tiktok-myth-vs-reality-1200x675.webp)

| What people worry it means | What it actually is |
|---|---|
| A secret code | A nonsense phrase from a song |
| Something explicit or dangerous | Nothing hidden at all |
| "Sixty-seven" of something | Two numbers, said separately |

### What Most People Don't Know

Saying "sixty-seven" misses the joke entirely. It's always "six seven", two separate numbers, and it usually comes with the hand gesture. Get the pronunciation wrong and you've shown you're outside the meme, which is exactly what the meme is for.

> **Key point:** 67 isn't a code for anything explicit or dangerous. Parents worried about hidden meanings can relax: it's nonsense on purpose.

## Where Did "6 7" Come From? 4 Steps From Song to Classroom

![How six seven spread, from a 2024 song to basketball edits to classrooms](/images/blog/what-does-67-mean-on-tiktok-origin-1200x675.webp)

### 1. The Song (2024)

The phrase comes from **"Doot Doot (6 7)"**, a 2024 drill track by Philadelphia rapper **Skrilla**. The "6 7" is often read as a nod to 67th Street in Philadelphia, though Skrilla has given vaguer answers about what it means.

### 2. The Basketball Edits (Early 2025)

The song was used in TikTok and Instagram Reels edits of NBA player **LaMelo Ball**, who is 6 feet 7 inches tall, so "six seven" became a height joke.

### 3. The Gesture

Basketball player and creator **Taylen "TK" Kinney** was filmed answering "six seven" with the see-saw hand motion. Clips of him, and of a young fan shouting it at a game (often called "67 kid"), spread widely.

### 4. Schools (Late 2025)

By late 2025, teachers were posting about classrooms erupting every time a lesson reached page 67 or the number 6 or 7.

### The 67 Hand Gesture

Both palms up, moving up and down alternately like a scale weighing two things. It goes with saying "six seven", and it's half the meme.

> **Pro tip:** If you're a teacher or parent, the fastest way to end it is to not react. The meme feeds on adults being confused or annoyed.

## Is 67 Still a Trend in 2026?

It peaked in late 2025, and like most memes it's now used more ironically, or to tease people who still say it. It still comes up in comments and school content, but on its own it won't carry a video the way it did at its peak.

| Stage | How 67 is used |
|---|---|
| Rise (early to mid 2025) | Basketball edits, sincere in-joke |
| Peak (late 2025) | Classrooms, Word of the Year, everywhere |
| Now | Ironic, teasing, nostalgic |

## Should Brands Use 67? 3 Rules

Only if it fits naturally and your audience is young. Brands that jumped on it late were often mocked for it.

![When it works for a brand to use the 67 meme, and when it backfires](/images/blog/what-does-67-mean-on-tiktok-brands-1200x675.webp)

- ✓ **Use it as a reply, not a campaign.** A quick "6 7" comment on a relevant video lands better than a planned ad.
- ✓ **Stay light.** The meme is about absurdity, so don't explain it in your caption.
- ✗ **Don't arrive late.** If your audience has moved on, it reads as cringe.
