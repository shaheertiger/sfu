Your channel page says 12.3K, a friend says they subscribed yesterday, and you can't find their name anywhere. To see your YouTube subscribers, open **YouTube Studio**. The **Dashboard** shows your current subscriber count and a **Recent subscribers** card; click **See all** to view the list. For growth over time, go to **Analytics** and check the subscriber figures in the Overview and Audience tabs.

![How to see your YouTube subscribers: exact count on the Dashboard, names under Recent subscribers, growth in Analytics](/images/blog/how-to-see-your-youtube-subscribers-hero-1200x675.webp)

One important limit: the list only shows people whose subscriptions are public. Subscribers who keep their subscriptions private are counted in your total, but YouTube never shows you their names.

This guide covers where each subscriber stat lives, how to see your count and the list, how to track gains and losses, and how to turn the numbers into more subscribers.

## Why You Can't See Every Subscriber (Privacy Comes First)

YouTube lets every viewer decide whether their subscriptions are public. If they're private, you get the number but not the name. That's why the Recent subscribers list is almost always shorter than your total.

![Subscribers you can see in YouTube Studio compared with subscribers who are counted but hidden](/images/blog/how-to-see-your-youtube-subscribers-hidden-1200x675.webp)

| What you want | Where to find it |
| --- | --- |
| Exact subscriber count | YouTube Studio → Dashboard (or Analytics → Overview) |
| Who subscribed recently | Dashboard → Recent subscribers → See all |
| Subscribers gained and lost | Analytics → Advanced mode → Subscribers metric |
| Which videos brought subscribers | Analytics → Advanced mode, grouped by content |
| Count on your phone | YouTube Studio app → Dashboard |
| Channels you subscribe to | YouTube → Subscriptions (or Your channel → Subscriptions) |

### What Most People Don't Know

YouTube regularly removes spam accounts and closed accounts from subscriber totals. So your count can drop slightly without anyone actively unsubscribing. And you can never see who unsubscribed: Analytics shows how many you lost, and when, but not their names.

> **Key point:** Your exact count is in YouTube Studio, not on your channel page. The names you see are only the public part of your audience, so use the list for saying thanks and Analytics for decisions.

## How to See Your YouTube Subscriber Count (Computer and Phone)

Menu names in YouTube Studio change between versions, so if a label doesn't match, look for the closest one.

![Steps to see your exact YouTube subscriber count in YouTube Studio](/images/blog/how-to-see-your-youtube-subscribers-count-1200x675.webp)

### On a Computer: 3 Steps

1. Go to studio.youtube.com and sign in to your channel.
2. The **Channel dashboard** opens. The **Channel analytics** card shows your current subscriber count and the change over the last 28 days.
3. For more detail, click **Analytics** in the left menu. The **Overview** tab includes subscribers for the selected date range.

The count in YouTube Studio is exact. The number visitors see on your channel page is rounded once you pass 1,000 subscribers (for example, 12,345 appears as 12.3K).

### On Your Phone: 3 Steps

1. Install and open the **YouTube Studio** app.
2. Sign in with the account that owns or manages the channel.
3. The dashboard shows your subscriber count; tap **Analytics** for trends.

In the main YouTube app, you can also tap your profile picture and go to your channel to see the public, rounded count.

## How to See Who Subscribed to Your Channel (5 Steps)

### Step 1: Open YouTube Studio on a Computer

The full list is easiest to view on desktop.

### Step 2: Find the Recent Subscribers Card on the Dashboard

### Step 3: Click See All

### Step 4: Change the Time Range, Not Just the Default

This is the step people miss. The list only covers the period you select, so a subscriber from two months ago won't appear in a 28-day view. Switch between ranges such as the last 7, 28 or 90 days, or longer, depending on what YouTube offers at the time.

### Step 5: Sort the List

For example, sort by the subscriber's own subscriber count to spot other creators who follow you. Each entry links to the subscriber's channel, so you can visit it or say thanks.

> **Pro tip:** Sorting by subscriber count once a month is a quick way to find creators in your niche who already watch you. They're the easiest people to approach about a collaboration.

| Reason | What's happening |

| Spam and closed accounts | YouTube removes them from totals, so they disappear |

## How to Track Subscriber Growth in Analytics (Gained vs Lost)

The subscriber list tells you who; Analytics tells you why your count is changing.

![How to track subscribers gained and lost in YouTube Analytics, step by step](/images/blog/how-to-see-your-youtube-subscribers-analytics-1200x675.webp)

1. In YouTube Studio, click **Analytics**.
2. Choose a date range in the top right, for example the last 28 days or the last 90 days.
3. Click **Advanced mode**.
4. Open the metric menu and choose **Subscribers**. Add **Subscribers gained** and **Subscribers lost** to see both sides.
5. Group by **Content** to see which videos and Shorts brought new subscribers, or by **Traffic source** to see where those viewers found you.

### What to Look For

- **Videos that convert well.** Some videos bring far more subscribers per view than others. Those topics are worth repeating.
- **Spikes in losses.** A sudden rise in unsubscribes after a particular upload can tell you a video didn't match what your audience expected.
- **Shorts vs long-form.** Compare how many subscribers each format brings, not just views.

## Why Your Subscriber Count Looks Different in Different Places

| Where | What you see |
| --- | --- |
| YouTube Studio | Exact count |
| Your public channel page | Rounded once you pass 1,000 |
| Analytics for a date range | Net change (gained minus lost), not your total |
| Third-party sites | Usually based on the public, rounded figure |

Counts can also lag slightly as YouTube processes activity and removes spam accounts.

## How to Hide or Show Your Subscriber Count

You can choose whether visitors see your subscriber count. At the time of writing, the option is in YouTube Studio under **Settings → Channel → Advanced settings**, as a checkbox to display the number of subscribers. Even if you hide it, you can still see the exact number in YouTube Studio.

Hiding your count doesn't affect eligibility for features that require a certain number of subscribers, such as live streaming from your phone. See [how to live stream on YouTube without 1,000 subscribers](/blog/how-to-live-stream-on-youtube-without-1000-subscribers) for what you can do while your channel is small.

### How to See the Channels You're Subscribed To

That's a different question, but it often gets mixed up with this one:

- **On a computer:** click **Subscriptions** in the YouTube sidebar, then **Manage** to see every channel you follow.
- **On a phone:** tap **Subscriptions**, then **All**.
- **Make your own subscriptions private:** go to youtube.com, open **Settings → Privacy** and turn on the option to keep all your subscriptions private.

## Live Sub Counters, Sub4Sub and "Who Unsubscribed" Tools (Use With Caution)

Search around and you'll find live subscriber counter sites, sub-for-sub groups and tools that promise to show who unsubscribed.

### The Real Risks

- **They can't see more than you can.** No outside tool can reveal private subscribers or who unsubscribed. They work from the public, rounded count.
- **Sub4sub breaks YouTube's rules.** Trading subscriptions counts as fake engagement under YouTube's policies, and YouTube can remove those subscribers.
- **Login requests.** Anything asking you to sign in with your Google account can get access to your channel.

### Safety Checklist

- Use YouTube Studio for your exact numbers.
- Never sign in to a counter site with your Google account.
- Grow with videos people want to subscribe for, not exchanges.

> **Bottom line:** YouTube Studio already shows everything YouTube lets anyone see. Tools that promise more are guessing or breaking the rules.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I see who unsubscribed from my YouTube channel?

No. YouTube Analytics shows how many subscribers you lost over a period, but not who they were.

### Why don't all my subscribers show in the list?

The Recent subscribers list only includes people whose subscriptions are public and who subscribed within the time range you selected. Private subscribers are still counted in your total.

### Can I see my YouTube subscribers on my phone?

Yes. The YouTube Studio app shows your exact subscriber count and analytics. The list of individual subscribers is easiest to view in YouTube Studio on a computer.

### How often does my YouTube subscriber count update?

The count in YouTube Studio updates frequently, but there can be short delays while YouTube processes activity and removes spam accounts. The public count on your channel page is rounded once you pass 1,000.

### Can other people see how many subscribers I have?

Yes, by default your subscriber count is public on your channel page. You can hide it in your channel's advanced settings in YouTube Studio.

### Why did my subscriber count drop?

Either people unsubscribed, or YouTube removed spam and closed accounts from your total. Check Subscribers lost in Analytics to see which.

### Does hiding my subscriber count affect monetization?

No. Hiding the count only changes what visitors see. YouTube still uses your real count for features and programs that need a certain number of subscribers.

### How do I find which videos got me subscribers?

In Analytics, open Advanced mode, choose the Subscribers metric and group by Content. It lists the videos and Shorts that brought the most subscribers.
