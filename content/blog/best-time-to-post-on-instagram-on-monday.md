The best time to post on Instagram on Monday is the hour when your own followers are most active, and Instagram shows you that directly in Insights under your audience's most active times. If you don't have that data yet, start by testing three windows on Monday: early morning before work, around lunch, and early evening, all in your audience's local time zone. Keep the slot that consistently gets the best reach and engagement over several weeks.

Generic "post at 11 a.m." advice ignores that a bakery in Chicago and a gaming creator with a global following will see very different Monday patterns. Here's how to find yours.

## Quick answer: Monday windows worth testing

These are starting points, not rules. They're based on how people typically structure a Monday, not on a study. Treat each one as a hypothesis to test against your own Insights.

| Window (audience's local time) | Why it might work on a Monday | Good fit for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early morning, before work | People check their phones on waking up and during the commute | News, motivation, quick tips, local businesses opening for the day |
| Late morning to lunch | A break after the first round of Monday emails and meetings | B2B, educational carousels, product launches |
| Early evening, after work | People wind down after the first workday of the week | Entertainment, Reels, lifestyle, food, fitness |
| Later evening | Second-screen scrolling while watching TV | Creators, entertainment, longer Reels |

Once you have a few weeks of results, narrow this down to one or two windows and stop testing the rest.

## Why Monday is different

Monday has its own rhythm. It's the start of the workweek for many people, which shapes when they have time to open Instagram and what they're in the mood for.

### Mornings are busy but habitual

Many people check social apps before they get out of bed or on the way to work. On a Monday, though, that window can be shorter: there's a week to plan, inboxes to clear and meetings to prepare for. Posts that are quick to take in, like a single image, a short Reel or a strong first carousel slide, tend to suit this window better than something that needs a lot of attention.

### Lunch is a natural pause

After a morning of catching up, a lunch break is a common moment to scroll. If your audience works office hours, this window is worth testing, especially for content that's useful at work: tips, how-tos and industry updates.

### Evenings belong to leisure

After work, people have more time and often want entertainment rather than information, so Reels and lighter posts often fit here. More accounts post in the evening too, so expect more competition in the feed.

### Not every audience works Monday to Friday

Shift workers, students, hospitality staff and people in countries with a different working week all have different Mondays. If your Insights show activity that doesn't fit the "office worker" pattern, trust the data over the assumption.

## How to find your best Monday time in Instagram Insights

Instagram shows when your followers are online, but only on a professional account (Creator or Business). If you're still on a personal account, follow our guide on [how to turn on Insights on Instagram](/blog/how-to-turn-on-insights-on-instagram) first. Insights only start collecting data after you switch, so do it as early as you can.

Menu labels move around between app versions, but the path generally looks like this:

1. Open your profile in the Instagram app.
2. Tap **Professional dashboard** (or **Insights**) near the top of your profile.
3. Open the section about your followers or audience (often labeled **Total followers** or **Followers**).
4. Scroll to **Most active times**.
5. Switch the view from **Hours** to **Days** to compare days, then back to **Hours** and select **Monday** (shown as **M**) to see hourly activity.

A few things to know about this data:

- Instagram only shows audience details once you pass a minimum follower count (100 followers at the time of writing).
- The hours are shown in your phone's time zone, not your followers' time zone. More on that below.
- "Most active" means when your followers are on Instagram, not when they're most likely to engage with you. It's a strong hint, not a guarantee.

### Pick the window before the peak

Try posting shortly before the busiest hour, so your post is already gathering engagement as more followers come online. If your Monday peak is 7 p.m., test 6 p.m. as well.

## Time zones: whose Monday are you posting for?

The Most active times chart uses your device's time zone. If your audience lives somewhere else, you need to convert.

### If most followers are in one region

Check **Top locations** in your audience Insights. If most of your followers are in one city or country, convert the peak hour from your time zone to theirs, and schedule for their Monday, not yours. For example, if you're in New York and most of your followers are in Los Angeles, a 9 a.m. peak on your chart may really mean 6 a.m. for them, which might be fine or might be too early depending on the account.

### If your audience is spread out

For a global audience, you won't find one perfect hour. Two practical options:

- **Post for your largest region** and let the rest catch up later. Reels in particular can keep finding viewers for days.
- **Split your Monday content**: one post timed for your largest region and a Story or second post for the next biggest.


## A simple four-week Monday test

You don't need complicated tools to find your best time. You need consistency and a way to compare like with like.

1. **Pick two or three windows** from the table above, adjusted using your Most active times chart.
2. **Post similar content in each window.** If you compare a Reel at 8 a.m. with a text carousel at 6 p.m., you're testing format, not timing.
3. **Rotate windows over four Mondays.** For example, Week 1 at 8 a.m., Week 2 at 12 p.m., Week 3 at 6 p.m., Week 4 at whichever looked strongest.
4. **Record results 48 hours after posting** so every post gets the same amount of time. Note reach, views, likes, comments, shares and saves.
5. **Look at the pattern, not one outlier.** One viral post doesn't make a window the winner. Look for the slot that's consistently good.
6. **Repeat every few months.** Audiences change as your account grows and seasons shift.

Reach and views show whether the timing got your post in front of people; shares, saves and comments show whether they had time and reason to engage. ## What to post on a Monday

Timing matters less than content, but start-of-week formats often fit well: weekly plans and checklists, quick tips people can save for later, announcements you want seen early in the week, and behind-the-scenes Reels. Whatever you post, make the first second of a Reel or the first slide of a carousel do the work, because people scroll fast on a busy Monday.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Monday a good day to post on Instagram?

It can be. Monday is a normal working day for many people, so they still check Instagram in the morning, at lunch and in the evening. Whether it's a good day for you depends on your audience, which you can check in Insights under Most active times by switching the view to Days.

### What time should I post on Instagram on Monday if I'm a new account?

If you don't have enough followers for audience Insights yet, test the three common windows: early morning, around lunch and early evening in your target audience's time zone. Post similar content in each over a few Mondays and compare reach and engagement after the same amount of time.

### Does posting at the "best time" guarantee more engagement?

No. Timing helps your post get seen by more of your followers early on, but the content itself decides whether people engage. A strong post at an average time will usually do better than a weak post at the perfect time.

### Should I post Reels and feed posts at different times on Monday?

You can test it. Reels can be recommended to people who don't follow you, so they may keep getting views long after you post, while feed photos and carousels rely more on your existing followers. Compare each format against itself across different windows rather than against each other.

### How often should I recheck my best Monday time?

Every few months, or whenever your audience changes noticeably, for example after a period of fast growth or when you start reaching people in a new country. Seasonal changes like school holidays and daylight saving time can shift activity as well.
