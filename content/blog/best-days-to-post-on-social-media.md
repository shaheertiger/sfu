You've seen the charts that crown one weekday as the best day to post, and your own results don't match them. That's normal. The best days to post on social media are the days your own audience is most active and most likely to engage, and they differ by platform: LinkedIn tends to suit weekdays, while TikTok, Instagram and Pinterest can do well on weekends too.

![Best days to post on social media: start with a hypothesis for each platform, then test it against your own analytics](/images/blog/best-days-to-post-on-social-media-hero-1200x675.webp)

The universal charts average together millions of very different accounts. Your audience, your time zone and your content type matter more than any average, so the "best day" from a chart is a guess about someone else's followers.

This guide gives you a starting day to test on nine platforms, a 5-step test you can run in four weeks, the mistakes that ruin the test, and a way to post on your best days without being online for each one.

## Why There's No Single Best Day to Post (Averages Mislead)

The real question is simple: on which days do your followers have the time and the mindset to engage with what you post? Each platform answers it differently, because people use each one for different things.

![A universal best-day chart compared with your own audience data](/images/blog/best-days-to-post-on-social-media-averages-1200x675.webp)

A chart that says "Wednesday wins" might be right for a large sample of brands. It can still be wrong for a bakery whose customers scroll on Sunday mornings, or a B2B consultant whose clients only open LinkedIn on weekdays.

### What Most People Don't Know

Most platforms already show you when your own followers are online. Instagram Insights, for example, shows your followers' most active times by day and hour. That's a better first hypothesis than any public chart, and it costs nothing to check.

Time zones matter too. A Tuesday post at 9 a.m. in New York is Tuesday afternoon in London and late Tuesday night in Sydney, so "the best day" depends on where your audience lives.

> **Key point:** Treat every published "best day" as a hypothesis. Your own analytics, measured over a few weeks, should overrule it.

## Best Days to Post by Platform: 9 Starting Points to Test

These are starting hypotheses based on how people typically use each platform, not findings from a study. Use them to decide what to test first, then let your own results overrule them.

![Work-led platforms suit weekdays, while free-time platforms can also do well on weekends](/images/blog/best-days-to-post-on-social-media-platforms-1200x675.webp)

| Platform | Days to test first | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Tuesday to Thursday | People use it around work; Mondays are busy and Fridays wind down |
| Facebook | Weekdays, plus one weekend day | Broad, mixed audience that checks in throughout the week |
| Instagram | Midweek and Sunday | Leisure scrolling happens on weekdays and weekends |
| TikTok | Thursday to Sunday | Entertainment viewing often increases toward the weekend |
| X | Monday to Thursday | News and conversation follow the workweek |
| Threads and Bluesky | Weekdays | Conversation-led feeds that reward joining in when people are around |
| Pinterest | Weekend and Sunday evening | People plan meals, projects and purchases in their free time |
| YouTube | Publish Thursday or Friday | Gives videos time to gather views before weekend viewing |
| Google Business Profile | A day or two before your busiest days | Customers check before they visit |

Treat this as a first draft. A B2B brand on Instagram and a meme account on LinkedIn would both break these patterns, and that's fine.

### LinkedIn: Why Midweek Is the First Test

LinkedIn is used mostly in a professional context: during work hours, on breaks or on the way to work. Mondays can be crowded with catching up, and Friday afternoons often see people switching off, which is why midweek is a common first test. If your audience is freelancers or founders, test weekends too.

### Facebook: Less Dependent on Any One Day

Facebook audiences span ages, interests and routines, and many people check in briefly every day. Local businesses and community Pages often find that posting before the weekend helps, because people are making plans. For hour-by-hour starting points, see the [best time to post on Facebook](/blog/best-time-to-post-on-facebook).

### Instagram: Work Breaks and Downtime

People scroll Instagram at lunch and on commutes as well as on weekends, so test weekdays against weekends. Our guide to the [best time to post on Instagram on Monday](/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram-on-monday) shows how to read your follower activity hour by hour.

### TikTok: The Day Matters Less Than You Think

The For You feed recommends videos to people who don't follow you, so a post can keep finding viewers for days. The day still matters for the first wave of views, and evenings and weekends are worth testing. See the [best time to post on TikTok](/blog/best-time-to-post-on-tiktok) for a starting time for each day.

### X, Threads and Bluesky: Follow the Conversation

These feeds reward joining conversations while they're happening. News, industry chatter and live events drive activity, and many follow the workweek. A product launch, a conference or a sports fixture in your niche can matter more than the day of the week.

### Pinterest: Plan Seasonal Pins Early

People use Pinterest to plan recipes, home projects, outfits, gifts and events, often in their free time. Pinterest content has a long life and is strongly seasonal, so publishing seasonal pins well ahead of the event can matter more than the exact day.

### YouTube: Publish Before People Watch

Viewing often increases in the evenings and on weekends. Many creators publish a day or two before those peaks so the video has time to reach subscribers and recommendations. A regular publishing day also helps subscribers know when to expect new videos.

### Google Business Profile: Think Like a Customer

Your Business Profile posts are seen by people searching for you or businesses like yours. Post offers and updates shortly before your busiest days: a restaurant might post on Thursday for the weekend, a B2B service at the start of the week.

## How to Find Your Own Best Days in 5 Steps (Free Method)

Your analytics will tell you more than any table. This process works on any platform and needs nothing but the analytics each platform already gives you.

![Five steps to find your best posting days: check analytics, form a hypothesis, run a fair test, compare, decide](/images/blog/best-days-to-post-on-social-media-test-1200x675.webp)

### Step 1: Check What Your Analytics Already Show

1. Open each platform's native analytics: Instagram Insights, Meta Business Suite for Facebook and Instagram, LinkedIn analytics, TikTok Studio, YouTube Studio, Pinterest Analytics and so on. Menu labels change between app versions, so search the app if a label has moved.
2. Look for an audience activity view, if there is one. If you don't have Instagram Insights yet, see [how to turn on Insights on Instagram](/blog/how-to-turn-on-insights-on-instagram).
3. Note your last two or three months of posts with the day each was published and its reach and engagement.

### Step 2: Write Down One Hypothesis

Combine the audience activity data with the starting points above. For example: "Our LinkedIn posts will get more engagement on Tuesday and Wednesday than on Monday and Friday." Write it down so you can't move the goalposts later.

### Step 3: Run a Fair Test (Change Only the Day)

This is the step most people get wrong. They put their best video on Tuesday and a plain link on Friday, then conclude Tuesday is better.

1. **Keep content comparable.** Rotate the same type of post across different days.
2. **Keep the time of day the same** while you're testing days, so you're only changing one thing.
3. **Test for at least four weeks.** One week can be distorted by a holiday, a news event or one unusually strong post.
4. **Measure after the same time window**, such as 48 hours or 7 days after posting, so every post has the same chance.

> **Pro tip:** Schedule the whole test in advance. If every test post goes out at the same time on its assigned day, you remove the biggest source of noise: you being late, early or busy.

### Step 4: Compare the Metrics That Match Your Goal

| Metric | What it tells you | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| Reach or impressions | Whether people saw the post | You're comparing visibility between days |
| Engagement rate (interactions ÷ reach) | How people responded, adjusted for reach | Posts on different days got very different reach |
| Clicks | Whether people visited your link | Your goal is traffic |
| Saves and shares | Whether the content felt worth keeping or passing on | You want content that travels |
| Follows or subscribers gained | Whether a post brought in new people | You're focused on growth |

For a fuller framework on comparing posts fairly, see our guide to [content performance analysis](/blog/content-performance-analysis).

### Step 5: Decide, Then Retest Every Quarter

Pick the days that are consistently strong, not the one with a single big hit. Build your schedule around them, and retest every quarter or when your audience changes, for example after growing into a new country or shifting your content focus.

## 5 Mistakes That Make Your Posting-Day Test Useless

![What makes a posting-day test reliable compared with the mistakes that ruin it](/images/blog/best-days-to-post-on-social-media-mistakes-1200x675.webp)

| Problem | Fix | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Copying another account's schedule | Start from your own audience activity data | Their audience isn't yours |
| Changing day, time, format and topic together | Change only the day during the test | You can see what made the difference |
| Ignoring time zones | Pick days and times in your audience's main time zone | Your Tuesday may be their Wednesday |
| Going quiet on "bad" days | Keep posting, but put your most important posts on strong days | A weaker day can still be worth a good post |
| Stopping after one test | Retest every quarter | Audiences and platforms change |

## Several Time Zones? How to Pick the Day When Your Audience Is Split

If your followers are spread across continents, there may be no single day and time that suits everyone. A few ways to handle it:

![Three ways to handle an audience split across time zones](/images/blog/best-days-to-post-on-social-media-timezones-1200x675.webp)

1. **Find your biggest segment.** Most platform analytics show top countries or cities. Plan your main posts for that segment's days.
2. **Rotate.** Alternate posting times through the week so each region gets some posts at a good local time.
3. **Lean on long-life formats.** Pinterest pins, YouTube videos and TikTok videos keep finding viewers for days, so the exact day matters less than it does on X or Threads.
4. **Separate the platforms.** Your best LinkedIn day may be a weak Pinterest day. Plan a separate schedule for each platform based on its own data.

## What to Do Next: A 4-Week Plan

1. **This week:** open the analytics for your two most important platforms and write one hypothesis for each.
2. **Weeks 1 to 4:** schedule comparable posts at the same time on your test days, so only the day changes.
3. **End of week 4:** compare engagement rate and your goal metric after the same time window.
4. **Then:** move your most important posts to the strongest days, keep posting on the others, and set a reminder to retest next quarter.

What helps:

- ✓ One change at a time (the day), with everything else held steady
- ✓ At least four weeks of data before deciding
- ✓ A separate schedule for each platform

What doesn't:

- ✗ Copying a chart or a competitor's schedule
- ✗ Judging a day on one viral post
- ✗ Stopping posting on weaker days altogether

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the single best day to post on social media?

There isn't one that works for everyone. The best day depends on the platform, your audience's routines and their time zones, so use the starting points in this guide as hypotheses and test them with your own analytics.

### Is it bad to post on weekends?

No, not necessarily. Platforms built around entertainment, inspiration and planning, like TikTok, Instagram and Pinterest, can perform well on weekends, while work-focused platforms like LinkedIn often see less activity, depending on your audience.

### How long should I test posting days before deciding?

Test for at least four weeks, keeping the content type and time of day consistent. Longer is better if you post infrequently, because you need several posts on each day to see a reliable pattern.

### Should I post on the same days on every platform?

Usually not. Each platform's audience behaves differently, so your best LinkedIn day may be a weak Pinterest day. Plan a separate schedule for each platform based on its own data.

### Does posting on the best day matter more than posting consistently?

Consistency usually matters more. A regular schedule your audience can rely on, with strong content, will generally do better than chasing the perfect day with irregular posts, so use your best days to decide where your most important posts go.

### Which metric should I use to compare days?

Use engagement rate (interactions divided by reach) as your main comparison, plus whichever metric matches your goal, such as clicks for traffic or follows for growth. Raw likes can mislead when reach differs a lot between days.

### Do scheduled posts perform worse than posts published live?

There's no good evidence that they do when the post is published through the platform's official tools. See our guide on [whether scheduled posts get fewer views](/blog/do-scheduled-posts-get-less-views) for the details.

### How often should I recheck my best days?

Recheck every quarter, and whenever your audience changes, for example after you grow into a new country or change your content focus.
