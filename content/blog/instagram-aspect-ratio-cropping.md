You posted a photo and Instagram trimmed off the top of someone's head, or your neat grid now cuts every graphic at the sides. Instagram crops photos because its feed only accepts images between **4:5 portrait and 1.91:1 landscape**, at the time of writing. If your image is taller or wider than that, the app makes you crop it to fit, and your profile grid then trims every post to a **3:4** thumbnail on top of that.

![Why Instagram crops photos: the feed accepts 4:5 to 1.91:1, and the profile grid shows a 3:4 crop](/images/blog/instagram-aspect-ratio-cropping-hero-1200x675.webp)

It isn't obvious because most phone cameras shoot 3:4 in portrait mode, which is just outside the range, and because the grid crop happens even to images that fit. Scheduling works differently again: Schedule For You doesn't crop or resize images, so a photo outside the allowed range is rejected by Instagram and you get a failed-post alert.

This guide covers the sizes to use, why each crop happens, how to fix a rejected image, and how to stop cropping before you post.

## Why Instagram Crops Your Photos (3 Different Crops)

![Three Instagram crops: images outside the 4:5 to 1.91:1 feed range, the 3:4 profile grid thumbnail and mixed-shape carousels](/images/blog/instagram-aspect-ratio-cropping-crops-1200x675.webp)

| Format | Aspect ratio | Recommended size | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Portrait feed post | 4:5 | 1080×1350 | Tallest feed shape allowed; takes up the most screen space |
| Square feed post | 1:1 | 1080×1080 | Safe default; fits the grid with little cropping |
| Landscape feed post | 1.91:1 | 1080×566 | Widest shape allowed; looks small in the feed |
| Reels | 9:16 | 1080×1920 | Full-screen vertical video |
| Profile grid thumbnail | 3:4 | Displayed crop | The grid shows a 3:4 crop of each post |

All of these values are correct at the time of writing. Instagram adjusts its formats from time to time, so check the [Instagram Help Center](https://help.instagram.com) if something looks different.

### 1. Your Image Is Outside the Feed Range

Divide the width by the height:

- **0.8 to 1.91:** inside the range, so no forced crop.
- **Below 0.8:** too tall. A 3:4 photo (0.75), a 2:3 photo (0.67) or a 9:16 phone screenshot (0.56) falls here.
- **Above 1.91:** too wide. A 2:1 banner or a panorama falls here.

### 2. The Profile Grid Shows a 3:4 Crop

Even an image that fits the feed range perfectly is cropped again on your profile. A 4:5 portrait loses a small strip on the left and right, and a square photo loses more from the sides. Your post isn't changed, but the thumbnail people see on your profile is. That matters most for text-heavy graphics, logos near the edges, and grid layouts split across several posts.

### 3. Carousels and Reels Covers Use One Shape

A carousel is shown at a single shape, generally set by the first item, so later slides in a different shape can be cropped to match. Reels are 9:16, but the cover is shown cropped in the profile grid.

### What Most People Don't Know

Instagram has started allowing taller 3:4 photos in its own app, so you may see uncropped 3:4 posts from other accounts. Posts published through Instagram's publishing API, which schedulers including Schedule For You use, are still documented as 4:5 to 1.91:1 at the time of writing.

> **Key point:** Resize 3:4 phone photos to 4:5 before you schedule them. It's the one size that works in the app, through schedulers, and in the grid with minimal trimming.

## How to Stop Instagram Cropping Your Photos (5 Steps)

![Instagram sizes that work, like 1080 by 1350 portrait, next to shapes that get cropped, like 3:4 phone photos and 9:16 screenshots](/images/blog/instagram-aspect-ratio-cropping-steps-1200x675.webp)

### Step 1: Shoot or Design for 4:5

If you create graphics, start with a 1080×1350 canvas. It's the tallest shape the feed allows, it fills the most screen space, and it loses only thin strips at the sides in the 3:4 grid. For photos, shoot at 4:5 if your camera app offers it, or leave room around the subject so you can crop later.

### Step 2: Keep a Safe Zone

Keep important text and faces inside the central part of the frame. Leave a margin on the left and right of 4:5 images so the 3:4 grid thumbnail doesn't clip anything.

### Step 3: Use "Fit" for Screenshots and Tall Images

Phone screenshots (about 9:16) and tall infographics can't fit the feed without losing a lot. Placing them on a 4:5 background with "fit" keeps everything visible.

### Step 4: Plan Grid Layouts Around the 3:4 Crop

Our [Instagram grid maker](/tools/instagram-grid-maker) splits an image into 3×1 to 3×4 tiles at 1080×1350, 1080×1440 (3:4) or 1080×1080 and numbers them in posting order. 3:4 tiles line up on the grid without being trimmed when you post them from the app; with 4:5 or square tiles, keep important detail away from tile edges.

### Step 5: Export at 1080 Pixels Wide

Instagram displays feed images at up to 1080 pixels wide at the time of writing. Smaller images get stretched and can look soft, and much larger ones are scaled down.

> **Pro tip:** Export every carousel slide at the same ratio. The step people get wrong is mixing a portrait first slide with landscape ones, and watching the later slides get cropped to match.

## How Scheduling Changes Cropping (No Crop Step)

![The Instagram app shows a crop frame, while a scheduled post has no crop step and out-of-range images are rejected](/images/blog/instagram-aspect-ratio-cropping-scheduling-1200x675.webp)

When you post from the Instagram app, cropping is interactive: the app shows the crop frame and lets you drag the image around. When you schedule a post with Schedule For You, there's no crop step:

- **Schedule For You doesn't crop or resize images.** It sends the file you uploaded to Instagram as is.
- **Instagram checks the shape when it publishes.** If the image is outside the 4:5 to 1.91:1 range, Instagram rejects the post.
- **You get a failed-post alert.** It tells you the post didn't publish, so you can fix the image and reschedule.

This is deliberate. Automatic cropping can cut off faces, logos or text without you noticing.

### How to Fix a Rejected Image (4 Steps)

1. **Check the ratio.** Divide width by height. If it's under 0.8 or over 1.91, it needs resizing.
2. **Resize to a supported shape.** Use our free [Instagram photo resizer](/tools/instagram-photo-resizer) and pick the portrait (1080×1350), square (1080×1080) or landscape (1080×566) preset.
3. **Choose fill or fit.** "Fill" crops the image to the new shape, and you decide what gets trimmed. "Fit" keeps the whole image and adds a background color around it, which suits screenshots and graphics.
4. **Replace the image in the post.** Open the failed post, swap in the resized image and reschedule it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why does Instagram crop my photos?

Instagram's feed only accepts images between 4:5 portrait and 1.91:1 landscape at the time of writing. Anything taller or wider has to be cropped to fit. Separately, the profile grid shows each post as a 3:4 thumbnail, which trims the sides.

### What Instagram aspect ratio avoids cropping?

1080×1350 (4:5) for portrait and 1080×1080 (1:1) for square both fit the feed without a forced crop. Keep key details away from the left and right edges, because the profile grid shows a 3:4 crop.

### Does Schedule For You crop images for Instagram?

No. Schedule For You doesn't crop or resize images. If an image is outside Instagram's allowed range, Instagram rejects the post and you get a failed-post alert so you can resize it and reschedule.

### Why do my posts look cut off on my profile grid?

At the time of writing, the grid shows every post as a 3:4 rectangle, regardless of the post's real shape. The full image is still visible when someone opens the post.

### What aspect ratio should Reels use?

9:16, ideally 1080×1920. The cover is cropped in the profile grid, so keep the main subject and any text near the center.

### Why can some accounts post 3:4 photos without cropping?

Instagram has started allowing taller 3:4 photos in its own app. Posts published through Instagram's publishing API are still documented as 4:5 to 1.91:1 at the time of writing, so resize to 4:5 for scheduling.

### How do I post a panorama on Instagram?

Split it into a seamless carousel, or resize it to 1.91:1. A 3:1 panorama is too wide for one feed image.
