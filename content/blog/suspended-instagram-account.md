You open Instagram and instead of your feed there's a screen saying "We suspended your account", with a number of days underneath. Your stomach drops.

A suspended Instagram account is one Instagram has taken offline because it believes the account broke its rules, with a set number of days for you to disagree before the decision becomes permanent. The fix is to use the "disagree with decision" option on the suspension screen itself, as soon as you see it. Don't use outside services, and don't share your password with anyone who offers help.

![Suspended Instagram account: disagree on the suspension screen before the countdown runs out](/images/blog/suspended-instagram-account-hero-1200x675.webp)

Not every Instagram problem is a suspension. Limits on following, a locked login and a hacked account all look scary in similar ways, but each has a different fix, and the wording on the screen is the clue.

This guide helps you work out which problem you have, walks through disagreeing with a real suspension, covers what to do while you wait, and what happens after the decision.

## Suspended, Disabled, Restricted or Hacked? Which Problem You Have

Start here. The wording on your screen tells you a lot.

| What you see | What it usually means | Where to go |
|---|---|---|
| "We suspended your account" with a countdown of days | Suspension: the account is offline pending your response | Disagree with the decision on that screen |
| "Your account has been disabled" | Disabled: the account is off, with an appeal window | Our guide to the [disabled account appeal](/blog/instagram-disabled-account-appeal) |
| "Try again later" or "We restrict certain activity" | Restriction: some actions are limited for a while | Wait it out and slow down |
| Asked to confirm it's you, or "suspicious login attempt" | Locked: a security check, not a penalty | Complete the check in the app |
| Password or email changed without you, or you're logged out | Hacked: someone else has control | Instagram's hacked-account help |

![The difference between a suspended, restricted, locked and hacked Instagram account](/images/blog/suspended-instagram-account-which-1200x675.webp)

### What Most People Don't Know

Instagram uses "suspended" and "disabled" in overlapping ways, and the screens have changed over the years. The key difference for you is the countdown. A suspension notice tells you that you have a certain number of days to disagree, and that the account will be permanently disabled if you don't, or if the review upholds the decision.

> **Key point:** The countdown is the most important detail on the screen. Disagree before it runs out, even if you're still gathering your thoughts.

### Restricted Is Lighter

Your account still works, but Instagram temporarily limits actions such as following, liking, commenting or sending messages, often because activity looked automated or unusually fast. Restrictions usually lift on their own after the time shown. Report it in the app if you think it's a mistake, and stop the behavior that triggered it.

### Locked or Hacked Isn't a Penalty

A locked account is a security step: Instagram wants to confirm it's really you, often after a login from a new device or location. A hacked account is one where someone else has changed your password, email or phone number. For a hacked account, start at instagram.com/hacked or the hacked-account pages on [help.instagram.com](https://help.instagram.com).

## How to Disagree With an Instagram Suspension in 6 Steps

The option to disagree is built into the suspension screen. That's the official route, and it's free. Menu labels change between app versions.

![Six steps to disagree with an Instagram suspension from the suspension screen](/images/blog/suspended-instagram-account-steps-1200x675.webp)

### Step 1: Log In Normally

Log in to the Instagram app or instagram.com with your normal details.

### Step 2: Read the Reason and the Days Left

On the suspension notice, read the reason Instagram gives and note how many days you have left.

### Step 3: Tap the Option to Disagree

This is the step people delay, waiting for a better explanation to come to them. Start now: the countdown keeps running.

### Step 4: Confirm Your Email or Phone

You may need to confirm your email or phone number with a code sent to you.

### Step 5: Complete Identity Verification

If asked, complete it. This can be a short video selfie or, for some accounts, a photo of an ID.

### Step 6: Submit and Write Down the Date

Keep any written explanation brief. Say what the account is used for and why you think the decision was wrong.

> **Pro tip:** A calm, specific two- or three-sentence note is easier to review than a long one. If the notice names a reason, read that section of Instagram's Community Guidelines first and address it directly.

### If There's No Button to Disagree

Sometimes the screen doesn't show the option, or the app glitches. Try updating the app, logging in on instagram.com in a browser, or using a different device. If it still doesn't appear, search [help.instagram.com](https://help.instagram.com) for "suspended account" to find the current form for your situation.

## What to Do While You Wait: 4 Things

Instagram doesn't promise a timeline for reviewing a suspension. Some people hear back quickly, and others wait much longer.

1. **Keep your contact details reachable.** Instagram may email or text you about the decision or ask for another verification step.
2. **Don't keep resubmitting.** Sending the same request repeatedly doesn't speed anything up. Submit once, then log in every few days to check.
3. **Pause scheduled posts.** They won't publish while the account is suspended.
4. **Let your audience know elsewhere.** A short, factual note on your other channels saves people from wondering whether you've vanished. Don't ask followers to mass-report or tag Instagram, which doesn't help a review.

## Paid "Recovery" Services (Use With Caution)

Suspensions bring out scammers. If you post about your account publicly, expect replies and DMs offering to restore it for a fee, often from accounts claiming to know someone at Meta.

![Safe ways to respond to an Instagram suspension compared with recovery scams](/images/blog/suspended-instagram-account-scams-1200x675.webp)

### The Real Risks

- **They aren't part of the process.** Paid "recovery" or "unban" services aren't connected to Meta.
- **Lost logins.** Many want your password or two-factor codes, which can turn a suspension into a stolen account.
- **Fake emails.** Messages that claim to be from Instagram may not be. Compare them with the official emails listed in the app's security settings before you click anything.

> **Bottom line:** The only way back is the free option on the suspension screen. Never pay anyone, and never share your password or two-factor codes.

## If the Decision Is Reversed: 4 Things to Do Straight Away

When a suspension is lifted, your account usually returns with its posts, followers and username.

- **Change your password** and turn on two-factor authentication, especially if a takeover played any part.
- **Remove access for apps you don't recognize** in your security settings.
- **Check Account Status** in settings to see whether any posts were removed and whether the account is still marked as at risk.
- **Back up your content** with Download your information in Accounts Center.

## If the Decision Is Upheld

If the review keeps the suspension in place, the account is generally disabled permanently and the decision usually can't be reversed. Instagram's terms discourage creating new accounts to get around enforcement, so check the current rules before starting over, and don't rely on anyone promising a second, paid review.

| Outcome | What happens | Your next step |
|---|---|---|
| Reversed | The account returns with posts, followers and username | Secure the account and check Account Status |
| Upheld | The account is generally disabled permanently | Read Instagram's current rules before starting over |
| No answer yet | Still under review | Keep contact details reachable and wait |
| Countdown ran out | The account is disabled | See the [disabled account appeal](/blog/instagram-disabled-account-appeal) guide |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does "we suspended your account" mean on Instagram?

It means Instagram has taken your account offline because it believes it broke the rules. The screen shows how many days you have to disagree. If you don't respond in time, or the review upholds the decision, the account is permanently disabled.

### How is a suspended account different from a restricted one?

A suspended account is offline and hidden from others. A restricted account still works, but certain actions, like following or commenting, are limited for a set period. Restrictions usually lift on their own, while suspensions need you to disagree with the decision.

### How long does an Instagram suspension review take?

Instagram doesn't guarantee a timeline. Reviews can be quick or take weeks. Submit your request once, finish any verification, and keep checking by logging in.

### Can someone else see my profile while it's suspended?

Generally no. While the account is suspended, your profile and posts are hidden from other people. They return if the suspension is lifted.

### Should I pay a service to get my suspended account back?

No. Instagram's process is free and happens inside the app or on its help center. Paid "recovery" or "unban" services aren't connected to Meta, and many are scams.

### What if there's no option to disagree?

Update the app, try instagram.com in a browser or another device, and search Instagram's help center for "suspended account" to find the current form.

### Will I get my followers back if the suspension is lifted?

Usually, yes. A reinstated account generally returns with its posts, followers and username.

### Can I make a new account if mine stays suspended?

Instagram's terms discourage creating new accounts to get around enforcement, so check the current rules first.
