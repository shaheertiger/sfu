In the TikTok vs YouTube Shorts decision, there's no universal winner: TikTok is built entirely around short video and an interest-based feed, so it can be quicker to test ideas, while YouTube Shorts sits inside YouTube, where search, subscribers and long-form videos can turn a short-form viewer into a long-term one. For most beginners, the practical answer is to make one vertical video and post it to both, then let your own results decide where to focus.

Below is a fair, side-by-side comparison that doesn't rely on user counts or growth claims, since those change constantly and are hard to verify.

## Is YouTube Shorts more popular than TikTok?

It depends on what you measure, and the published figures are not easy to compare. Both platforms report huge audiences, but they count things differently: YouTube's numbers often describe everyone who uses YouTube, not only Shorts viewers, while TikTok's describe the whole app. Numbers also change from quarter to quarter.

For a beginner, "more popular" is the wrong question. The useful questions are:

- **Where does your target audience already watch?** A gaming or tech audience may already live on YouTube; trends, comedy and community-driven niches are often very active on TikTok.
- **Where do you want to end up?** If your goal is long tutorials, reviews or podcasts, Shorts can feed viewers straight into that. If you want to stay purely short-form, TikTok's whole product is designed around it.
- **Which app do you actually enjoy using?** You'll learn the format faster on a platform you scroll yourself.

## Discovery: how new creators get seen

### TikTok

TikTok's For You feed shows videos based on what each viewer watches and interacts with, so a new account can reach people who don't follow it. In practice, this means your first few seconds matter a lot. If viewers keep watching, the video can keep being shown to more people; if they swipe away, it usually stalls. TikTok search also matters more than many beginners expect, so say and write the words people would search for.

### YouTube Shorts

Shorts are recommended in a swipeable feed too, but they also appear in YouTube search, on your channel and to your subscribers. A key difference is what happens next: a viewer who likes your Short can subscribe and then find your longer videos on the same channel. You can also link a Short to one of your long-form videos, which makes Shorts a useful trailer for deeper content.

### What that means for you

TikTok tends to reward fast iteration on short ideas. Shorts tends to reward creators who think of short videos as part of a bigger channel. Neither one guarantees views; a clear hook and a video people watch to the end matter on both.

## Video length and format

Both platforms favor vertical 9:16 video at 1080×1920. If you shoot once in that format, you can use the same file on both. Our guide to [TikTok video size and ratio](/blog/tiktok-video-size-ratio) covers safe zones so captions and buttons don't cover important parts of the frame.

Length is where they differ most. At the time of writing, YouTube Shorts can be up to 3 minutes, while TikTok lets you record several minutes in the app and upload longer files. If a video runs past a few minutes, it isn't a Short on YouTube; it's a regular video.

## Monetization: how each platform pays (hedged)

Rules change often, so treat the details below as a summary at the time of writing and check each platform's official help pages before planning around them.

### YouTube Shorts

Shorts earnings come through the YouTube Partner Program. Ads that run between Shorts in the feed are pooled, part of that revenue is allocated to creators, and eligible creators receive 45% of their allocated share. For full ad revenue sharing, you need 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months or 10 million public Shorts views in 90 days. A lower tier for fan funding features, such as channel memberships and Super Thanks, opens earlier in available countries. Our guide on [when YouTube starts paying you](/blog/when-does-youtube-start-paying-you) explains both tiers.

### TikTok

TikTok's main payout program for creators is the Creator Rewards Program. At the time of writing, you need to be 18 or older, have at least 10,000 followers and 100,000 video views in the last 30 days, and be in an eligible country. Only videos longer than one minute qualify. TikTok also offers other ways to earn, such as LIVE gifts and brand partnerships, with their own rules.

### Which pays more?

There's no honest single answer. Payouts depend on your niche, where your viewers live, how long they watch and the season, so any "per 1,000 views" figure you see online is a snapshot at best. For a beginner, both programs are a later step; your first goal is building an audience that watches.

## Audience and community

Comments, duets, stitches and replies with video make TikTok feel conversational, and trends move fast. That's great for experimenting and joining conversations, but it can also mean videos have a short shelf life.

Shorts viewers are on YouTube, where people also come to search and watch longer. A Short can keep picking up views from search over time, especially for how-to and evergreen topics. Subscribers you earn through Shorts can watch your long-form videos and live streams later.

## Editing tools for beginners

You don't need expensive software for either platform.

- **TikTok:** the in-app editor handles trimming, text, captions, sounds and effects. CapCut, made by the same company, offers more control on phone or desktop.
- **YouTube Shorts:** the YouTube app's Shorts camera lets you record, trim and add text and music. YouTube Create is a free mobile editing app from YouTube for both Shorts and longer videos.

If you edit in one app and post to the other platform, export a clean file without another app's watermark. Upload the original rather than downloading a posted video and reposting it.

## Can you post the same video on TikTok and YouTube Shorts?

Yes, as long as it's your own content. Many creators post the same vertical video to both. A few tips make it work better:

1. **Shoot in 9:16** and keep text away from the edges and bottom of the frame.
2. **Keep it under 3 minutes** if you want it to count as a Short.
3. **Export a clean master file** without on-screen watermarks.
4. **Rewrite the text for each platform.** On TikTok, write a caption with keywords and a strong first line. On YouTube, write a clear title (up to 100 characters) and a short description. Our free [TikTok caption generator](/tools/tiktok-caption-generator) gives you starting ideas for the TikTok side.
5. **Compare results after a few weeks**, not after one video.

Schedule For You can publish that one file to both: TikTok with the privacy setting you choose, and YouTube as a normal upload with its own title and description. YouTube then decides whether a vertical or square video counts as a Short; there's no separate "Shorts" switch. You can also schedule the same video to Instagram as a Reel from the same post.

## How to choose: a simple decision guide

- **Choose TikTok first** if you want to experiment quickly with short ideas, follow trends and build a community around short-form content.
- **Choose YouTube Shorts first** if you plan to make longer videos too, your topic is searchable (tutorials, reviews, explainers) or your audience already watches on YouTube.
- **Do both** if you can make one video and adapt the text. It costs little extra time, and the data will show you which platform suits your content.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is TikTok or YouTube Shorts better for beginners?

Neither is better for everyone. TikTok is built only around short video, which makes it easy to experiment and get feedback. YouTube Shorts is better if you want to grow a channel that also includes long-form videos. Many beginners post to both and compare.

### Is YouTube Shorts more popular than TikTok?

Both have very large audiences, but the companies measure them differently, so direct comparisons are unreliable. Focus on where your target audience watches and which platform fits your goals rather than headline numbers.

### Does YouTube Shorts pay more than TikTok?

It varies too much to say. YouTube shares a portion of Shorts ad revenue with Partner Program members, while TikTok's Creator Rewards Program pays for eligible videos over one minute. Your niche, audience location and watch time affect earnings on both.

### Can I upload TikTok videos to YouTube Shorts?

You can upload your own videos to both, ideally using the original file without TikTok's watermark. Keep the video under 3 minutes so YouTube can treat it as a Short, and write a YouTube title instead of reusing your TikTok caption.

### How long can a YouTube Short be compared to a TikTok?

At the time of writing, YouTube Shorts can be up to 3 minutes long. TikTok allows several minutes when recording in the app and longer uploads. Check each platform's help center, because limits have changed several times.
