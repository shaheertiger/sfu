You've designed a graphic that looks crisp on your laptop, then it shows up small, cropped or fuzzy in the LinkedIn app. The best image sizes for LinkedIn posts are **1080×1080 for square (1:1)**, **1080×1350 for portrait (4:5)** and **1200×627 for landscape (1.91:1)**.

![Image sizes for LinkedIn posts: 1080×1350 portrait, 1080×1080 square and 1200×627 landscape](/images/blog/image-sizes-for-linkedin-posts-hero-1200x675.webp)

Square and portrait images take up more room in the mobile feed, so they're the safer choice for most posts. Landscape 1200×627 is still the standard for link previews and wide images like charts.

Most guides list the numbers and stop. This one also explains why mobile changes the answer, gives every other LinkedIn image size (profile photos, banners, company pages, document carousels, article covers) and shows how to keep images sharp. LinkedIn adjusts its layouts from time to time, so treat values marked "at the time of writing" as current guidance rather than permanent rules.

## Why Image Size Matters More on LinkedIn Mobile (Square and Portrait Win)

![Bar chart of how much of a phone screen each LinkedIn post shape fills: portrait 4:5 most, square next, landscape least](/images/blog/image-sizes-for-linkedin-posts-mobile-1200x675.webp)

LinkedIn is read heavily on phones. In a vertical feed, the shape of your image decides how much of the screen it fills while someone scrolls.

| Shape | Size | Screen space on mobile | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portrait | 1080×1350 (4:5) | The most: the tallest format shown in full | Quote cards, sectioned infographics, photos of people |
| Square | 1080×1080 (1:1) | Good | A dependable default; easy to reuse from Instagram |
| Landscape | 1200×627 (1.91:1) | The least | Charts, screenshots, link previews |

### What Most People Don't Know

LinkedIn compresses images. Uploading at exactly the recommended size gives you more control over how they look than uploading a huge file and letting LinkedIn scale it down. Bigger isn't sharper.

> **Key point:** If you only make one version of a graphic, make it square. If you want the most attention on mobile, make it portrait. Use landscape when the content itself is wide.

## LinkedIn Post Images: Which Size Should You Choose?

### Landscape: 1200×627

LinkedIn's long-standing standard for single images and link previews. It displays fully on desktop and mobile without cropping. The downside is height: on a phone it occupies less of the screen than square or portrait. Use it for charts, screenshots and anything wide.

### Square: 1080×1080

A dependable default. Square images show at a good size on mobile, don't get cropped, and are easy to repurpose from Instagram.

### Portrait: 1080×1350

A 4:5 portrait image is the tallest format that shows in full in the feed. Keep key text away from the very top and bottom edges in case the preview trims slightly on some devices.

### Multi-Image Posts

When you add several images to one post, LinkedIn arranges them in a grid, and images get cropped to fit. Use the same aspect ratio for every image in the set and keep the important part of each near the center.

### File Types

JPG and PNG are the safest choices. Use PNG for graphics with text and sharp edges, and high-quality JPG for photos.

## How to Make a LinkedIn Post Image That Stays Sharp (5 Steps)

![Five steps to a sharp LinkedIn image: design at the target size, use PNG for text, export fresh, check text on a phone, then upload](/images/blog/image-sizes-for-linkedin-posts-steps-1200x675.webp)

### Step 1: Pick the Shape First

Square or portrait for most posts; landscape for wide content.

### Step 2: Design at the Exact Size

Design at the target size, or an exact multiple of it, and export at that size. Uploading odd sizes forces LinkedIn to scale and sometimes crop your image.

### Step 3: Check Text Size on a Phone

Text that looks fine on a laptop can be unreadable in the feed. If you have to zoom in to read it, it's too small. This is the step people skip.

> **Pro tip:** Before you upload, send the exported image to your own phone and look at it at arm's length. If a headline is hard to read there, it'll be hard to read in the feed.

### Step 4: Export Fresh From Your Master File

Each time an image is saved as a JPG, it loses a little quality. Keep a master file in your design tool and export fresh each time instead of re-editing old exports.

### Step 5: Choose PNG or JPG

PNG for text and graphics, JPG for photos.

## Profile Photo and Banner Sizes (400×400 and 1584×396)

### Profile Photo: 400×400

LinkedIn recommends at least 400×400 pixels. It displays as a circle, so frame your face in the center with a little space around it. A plain background and good lighting do more for clarity than a higher resolution.

### Profile Banner: 1584×396

The background image at the top of your profile is 1584×396 (4:1). On desktop, your profile photo overlaps the lower left of the banner; on mobile, it overlaps the center. Keep logos, text and faces toward the right side and the upper half.

## Company Page Logo and Cover Sizes

### Logo: 400×400

At the time of writing, LinkedIn recommends a square logo of 400×400 pixels. It's shown at small sizes next to posts, in search results and in comments, so use a simplified version of your mark if the full logo includes small text.

### Cover Image: 1128×191

The company page cover is 1128×191 at the time of writing: a very wide, short banner that crops differently between desktop and mobile. Treat it as a background, like a pattern or a brand color block with a short tagline in the center.

## LinkedIn Carousel (Document Post) Size: 1080×1080 or 1080×1350 PDF

![Checklist for LinkedIn document carousels: same-size pages, large text and a clear first page, and what to avoid, like mixed sizes and tiny text](/images/blog/image-sizes-for-linkedin-posts-carousel-1200x675.webp)

LinkedIn doesn't have a native image carousel the way Instagram does. The popular "carousel" format is a document post: you upload a PDF (or a PowerPoint or Word file), and LinkedIn displays it as swipeable pages.

| Spec | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Page size | 1080×1080 (square) or 1080×1350 (portrait) |
| Consistency | Every page the same size |
| File format | One PDF for the whole set |
| Limits | 100 MB and 300 pages at the time of writing, per LinkedIn's help center |
| Practical length | Around 5 to 15 pages reads well on a phone |

Design tips:

- Put a clear title and a reason to swipe on page one.
- Use large text, because a phone shows each page small.
- Number your pages if the order matters.
- Add a document title when uploading; it shows above the document in the feed.

## Article and Newsletter Cover Image Size (1200×644)

LinkedIn articles and newsletter issues take a cover image at the top. At the time of writing, LinkedIn recommends 1200×644. The cover also appears when the article is shared in the feed, where it can be cropped slightly, so keep any text centered with generous margins.

## Blurry or Cropped LinkedIn Image? Fixes

| Problem | Fix | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Image looks blurry | Export from your original at the recommended size | Small images get enlarged; odd sizes get scaled |
| Text looks soft | Use PNG for images with text | PNG keeps sharp edges |
| Image cropped in a multi-image post | Use one aspect ratio for the set, subject centered | The grid crops to fit |
| Banner hidden behind your photo | Move key content right and up | The photo overlaps the lower left on desktop and center on mobile |
| Quality drops each edit | Re-export from the master file | Repeated JPG saves lose quality |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best image size for a LinkedIn post?

For most posts, 1080×1080 (square) or 1080×1350 (portrait) gives the most visibility on mobile. Use 1200×627 for wide images like charts and screenshots, and for link previews.

### Does LinkedIn crop post images?

Single images in standard ratios (1.91:1, 1:1 and 4:5) display without heavy cropping. Multi-image posts are arranged in a grid and cropped, and very tall or very wide images can be trimmed.

### What size is a LinkedIn carousel?

LinkedIn carousels are usually document posts. Design each page at 1080×1080 or 1080×1350, keep every page the same size, and upload them together as one PDF.

### What size is the LinkedIn banner?

The personal profile background is 1584×396. The company page cover is different, 1128×191 at the time of writing.

### Why does my LinkedIn image look blurry?

Usually a small image LinkedIn enlarged, an odd size that got scaled, or a JPG re-saved many times. Export from your original at the recommended size, and use PNG for images with text.

### Can I use the same image on LinkedIn and Instagram?

Yes. Square 1080×1080 and portrait 1080×1350 work on both. Check text is large enough for LinkedIn's mobile feed and adjust the caption for each audience.

### What size is a LinkedIn profile picture?

At least 400×400 pixels. It's shown as a circle, so center your face.

### PNG or JPG for LinkedIn?

PNG for graphics with text and sharp edges, high-quality JPG for photos.
