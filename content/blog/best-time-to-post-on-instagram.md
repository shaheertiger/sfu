You've finished a post you're proud of and you're hovering over Share, wondering whether 9 a.m. or 7 p.m. will get it seen. The best time to post on Instagram is whenever your own followers are most active, and Instagram shows you that in Insights under **Most active times**. Until you have that data, test a few sensible windows (before work, lunchtime and early evening in your audience's time zone) and keep the ones that consistently bring the most reach.

![The best time to post on Instagram comes from your own Insights: start with test windows, read Most active times, then keep what works](/images/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram-hero-1200x675.webp)

No single hour works for every account. Instagram doesn't publish a best time, and your followers' routines are not the same as anyone else's. Our [hour-by-hour Instagram heatmap](/best-time-to-post/instagram) shows when Instagram activity peaks overall; your Insights show when *your* followers are on, and that's the number to trust once you have it.

This guide gives you day-by-day starting points, clearly labeled as hypotheses, then shows how to read your Insights, adjust for time zones and Reels, and run a four-week test that tells you your real best time.

## Why There's No Universal Best Time to Post on Instagram

![What decides your best time to post on Instagram: audience type, location, format and season, compared with a single universal hour](/images/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram-why-1200x675.webp)

Instagram ranks posts by how likely each person is to care about them, not purely by how recent they are. Timing still matters, because a post that gets early likes, comments, shares and saves from your followers sends a positive signal. But "early engagement" depends entirely on when your followers are around, and that differs by:

- **Audience type:** a B2B audience may scroll at lunch; a gaming audience may be online late at night.
- **Location:** a 9 a.m. post in London lands at 4 a.m. in New York.
- **Format:** Reels can keep finding new viewers for days, while a feed photo relies more on the hours after you post.
- **Season:** holidays, school terms and daylight saving changes shift routines.

### What Most People Don't Know

The busiest hour on your chart isn't always the best hour to post. It's when the most followers are online, and also when the most accounts they follow are posting. Posting shortly before the peak gives your post time to collect reactions as activity rises.

> **Key point:** The goal isn't to find "the" best time to post on Instagram. It's to find yours, and the only reliable source for that is your own audience data and your own results.

## Best Time to Post on Instagram by Day (7 Starting Points to Test)

![Day-by-day starting times to test on Instagram, from 7 AM on Monday to 11 AM on Sunday, in your audience's local time](/images/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram-by-day-1200x675.webp)

The windows below are **hypotheses, not research findings**. They're based on common daily routines for people who work or study on weekdays, not on a study of Instagram data. Use them to choose what to test first, in your audience's local time.

| Day | Start with | Then test | Reasoning (hypothesis) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | 7 AM | 12 PM | Habitual phone checks before work; a break after catching up |
| Tuesday | 10 AM | 6 PM | The week has settled; people wind down after work |
| Wednesday | 12 PM | 7 PM | A mid-week lunch pause; evening leisure time |
| Thursday | 11 AM | 7 PM | Weekend planning starts; more evening scrolling |
| Friday | 11 AM | 1 PM | Attention may drop as people head out in the evening |
| Saturday | 11 AM | 1 PM | Slower mornings; scrolling between plans |
| Sunday | 11 AM | 7 PM | Relaxed browsing; planning for the week ahead |

If your audience doesn't follow a weekday routine, such as students on break, shift workers, parents of young children or followers in countries with a different working week, ignore the table and go straight to your Insights.

### What Each Day's Slot Suits

- **Monday:** mornings are short and busy, so quick-to-read posts suit the 7 AM slot. Our guide to the [best time to post on Instagram on Monday](/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram-on-monday) walks through a four-week Monday test.
- **Wednesday:** lunch is a natural pause for useful posts like carousels and tips; evenings suit Reels and entertainment.
- **Thursday:** evenings often get busier as people start planning the weekend.
- **Friday:** post earlier in the day, before evening plans take over.
- **Saturday:** very early posts usually reach fewer people, so wait until late morning.
- **Sunday:** relaxed late-morning browsing, then a second round in the evening as people get ready for the week.

## How to Find Your Best Time in Instagram Insights (Free, 2 Minutes)

![Five steps to find your best time to post in Instagram Insights, ending with the Hours view for each day](/images/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram-insights-1200x675.webp)

You need a professional account (Creator or Business) to see Insights. If you're not there yet, our guide on [how to turn on Insights on Instagram](/blog/how-to-turn-on-insights-on-instagram) explains the switch. Menu labels change between app versions, so look for the closest match if something is worded differently.

### Step 1: Open Your Professional Dashboard

Go to your profile and open your **Professional dashboard** or **Insights**.

### Step 2: Go to Your Audience, Not Your Content

Tap the section about your followers or audience. This is where most people go wrong: the content section shows how posts performed, while the audience section shows when followers are online.

### Step 3: Find Most Active Times

Scroll to **Most active times**. The chart has stayed in the audience section for a long time, even as the menus around it have moved.

### Step 4: Switch Between Days and Hours

Use the **Days** view to see which days your followers are most active, then the **Hours** view, tapping each day to see its own hourly pattern.

### Step 5: Pick the Shoulder, Not the Peak

Note the hour before each day's peak. That's your first slot to test for that day.

> **Pro tip:** Most active times means online, not ready to engage. Instagram also only shows audience data once you have enough followers (100 at the time of writing). Below that, use the day-by-day table above.

### Check What Your Own Posts Tell You

Most active times shows when followers are online. Your past posts show when they actually responded. Open Insights, sort your recent posts by reach or views, and note the day and hour each top post went out. If your best performers cluster in one window, that's strong evidence.

| What you see | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| One or two days clearly busier | Your audience has a weekly rhythm | Start testing on those days |
| A flat chart across the day | Followers are spread across zones or routines | Check Top locations, then test three windows |
| Top posts cluster at one hour | Real evidence, not just "online" data | Make that hour your default slot |
| No audience data shown | Too few followers for Insights yet | Use the starting points and track results by hand |

## Time Zones: Post for Your Audience's Clock, Not Yours

![Converting your best time to post on Instagram to your audience's time zone: check Top locations, convert the peak, schedule for the largest region](/images/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram-time-zones-1200x675.webp)

The Most active times chart uses your phone's time zone. If your audience lives elsewhere, convert.

1. Check **Top locations** under your audience in Insights.
2. If one region dominates, convert your chart's peak hours into that region's local time and schedule for it.
3. If your followers are spread across regions, prioritize the largest one, and use Reels for the rest, since they can keep picking up views after the first few hours.

For followers across the US, post by Eastern time, then check how it lands elsewhere: an 11 AM Eastern post goes out at 8 AM Pacific, and a 7 PM Eastern post at 4 PM Pacific. If most of your followers are on the West Coast, schedule by Pacific time instead.

If most of your followers are in one country, start from that country's own data: we publish the [best time to post on Instagram](/best-time-to-post/instagram) as an hour-by-hour heatmap, plus country pages such as [Instagram in the UK](/best-time-to-post/instagram/in-uk), [Instagram in India](/best-time-to-post/instagram/in-india) and [Instagram in Pakistan](/best-time-to-post/instagram/in-pakistan), each in local time with conversions to other zones.

## Best Time to Post Reels vs Feed Posts vs Stories

Reels and feed posts behave differently, so test them separately.

| Format | Who sees it first | How much timing matters | Starting point to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reels | Followers, then people who don't follow you | Less: they can find viewers for days | Evenings and weekends vs a midday slot |
| Feed photos and carousels | Mostly followers | More: early reactions count | Shortly before your followers' peak |
| Save-worthy carousels (tips, checklists) | Followers in a "useful" mindset | Medium | Morning or lunchtime |
| Stories | Followers, for 24 hours | Least | A few spread through the day |

**Reels** can be recommended to people who don't follow you, so they aren't limited to your followers' peak hours. Early reactions from followers still help a Reel get going. See the [best time to post Reels on Instagram](/blog/best-time-to-post-reels-on-instagram) for a Reels-only test.

**Stories** last 24 hours, so exact timing matters less. Post a few spread through the day so they're near the front of the Stories bar when followers open the app. You can schedule them too: in Schedule For You a Story goes out as one 9:16 image or video with no caption, so add stickers, polls or music in the app instead.

## A 4-Week Testing Plan That Finds Your Real Best Time

![A four-week test for the best time to post on Instagram: pick three windows, keep the format steady, measure at 48 hours, keep the winners](/images/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram-test-plan-1200x675.webp)

1. **Pick three windows** from the table or your Insights.
2. **Hold the format steady.** Compare Reels with Reels and carousels with carousels.
3. **Rotate windows** over three to four weeks so each window gets several posts.
4. **Measure after the same time**, for example 48 hours, so late posts aren't at a disadvantage.
5. **Record reach, views, saves, shares and follows** for each post.
6. **Keep the winners, drop the rest**, and retest every few months, especially after daylight saving changes.

| Mistake | Why it skews the test | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Comparing a Reel with a photo | Formats reach people differently | Test one format at a time |
| Measuring some posts after 2 hours, others after 3 days | Older posts have had longer to grow | Pick one checkpoint, such as 48 hours |
| One post per window | One great or weak post decides the result | Several posts per window |
| Posting late because you forgot | The slot you meant to test never gets tested | Schedule the test posts in advance |

## What to Do Next: 3 Situations

- **You have Insights:** open Most active times, pick the hour before each day's peak, and test it against one window from the day-by-day table for four weeks.
- **You're under the follower threshold:** start with the day-by-day table, post at the same three times for a month, and note reach at 48 hours in a simple spreadsheet.
- **Your audience is in another time zone:** check Top locations, convert the peak to their local time, and schedule so you're not setting alarms.

What helps:

- ✓ Testing one format at a time
- ✓ Posting just before your followers' peak
- ✓ Retesting after daylight saving and school holidays

What doesn't:

- ✗ Copying a "best time" chart without testing it
- ✗ Judging a window on one post
- ✗ Deleting and reposting a post that started slowly

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best time to post on Instagram today?

Check Most active times in your Instagram Insights, tap today in the Hours view and aim for just before the busiest hour. Without Insights, test early morning, lunchtime or early evening in your audience's local time.

### What is the best time to post on Instagram on Saturday?

Start with 11 AM and test 1 PM in your audience's time zone. Saturday mornings start slowly, so very early posts usually reach fewer people. Check Most active times for your own Saturday pattern.

### What is the best day to post on Instagram?

It depends on your audience. Switch Most active times to the Days view to see which days your followers are online most, then compare how your own posts performed on each day.

### What is the best time to post Reels on Instagram?

Test Reels separately from feed posts. Evenings and weekends are worth trying because people often have more time for video, but because Reels can reach non-followers over several days, timing usually matters a little less than for photos.

### Does posting at the best time guarantee more likes?

No. Good timing helps your followers see a post while it's fresh, but the content decides whether they engage. A strong post at an average time will usually beat a weak post at a perfect time.

### Should I post at the same time every day on Instagram?

Consistency helps you and your audience, but the best hour can vary by day. Start with one reliable slot per posting day, then adjust individual days as your Insights and results show differences.

### Which time zone does Instagram Insights use?

Most active times uses your phone's time zone. If most of your followers live elsewhere, convert the peak hours into their local time before you schedule.

### Do scheduled posts get less reach than posts shared live?

There's no published evidence that Instagram treats posts from approved scheduling tools differently. Our guide on [whether scheduled posts get fewer views](/blog/do-scheduled-posts-get-less-views) covers the details.
