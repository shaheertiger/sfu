The best time to post on Instagram is whenever your own followers are most active, and Instagram shows you that in Insights under **Most active times**. Until you have that data, test a few sensible windows, such as before work, at lunch and in the evening in your audience's time zone, then keep the ones that consistently bring the most reach.

Any article that names one perfect hour for everyone is guessing. This guide gives you day-by-day starting points, clearly labeled as hypotheses, and shows how to turn them into a schedule based on your real audience.

## Best time to post on Instagram by day: starting points to test

The windows below are **hypotheses, not research findings**. They're based on common daily routines for people who work or study on weekdays, not on a study of Instagram data. Use them to choose what to test first, in your audience's local time.

| Day | Windows to test first | Reasoning (hypothesis) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Early morning; lunchtime | Habitual phone checks at the start of the week; a break after catching up |
| Tuesday | Mid-morning; early evening | The week has settled into a routine; people wind down after work |
| Wednesday | Lunchtime; evening | A mid-week pause; evening leisure time |
| Thursday | Late morning; evening | Planning for the weekend starts; more evening scrolling |
| Friday | Late morning; early afternoon | Attention may drop off as people head out in the evening |
| Saturday | Late morning; early afternoon | Slower mornings; scrolling between plans |
| Sunday | Late morning; evening | Relaxed browsing; planning for the week ahead |

If your audience doesn't follow a weekday routine, such as students on break, shift workers, parents of young children or followers in countries with a different working week, ignore the table and go straight to your Insights.

For a closer look at a single day, see our guide to the [best time to post on Instagram on Monday](/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram-on-monday), which walks through a four-week Monday test.

## Why there's no universal best time

Instagram ranks posts by how likely each person is to care about them, not purely by recency. Timing still matters because a post that gets early likes, comments, shares and saves from your followers sends a positive signal. But "early engagement" depends entirely on when your followers are around, and that differs by:

- **Audience type:** a B2B audience may scroll at lunch; a gaming audience may be online late at night.
- **Location:** a 9 a.m. post in London lands at 4 a.m. in New York.
- **Format:** Reels can keep finding new viewers for days, while a feed photo relies more on the hours after you post.
- **Season:** holidays, school terms and daylight saving changes shift routines.

So the goal isn't to find "the" best time. It's to find yours.

## How to find your best time to post in Instagram Insights

You need a professional account (Creator or Business) to see Insights. If you're not there yet, our guide on [how to turn on Insights on Instagram](/blog/how-to-turn-on-insights-on-instagram) explains the switch.

1. Go to your profile and open your **Professional dashboard** or **Insights**.
2. Tap the section about your followers or audience.
3. Find **Most active times**.
4. Use the **Days** view to see which days your followers are most active.
5. Use the **Hours** view and tap each day to see its hourly pattern.

Menu names change between app versions, but the Most active times chart has stayed in the audience section for a long time.

### How to read the chart

- **Look for the shoulders, not just the peak.** The highest bar is when the most followers are online, and also when more accounts are posting. Try posting an hour before the peak so your post is already gathering engagement as activity rises.
- **Compare days.** If one or two days are clearly busier, start there.
- **Remember the limits.** Instagram only shows audience data once you have enough followers (100 at the time of writing), and "most active" means online, not ready to engage.

### Check what your own posts tell you

Most active times shows when followers are online. Your past posts show when they actually responded. Open Insights, sort your recent posts by reach or views, and note the day and hour each top post went out. If your best performers cluster in a time window, that's strong evidence.

## Time zones: post for your audience, not for you

The Most active times chart uses your phone's time zone. If your audience lives elsewhere, convert.

1. Check **Top locations** under your audience in Insights.
2. If one region dominates, convert your chart's peak hours into that region's local time and schedule for it.
3. If your followers are spread across regions, prioritize the largest one, and use Reels for the rest, since they can keep picking up views after the first few hours.

When your best time is 6 a.m. for you but noon for your audience, a scheduler takes care of it. Schedule for their clock and go back to sleep.

## Best time to post Reels vs feed posts on Instagram

Reels and feed posts behave differently, so test them separately.

### Reels

Reels can be recommended to people who don't follow you, so they aren't limited to your followers' peak hours. Still, early reactions from followers help a Reel get going. Test posting Reels in the evening or on weekends, when people often have more time for video, and compare against a midday slot.

### Feed photos and carousels

Photos and carousels are shown mostly to followers, so they're more sensitive to timing. Posting them shortly before your followers' peak is a good default. Carousels that people save, such as tips or checklists, can work at lunch or in the morning when people are in a "useful" mindset.

### Stories

Stories last 24 hours, so exact timing matters less. Post a few spread through the day so they're near the front of the Stories bar when your followers open the app.

## A simple testing plan

1. **Pick three windows** from the table or your Insights.
2. **Hold the format steady.** Compare Reels with Reels and carousels with carousels.
3. **Rotate windows** over three to four weeks so each window gets several posts.
4. **Measure after the same time**, for example 48 hours, so late posts aren't at a disadvantage.
5. **Record reach, views, saves, shares and follows** for each post.
6. **Keep the winners, drop the rest**, and retest every few months.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best time to post on Instagram today?

Check Most active times in your Instagram Insights, tap today's day in the Hours view and aim for just before the busiest hour. Without Insights, test early morning, lunchtime or early evening in your audience's local time.

### What is the best day to post on Instagram?

It depends on your audience. Switch Most active times to the Days view in Insights to see which days your followers are online most, then compare how your own posts performed on each day. Weekdays suit some accounts, weekends others.

### What is the best time to post Reels on Instagram?

Test Reels separately from feed posts. Evenings and weekends are worth trying because people often have more time for video, but because Reels can reach non-followers over several days, timing usually matters a little less than for photos.

### Does posting at the best time guarantee more likes?

No. Good timing helps your followers see a post while it's fresh, but the content decides whether they engage. A strong post at an average time will usually beat a weak post at a perfect time.

### Should I post at the same time every day on Instagram?

Consistency helps you and your audience, but the best hour can vary by day. Start with one reliable slot per posting day, then adjust days individually as your Insights and results show differences.
