The handle you want belongs to an account that hasn't posted in years, or a creator just announced a rebrand, and you want to grab the old name the moment it frees up.

To catch a released Instagram username, you have to try it yourself: type it into **Edit profile** and save it the moment Instagram accepts it. Instagram doesn't publish when names are released. The one predictable release is a name change: Instagram holds the old name for about **14 days** so the owner can switch back, and after that anyone can claim it.

![How to catch a released Instagram username: the 14-day hold after a name change, then check it in Edit profile](/images/blog/how-to-catch-released-instagram-usernames-hero-1200x675.webp)

Names from deleted, disabled or inactive accounts free up on no schedule at all, and some never do. That's why so many people turn to bots and sellers, and why both tend to end badly.

This guide covers when names become available, how to check a name the right way, how to time a release you're watching, the risks of auto-claim tools, and what to do if you can't get the name.

## When Do Instagram Usernames Get Released? 6 Situations

| Situation | When the name can free up |
| --- | --- |
| Owner changes their username | After the hold of about 14 days, if they don't switch back |
| Owner deletes their account | After deletion completes (Instagram allows up to 30 days to cancel), and not always straight away |
| Account deactivated (temporarily) | Not released. The owner can reactivate and keep it |
| Account disabled or suspended by Instagram | Usually held. Instagram doesn't say when or if it's released |
| Account inactive for years | Not released on its own. Instagram has no inactive-name policy you can use |
| Name breaks Instagram's rules or is reserved | Never available |

![When Instagram usernames get released: predictable after a name change, unpredictable for everything else](/images/blog/how-to-catch-released-instagram-usernames-when-1200x675.webp)

### What Most People Don't Know

The 14-day hold is widely reported, and the app sometimes shows its own countdown when you change a name, so trust the number the app gives you. You can also only change your own username a limited number of times in a 14-day window, so don't burn changes testing names on your main account.

An inactive account is the hardest case. Instagram doesn't free names just because an account looks unused, and there's no request form for inactive names.

> **Key point:** The only release you can plan around is a name change. Everything else, from deleted accounts to inactive ones, frees up on Instagram's timing, if at all.

## How to Check an Instagram Username the Right Way (3 Steps)

The only reliable test is trying to save the name on a logged-in account. Menu labels change between app versions, so look for the same options if yours differ.

![Three steps to check whether an Instagram username is really available](/images/blog/how-to-catch-released-instagram-usernames-check-1200x675.webp)

### Step 1: Open Edit Profile

Open your profile and tap **Edit profile**.

### Step 2: Type the Name Under Username

Tap **Username** and type the name you want.

### Step 3: Save the Moment You See a Tick

If Instagram shows a tick, save straight away. If it says the name isn't available, it's still held.

The step people get wrong is checking from the outside. Visiting instagram.com/name or searching is a clue, not proof: a held or disabled name can look free there. Our guide on [checking if an Instagram username is available](/blog/how-to-check-if-an-instagram-username-is-available) explains why.

> **Pro tip:** Check from the account you actually want the name on. You can only claim a name by saving it on a logged-in account, so a check from a second account wastes the moment.

| Check method | What it tells you | Reliable? |
|---|---|---|
| Edit profile → Username | Whether you can save the name right now | Yes, it's the only real test |
| instagram.com/name | Whether a public profile shows | No, held names can look free |
| Instagram search | Whether an account appears | No, disabled accounts may not show |

## How to Time a Release You're Watching: 4 Tips

- **Know the date.** If a creator or brand announces a rename, the old name is likely to free up about two weeks later, if they don't switch back. Write the date down.
- **Check now and then, not constantly.** Rapid repeated attempts can trigger Instagram's spam limits and temporary blocks on your account.
- **Have a backup ready.** Popular names get grabbed quickly. A close variation saved today beats waiting months for a name that may never free up.
- **Deleted accounts take longer.** Allow for the 30-day cancellation window, and accept that some names never come back.

| Scenario | When to start checking | Odds |
|---|---|---|
| A creator announced a rename | About 14 days after the change | Good, if they don't switch back |
| The owner deleted the account | After the 30-day cancellation window | Uncertain |
| The account looks abandoned | No date to work from | Low |
| The account was disabled | No date to work from | Low |

## Username Bots and Sellers (Use With Caution)

Tools that "auto-claim" usernames use automated logins to hammer Instagram until a name frees up. Sellers offer rare names for a price.

![Safe ways to get an Instagram username compared with bots and sellers](/images/blog/how-to-catch-released-instagram-usernames-risks-1200x675.webp)

### The Real Risks

- **Restricted or disabled accounts.** Instagram's terms forbid automated access, and accounts using these tools get restricted or disabled. The 14-day hold was introduced largely to stop this kind of grabbing.
- **Stolen logins.** Many claiming tools ask for your password.
- **Lost money.** Buying a username is against Instagram's terms. Instagram can take the name back, and sellers often recover the account after you pay.

### The Legitimate Route for Brands

If an account is using your registered trademark or impersonating your business, report it through Instagram's trademark and impersonation forms on [help.instagram.com](https://help.instagram.com). That route can transfer a name, but only for genuine rights issues.

> **Bottom line:** Check the name yourself, by hand, from your own account. Bots and sellers put both the name and your account at risk.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does Instagram hold a username after it's changed?

About 14 days. The previous owner can switch back during that time. After it ends, anyone can claim the name if it meets Instagram's rules.

### Do inactive Instagram usernames get released?

Not on a schedule. Instagram doesn't free names just because an account looks unused, and there's no request form for inactive names.

### Can I get a username from a deleted account?

Sometimes, after the deletion finishes. Instagram gives owners up to 30 days to cancel a deletion, and some names stay unavailable afterwards.

### Is it allowed to use a bot to claim a username?

No. Automated access breaks Instagram's terms and can get your account restricted or disabled. Many claiming tools also put your login at risk.

### Can I buy an Instagram username?

Buying or selling usernames is against Instagram's terms, and the name can be taken back. It's also a common scam.

### Why does a username look free but Instagram won't let me take it?

It's probably held, disabled or reserved. Visiting the profile URL or searching can make a held name look free, so only Edit profile gives a real answer.

### Does a deactivated account release its username?

No. A temporarily deactivated account keeps its name, and the owner can reactivate it.

### Can I get a username that uses my trademark?

Possibly. Report the account through Instagram's trademark form on its help center. That route only works for genuine rights issues.
