To catch a released Instagram username, you have to try it yourself: type it into **Edit profile** and save it the moment Instagram accepts it. Instagram doesn't publish when names are released. The one predictable release is a name change: when someone changes their username, Instagram holds the old one for about **14 days** so they can switch back, and after that anyone can claim it. Names from deleted, disabled or inactive accounts free up on no schedule at all, and some never do.

Here's how releases work, how to check a name, and what to avoid.

## When Instagram usernames become available

| Situation | When the name can free up |
| --- | --- |
| Owner changes their username | After the hold of about 14 days, if they don't switch back |
| Owner deletes their account | After deletion completes (Instagram allows up to 30 days to cancel), and not always straight away |
| Account deactivated (temporarily) | Not released; the owner can reactivate and keep it |
| Account disabled or suspended by Instagram | Usually held; Instagram doesn't say when or if it's released |
| Account inactive for years | Not released on its own; Instagram has no inactive-name policy you can use |
| Name breaks Instagram's rules or is reserved | Never available |

The 14-day hold is widely reported and the app sometimes shows its own countdown when you change a name, so trust the number the app gives you. You can also only change your own username a limited number of times in a 14-day window.

## How to check a username the right way

1. Open your profile and tap **Edit profile**.
2. Tap **Username** and type the name you want.
3. If Instagram shows a tick, save straight away. If it says the name isn't available, it's still held.

Visiting instagram.com/name or searching is a clue, not proof: a held or disabled name can look free there. ## Tips for catching a name you're watching

- **Know the date.** If a creator or brand announces a rename, the old name is likely to free up about two weeks later, if they don't switch back.
- **Check from the account you want it on.** You can only claim a name by saving it on a logged-in account.
- **Check now and then, not constantly.** Rapid repeated attempts can trigger Instagram's spam limits and temporary blocks on your account.
- **Have a backup ready.** Popular names get grabbed quickly. A close variation saved today beats waiting months for a name that may never free up.

## Avoid username bots and sellers

Tools that "auto-claim" usernames use automated logins to hammer Instagram until a name frees up. Instagram's terms forbid automated access, accounts using them get restricted or disabled, and many of these tools ask for your password. The 14-day hold was introduced largely to stop this kind of grabbing.

Buying a username is also against Instagram's terms. Instagram can take the name back, and sellers often recover the account after you pay. If an account is using your registered trademark or impersonating your business, report it through Instagram's trademark and impersonation forms on [help.instagram.com](https://help.instagram.com) instead; that route can transfer a name, but only for genuine rights issues.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does Instagram hold a username after it's changed?
About 14 days. The previous owner can switch back during that time. After it ends, anyone can claim the name if it meets Instagram's rules.

### Do inactive Instagram usernames get released?
Not on a schedule. Instagram doesn't free names just because an account looks unused, and there's no request form for inactive names.

### Can I get a username from a deleted account?
Sometimes, after the deletion finishes. Instagram gives owners up to 30 days to cancel a deletion, and some names stay unavailable afterwards.

### Is it allowed to use a bot to claim a username?
No. Automated access breaks Instagram's terms and can get your account restricted or disabled. Many claiming tools also put your login at risk.

### Can I buy an Instagram username?
Buying or selling usernames is against Instagram's terms, and the name can be taken back. It's also a common scam.
