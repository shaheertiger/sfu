Your child wants to be a YouTuber, or you want to make videos for children, and you're not sure where the rules start. How to start a YouTube channel for kids depends on what you mean. If you're making videos for a child audience, create the channel as an adult and mark it **made for kids** in YouTube Studio. If your child wants their own channel, YouTube generally requires account holders to be at least 13 (the age varies by country), so a younger child's channel should be owned and run by a parent.

![Two safe ways to start a YouTube channel for kids: a channel made for kids, or a parent-managed channel a child takes part in](/images/blog/how-to-start-a-youtube-channel-for-kids-hero-1200x675.webp)

It isn't obvious because the two situations sound the same but follow different rules. One is about children's privacy law and turns off features like comments and personalized ads. The other is about age limits, supervision and keeping your child's details private.

This guide covers both setups step by step, what the made for kids setting changes, options for children under 13 and teens, and a privacy checklist to run before every upload.

## Why "a YouTube Channel for Kids" Means Two Different Things

![A channel made for kids compared with a child running or starring in a channel: who owns it, the key setting and the main concerns](/images/blog/how-to-start-a-youtube-channel-for-kids-two-meanings-1200x675.webp)

| | A channel made for kids | A kid running (or starring in) a channel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who owns the account | An adult or a business | An adult if the child is under YouTube's minimum age; a teen may own it where allowed |
| Who the videos are for | Children | Any audience, often other kids or family |
| Key setting | Audience set to "made for kids" | Audience set honestly, often "not made for kids" |
| Main concerns | Children's privacy law (COPPA in the US), limited features | Age rules, privacy, comments, supervision, contact from strangers |
| Typical content | Songs, stories, crafts, learning videos, toy play | Gaming, hobbies, reviews, vlogs, school projects |

Many family channels are both: a parent runs a channel where a child appears in videos aimed at children. In that case, every section below applies.

### What Most People Don't Know

The made for kids setting isn't optional and isn't just for US creators. In the US, the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) limits how data from children under 13 can be collected. After a 2019 settlement with the Federal Trade Commission, YouTube began requiring every creator, everywhere, to say whether their channel and each video is made for kids. YouTube has said creators may be responsible for complying with COPPA themselves.

> **Key point:** Label your audience honestly. YouTube may change a setting it believes is wrong, and mislabeling can create compliance problems for you, not just for YouTube.

## How to Set Up a Made-for-Kids Channel (6 Steps)

![Key steps to set up a YouTube channel made for kids: an adult account, the audience setting, and checking each upload](/images/blog/how-to-start-a-youtube-channel-for-kids-steps-1200x675.webp)

Content is usually made for kids if children are the main audience, or if it features things like child actors, toys, animated characters, nursery rhymes or games aimed at young children. YouTube's help center lists the factors it considers. Menu labels change between versions of YouTube Studio, so look for the closest match.

### Step 1: Sign In With an Adult Google Account

Ideally use a brand account, so you can add managers later without sharing a password.

### Step 2: Create the Channel and Name It

Create it from the YouTube app or YouTube Studio. If you're stuck, our free [YouTube name generator](/tools/youtube-name-generator) gives ideas that fit YouTube's limits (channel names up to 50 characters at the time of writing).

### Step 3: Set the Channel Audience

In YouTube Studio, go to **Settings → Channel → Advanced settings** and choose whether the channel is made for kids. You can also set this per video.

### Step 4: Write a Clear Description

Use up to 1,000 characters to say what the channel offers and who it's for. Parents read these. The [YouTube bio generator](/tools/youtube-bio-generator) can help you draft it.

### Step 5: Plan Appropriate, Original Content

Avoid misleading thumbnails, loud "shock" titles and anything that copies other creators' characters or music without permission.

### Step 6: Check Each Upload's Audience Setting

Review it in YouTube Studio before the video goes public.

> **Pro tip:** Step 6 is the one people skip. A channel default doesn't guarantee every upload is labelled the way you meant, so check each video in Studio before it goes public.

## What the Made for Kids Setting Turns Off (and What It Costs)

To protect children's data, YouTube switches off a number of features on videos marked made for kids. At the time of writing, these include:

| Turned off | What it means for you |
| --- | --- |
| Comments | No comment section to manage |
| Personalized ads | Non-personalized ads can still run, often for lower income |
| Notifications for the video | Subscribers aren't alerted the usual way |
| Live chat, Super Chat and Super Stickers | No live fan funding |
| Channel memberships and merch features | Fewer ways to earn |
| Cards and end screens | Harder to point viewers to the next video |
| Saving to playlists or Watch later | Viewers can't save it there |
| Playback in the miniplayer | It plays only in the full player |

Check YouTube's current help page for the full list. A channel made for kids earns and grows differently: fewer interactive features, and ads that aren't personalized.

## When Your Kid Wants Their Own Channel: Under 13 vs Teens

YouTube's terms generally require you to be at least 13 to have your own account, and the minimum age is higher in some countries. Teens under 18 also need a parent's or guardian's permission to use YouTube under its terms.

| Age | Options | What the child can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 13 (or your country's minimum) | YouTube Kids | Watch only, with filters and parental controls |
| Under 13 | A supervised account through Family Link | Watch, but not upload or comment, at the time of writing |
| Under 13 | A parent-managed channel | Take part in making videos the parent uploads |
| 13 and over (where allowed) | Their own channel, with parent permission until 18 | Upload, with supervision tools where available |

For teens, agree house rules: what topics are fine, what personal details stay private, and how comments are handled. YouTube also limits some features for younger users; for example, its policies say minors under 16 can only live stream with an adult present.

### How to Set Up a Parent-Managed Channel for Your Child

1. **Create the channel on your own Google account**, or a brand account you control. Keep the password and two-step verification codes to yourself.
2. **Choose a name that doesn't reveal your child's full name**, school or town. A nickname or a theme works better.
3. **Set the audience correctly.** If the videos are aimed at children, mark them made for kids. If they're aimed at a general audience (for example, a teen's gaming commentary), choose not made for kids, and expect YouTube to restrict comments on some videos that feature minors.
4. **Start with private or unlisted uploads** so your child can learn editing and see how things look before anything is public.
5. **Use YouTube Studio's comment settings.** Hold potentially inappropriate comments for review, add blocked words, or turn comments off.
6. **Review every video together before publishing.**

## The Privacy Checklist to Run Before Every Upload

![Privacy do's and don'ts for kids' YouTube videos, like checking backgrounds, next to risks like showing school uniforms or real-time locations](/images/blog/how-to-start-a-youtube-channel-for-kids-privacy-1200x675.webp)

- **No identifying details:** full names, school names, uniforms, house numbers, street signs, car plates or recognizable landmarks near home.
- **No real-time location:** post after you've left a place, not while you're there.
- **Check the background** of every shot, including mail on a table and screens.
- **Keep titles, descriptions and tags free of personal details.**
- **Don't film children in situations that could embarrass them** later, such as bath time, tantrums or private moments.
- **Ask for your child's agreement** as they get older, and respect it if they want a video taken down.
- **Watch for contact attempts** through other platforms or email, and never move conversations with strangers off-platform.

If the channel earns money, the payments go to the account holder's AdSense account, which must belong to an adult. Some places have laws about earnings from children who appear in online content, so check the rules where you live.

## A Child's Own Account With a Changed Birthday (Use With Caution)

Some kids under 13 set up their own account with an older birth date, or a parent does it for them to skip the age rules.

### The Real Risks

- **It breaks YouTube's terms**, and an account that's found to belong to an under-age user can be removed, with its videos.
- **It switches off the protections** built for children's accounts, like supervised content settings.
- **The child holds the password**, so a parent can't control uploads, comments or who gets in touch.

> **Bottom line:** Under the minimum age, the channel belongs to a parent. It's the only setup that's both allowed and safe.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a kid have a YouTube channel?

Not their own under YouTube's minimum age, which is 13 in most countries, but they can appear in videos on a channel a parent owns and runs. Teens who meet the age requirement can have their own channel with a parent's permission.

### What does "made for kids" mean on YouTube?

It's an audience setting every creator must use to say whether a channel or video is aimed at children. Videos marked made for kids have features like comments, personalized ads and notifications switched off to protect children's privacy under laws such as COPPA.

### Can you make money from a YouTube channel for kids?

Yes. Made for kids content can join the YouTube Partner Program and earn from ads, but it only shows non-personalized ads and several fan funding features are switched off, which can mean lower earnings.

### How do I make a YouTube channel for my child under 13?

Create the channel on your own Google account, choose a name that doesn't reveal personal details, set the audience correctly and manage every upload yourself. For watching rather than creating, use YouTube Kids or a supervised account through Family Link.

### What happens if I mark my video as made for kids by mistake?

You can change the audience setting in YouTube Studio for that video or the whole channel. YouTube may override settings it believes are wrong, so be honest about who the content is for.

### Can kids under 16 live stream on YouTube?

Only with an adult present, according to YouTube's policies at the time of writing.

### Who gets paid if a kids' channel earns money?

The account holder, through an AdSense account that must belong to an adult. Check local laws on earnings from children who appear in online content.

### Is YouTube Kids the same as a kids' channel?

No. YouTube Kids is a separate app for watching, with filters and parental controls. It isn't for uploading.
