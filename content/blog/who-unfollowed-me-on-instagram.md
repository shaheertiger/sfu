## Why Instagram Won't Tell You Who Unfollowed You

![What Instagram shows, like your follower count and who follows you now, next to what it never shows, like who unfollowed you and when](/images/blog/who-unfollowed-me-on-instagram-hidden-1200x675.webp)

Instagram sends a notification when someone follows you. It sends nothing when someone leaves. Your profile shows a follower count and a Followers list, and both describe the present moment only. There's no history, no "recently unfollowed" tab and no log you can open.

That's a deliberate choice. Unfollowing is meant to be quiet, so people can curate their feed without awkwardness. The same applies to you: nobody is told when you unfollow them.

So every method that answers "who unfollowed me?" works the same way. It takes a snapshot of your followers, takes another one later and spots the difference. The only question is who takes the snapshots: you, with Instagram's own export, or an app you hand your password to.

### What Most People Don't Know

A drop in your follower count doesn't always mean someone chose to unfollow you. Followers also disappear when they:

- **Deactivate their account**, temporarily or for good
- **Delete their account**
- **Block you**, which removes the follow in both directions
- **Get removed by Instagram**, which regularly clears out accounts it identifies as fake or spam
- **Get removed by you**, if you tapped Remove on your own Followers list

A sudden drop of a handful of followers, with nobody you recognize missing, is often a spam cleanup rather than real people leaving.

> **Key point:** You can only compare snapshots. Instagram has no record of past followers you can view, so request your first export today, even if you only check it next month.

## How to Find Who Unfollowed You by Comparing 2 Exports (Free, No Login)

![Four steps: export your followers now, export again later, rename the older file, then compare both in the checker](/images/blog/who-unfollowed-me-on-instagram-steps-1200x675.webp)

This is the method we recommend. It uses Instagram's official data download, it never asks for your password, and the checker reads your files in your browser without uploading them. Menu labels change between app versions, so look for the closest match if something is worded differently.

### Step 1: Request Your First Export (Your "Before" List)

1. Open Instagram and go to your profile.
2. Tap the menu icon in the top corner, then **Accounts Center**.
3. Tap **Your information and permissions**, then **Download your information**.
4. Choose to download or transfer information and select your Instagram account.
5. Choose **Some of your information** so the file is small and quick to prepare.
6. Under connections, tick **Followers and following**. Leave everything else unticked.
7. Choose to download to your device, set the date range to **All time**, and pick **JSON** as the format.
8. Submit the request.

Instagram tells you when the file is ready, by email and in the same Download your information screen. Small requests like this are often quick, but there's no fixed time. Our guide to [how long an Instagram data export takes](/blog/how-long-does-instagram-data-export-take) covers what to do if it seems stuck. Download the file before the link expires.

### Step 2: Keep the Followers File Somewhere Safe

Unzip the download and look for a folder about connections, then one for followers and following. The file you need is usually named something like `followers_1.json`. Copy it into a folder with the date in its name, such as "Instagram October", so you know which snapshot is which.

Your export holds personal information about you and the people you're connected to, so keep it private.

### Step 3: Request a Second Export Later

Wait a few weeks or a month, then repeat Step 1 with the same settings: Some of your information, Followers and following, All time, JSON. Using the same settings matters, because the checker expects the same kind of file both times.

### Step 4: Rename the Older File (the Step People Miss)

Both exports name the followers file the same way, and the checker sorts files into its two slots by name. A file whose name contains "following" goes into the Following slot, and a file whose name contains "followers" goes into the Followers slot.

So rename the **older** followers file to something like `following-october.json`. Leave the newer one as it is.

> **Pro tip:** Only rename a copy. Keep the original file as it came from Instagram so you can reuse it for a [who doesn't follow me back](/blog/who-doesnt-follow-me-back-on-instagram) check later.

## Why Someone Looks Like an Unfollower When They Aren't (5 False Alarms)

Before you take an unfollow personally, check it. The export is a list of usernames on one date, which leads to a few predictable mistakes:

| What you see | What may have happened | How to check |
|---|---|---|
| A friend appears in the list | They changed their username, so the old name is missing | Search their real name in Instagram |
| A profile says it isn't available | They deactivated or deleted their account | Check again in a few weeks |
| You can't find their profile at all | They blocked you, or deleted the account | Ask a mutual friend, or let it go |
| Several unknown accounts are listed | Instagram removed spam or fake accounts | Tap Open: they often no longer exist |
| Someone you removed is listed | You tapped Remove on your Followers list | That's expected |

The opposite mistake happens too. If someone unfollowed and then followed you again between your two exports, they won't show up at all.

## How to Check One Person Without an Export (10 Seconds)

If you only suspect one or two people, check them directly in the app:

1. Open their profile.
2. Look near their name and the Follow button. If they follow you, Instagram typically shows a **Follows you** label.
3. No label? Go to your own profile, tap **Followers** and search for their username. If they don't appear, they don't follow you now.

This tells you whether they follow you today, not whether they ever did. For the bigger picture, the two-export method is the only reliable option. Our guide to [followers vs following on Instagram](/blog/followers-vs-following-on-instagram) explains how the two lists relate.

## Unfollower Tracker Apps: Instant Lists, Real Risks (Use With Caution)

![Bar chart rating account safety: comparing your own exports highest, checking by hand high, tracker apps that need your password lowest](/images/blog/who-unfollowed-me-on-instagram-methods-1200x675.webp)

App stores are full of apps promising "who unfollowed me" lists, recent unfollowers and "ghost followers". The pitch is that you log in once and the app tracks changes for you, with no files to download. To do that, most of them need your Instagram username and password.

### The Real Risks

- **You hand over full control.** With your password, an app can post, message, follow and change settings as you.
- **It can get your account restricted.** Logins from unfamiliar servers and automated checks can look like a takeover. That can mean locked logins, action blocks or, in serious cases, a disabled account. If that happens, see our guide to the [disabled account appeal](/blog/instagram-disabled-account-appeal).
- **It breaks Instagram's terms**, which prohibit accessing accounts through unauthorized automated means.
- **Your data may be sold or leaked.** Some of these apps make their money from the data they collect.
- **Many charge subscriptions** for something two free exports give you.

### If You Still Want to Use One

- Never type your Instagram password into an app's own login form. A legitimate connection sends you to Instagram's real login screen.
- Read recent reviews for reports of lockouts or hacked accounts.
- Don't use bulk-unfollow or "unfollow all" features.
- Afterwards, review the apps connected to your account in Instagram's security settings and remove anything you don't recognize.
- If you've already used one, change your password and turn on two-factor authentication.

> **Bottom line:** A tracker app saves you two downloads and puts your whole account at risk. Two exports give you the same answer with none of that.

## What to Do After You Find Out Who Unfollowed You

![Checklist of calm responses to unfollows, next to reactions that get Instagram accounts limited](/images/blog/who-unfollowed-me-on-instagram-next-1200x675.webp)

1. **Check the list for false alarms.** Tap Open on each name. Renamed, deactivated and removed spam accounts aren't real unfollows.
2. **Decide whether to unfollow back.** If it was a follow-for-follow account, unfollowing is fair. If it was a friend or a brand you enjoy, keep following. Our guide to [what follow back means on Instagram](/blog/what-does-follow-back-mean-on-instagram) covers when following back makes sense.
3. **Look for a pattern, not a person.** Did the drop come after a quiet month, a topic change or a run of ads? That's more useful than any one name.
4. **Save the newer export as your next "before" list.** Next month, compare against it.
5. **Check monthly, not daily.** People follow and unfollow constantly for reasons that have nothing to do with you.

What keeps your account safe:

- ✓ Comparing your own exports
- ✓ Checking one profile by hand
- ✓ Unfollowing gradually, if at all

What gets accounts limited:

- ✗ Tracker apps that need your password
- ✗ Mass unfollowing in one sitting
- ✗ Follow-for-follow and follow-unfollow schemes
- ✗ Bought followers, which Instagram often removes later

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I see who unfollowed me on Instagram?

Not directly. Instagram only shows who follows you now. Compare two exports of your followers list from different dates to see who's missing from the newer one.

### Does Instagram notify you when someone unfollows you?

No. Instagram notifies you about new followers, not about people who leave, and nobody is told when you unfollow them either.

### How can I track unfollows on Instagram for free?

Request a followers export every month and compare the latest one with the previous one in our free [unfollowers checker](/tools/instagram-unfollowers-checker), after renaming the older file so its name contains "following".

### Why did my follower count drop but nobody unfollowed me?

Accounts that deactivate, get deleted, block you or get removed by Instagram as spam also leave your follower count. A small drop with no names you recognize is often a cleanup.

### Can I see who unfollowed me before my first export?

No. There's no history to download. The method only works from the date of your first export onwards, which is why it's worth requesting one now.

### Are "who unfollowed me" apps safe?

Apps that ask for your Instagram password aren't safe. They get full access to your account, can trigger restrictions and break Instagram's terms.

### How often should I check who unfollowed me?

Once a month is plenty. Checking daily mostly shows normal churn and makes it easy to overreact.

### Does the checker upload my followers list?

No. The files are read in your browser and nothing is sent to a server or stored.
