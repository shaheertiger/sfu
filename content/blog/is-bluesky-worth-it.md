You've watched people you follow move to Bluesky and you're wondering whether to bother. Bluesky is worth it if your audience overlaps with the people who moved there: journalists, academics, developers, artists, writers and anyone who left X for reasons of tone. If your customers are on Instagram and TikTok and nowhere else, you can skip it for now.

![Is Bluesky worth it in 2026? Yes for writers, developers, academics, artists and news accounts; skip it for local and consumer brands](/images/blog/is-bluesky-worth-it-hero-1200x675.webp)

At the time of writing it's a smaller network than X, Threads or Instagram, with tens of millions of accounts and a few million active daily. But engagement per follower is high, and reach doesn't depend on a single algorithm.

This guide covers how Bluesky works and why that matters, who gets value from it, who should skip it, what's missing, and a 60-day test that tells you whether it's worth it for your account.

## How Bluesky Works (and Why Reach Feels Different)

Bluesky is a text-first social network built on the AT Protocol, an open standard. Posts are 300 characters with up to four images or a video. The difference from X and Threads is control: you choose your feeds, you can subscribe to community-made feeds and moderation lists, and your handle can be your own domain, which doubles as verification.

![How Bluesky's feeds compare with algorithm-led platforms like X and Threads](/images/blog/is-bluesky-worth-it-how-1200x675.webp)

| | Bluesky | X | Threads |
|---|---|---|---|
| Default feed | Following, chronological | Algorithmic "For you" | Algorithmic |
| Custom feeds | Yes, made by the community | No | No |
| Post length | 300 characters | 280 without a paid subscription | 500 characters |
| Ads | None at the time of writing | Yes | Yes |
| Your own domain as handle | Yes | No | No |

### What Most People Don't Know

Growth on Bluesky comes from feeds and reposts rather than a discovery algorithm. A new account grows slowly unless it's picked up by a popular feed or a well-followed account reposts it. That's why some people try it for a week, see nothing, and leave, while others in the right niche find it far more engaged than X.

> **Key point:** Bluesky rewards being in the right community, not posting the most. Whether it's worth it depends on who's already there.

## Who Gets Value From Bluesky: 5 Kinds of Accounts

![The five kinds of accounts that get the most value from Bluesky](/images/blog/is-bluesky-worth-it-who-1200x675.webp)

### 1. Writers, Journalists and Newsletters

Link posts aren't suppressed, and the audience reads. If your work lives behind a link, this matters more than follower count.

### 2. Developers and Open-Source Projects

The tech community is large and active, and the open protocol means many developers are building on it.

### 3. Academics and Researchers

Many fields rebuilt their networks there, so it's where conference chatter and paper threads happen.

### 4. Artists and Photographers

Image posts do well, and custom feeds surface niche work that an algorithm might bury. See [is Bluesky good for artists](/blog/is-bluesky-good-for-artists) for more.

### 5. Political and News Accounts in the US and Europe

This is the most active segment of the network.

## Who Should Skip Bluesky, for Now

| Account type | Why Bluesky is a weak fit | Where to focus instead |
|---|---|---|

| Video-first creators | Video exists, but the network is built around text and images | YouTube, TikTok |

## What's Missing on Bluesky (5 Gaps)

![What Bluesky does well compared with what it's missing](/images/blog/is-bluesky-worth-it-gaps-1200x675.webp)

- **No native scheduling.** You post live, or use a scheduler connected with an app password.
- **No ads.** Reach is organic only.
- **No analytics** beyond what you count yourself.
- **No DMs with media.**
- **A smaller audience** than the big three.

None of these are deal-breakers for the five account types above, but they matter if you're used to Instagram Insights or running ads.

## Is Bluesky Worth It for You? A 60-Day Test

Don't decide from someone else's experience. Run a test.

![A 60-day Bluesky test in four steps: set up, follow, post, judge by replies and clicks](/images/blog/is-bluesky-worth-it-test-1200x675.webp)

### Step 1: Set Up Properly

Use a clear handle (your own domain if you have one), a real photo and a bio that says what you post about. See [how to change your Bluesky handle](/blog/how-to-change-bluesky-handle) to use a domain.

### Step 2: Follow 200 Relevant Accounts and Subscribe to 2 or 3 Feeds

Follow people in your field and subscribe to the community feeds where your topic lives. This is how your posts get seen early on.

### Step 3: Post Consistently for 60 Days

This is where most tests fail: people post for a week and stop. Reuse what you already write for X, since most of it fits Bluesky's 300 characters, and reply to people in your niche.

### Step 4: Judge by Replies and Link Clicks, Not Followers

Follower counts grow slowly on Bluesky. Replies and clicks tell you whether the right people are there.

> **Pro tip:** Write down your X or Threads replies and clicks for the same 60 days. Comparing the two is more useful than any Bluesky number on its own.

For more on building an audience, see [how to get followers on Bluesky](/blog/how-to-get-followers-on-bluesky).

### After 60 Days: Keep Going or Stop?

| You see | It means | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| Replies from people in your field, even a few a week | The right audience is there | Keep posting and join more feeds |
| Link clicks close to what X or Threads give you | Bluesky can send real traffic | Make it a regular platform |
| Steady, slow follower growth | Normal for Bluesky | Judge replies and clicks instead |
| No replies or clicks after 60 consistent days | Your audience isn't there yet | Stop, and recheck in six months |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Bluesky still growing in 2026?

It grows in bursts around events on other platforms and is steadier between them. At the time of writing the active base is a few million daily users, concentrated in news, tech, academia and the arts.

### Is Bluesky better than Threads?

It's different rather than better. Threads has a far bigger audience from Instagram and an algorithmic feed; Bluesky is smaller, chronological by default, with custom feeds and an open protocol. Many accounts post to both from one draft.

### Does Bluesky have ads?

No, not at the time of writing. Reach is organic only.

### Can you schedule posts on Bluesky?

Not in the Bluesky app. A scheduler connected with an app password can, and can post the same text to X and Threads at the same time.

### Is Bluesky good for businesses?

It depends on the business. B2B, media, tech and creative businesses whose audience is there can do well; local and consumer brands usually get more from Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and Google Business Profile.

### How long should I try Bluesky before deciding?

Give it about 60 days of consistent posting. Growth on Bluesky comes from feeds and reposts, so a week or two isn't enough to judge.

### Is Bluesky safe to use?

Yes, in the way most mainstream social networks are: it has a moderation team, content labels, and strong blocking and muting tools. See our guide on [whether Bluesky is safe](/blog/is-bluesky-safe) for the details.

### How does Bluesky make money without ads?

Mostly through venture funding at the time of writing, plus small amounts from selling custom domains. See our guide to [how Bluesky makes money](/blog/how-does-bluesky-make-money) for the details.
