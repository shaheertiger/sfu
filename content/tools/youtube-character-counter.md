![YouTube character limits at a glance, in 3 groups: Video title, Description and Tags.](/images/tools/youtube-character-counter-youtube-character-limits-at-1200x675.webp)

The counter checks your text against all four in-tool limits at once, so you can paste anything and see straight away where it fits.

### Video title (100 characters)
YouTube won't let you save a title longer than 100 characters. That's a hard cap, but it isn't the number that matters most: search results, the home feed and suggested videos usually cut titles off well before 100, especially on phones. Aim to get the point across in roughly the first 60–70 characters and treat the rest as extra. The [YouTube title checker](/tools/youtube-title-checker) scores a title on length, keyword placement and readability if you want a second opinion.

Shorts titles follow the same 100-character limit. Live streams and premieres do too.

### Description (5,000 characters)
The description field takes up to 5,000 characters: room for a summary, timestamps for chapters, links, credits and a standard footer. Only the first couple of lines show before viewers tap **more**, so the opening sentence has to carry the description on its own. Long descriptions are fine; padding isn't.

### Tags (500 characters in total)
Tags have one shared budget of 500 characters across all of them, not a limit per tag. The commas YouTube puts between tags count, and tags that contain spaces are widely reported to use extra characters because YouTube wraps them in quotation marks. So a list that measures 490 characters in the counter may still be rejected; keep 20 or 30 characters spare. Our [YouTube tag generator](/tools/youtube-tag-generator) builds a comma list that stays inside the limit.

### Comments (10,000 characters)
A YouTube comment can be up to 10,000 characters. You'll rarely need that much, but creators writing long pinned comments with links, corrections or full credits sometimes do. Replies follow the same limit.

### Channel description, name and handle
These live on your channel rather than on a video, so they aren't separate rows in the counter, but you can still paste text and read the character total. At the time of writing, the channel description allows 1,000 characters, channel names up to 50, and @handles 3 to 30 characters of letters, numbers, underscores, hyphens, periods and middle dots. Check a handle against those rules with the [YouTube handle checker](/tools/youtube-handle-checker).

## Why You Need a YouTube Character Counter

![Key point from the YouTube Character Counter page: YouTube Studio does show a counter while you type, but only in the field you're typing in, and only once you're already in the upload flow.](/images/tools/youtube-character-counter-key-point-1200x675.webp)

YouTube Studio does show a counter while you type, but only in the field you're typing in, and only once you're already in the upload flow. Most titles and descriptions get written somewhere else first: in a script document, a spreadsheet of upcoming videos, a notes app, or a message to a client for approval. By the time the text reaches YouTube Studio, a title that's 112 characters long means rewriting on the spot.

The counter lets you check text where you write it. Paste a draft and you see, at once, whether it would fit as a title, a description, a tag list or a comment, plus word and line counts that help when you're aiming for something skimmable rather than just under the limit.

### What it counts
- **Every character you type**, including spaces, punctuation and line breaks
- **Emoji**, counted by the code points that make them up, so simple emoji count as one and some combined emoji (skin tones, families, flags) count as more
- **Words**, separated by spaces or line breaks
- **Lines**, so you can see how many lines a description or comment runs to

### How YouTube's count can differ
YouTube's own counter and this one agree for plain text. They can differ slightly for complex emoji and for the hidden quotation marks on multi-word tags described above. If you're within a few characters of a limit, check the final version in YouTube Studio.

### What YouTube won't accept
At the time of writing, YouTube rejects the angle brackets `<` and `>` in titles and descriptions. The counter doesn't block them, so replace them with something else (an arrow emoji or a dash) before you paste.

## How to Use the YouTube Character Counter

![How to Use the YouTube Character Counter, in 3 steps: checking tags, checking a description with chapters and privacy.](/images/tools/youtube-character-counter-how-to-1200x675.webp)

1. Write or paste your title, description, tag list or comment into the box above.
2. Read the three totals: characters, words and lines.
3. Look at the limits list. Each row says how many characters you have **left** or how many you're **over** for a title, description, tags and a comment.
4. Edit in the box until the field you need shows characters left.
5. Copy the text back into YouTube Studio, your script or your scheduling tool.

### Checking tags
Type or paste your tags exactly as you'll enter them, separated by commas, for example `sourdough, sourdough for beginners, bread recipe`. The tags row counts the commas and spaces too, which gets you close to YouTube's number. Leave headroom for multi-word tags.

### Checking a description with chapters
Paste the full description, including timestamps like `0:00 Intro` on their own lines. Each line break counts as a character, and the lines total tells you how long the chapter list is. YouTube needs the first timestamp to be `0:00` for chapters to appear, along with at least three timestamps.

### Privacy
The counter runs in your browser. Nothing you paste is uploaded or stored, so it's safe for unreleased titles and client drafts.

## Worked Examples: Fitting YouTube's Limits
### Trimming a title that's over 100
"I Tried Cooking Every Single Dinner From Scratch for 30 Days Straight and Here Is Exactly What Happened to My Budget" is 116 characters, so YouTube won't save it. Cutting filler words ("Every Single", "Straight", "Here Is Exactly") and moving the number up front gives "30 Days of Cooking From Scratch: What It Did to My Budget", which is 57 characters. It's under the limit and fully visible in most places titles get cut.

### Writing a description that works when collapsed
A description that fits easily under 5,000 characters can still fail if the first lines are wasted on links or hashtags. A simple structure that works for most videos:

1. **One or two sentences** saying what the viewer gets, such as "Cook from scratch for 30 days and see what it does to your grocery budget." (74 characters).
2. **Chapters**, one timestamp per line, starting at `0:00`.
3. **Links** mentioned in the video, each on its own line with a short label.
4. **Your standard footer**: channel links, newsletter, credits and disclosures.

Paste the whole thing into the counter to see the total and the line count, then check that step 1 makes sense on its own.

### Measuring a tag list
`sourdough, sourdough for beginners, sourdough starter, bread recipe, how to make sourdough bread, no knead bread, baking` is 120 characters in the counter, commas and spaces included. Five of those seven tags contain spaces, so YouTube's own count may come out a little higher. At 120 there's plenty of room; the headroom only matters as you get close to 500.

## YouTube Character Counter Use Cases
### Creators writing titles in batches
If you plan a month of videos at once, write all the working titles in one list and check each against the 100-character cap and the 60–70 character visible range before you film. Titles that need trimming tell you early that the idea isn't sharp enough yet.

### Editors and channel managers
When you upload for a creator, you're often handed a description in a document with links, sponsor copy and credits. Paste it in before you start the upload to see whether it fits under 5,000 characters, and whether the sponsor line sits in the first two lines where viewers actually see it.

### Brands and small businesses
A product demo, a tutorial or a local business video needs a description with a phone number, address, links and a call to action. The counter shows how much room the essentials take and how much is left for useful detail. Keep the most important link in the opening lines.

### Agencies sharing drafts for approval
Clients approve titles and descriptions in email, chat or shared documents, not YouTube Studio. Run the approved text through the counter before it goes into the upload so a last-minute "can we add one more line?" doesn't push it over a limit.

### Podcasters and long-form channels
Episode descriptions with guest bios, show notes, timestamps and links run long fast. The line count helps you keep the chapter list readable, and the character count tells you when it's time to move full show notes to your website and link to them.

### Community managers writing pinned comments
A pinned comment is often the place for corrections, links mentioned in the video, or a question to start discussion. The 10,000-character limit is generous, but long comments collapse after a few lines, so use the line count to keep the important part on top.

## Best Practices for YouTube Titles, Descriptions and Tags

![Best Practices for YouTube Titles, Descriptions and Tags: do front-load every field, keep hashtags few and relevant and reuse a standard footer. Don't write to the limit.](/images/tools/youtube-character-counter-best-practices-1200x675.webp)

### Front-load every field
Titles get cut off, descriptions get collapsed and comments get truncated. In each case, put the most important words first: the topic in the first half of the title, the value of the video in the first sentence of the description, the key correction in the first line of a pinned comment.

### Don't write to the limit
A 100-character title and a 5,000-character description are ceilings, not targets. Shorter titles are easier to read on a phone, and a description with a strong first paragraph, chapters and a few links usually serves viewers better than one stuffed with keywords.

### Keep hashtags few and relevant
Hashtags in the title or description count as characters like any other text. At the time of writing, YouTube shows up to three hashtags above the title and ignores all of a video's hashtags if there are more than 60, so a handful of relevant ones is plenty.

### Reuse a standard footer
Links to your website, newsletter, gear list and social profiles rarely change. Write that footer once, measure it, and you'll know exactly how much of the 5,000 characters is left for each video's own text.

### Be careful with styled text
Bold or italic Unicode letters can make a description line stand out, but they're harder for screen readers and for YouTube search to read, and they still count as characters. Use them sparingly, for a heading or two. The [YouTube bold text generator](/tools/youtube-bold-text-generator) shows how each style looks.

### Write in your language, check in YouTube
Non-Latin scripts and emoji are counted one per code point here. If your title is close to 100 characters and uses complex emoji, confirm the final count in YouTube Studio.

## Frequently asked questions
### What is the YouTube title character limit?
A YouTube title can be up to 100 characters, including spaces and emoji. Titles are often cut off in search and on phones well before that, so put the key words in roughly the first 60–70 characters.

### How many characters can a YouTube description have?
A YouTube description can have up to 5,000 characters. Only the first couple of lines show before viewers expand it, so lead with the most important information.

### What is the character limit for YouTube tags?
All of a video's tags together can use up to 500 characters. Commas count, and tags with spaces may use extra characters for the quotation marks YouTube adds, so leave a little spare.

### How long can a YouTube comment be?
A YouTube comment can be up to 10,000 characters, and replies have the same limit. Long comments are collapsed after a few lines.

### Does the counter count spaces, line breaks and emoji?
Yes. Every space and line break counts as one character, and emoji are counted by their code points, so some combined emoji count as two or more.

### How long can a YouTube channel description be?
The channel description (the "About" text) can be up to 1,000 characters at the time of writing. Paste it into the counter and read the character total.

### Is my text saved or sent anywhere?
No. The counter works in your browser and doesn't upload or keep what you paste.
