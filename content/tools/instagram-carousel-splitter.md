## What the carousel splitter does
Upload a wide image to the Instagram carousel splitter, choose how many slides you want (from 2 to 10) and whether they should be portrait or square, and the splitter cuts it into equal pieces. Each slide is exported at 1080×1350 or 1080×1080 and numbered in order. Post them as one carousel and the image flows from one slide to the next as followers swipe.

### Slide shapes
- **Portrait, 1080×1350 (4:5):** takes up more of the screen in the feed, so the panorama looks bigger
- **Square, 1080×1080 (1:1):** shows more of a short, very wide image without as much cropping top and bottom

### Why equal slides matter
A seamless carousel only works if every slide is exactly the same size and the cuts line up. Splitting by hand in a photo editor is fiddly; the splitter divides the width evenly so the edge of one slide meets the next.

### Private by design
The image never leaves your device. Splitting happens in the browser and the slides download directly.

## How to split an image for an Instagram carousel
### 1. Pick or design a wide image
Phone panoramas, landscape photos and wide graphics designed for a carousel all work. For full sharpness, the source should be about 1,080 pixels wide per slide, so five portrait slides want an image around 5,400 pixels wide.

### 2. Choose the number of slides
More slides means each one shows a narrower strip at a larger size. Fewer slides keeps more of the scene on screen at once. For most panoramas, three to five slides is a comfortable swipe.

### 3. Choose portrait or square
Match the shape to your image. A tall-enough scene benefits from portrait; a very thin panorama usually looks better square so less is cropped from the top and bottom.

### 4. Download and keep the order
Download every slide and keep the numbering. When you upload, add them in order from slide 1 so the picture runs left to right.

## Designing a carousel that swipes smoothly
### Put something across the seams
A road, a shoreline or a line of text that crosses from one slide into the next nudges people to keep swiping. If every slide ends on empty sky, the carousel can feel like separate photos.

### Make slide 1 a strong opener
The first slide is what appears in the feed and on your profile grid. Make sure it holds an interesting part of the picture on its own, not just the dim left edge of a sunset.

### Keep one shape throughout
Instagram shows every slide in a carousel at the same shape, so mixing sizes leads to unexpected crops. The splitter outputs identical slides, and if you add other images to the same carousel, resize them first with the [Instagram photo resizer](/tools/instagram-photo-resizer). ## Frequently asked questions
### How do I make a seamless Instagram carousel?
Create or choose one wide image, split it into equal slides with this tool, and upload the slides in order as a single carousel post. Because each slide is the same size, the edges line up as people swipe.

### What size should seamless carousel slides be?
Each slide should be 1080×1350 for portrait or 1080×1080 for square. Your source image should be roughly 1,080 pixels wide per slide to stay sharp.

### How many slides can I split my image into?
Between 2 and 10. Pick fewer slides for a quick swipe or more for a long, detailed panorama.

### Why does my carousel show a gap or jump between slides?
Usually the slides were different sizes or were uploaded out of order, or Instagram cropped them to a different shape. Using equal slides from the splitter and keeping their numbering avoids both.

### Does the carousel splitter upload my photo?
No. The image is processed in your browser and the slides save straight to your device.
