## What the grid maker does
The Instagram grid maker cuts one image into a set of tiles that, posted in the right order, rebuild the full picture on your Instagram profile. Choose a 3×1, 3×2, 3×3 or 3×4 grid, pick portrait or square tiles, preview the result and download every tile numbered in the order to post them.

### Grid sizes, from 3x1 to a 3x4 grid
| Grid | Posts | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| 3×1 | 3 | A banner-style row, launches, quick announcements |
| 3×2 | 6 | A wide photo or a bold headline graphic |
| 3×3 | 9 | A full profile takeover or rebrand reveal |
| 3×4 | 12 | Tall artwork or posters with lots of detail |

### Tile shapes
Each tile is exported at 1080×1350 (4:5 portrait) or 1080×1080 (1:1 square). Portrait tiles match the most common feed shape; square tiles suit older-style layouts and graphics designed on a square canvas.

### Everything stays on your device
The Instagram grid maker splits your image in the browser. Nothing is uploaded and there's no account to create.

## How to make an Instagram grid
### 1. Upload a high-resolution image
A 3×3 grid of portrait tiles needs a source that's 3,240 pixels wide to keep each tile at full 1080-pixel width. Smaller images work but may look soft.

### 2. Choose the grid and tile shape
Pick the number of rows and whether tiles should be portrait or square. The preview shows where each cut falls on your image.

### 3. Download the numbered tiles
Tiles are numbered in posting order. Tile 1 is the bottom-right piece, because Instagram adds each new post to the top-left of your grid. Post them from 1 upward and the last tile you post completes the top-left corner.

### 4. Post them in sequence
Publish the tiles one after another without other posts in between. If anything else lands in the middle, every following tile shifts by one place and the picture breaks.

## Getting a clean result on your profile
### Mind the 3:4 grid crop
At the time of writing, Instagram's profile grid shows each post cropped to 3:4, which trims a little from the sides of portrait tiles and more from square ones. The full tile still appears when someone opens the post, but on the grid there will be small gaps at the seams. Keep faces, text and logos away from tile edges.

### Make each tile work alone
Followers see your tiles one at a time in their feed, not as a grid. A tile that's just a slice of sky or half a word can look like a mistake. Give each piece something readable, or write captions that explain the series.

### Watch pinned posts
Pinned posts appear at the start of your profile grid, which can push your tiles out of alignment. Unpin posts before you start, or plan the grid with the pinned slots in mind.

## Frequently asked questions
### What order do I post Instagram grid tiles in?
Start with tile 1, which is the bottom-right piece, and work up to the highest number, which is the top-left. Instagram places each new post at the top-left of your grid, so this order rebuilds the image.

### How do I make a 3x3 puzzle grid on Instagram?
Upload your image, choose the 3×3 grid and a tile shape, then download the nine numbered tiles. Post them from tile 1 to tile 9 with nothing in between, and the full picture appears across the top three rows of your profile.

### What size should Instagram grid tiles be?
The grid maker exports tiles at 1080×1350 for portrait or 1080×1080 for square. Portrait is the more common choice for feed posts.

### Why do the edges of my grid not line up perfectly?
At the time of writing, the profile grid crops each post to 3:4, so a sliver at the sides of each tile is hidden on the grid. Keeping important detail away from tile edges avoids awkward cuts.

### Can I post a grid as a carousel instead?
You can, but a carousel shows one slide at a time rather than building a picture on your profile. For a seamless swipe, use the carousel splitter, which cuts a wide image into slides.

### Is my image uploaded anywhere?
No. The image is split in your browser and the tiles download straight to your device.
