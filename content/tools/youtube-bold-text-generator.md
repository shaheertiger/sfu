### The best choice for YouTube

Sans bold blends in with YouTube's interface and keeps descriptions readable. Serif bold is a little more formal. The decorative styles are best for a single word, since long runs of them are hard to read on a TV screen or small phone.

## Where bold text works on YouTube

### Comments: use YouTube's formatting first

Comments already support simple formatting. Wrap a word in asterisks for bold (`*bold*`), underscores for italic (`_italic_`), or hyphens for strikethrough (`-strike-`). That's real formatting, so prefer it in comments and replies. Unicode bold also pastes into comments if you want a style the shortcuts don't offer. For reply ideas you can edit yourself, try the [YouTube comment generator](/tools/youtube-comment-generator).

### Video descriptions

Descriptions can be up to 5,000 characters and have no formatting options, so Unicode bold is useful for section labels like "𝗖𝗵𝗮𝗽𝘁𝗲𝗿𝘀," "𝗟𝗶𝗻𝗸𝘀" and "𝗚𝗲𝗮𝗿 𝗜 𝘂𝘀𝗲." Keep timestamps in plain digits, such as 0:00 and 2:15, so YouTube can still turn them into chapters and clickable links.

### Video titles: proceed carefully

Titles allow 100 characters. Unicode bold often displays in titles, but YouTube may strip, reject or alter some special characters, and how titles appear can differ across the app, TV and embedded players. More importantly, titles drive search, and a bold keyword may not match what viewers type. If you try it, bold one non-keyword word and check the published title on several devices.

### Channel name and handle

Your handle must be 3–30 characters of letters, numbers, underscores, hyphens and periods, so styled letters won't work there. Your channel name is more flexible, but YouTube may limit unusual characters, and styling it makes your channel harder to search for. ## Best practices for bold text on YouTube

### Protect your searchable words

YouTube search and Google both read your title and description. Keep the topic, product names and phrases you want to rank for in plain text, and use bold only for labels and calls to action.

### Keep links and timestamps plain

URLs, @handles, hashtags and timestamps only work in normal characters. A bold timestamp won't become a chapter, and a bold link won't be clickable.

### Mind accessibility

Viewers using screen readers may hear bold Unicode read as symbols or not at all. Don't put the only copy of important information, like a discount code or a safety note, in a styled form.

### Count characters before you paste

Some styled characters count as two. If your description is close to 5,000 characters, trim before you publish.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you bold text in a YouTube title?

Not with formatting. You can paste Unicode bold characters, and they often display, but YouTube may remove or reject some characters, and styled words can hurt search. Test on a private or unlisted video first.

### How do I bold text in a YouTube comment?

Put asterisks around the text, like `*this*`. YouTube turns it into real bold. You can also use underscores for italic and hyphens for strikethrough.

### Does bold text work in YouTube descriptions?

Descriptions don't support formatting, but Unicode bold characters display there. Use them for short section headings and keep links and timestamps plain.

### Will Unicode bold hurt my video's ranking?

YouTube hasn't said styled text is penalized, but search matches the characters you use. If your keyword is styled, the video may not appear for that keyword. Keep keywords plain.
