## X Username Rules at a Glance
An X username, still called a Twitter handle by most people, has to be 4 to 15 characters long and can only use letters, numbers and underscores. This X username checker tests a handle against those rules as you type. When it passes, a button opens x.com/yourhandle so you can see whether an account already has it.

## X Username Rules in Detail (4–15 Characters, Letters, Numbers, Underscores)
### Length: 4 to 15 characters
New X usernames need at least 4 characters and can't go past 15. That's tight next to Instagram's 30 or TikTok's 24, so a handle that works elsewhere often won't fit here. The checker shows the length as a count out of 15, and if you go over it tells you by how much: "Too long: 19 characters, the limit is 15." Some very old accounts kept handles shorter than 4 characters, but X doesn't let anyone register new ones that short, so a 3-letter name fails with "Too short: X needs at least 4 characters."

### Letters, numbers and underscores only
The underscore is the only symbol X allows. A period, hyphen, apostrophe, ampersand, emoji or accented letter such as é fails with "Only letters, numbers and underscores are allowed." That catches a lot of people bringing a handle over from Instagram, where periods are fine. golden.hour has to become golden_hour or goldenhour on X.

### No spaces
A space anywhere in the handle fails the check with "Spaces aren't allowed." Your display name, the bold name above your @handle, can have spaces; the handle itself can't. If you want two words to read clearly, use an underscore (jane_doe) or rely on the display name to show the full name.

### Underscores can go anywhere
X doesn't limit where an underscore goes or how many you use. _pixel_ is a valid handle, and so is pixel__studio. Valid doesn't mean wise, though: underscores at the edges and in pairs are hard to hear when someone reads your handle aloud and easy to forget when someone types it.

### Capitals don't make a handle unique
You can register @PixelStudio with capitals and X will show it that way, but X treats it as the same handle as @pixelstudio. If either exists, the other can't be claimed. The checker keeps your capitals so you can see how the handle will look.

### Words X blocks
X also refuses handles that contain certain words, such as "Twitter" and "Admin", and names that impersonate other people or brands. Those rules aren't in this checker, because X can change them without notice and its sign-up form is the authority on them.

### How the rules compare with other platforms
X is one of the strictest platforms on length. If a handle passes here, it usually fits Instagram, TikTok and Pinterest too, but not always Facebook, which needs 5 characters and doesn't allow underscores. All values are at the time of writing.

X doesn't offer a public availability check, so no website can confirm a handle is free. ## How to Check if an X Username Is Available
1. **Type the handle you want.** Leave out the @, or paste it with one; the checker removes it either way.
2. **Read the format result.** "Follows X's rules" means it can be registered if nobody holds it. "Not allowed" comes with the reason, such as a period or a 16th character.
3. **Open the profile.** Tap the **Open x.com/…** button. A profile means the name is taken; an "account doesn't exist" message means it may be free.
4. **Try a variation if needed.** Pick one of the suggestions, or paste your shortlist into **Check a list** and open the profile links one by one.
5. **Claim it on X.** On a new account, enter it during sign-up. On an existing one, go to Settings, then Your account, then Account information, then Username (menu labels change between app versions). X tells you as you type whether the name is available, and that's the definitive check.
6. **Update your links.** After a rename, your old handle stops pointing to you. Update the link in your other bios, your website and your email signature.

## Best Practices for Picking an X Handle
### Keep it short enough to type
Fifteen characters is the ceiling, not the target. Shorter handles are easier to type on a phone and take less room in replies, where your @handle appears in front of what people write.

### Use the display name for the details
Your display name can hold spaces, capitals and up to 50 characters, so it can carry what the handle can't, like "Riverside Bakery · Portland". Keep the handle clean and put the description in the name and bio. The [X bio generator](/tools/x-bio-generator) helps with the 160-character bio, and the [X name generator](/tools/x-name-generator) gives display name and handle ideas if you're starting from nothing.

### Avoid lookalike characters
Mixing the letter O and the number 0, or l and 1, makes a handle easy to mistype and easy to impersonate. If your preferred handle is taken, a clear modifier (hq, shop, studio, your city) is better than a near-copy of someone else's.

### Match across platforms where you can
People who find you on one network will look for the same name on the next. If the exact handle isn't free everywhere, keep the same base and use one consistent modifier, like riversidebakery on X and riverside.bakery on Instagram.

### Know what the checker doesn't test
The checker follows X's published format, not X's private records. It can't see blocked words, names held by suspended or deactivated accounts, or names X reserves. Treat a pass plus an empty profile page as "worth trying," and let X's form have the last word.

### Don't trust tools that promise more
No website can see X's username database. A tool that asks for your X password or claims to reserve a handle for you can't do what it says and may be after your account. This checker never asks you to sign in.

### Accessibility: use capitals for screen readers
Screen readers read a lowercase run like @riversidebakery as one long word. Writing it as @RiversideBakery in your bio and posts helps them read it as two words, and it doesn't change the handle.

### How many characters can an X username have?
An X username can have 4 to 15 characters at the time of writing. Older accounts may have shorter handles, but new ones need at least 4.

Yes. Twitter became X, and the username rules didn't change with the rename. Your @handle works the same way, and twitter.com profile links now lead to x.com.

### Can an X username have a period or a hyphen?
No. Only letters, numbers and underscores are allowed. Replace a period or hyphen with an underscore, or remove it.
