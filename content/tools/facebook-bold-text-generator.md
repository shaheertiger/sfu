### Bold isn't formatting here

What you copy is a string of Unicode symbols that look like heavy letters. Facebook displays them as text, but spell check, translation and search won't recognize them as words.

## Where bold text works on Facebook

### Long posts on Pages and profiles

Bold makes structure possible in a long post. A simple pattern: a bold headline, a short plain paragraph, then bold labels such as "𝗪𝗵𝗲𝗻," "𝗪𝗵𝗲𝗿𝗲" and "𝗛𝗼𝘄 𝘁𝗼 𝗷𝗼𝗶𝗻," each followed by a line of plain text.

### Group posts and Group rules

Some Groups offer a formatting toolbar on desktop with real bold, headings and lists. If you see it, use it; real formatting is better for search and accessibility. Where it isn't available, including the Group rules section, Unicode bold is a handy way to number and title each rule.

### Reels captions and comments

Short bold phrases work in Reels captions and in comments. In a long comment thread, a bold "𝗨𝗽𝗱𝗮𝘁𝗲:" at the start of your reply helps people spot new information.

### Events and fundraisers

Event details and fundraiser descriptions accept Unicode bold. Use it for the date, the venue label or the goal so the essentials stand out, and repeat critical details in plain text.

## Best practices for bold text on Facebook

### Choose bold or caps, not both

Bold Unicode in all capitals reads like shouting. Sentence case in sans bold is usually enough emphasis.

### Plan edits ahead

You can't select a styled word and un-bold it, and autocorrect won't catch typos in styled letters. Proofread before you generate, and regenerate the whole phrase if you need to change it.

### Keep important details readable for everyone

Screen readers may skip Unicode bold or read it as a list of symbols, so someone might miss a time, price or address written only in bold. Keep those details in plain text, and use bold just for the label in front of them.

### Skip bold in ads and names

Ad text goes through Meta's review, and unusual characters can work against you there. Names on profiles and Pages have their own rules, so don't bold them either.

### Watch out when cross-posting

If you share the same post to Instagram, check the caption there too: hashtags and mentions written in bold won't work on either platform.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I make text bold on Facebook?

Type your text into this generator, tap a bold style to copy it, then paste it into your Facebook post or comment. Facebook doesn't have a bold button for regular posts, so Unicode is the usual workaround.

### Can I bold only part of a Facebook post?

Yes. Generate just the words you want bold, copy them, and paste them where they belong. The rest of the post stays in Facebook's normal text.

### Why doesn't my bold text show up for some friends?

Their phone or browser doesn't have a font that includes those characters. Sans and serif bold are the safest; black squared and bubble letters are the most likely to appear as boxes.

### Can I edit bold text after posting?

You can edit the post, but you'll need to paste a new bold version of any words you change. You can't remove the bold with a formatting button because it isn't formatting.

### Does Facebook allow bold text in Messenger?

You can paste Unicode bold into Messenger chats and it will display like any other text. Messenger itself doesn't offer a bold option.
