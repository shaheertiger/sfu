## What the Facebook Bio Generator Does
This free Facebook bio generator writes 12 short bios for the Intro section of your Facebook profile, each one under the 101-character limit Facebook uses for that field at the time of writing. You type what you do and what you're into, pick a tone, and copy the bio you like. Every result shows its character count, so you know it fits before you paste it.

The bio is the short line under your profile picture and name, at the top of the Intro box. It's the first thing people read when they open your profile from a comment, a group post or a friend request. Most people either leave it empty or write something in a hurry. The generator gives you a dozen well-formed starting points, so you choose and adjust instead of facing a blank line.

### How it works
The bios are built from hand-written templates for each tone, filled in with your words and shuffled. There's no AI model behind it, and nothing you type is sent to a server: it all runs in your browser. Any template that would go over 101 characters with your words is skipped, and duplicates are removed. Press **More ideas** for a fresh set from the same inputs.

### Why a short bio is hard to write
A good Facebook bio does three jobs in roughly 15 words: says who you are, hints at what you care about, and sounds like you. Seeing 12 variations side by side makes it easier to spot the shape that fits, even if you end up rewriting half the words.

## Facebook Bio Types You Can Generate
The generator has five tones. Below, each type shows real output for a sample person, so you can see what you'll get. Your results use your own words.

### Friendly personal bios
Warm and approachable, with an emoji at the start or end. Good for most personal profiles, especially if you mainly use Facebook for family, friends and groups. For a "dad of three" into "backyard grilling":

- Glad you're here! Backyard grilling from a dad of three ✌️
- ☕ Backyard grilling • dad of three • Say hi

### Professional bios for freelancers and job seekers
Clear and to the point, sometimes ending in a short call to action such as "Message me anytime". With emoji switched off, for a copywriter in "B2B tech":
- Copywriter sharing what works in B2B tech
- Experienced copywriter. Writing about B2B tech.
- B2B tech · Copywriter · Say hi

### Bios for business owners
Owners, realtors and other people who are the face of their business often get found through their personal profile. The professional and friendly tones both work. For a "bakery owner" and "sourdough":
- Bakery owner specializing in sourdough. Posts about what I love
- 📈 Sourdough · Bakery owner · Message me anytime
- Bakery owner. Practical insights on sourdough.

### Funny bios
Self-aware and light, for people whose posts are mostly jokes anyway. For a "teacher" with a weakness for "bad puns":

Funny templates are the ones most likely to need a tweak, because they slot your words into a set phrase. Read each one aloud before you use it.

### Minimal bios
A few words and a separator. Good for students, private people and anyone who'd rather let the Intro details speak. For a "biology student" into "trail running", emoji off:

- Trail running, simply.

### Aesthetic bios
Lowercase, soft and a little dreamy, for artists, photographers and lifestyle accounts. For an "artist" who paints "watercolor":

- a little corner for watercolor ✨
- watercolor, coffee and quiet places 🕊️

## Facebook Bio Best Practices the Generator Bakes In
### The 101-character limit
Every bio the tool shows fits within 101 characters, counted the way most text tools count them: letters, spaces, punctuation and each emoji. Facebook's own counter can treat some emoji, such as flags or skin-tone variants, as more than one character, so if a bio is right at the edge, paste it into Facebook and let Facebook decide.

| Field | Where it appears | At the time of writing |
|---|---|---|
| Bio | Top of the Intro section on your profile | 101 characters |
| Details | Intro: work, education, current city, hometown, relationship | Separate fields, mostly chosen from lists |
| Links | Intro: website and social links | Separate fields |
| Featured | Intro: pinned photos or stories | Separate section |
| Name | Above your profile | Your real name, under Facebook's name rules |

### One line, no line breaks
The profile bio is a single short block of text. So where a template would split lines on another platform, the Facebook version joins them with a middle dot (·), as in "Sourdough · Bakery owner · Message me anytime". You get the same structure without breaks that might be dropped.

### Emoji used with restraint
With emoji on, each bio uses one emoji, sometimes two, chosen to match the tone: a wave or a coffee cup for friendly, a pin or briefcase for professional, a moon or sparkle for aesthetic. Turn emoji off and any emoji that sat between two words becomes a separator, so the bio still reads cleanly.

### Plain, searchable words
The bios use ordinary text, not styled Unicode letters. That keeps them readable by screen readers and lets the words you typed, such as your job or your hobby, mean what they say. If you want one word to stand out, add it from the [Facebook font generator](/tools/facebook-font-generator) yourself, but keep the important words plain.

### Your words, not filler
Every template uses your role or your topic, usually both. Generic lines such as "living my best life" aren't in the pool, because they could belong to anyone.

## How to Use the Facebook Bio Generator
1. **Fill in "What you do".** A short noun works best: "nurse", "baker", "dad of three", "student". The field takes up to 40 characters.
2. **Fill in "What you post about".** Short and specific: "hiking and dogs", "sourdough", "homes in Denver". The field takes up to 60 characters, but shorter inputs leave room for more templates to fit under 101.
3. **Pick a tone.** Friendly, professional, funny, minimal or aesthetic.
4. **Turn emoji on or off.**
5. **Copy a bio.** Each result shows its count, such as "47 / 101 characters". Tap the copy button.
6. **Paste it into Facebook.** On your profile, find the Intro section, choose to add or edit your bio, paste and save. Menu labels change between Facebook versions and between the app and the website, so look for "Add bio" or "Edit bio" under Intro if the wording has moved.

### Tips for better inputs
| Instead of | Try | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "I work as a registered nurse" | "nurse" | The templates add their own grammar around your words |
| "lots of things" | "hiking and dogs" | Specific topics make specific bios |
| "photography, travel, food, fitness, books" | "street photography" | One or two topics fit; five don't |
| A phrase ending in a full stop | The same phrase without it | Avoids double punctuation |

If you see fewer than 12 bios, your inputs are long enough that some templates go over 101 characters and are skipped. Shorten either field and more appear.

## How to Get the Most Out of Your New Bio
### Make one word specific
Swap one generic word for a precise one. "Nurse who loves hiking and dogs" becomes "ICU nurse, two beagles, every trail in Ohio". The template gives you the shape; the detail makes it yours.

### Put the most important words first
On some screens only the start of the bio is visible at a glance, so lead with what you most want people to know.

### Don't repeat your details
If your job and city already show in the Intro details, spend the bio on something those fields can't say: a passion, a promise or a bit of humor.

### Check who can see it
At the time of writing, the bio is shown publicly on most profiles. Don't add anything you wouldn't put on a public sign, such as a phone number or your street.

### Use the same voice everywhere
If you also have an Instagram, X or TikTok profile, reuse the idea but respect each limit. The [Instagram bio generator](/tools/instagram-bio-generator) and [X bio generator](/tools/x-bio-generator) work the same way with their own limits.

### Update it when things change
New job, new city, new hobby: a bio that's two years out of date is worse than none. A fresh set takes seconds.

### Know the limits of the tool
It writes profile bios only, from English templates on this page, and it can't post to Facebook for you. It counts characters, not how Facebook wraps text on each screen.

## Frequently asked questions
### How long can a Facebook bio be?
A Facebook profile bio can be up to 101 characters at the time of writing. That includes spaces, punctuation and emoji, and every bio this generator shows already fits.

### Where is the bio on a Facebook profile?
The bio sits at the top of the Intro section, under your profile picture and name. On the app and the website, look for "Add bio" or "Edit bio" under Intro.

### Is this Facebook bio generator free?
Yes. It's free, needs no account and runs in your browser. Press **More ideas** for as many sets as you like.

### Is this an AI bio generator?
No. It fills hand-written templates with your words and shuffles them, all on your device. That keeps it fast and private, and you should still edit the result so it sounds like you.

### Can I use emoji and line breaks in my Facebook bio?
You can use emoji, and each one counts toward the 101 characters. The bio is a single line, so this generator joins parts with a middle dot (·) instead of line breaks.

### Can I use these bios for a Facebook Page?
You can use them as a starting point, especially the professional tone. A Page's intro is a separate field from a profile bio, so check its limit when you paste.

### Can I use the same bio on Instagram, TikTok or X?
Often, yes, with small changes. Each platform has its own limit: our generators for Instagram, TikTok and X each count against that platform's limit.

### How often should I update my Facebook bio?
Update it whenever something in it stops being true, such as a new job, city or main interest. Otherwise, a quick check every few months is plenty.
