## What makes a good TikTok caption
A TikTok caption generator gives you a head start on the words under your video, which decide whether viewers understand what they're watching and what to do next. This one builds ideas around the habits below.

### A first line that earns the next second
Only the opening words show before the caption is cut off in the feed, so the first line has to stand on its own. A question, a bold claim or a "wait until the end" tease gives people a reason to keep watching.

### A clear subject
Say what the video is about in plain words. "3 ways to fix a squeaky door" tells viewers and TikTok search more than "life hack!!".

### One call to action
Ask for one thing: a follow, a comment, a save or a visit to the link in your bio. Several requests in one caption usually means none of them gets done.

### A few relevant hashtags
Two to five hashtags that describe the topic are easier to read than a wall of tags, and they keep the focus on your first line.

## TikTok caption limits and rules
### How long can a TikTok caption be?
TikTok captions can be up to 4,000 characters at the time of writing. Each idea shows its count against that limit, but shorter captions usually read better because most viewers only see the start.

### What the generator doesn't do
It's template-based, not AI. It doesn't suggest trending hashtags or sounds, and it can't predict views. To spot trends, browse TikTok itself. If your videos stall, our guide on [why a TikTok isn't getting views](/blog/why-is-my-tiktok-not-getting-views) covers what to check.

### Captions for photo posts
The same ideas work for TikTok photo posts. Lead with what the swipe reveals, such as "Slide 4 is the one everyone asks about".

## Caption ideas by style
These examples show the kind of first lines each style produces for a video about a budget meal-prep routine.

### Hook-first
"You don't need $100 for a week of lunches. Here's what $30 gets me." Follow with one line of context and a save prompt.

### Storytime
"I used to spend $15 a day on lunch until my landlord raised the rent." It sets up a story the video pays off.

### How-to
"Meal prep 5 lunches in 45 minutes: step 1 is the one people skip." Promising a specific number of steps tells viewers what they'll get.

### Promo
"Our new glass containers fit a full week of prep. Link in bio for the launch price." Keep the offer and the action in one sentence.
