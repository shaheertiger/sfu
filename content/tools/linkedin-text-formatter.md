## What the formatter does
LinkedIn's composer doesn't offer bold, italic or list buttons for regular posts at the time of writing. This formatter fills that gap: write your post, select a word or phrase, apply a style, and copy the finished text into LinkedIn. It also counts characters against the 3,000 limit and previews where the "…more" link is likely to fall.

### Text styles
- **Bold** and *italic*, plus bold italic
- Underline and strikethrough
- Monospace, useful for code, commands or file names

These are Unicode characters that look styled, not real formatting, which is why they survive being pasted into LinkedIn.

### Lists
Add bullet points or a numbered list to break a dense paragraph into steps or takeaways. Lists are plain text with a symbol or number at the start of each line, so they paste cleanly into posts and comments.

### Live count and "…more" preview
The counter shows how many of LinkedIn's 3,000 characters you've used. The preview marks roughly where the feed truncates a post, about the first 200 characters or three lines. LinkedIn varies this by device and screen width, so treat the marker as a guide, not an exact cut.

## How to format a LinkedIn post
### 1. Write or paste your draft
Start in the editor with plain text. Get the wording right first; styling is easier to judge once the post reads well without it.

### 2. Select and style
Highlight a word or short phrase and choose a style. Keep it to the few words you'd underline on paper: a key number, the main claim, a section label.

### 3. Add structure
Turn a run of related sentences into a bulleted or numbered list. Leave a blank line between sections so the post doesn't arrive as one block.

### 4. Check the preview, then copy
Look at what sits above the "…more" marker. If that opening doesn't give someone a reason to expand the post, rewrite it. When you're happy, copy the result and paste it into LinkedIn.

## Writing for the LinkedIn feed
### The hook is the first two lines
Most people decide whether to tap "…more" from the opening alone. Put the outcome, the surprising detail or the question first, and move background to later. A preview that shows only "I've been thinking a lot lately about…" wastes the space.

### Use 3,000 characters as a ceiling, not a target
The limit leaves room for a detailed story, but a long post still needs short paragraphs and a clear point. If you're past 2,000 characters, check whether a list or a link to a longer article would serve readers better.

### Lists beat long paragraphs on mobile
On a phone, a paragraph of five sentences becomes a tall wall of text. A three-item list with a bold label on each item can be scanned in seconds.

## Accessibility and search
### Screen readers and styled text
Unicode bold and italic are separate characters from the regular alphabet. Some screen readers read them letter by letter or skip them, so a fully styled sentence can become unreadable. Style a few words, never the whole post.

### Keep key terms plain
LinkedIn search and hashtags may not match styled letters to their plain equivalents. Leave names, job titles, company names, hashtags and @mentions unstyled so people can find them. For more styles beyond these, the [LinkedIn font generator](/tools/linkedin-font-generator) has the full set.

### Images and links still matter
Formatting improves readability, but a well-sized image often does more for attention in the feed. See [image sizes for LinkedIn posts](/blog/image-sizes-for-linkedin-posts) before you attach one.

## Frequently asked questions
### How do I bold text on LinkedIn?
LinkedIn's regular post composer doesn't have a bold button at the time of writing. Write the post here, select the words, apply bold and paste the result into LinkedIn. The bold letters are Unicode characters, so they keep their look.

### What is the LinkedIn post character limit?
A LinkedIn post can be up to 3,000 characters. The formatter counts as you type and shows how many you have left.

### Where does LinkedIn cut off a post with "…more"?
Roughly after the first 200 characters or three lines, depending on the device and how the post uses line breaks. The preview gives an approximate position so you can make the opening count.

### Will formatted text look the same on mobile?
Usually, yes, because the styles are characters rather than formatting. Very old devices or unusual fonts can occasionally show a box instead of a styled letter, which is another reason to style sparingly.

### Do bullet points from this tool work in LinkedIn comments?
Yes. Lists are plain text, so they paste into comments, posts and your About section the same way.

### Is my post saved or uploaded anywhere?
No. The formatter runs in your browser and nothing is sent to a server.
