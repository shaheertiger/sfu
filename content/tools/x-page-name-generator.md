## What makes a good brand name on X
On X (formerly Twitter), a business account has two names to get right: a display name of up to 50 characters and an @handle of only 4 to 15. The best brand accounts use the display name for the full, recognizable company name and squeeze a clear, unmistakable version into the handle. This generator helps with the squeezing.

### The handle is the hard part
Long company names rarely fit in 15 characters; "Brightwater Analytics" has 20 letters. Your handle needs a shortened form people still connect to the brand.

### It should match your other channels
If your Instagram, TikTok and YouTube handles already exist, aim for the same handle on X. When that's not possible, use the same fallback pattern you used elsewhere so customers can predict it.

### It must not lean on someone else's brand
Avoid handles that include a competitor's or partner's name, or that suggest an official connection you don't have. X's rules on impersonation and misleading accounts apply to businesses as well as people.

## How to use the X page name generator
Start from your brand, not from what's trendy.

### Step by step
1. **Enter your brand or product word.** Use the core of your company name ("brightwater") or what you sell.
2. **Choose a style.** Professional and minimal suit most companies; cool, aesthetic and funny fit consumer brands; gaming fits studios.
3. **Copy from 30 ideas.** Every handle idea respects X's rules: 4–15 characters, letters, numbers and underscores. Tap to copy.
4. **Regenerate or rephrase.** Try an abbreviation or product name as the keyword.
5. **Verify before launch.** The generator can't check X. Try the handle when you sign up or in your account settings, and run a trademark search for any new brand name.

### Shortening tricks that keep the brand clear
- Drop a generic word: "Brightwater Analytics" becomes **@brightwater**.
- Add a common brand suffix: **@brightwaterhq**, **@getbrightwater**, **@brightwaterapp**.
- Use an established abbreviation only if customers already use it: **@bwanalytics**.
- Avoid vowel-stripping ("@brtwtr") unless your logo already does it.

## X name rules and limits for brand accounts
Values at the time of writing; check [X's help center](https://help.x.com) if a name is rejected.

| Field | Limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Display name | 50 characters | Full brand name, spaces and most symbols allowed |
| Handle (@username) | 4–15 characters | Letters, numbers, underscores only |
| Bio | 160 characters | What you do, who it's for, a link |
| Standard post | 280 characters | URLs count as 23 |

### Use the display name to do more
Your display name can carry the full company name plus a short descriptor, such as "Brightwater Analytics | Data for Retail". Keep the brand name first so search and recognition still work.

### Plan extra handles early
Larger brands often add separate accounts for support, careers or regions (for example **@brightwaterhelp** or **@brightwater_uk**). Keep the shared root short enough that these extensions still fit inside 15 characters.

## Frequently asked questions
### Is the X page name generator free?
Yes. It runs in your browser with no account, and your keywords aren't uploaded.

### What's the difference between a display name and a handle on X?
The display name is the bold name shown on posts and your profile, up to 50 characters. The handle is the unique @username, 4–15 characters, used for mentions and your profile URL.

### My brand name is longer than 15 characters. What should I do?
Use the display name for the full name and pick a shortened handle: drop a generic word, add "hq" or "app", or use a well-known abbreviation. Keep the same handle pattern across your other networks.

### Can I change my brand's X handle later?
Yes, you can change it in your account settings. Old mentions and links that used the previous handle may stop pointing to you, so avoid frequent changes.

### Does the generator check trademarks or availability?
No. It only suggests names. Check availability on X and search trademark records for any new brand name before you announce it.
