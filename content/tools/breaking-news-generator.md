## What this breaking news generator makes
This breaking news generator turns your headline into a TV-style news graphic: a white headline bar near the bottom of the frame with a thin red rule, a red **BREAKING NEWS** tab on its top-left corner, and a red **LIVE** badge and a time stamp in the top-right corner. Put it over your own photo or a plain background, then download it as a PNG or JPG for a group chat, a meme account or a playful brand post.

The design is generic on purpose. It doesn't copy any real channel's graphics, logo or colors, and the **BREAKING NEWS** label can't be changed. The tool itself shows a reminder whenever this style is on: "For jokes and memes only. Don't make fake news that could mislead people or pass for a real broadcast."

### Nothing is uploaded
The image is drawn in your browser. Your photo never leaves your device, there's no sign-up and no watermark.

## How to make a breaking news meme
### 1. Add a photo, or use a plain background
Tap **Add a photo or drop it here (optional)**. A reaction photo, your pet looking guilty or a picture of the office kitchen all work. Without a photo, pick a **Background color**; the default dark slate looks like a studio backdrop.

### 2. Type the headline
Replace "Type your headline here" with your joke. Short is funnier: one or two lines in the bar reads like real news. Press Enter for a line break where you want one. If the text is too long, it shrinks to fit and the tool says so.

### 3. Adjust the look
- **Font**: Sans looks most like a news ticker; Serif and Rounded are there too. **Bold** is on by default.
- **Font size**: starts at 64; raise it for a short punchline.
- **Alignment**: Left by default, like a real lower-third, or Center and Right.
- **LIVE badge** and **Time stamp**: both on by default. The time starts at your current time, and you can type any time in **Time shown**, such as 03:00 for a midnight-snack scandal.

### 4. Pick a size and download
Choose an **Output size** and **PNG** or **JPG**, then press the download button. If you added a photo, **Original** keeps its shape.

### Switch styles in one tap
The breaking news generator is a mode of the [add text to image](/tools/add-text-to-image) tool, so under **Style** you can also switch to **Plain**, **Band** or **Outline** without losing your text and photo.

## Fake breaking news generator ideas that stay funny
The best fake breaking news is obviously a joke the moment someone reads it. Some ideas:

- **Everyday drama**: "BREAKING: Local man finally replies to email from March." "Office coffee machine fixed; productivity expected to rise."
- **Pets**: a photo of your dog next to an empty plate with "Suspect remains silent. Investigation ongoing."
- **Celebrations**: "Sources confirm Maya is turning 30 and would like cake." A breaking news birthday card lands well in a group chat.
- **Sports and games**: "Team loses fourth game in a row; fans 'not even surprised'." Keep it about your own team, not real players.
- **Brand humor**: a bakery's "BREAKING: Cinnamon rolls back in stock at 8am," or a creator's "New video drops tonight." Your own news, told in a playful format.

## Use the breaking news generator responsibly
A breaking news graphic borrows the authority of real journalism, which is exactly why it's funny and exactly why it can go wrong. Follow these rules:

### Make the joke obvious
If a stranger could scroll past it and believe it, rewrite it. Absurd stakes, everyday subjects and a punchline are what make it read as parody.

### Don't imitate real broadcasters
Don't add a real network's logo, name, anchors or on-screen branding, and don't recreate a real broadcast's graphics. That is what turns a meme into something that "passes for a real broadcast", and it can also infringe trademarks.

### Never fake real news
Don't make headlines about real disasters, emergencies, elections, crimes, health scares or deaths, even as a joke. Images like these get screenshotted and shared without context, and they can cause real panic or harm.

### Leave real people out of it
Don't put invented events or quotes on real people, and don't target someone who isn't in on the joke. Celebrate a friend who'll laugh; don't embarrass a classmate or a coworker.

### Label parody when you post
Say it's a joke in the caption, for example with "parody" or "#parody". Platforms have rules on misleading and manipulated media, and a clear label helps your post stay on the right side of them. Before posting a photo of other people, you can hide faces with the [blur image](/tools/blur-image) tool, and the [remove EXIF data](/tools/remove-exif-data) tool strips location details from a photo before you share it.

## Frequently asked questions
### Is this breaking news generator free?
Yes. It's free, runs in your browser, needs no sign-up and adds no watermark.

### Can I change the "BREAKING NEWS" label?
No. The red label always reads BREAKING NEWS. You can change the headline, font, size, alignment, time and whether the LIVE badge shows.

### Can I add my own photo?
Yes. Add any JPG, PNG, WebP or GIF image. iPhone HEIC photos may need converting to JPG first. Your photo stays on your device.

### How do I change or hide the time?
Type a new time in **Time shown**, or untick **Time stamp** to hide it. Untick **LIVE badge** to remove the red LIVE bug.

### Is it legal to make fake breaking news?
Obvious parody shared as a joke is generally fine, but laws differ by country. Making something that could pass for real news, imitating a real broadcaster's branding, or making false claims about real people can break platform rules and, in some cases, the law. Keep it clearly a joke and label it as parody.

### What's the best size for an Instagram story?
Choose **9:16 · Stories/Reels** (1080×1920). The headline bar sits near the bottom of the frame, so preview the story before you share it and shorten the headline if the reply bar covers part of it.
