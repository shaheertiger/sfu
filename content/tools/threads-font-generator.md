### Bold styles (serif, sans and italic)

The four bold styles are the workhorses. **Bold (sans)** is the closest match to the clean typeface Threads uses, so it reads as emphasis rather than decoration. **Bold (serif)** looks more like a printed headline. The two bold italic styles suit a quote or a single punchy word. For a page with only the bold styles, see the [bold text generators](/tools/instagram-bold-text-generator) for other platforms, which use the same nine bold variants.

### Italic and sans-serif

**Italic** and **Italic (sans)** add a lighter emphasis, useful for a book title, a product name or an aside. **Sans-serif** looks almost like normal text, which makes it a subtle choice when you only want a slight change.

### Script and bold script

Script styles give a handwritten, cursive look that's popular in bios for lifestyle, beauty and wedding accounts. Plain **Script** borrows a few letters (ℋ, ℯ, ℴ, ℊ and others) from a separate symbol block, so some letters sit slightly differently from the rest. **Bold script** is more consistent and easier to read on a phone.

### Gothic and bold gothic

Gothic (also called fraktur or blackletter) looks like an old newspaper masthead or a band logo. It's eye-catching, but it's the hardest style to read at small sizes, so keep it to a word or two.

### Double-struck and monospace

**Double-struck** (𝔻𝕠𝕦𝕓𝕝𝕖) has outlined letters with a technical feel. **Monospace** looks like a typewriter or code editor, which suits developer accounts and changelog-style posts.

### Bubble, squared and their black versions

**Bubble** puts each letter in a circle, and **Black bubble** and **Black squared** use filled shapes. The squared and black styles only exist as capitals, so "hot take" comes out as 🅷🅾🆃 🆃🅰🅺🅴 whatever case you type. On some phones these render as emoji-sized symbols, so they work best for a short label.

### Wide, small caps and tiny

**Wide (vaporwave)** uses full-width characters, including a wider space, for the spaced-out aesthetic look. **Small caps** gives capital shapes at lowercase height, though s and x look like normal lowercase because Unicode has no small-cap versions of them. **Tiny** raises letters into superscript; q has no superscript form and stays as it is.

### Upside down, strikethrough, slash and underline

**Upside down** flips letters and reverses the order, so it reads correctly when you turn your phone around. The last three styles add a line through or under each character. They work by attaching a combining mark to every letter, which means each styled letter counts as two characters.

## Best Practices and Caveats for Threads Text

### Style a few words, not the whole post

A fully styled post is tiring to read and looks spammy. One styled hook, label or keyword per post is plenty.

### Screen readers read these characters poorly

This is the biggest drawback. Unicode styles are math and symbol characters, not real formatting. Many screen readers read them out letter by letter, as symbol names such as "mathematical bold capital H", or skip them entirely. If your audience includes people who use screen readers, keep every important word in plain text and use styling only for decoration.

### Styled words may not be searchable

To search, 𝗰𝗼𝗳𝗳𝗲𝗲 and "coffee" are different strings. Keep your topic, your product names and anything you'd like people to find in plain text.

### Keep the count in mind

Each styled letter is still one visible character, but the strikethrough, slash and underline styles add a second character to every letter. Threads posts allow 500 characters at the time of writing, so a heavily underlined post fills up twice as fast. Our guide to the [Threads character limit](/blog/threads-character-limit) explains how links and the topic tag count.

### Your username can't use fonts

Threads usernames come from your Instagram username and only allow plain letters, numbers, periods and underscores. Fonts work in your display name, bio, posts and replies, but not in your @handle.

### Check a second device

Rendering depends on the reader's phone and fonts. If you rely on a less common style (squared, tiny or gothic), look at the post from a second device or a browser before you pin it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I change my font on Threads?

You can't change the font itself, but you can paste Unicode characters that look like a different font. Type your text into this Threads font generator, copy a style and paste it into your post, reply or bio.

### Does Threads have a bold text option?

Threads doesn't offer a font menu for post text, so most people use Unicode bold instead. Copy the Bold (sans) or Bold (serif) style here and paste it where you want emphasis. Menu options change between app versions, so check your composer too.

### Will people see my styled Threads text on every phone?

Most current phones and browsers display these styles correctly. Older devices or unusual fonts can show empty boxes for some characters, especially the squared and tiny styles, so test on a second device if it matters.

### Can I use fancy fonts in my Threads bio?

Yes. Your bio accepts Unicode text, so paste a styled word or phrase into Edit profile. Keep your main keywords plain so people can still find you.

### Do styled letters count as more than one character on Threads?

Most styles add one visible character per letter, but strikethrough, slash through and underline add a combining mark to each letter, so they take up two. Leave some room under the 500-character limit if you use them.

### Are Threads fonts bad for accessibility?

They can be. Screen readers often read Unicode styles as symbol names or skip them, so styled text can be confusing or invisible to blind and low-vision readers. Use styles sparingly and never for essential information.
