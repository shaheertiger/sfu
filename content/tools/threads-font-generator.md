## What the Threads Font Generator Actually Does

Threads doesn't let you choose a typeface. Every post, reply and bio is shown in the app's own font, and there's no font menu to change it. This Threads font generator works around that by replacing your letters with look-alike Unicode characters: 𝐛𝐨𝐥𝐝, 𝑖𝑡𝑎𝑙𝑖𝑐, 𝓼𝓬𝓻𝓲𝓹𝓽, 𝔤𝔬𝔱𝔥𝔦𝔠, ⓑⓤⓑⓑⓛⓔ and 19 more. Because they're characters rather than formatting, they survive copy and paste, and everyone who reads your post sees the same thing you do.

The characters come mostly from Unicode's Mathematical Alphanumeric Symbols block, which was created so that scientists could write 𝐁 and 𝔅 as distinct symbols. Phones and browsers include fonts for them, which is why they display almost everywhere. Threads treats them as ordinary text, exactly like emoji.

### What happens to your text

- **Latin letters (A–Z, a–z)** are swapped for the styled version.
- **Digits (0–9)** are swapped only in styles that have styled digits: Bold (serif), Bold (sans), Sans-serif, Double-struck, Monospace, Bubble, Black bubble, Wide and Tiny. Other styles leave numbers plain.
- **Accented letters** keep their accent: "café" becomes 𝐜𝐚𝐟𝐞́ in Bold (serif), with the accent placed on the styled letter.
- **Everything else**, such as punctuation, emoji, ß, ø and non-Latin scripts, passes through unchanged.

### Why people style their Threads text

- **A hook that stands out.** One bold line at the top of a post ("𝗛𝗼𝘁 𝘁𝗮𝗸𝗲:") can stop a scroll without shouting in capitals.
- **A bio with some personality.** Your Threads bio has 150 characters at the time of writing and no formatting options, so a styled word or two is the only way to add emphasis there.
- **Structure in longer posts.** With 500 characters per post, a bold label for each point makes a list easier to skim.
- **A recognizable series.** A recurring post such as "𝔉𝔯𝔦𝔡𝔞𝔶 𝔣𝔦𝔫𝔡𝔰" is easy to spot when it looks the same every week.

## Best Practices for Styled Threads Text

### Style a few words, not the whole post

A fully styled post is tiring to read and looks spammy. One styled hook, label or keyword per post is plenty.

### Screen readers read these characters poorly

This is the biggest drawback. Unicode styles are math and symbol characters, not real formatting. Many screen readers read them out letter by letter, announce symbol names such as "mathematical bold capital H", or skip them entirely. If your audience includes people who use screen readers, keep every important word in plain text and use styling only for decoration.

### Keep searchable words plain

To a search box, 𝗰𝗼𝗳𝗳𝗲𝗲 and "coffee" are different strings. Keep your topic, product names and anything you'd like people to find in plain text.

### Mind the character count

Most styles turn each letter into one visible character, but strikethrough, slash through and underline add a second, invisible character to every letter. Threads posts allow 500 characters at the time of writing, so an underlined post fills up twice as fast. Our guide to the [Threads character limit](/blog/threads-character-limit) explains how links and the topic tag count too.

### Pick one or two styles and stick to them

Mixing gothic, bubble and script in one post looks chaotic. Choose a heading style and maybe an accent style, and reuse them so your posts look consistent.

### Check on a second device

Rendering depends on the reader's phone and fonts. Bold, italic and script are widely supported; squared, tiny and some gothic letters are the most likely to show as empty boxes on older devices.

### Don't use fonts to impersonate

Styled names can make an account look official or like someone else's. Pretending to be a brand or another person breaks Threads' rules and misleads people.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I change my font on Threads?

You can't change the font itself, but you can paste Unicode characters that look like a different font. Type your text into this Threads font generator, copy a style and paste it into your post, reply or bio.

### Does Threads have a bold text option?

Threads doesn't offer a font menu for post text, so most people use Unicode bold instead. Copy Bold (sans) or Bold (serif) here and paste it where you want emphasis. Menu options change between app versions, so check your composer too.

### Will people see my styled Threads text on every phone?

Most current phones and browsers display these styles correctly. Older devices or unusual fonts can show empty boxes for some characters, especially the squared and tiny styles, so test on a second device if it matters.

### Can I use fancy fonts in my Threads bio?

Yes. Your bio accepts Unicode text, so paste a styled word or phrase into Edit profile. Keep your main keywords plain so people can still find you.

### Do styled letters count as more than one character on Threads?

Most styles add one visible character per letter, but strikethrough, slash through and underline add a combining mark to each letter, so they take up two. Leave room under the 500-character limit if you use them.

### Will styled fonts hurt my reach on Threads?

There's no public evidence that Threads penalizes Unicode text, but styled words are harder to search and some readers find them annoying. Use them for emphasis, not for the words you want to be found by.

### Are Threads fonts bad for accessibility?

They can be. Screen readers often read Unicode styles as symbol names or skip them, so styled text can be confusing or invisible to blind and low-vision readers. Use styles sparingly and never for essential information.
