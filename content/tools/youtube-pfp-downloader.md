## What the YouTube PFP downloader does
The YouTube PFP downloader finds a channel's profile picture (its "PFP", avatar or "DP") and gives it to you as a full-size image file instead of the small circle YouTube shows next to videos. Paste almost any YouTube link and you get the picture in three fixed sizes, the original upload, and the channel banner when the channel has one.

On YouTube itself there's no download button for a profile picture. Right-clicking the circle on a computer usually gets you a small, low-resolution copy, and on a phone you can't right-click at all. The downloader skips that: it reads the channel's public page the same way your browser would, finds the address of the picture on YouTube's image servers, and requests it at the size you choose.

## Available Image Types
Each lookup returns two kinds of image from the same channel.

### Profile picture (PFP, avatar, DP)
The profile picture is the image YouTube shows in a circle next to the channel name: on the channel page, under every video, beside comments and in subscription lists. People call it a PFP (profile picture), an avatar, a channel icon, or a DP (display picture). They're all the same file, and the downloader returns it in four sizes.

The file itself is square. YouTube crops it into a circle on screen, so the corners you download may contain parts of the picture you've never seen on YouTube, such as background or text the creator expected to be cropped away.

### Channel banner
The banner, or channel art, is the wide header across the top of a channel page. When a channel has one, the downloader shows a preview with **View full banner** and **Download banner** buttons that fetch the full-size file as uploaded. If a channel has no banner, that section simply doesn't appear.

At the time of writing, YouTube recommends 2,560 × 1,440 px banners and keeps the important part inside a central safe area of about 1,546 × 423 px, because TVs show the whole image while phones show only the middle strip. When you download a banner, you get the whole image, including the parts phones never show.

### What isn't included
Video thumbnails belong to individual videos, not the channel, so they aren't part of this tool. Paste a video link into the [YouTube video ID finder](/tools/youtube-video-id-finder), which links to each thumbnail size. Older profile pictures aren't available either: YouTube only serves the current one.

## Image Resolutions
YouTube stores one profile picture per channel and resizes it on request. That's why the downloader can offer several sizes from a single upload, and why it's worth picking the size for the job rather than always grabbing the biggest.

### 88 × 88: small
A small square for places where the picture appears at icon size: a mock-up of a comment thread, a list of channels in a spreadsheet, a favicon-style badge. The files are tiny, so they're handy when you're collecting pictures for dozens of channels.

### 240 × 240: medium
Big enough to look sharp on a slide, in a newsletter or in a research document, and still light. It's the sensible default for mood boards and competitor comparisons.

### 800 × 800: large, the "HD" size
At the time of writing, YouTube recommends uploading profile pictures at 800 × 800 px and shows them on screen far smaller, at around 98 px. That makes 800 the most useful high-resolution size for most channels. If the owner uploaded something smaller, the 800 version can come out soft or look identical to the original, so check it before you print it.

### Original: exactly as uploaded
The original is the file at the resolution the channel owner uploaded. It can be larger than 800 px or, on old channels, smaller. Choose it when you want the best quality available and don't mind an unpredictable size.

### Banner resolution
Banners always download at their original size. No smaller banner sizes are offered, because a cropped or shrunken banner is rarely useful; resize it yourself afterwards if you need to. To make your own banner the right shape, use the [YouTube photo resizer](/tools/youtube-photo-resizer).

### File formats
The file type is whatever YouTube serves for that picture: usually JPEG, sometimes PNG or WebP. The downloader names the file after the channel and the size, such as `channel-name-pfp-800.jpg`, `channel-name-pfp-original.png` or `channel-name-banner.jpg`, so a folder of downloads stays easy to sort.

## Profile Picture Downloader Features
### Paste almost any YouTube link
The input box accepts most of the ways people share a channel. You don't need to tidy the link first: extra parameters, `m.youtube.com` mobile links and `music.youtube.com` links are all handled.

- **Handle links:** `https://www.youtube.com/@channelname`
- **Channel ID links:** `https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC…`
- **Custom and legacy links:** `https://www.youtube.com/c/CustomName` and `https://www.youtube.com/user/OldName`
- **Channel sub-pages:** anything after the channel part, such as `/videos` or `/shorts`, is ignored

### Type just the @handle
Enter the handle with or without the @: `@channelname` or `channelname`. Handles are 3 to 30 characters of letters, numbers, underscores, hyphens, periods and middle dots, so text that breaks those rules won't match a channel. Not sure of the spelling? The [YouTube handle checker](/tools/youtube-handle-checker) opens youtube.com/@name so you can confirm it.

### Paste a channel ID
A channel ID starts with `UC` and is 24 characters long. Paste it on its own and the downloader goes straight to that channel. It's the most reliable input, because the ID never changes even when a creator renames their handle.

### Start from a video, Short or youtu.be link
Only have a clip someone sent you? Paste a watch link, a `youtu.be` short link, a `/shorts/` link, a `/live/` link or an `/embed/` link. The downloader asks YouTube which channel uploaded the video, then fetches that channel's pictures. Private and removed videos can't be traced back to a channel, so use a public video.

### Preview before you download
After a lookup you see the channel name, a round preview of the picture as YouTube shows it, and an **Open the channel** link, so you can confirm you've found the right channel before saving anything. That matters when several channels use similar names.

### No account, no app
It works in any desktop or mobile browser and you don't need to sign in or install anything. Lookups are capped per visitor each day to keep the tool free; see the FAQ for the number.

## Download Options
### View or download each size
Every size has two links. **View** opens the image in a new tab at that size, which is the quickest way to check quality. **Download** saves the file straight to your device with the channel name in the file name. The download passes through our server so your browser treats it as a file to save rather than a page to open.

### How to download a YouTube profile picture
1. Open the channel or one of its videos on YouTube and copy the link. On the YouTube app, tap **Share**, then **Copy link**. On a computer, copy the address bar.
2. Paste the link, @handle or channel ID into the box above.
3. Select **Get profile picture**.
4. Check the preview and channel name, then select **Download** next to the size you want, or **View** to open it first.
5. Scroll down and select **Download banner** if you want the header art too.

### On iPhone, iPad and Android
On iPhone and iPad, Safari saves downloads to the Files app (in Downloads) by default. To put the picture in Photos instead, select **View**, then press and hold the image and choose to save it. On Android, downloads go to your Downloads folder and show up in your gallery app. Menu labels vary by browser and change between versions.

### The manual way, on a computer
You can get the same file without a tool on a desktop browser:

1. Open the channel page.
2. Right-click the profile picture and choose **Open image in new tab** (or copy the image address and paste it into a new tab).
3. Look at the end of the address. YouTube image links end with a size code after an equals sign, such as `=s160-c-k-c0x00ffffff-no-rj`.
4. Replace everything after the `=` with `s800` for an 800-pixel version or `s0` for the original, and press Enter.
5. Right-click the larger image and save it.

That's what the downloader does for you. The manual way is fine for one picture on a laptop. It's awkward on a phone, where you can't easily see or edit the image address, and slow when you need pictures for twenty channels.

### When a lookup fails
- **"We couldn't find that channel"**: the link is mistyped, the handle doesn't exist, the channel was deleted or terminated, or the video is private or removed. Try the channel ID or another video from the same channel.
- **"YouTube didn't respond"**: YouTube didn't answer in time. Wait a minute and try again.
- **"Daily limit reached"**: you've used the day's lookups. The count resets at midnight UTC.

## Best Practices and Caveats
### Use the pictures fairly
The image is someone else's work and often their face. Saving it for reference is one thing; publishing it is another. Before you post a downloaded picture anywhere, ask whether you'd be comfortable if the creator saw how you used it. If not, ask permission or don't use it. Copyright and personality rights differ by country, and this page isn't legal advice.

### Never impersonate a channel
Using another channel's picture and name to look like them breaks YouTube's impersonation rules and those of every major platform. Accounts that do it get removed, and the real creator can report them. If you run a fan, clip or commentary account, make that obvious in the name and bio and use your own avatar.

### Download fresh before you present
Creators update their pictures, especially around rebrands and holidays. The downloader always fetches the current one, so if you saved an avatar months ago for a client deck, grab it again before the meeting.

### Check the corners and the safe area
Because the profile picture is a square shown as a circle, and the banner is a wide image that phones crop, both files can show more than you see on YouTube. Look at the full file before you place it in a design.

### What the downloader can't get
- **Private, deleted or terminated channels**: if YouTube doesn't show the page publicly, there's nothing to read.
- **Accounts without a channel page**: the tool reads the public channel page, so an account that has none can't be looked up.
- **Previous profile pictures**: YouTube serves only the current one.

### Privacy and the daily limit
You don't need an account. Your lookup goes to our server, which reads the public YouTube page and sends back the image addresses. To keep the tool free, lookups are capped for each visitor every day, counted with an anonymous code that changes daily rather than with your name or account.

Paste the video, Shorts or youtu.be link instead of a channel link. The downloader finds the channel that uploaded it and returns that channel's profile picture and banner. The video has to be public.

### What's the highest resolution for a YouTube profile picture?
The highest resolution is whatever the owner uploaded, which you get with **Original**. For most channels that's 800 × 800 px or more, because that's the size YouTube recommends at the time of writing. No tool can make a small upload sharper than the file YouTube has.

### What is a YouTube DP?
DP stands for "display picture" and means the same as PFP or profile picture: the round image next to a channel's name. A YT DP downloader and a YouTube PFP downloader do the same job, and this tool is both.

### Can I download a channel's banner too?
Yes. If the channel has header art, it appears below the profile picture sizes with **View full banner** and **Download banner** buttons, always at full size. If nothing appears, the channel has no banner.

No, this tool gets channel images only. Paste the video link into the [YouTube video ID finder](/tools/youtube-video-id-finder) to open the video's thumbnails at 1280 × 720 (HD videos only), 480 × 360 and 320 × 180.

### Does it work for any YouTube channel, and on my phone?
It works for any channel with a public channel page, in any desktop or mobile browser. Private, deleted and terminated channels can't be looked up.

### Is there a limit on how many profile pictures I can download?
Yes, each visitor gets 60 lookups a day at the time of writing, resetting at midnight UTC. One lookup returns every size and the banner, so each channel uses only one.
