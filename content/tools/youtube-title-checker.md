## How the title score works
The checker runs your title through a short list of rules and adds up the points into a score out of 100. Every rule is shown, so you can see why a title scored the way it did. It's a checklist for clarity and readability, not a prediction of views: no tool can tell you how a title will perform, and YouTube doesn't publish a title formula.

### The checks
| Check | What passes |
|---|---|
| Length | Under YouTube's 100-character limit, ideally around 70 or fewer |
| Keyword placement | Your target keyword appears in the first half of the title |
| Number | The title includes a number (steps, a year, a time, a price) |
| Capitalization | Not written in ALL CAPS |
| Punctuation | No pile-ups like "!!!" or "???" |
| Emoji | One or two at most |
| Context | Uses brackets or parentheses for extra context |
| Specific wording | Includes a concrete or benefit word rather than only vague ones |

### Pass or tip
A pass means the title meets that rule. A tip explains what to try instead. A tip isn't an error: a title without a number can be excellent, so use the tips as prompts to consider, not orders.

## How to use the title checker
### Paste your working title
Start with the title you'd publish today. The checker reads it as-is, including emoji and punctuation, and shows the character count against the 100 limit.

### Add a target keyword (optional)
Enter the phrase you expect people to search for, such as "sourdough starter" or "budget gaming PC." The checker then tests whether it appears in the first half of the title, where it's least likely to be cut off.

### Rewrite and compare
Change one thing at a time and see which checks flip. Keep the version that's clearest to someone who has never heard of your channel.

## Writing YouTube titles people click and understand
### Front-load the topic
YouTube truncates long titles in search results, on mobile and in suggested videos, and the cut-off point differs by surface. If the subject is in the first few words, the title still makes sense when it's shortened. That's the reason behind the roughly 70-character aim and the first-half keyword check.

### Be specific about the payoff
"I Tried Meal Prep" is vague. "I Meal Prepped for 7 Days on $40 (Full Recipes)" tells the viewer the scope, the constraint and what they'll get. Numbers and parentheses do a lot of that work, which is why both are on the checklist.

### Match the title to the video
A title that promises something the video doesn't deliver may get a click, but viewers who leave quickly are a poor signal for your channel and frustrate your audience. Write the title after you know what the video actually shows.

## Frequently asked questions
### How long can a YouTube title be?
Up to 100 characters. Aim for around 70 or fewer so the important part isn't cut off in search results and on mobile.

### Does a high score mean my video will get more views?
No. The score measures whether the title follows common clarity guidelines. Views depend on the video, the thumbnail, your audience and how YouTube recommends content, none of which this tool can predict.

### Should my title include my target keyword?
If there's a phrase your viewers would search for, including it naturally near the start helps people and YouTube understand the topic. Don't stuff in extra keywords; one clear phrase is enough.

### Are emoji okay in YouTube titles?
One or two can add personality, especially for lifestyle or gaming content. More than that can make a title harder to read, and some emoji display differently across devices.

### What do brackets in a title do?
They add context without cluttering the main phrase, for example "(Full Tutorial)" or "[2026 Update]." The checker treats them as a tip, not a requirement.

### Is the title I paste stored anywhere?
No. Scoring happens in your browser and nothing is uploaded.
