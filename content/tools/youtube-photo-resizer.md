## Current YouTube Image Sizes and Specs

![Current YouTube Image Sizes and Specs, in 3 groups: Profile picture: 800×800, Channel banner: 2560×1440 and Video thumbnail: 1280×720.](/images/tools/youtube-photo-resizer-current-youtube-image-sizes-1200x675.webp)

### Profile picture: 800×800
YouTube recommends uploading a square profile picture at 800×800 pixels, and at the time of writing it displays it as a circle at about 98×98 on the channel page and smaller still beside comments and in search. That means two things. First, the corners of your square never show, so anything you put there is wasted. Second, the image is tiny most of the time, so a busy group photo or a logo with small words turns into a smudge.

The resizer's preview shows the profile picture preset as a circle, so you can see what survives before you download. Keep your face or logo in the middle two-thirds of the frame.

### Channel banner: 2560×1440
The banner preset outputs 2560×1440, the size YouTube asks for. The catch is that very little of it is visible everywhere. At the time of writing, only the central area of about 1546×423 pixels is safe on every device; TVs show the whole image, while phones show a thin horizontal strip from the middle. The maximum file size is 6 MB.

So design the banner as a wide middle strip with a generous background around it. Put your channel name, upload schedule or tagline in that center strip, and fill the rest with color, texture or a photo that still looks fine when trimmed. If you're starting from a wide photo, "fill" mode crops it to 16:9 from the center, which is usually what you want.

### Video thumbnail: 1280×720
Custom thumbnails should be 1280×720 (16:9), at least 640 pixels wide, and under 2 MB in JPG, PNG or GIF, at the time of writing. At the time of writing, custom thumbnails also need a verified channel, which you can set up in YouTube Studio.

Thumbnails are where the resize matters most. A screenshot from a phone, a 4:3 camera still or a square design will all be letterboxed or cropped by YouTube if you upload them as they are. Resize to 1280×720 here and the thumbnail fills the frame exactly.

Watch the file size: a detailed photo saved as PNG at 1280×720 can come close to the 2 MB limit. If YouTube refuses the upload, switch the format to JPG and download again.

### Shorts / vertical: 1080×1920
The vertical preset produces a 9:16 frame at 1080×1920, the standard full-screen size for Shorts. Shorts are videos, so this preset is for still images you'll use inside one: a title card, an end screen, a photo you want to show full-screen, or a frame you'll animate in your editor. On the mobile app, a Short's cover is usually picked from a frame of the video itself, so building a strong opening frame is the practical way to control how it looks in the Shorts feed.

YouTube shows the title, buttons and channel name on top of a Short, so keep important text away from the bottom quarter and the right edge.

## What the YouTube Image Resizer Does
Every YouTube image slot expects a particular shape. Upload a 4:3 phone photo as a banner and YouTube crops away most of it; upload a tall selfie as a thumbnail and you get a thin strip of face. Getting the shape right before you upload means you decide what gets cropped, not YouTube.

The resizer does three things:

1. **Changes the shape** to the preset's exact aspect ratio, either by cropping (fill) or by adding a colored border (fit).
2. **Changes the pixel dimensions** to the exact size in the preset, so the file is neither too small to look sharp nor needlessly huge.
3. **Exports a clean file** as PNG or JPG, named with the preset and size so you can find it later, for example `my-photo-channel-banner-2560x1440.png`.

It runs entirely in your browser. The picture is drawn on a canvas on your device and downloaded from there; nothing is uploaded to our servers, and you don't need an account.

What it doesn't do: it doesn't move the crop off-center, add text, remove backgrounds or upload anything to YouTube for you. It's a quick, exact resize. For text on an image, use the [add text to image tool](/tools/add-text-to-image) first, then bring the result back here.

## Supported Formats

![YouTube Image Resizer: supported Formats, in 3 groups: What you can upload, What you can download and File size.](/images/tools/youtube-photo-resizer-supported-formats-1200x675.webp)

### What you can upload
The resizer opens any image your browser can read. In practice that means:

| Input format | Works? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| JPG / JPEG | Yes | The usual format for phone and camera photos |
| PNG | Yes | Transparency is kept in fill mode with PNG output |
| WebP | Yes | Common for images saved from websites |
| GIF | Yes, as a still | Only the first frame is used; the animation is dropped |
| HEIC (iPhone) | Depends on the browser | Safari usually opens it; other browsers may show "That image could not be opened" |

If a file won't open, export it from your photo app as JPG first. On an iPhone, sharing a photo to the Files app or emailing it to yourself often converts it automatically.

### What you can download
Every preset downloads as **PNG** or **JPG**, your choice. PNG is lossless, so text and sharp edges stay crisp, and files are bigger. JPG is saved at high quality (92%), so photos look the same and files are much smaller. Both formats are accepted by YouTube for profile pictures, banners and thumbnails at the time of writing.

### File size
The resizer has no upload limit of its own, because nothing is uploaded. Very large source photos may take a second to load on an older phone. The limits that matter are YouTube's: 2 MB for a thumbnail and 6 MB for a banner, at the time of writing. A 1280×720 JPG is normally a small fraction of 2 MB; a 2560×1440 PNG of a detailed photo can get large, so choose JPG for photographic banners.

## How to Use the YouTube Image Resizer

![How to Use the YouTube Image Resizer, in 4 steps: choose your image, pick a preset, choose fill or fit and pick a format.](/images/tools/youtube-photo-resizer-how-to-1200x675.webp)

1. **Choose your image.** Click the upload area or drop a JPG, PNG, WebP or GIF onto it. Animated GIFs come out as a single still frame. Use the largest version of the image you have: shrinking a big image always looks better than enlarging a small one.
2. **Pick a preset.** Choose profile picture, channel banner, video thumbnail or Shorts / vertical. The preview updates straight away, and the profile picture preview is shown as a circle.
3. **Choose fill or fit.** **Fill (crop)** scales the image until it covers the whole frame and trims whatever spills over, keeping the center. **Fit (add background)** keeps the entire image visible and fills the leftover space with a color you pick.
4. **Pick a format.** PNG is lossless and best for logos, text and flat graphics. JPG makes smaller files and suits photos.
5. **Download.** The button shows the exact output, for example "Download 1280×720 JPG". Upload the file in YouTube Studio under Customization (profile picture and banner) or in a video's details (thumbnail). Menu names change between versions of YouTube Studio, so look for "Branding" or "Customization" if the labels have moved.

### When to use fill and when to use fit
| Your image | Preset | Best mode | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phone selfie (portrait) | Profile picture | Fill | Crops to a square around your face |
| Wide logo with words | Profile picture | Fit | Keeps the whole logo, adds a background |
| Landscape photo | Banner | Fill | Trims to 16:9 from the center |
| Square artwork | Banner | Fit | Shows the full artwork on a colored strip |
| 4:3 camera still | Thumbnail | Fill | Removes a little top and bottom |
| Screenshot from a phone | Thumbnail | Fit | Avoids cutting off most of the screen |
| Landscape photo | Shorts / vertical | Fit | Shows the full photo with color above and below |

Fill always crops from the center. If your subject sits near an edge, crop it roughly in your phone's photo editor first, then run it through the resizer for exact pixels.

## Best Practices for YouTube Images

![Best Practices for YouTube Images: do use the banner's safe area, keep thumbnails honest and readable, pick the right format and start from a big source image.](/images/tools/youtube-photo-resizer-best-practices-1200x675.webp)

### Design for the smallest size it appears at
Your profile picture is mostly seen at thumbnail size beside a video title or a comment. Thumbnails are often seen at a couple of centimeters wide on a phone. Before you download, step back from the screen: if you can't tell what the image is, simplify it.

### Use the banner's safe area
Keep text and logos within the central strip of the banner. Anything outside it will be cut on phones and most laptops. A plain or gently textured background around that strip is safer than a busy photo.

### Keep thumbnails honest and readable
A face, a clear object and three to five words at most is a dependable recipe. Thumbnails that don't match the video lead to people clicking away, and YouTube can remove thumbnails that break its policies.

### Pick the right format
Use PNG for text, logos and flat colors, which stay crisp. Use JPG for photos and to keep thumbnails under 2 MB. The resizer exports JPG at high quality, so the difference is rarely visible.

### Start from a big source image
The resizer can enlarge a small image to 2560×1440, but it can't add detail that isn't there. A 600-pixel-wide photo stretched into a banner will look soft. Use the original file, not a screenshot or a copy downloaded from social media.

### Only use images you have the right to use
Use your own photos, artwork you've licensed, or images you have permission for. Uploading someone else's photo or logo as your channel art can lead to a copyright claim or an impersonation report.

### Caveats and limits
- **Still images only.** The download is a single PNG or JPG frame. Animated GIFs lose their animation.
- **Center crop only.** Fill mode always keeps the middle of the image. Pre-crop if your subject is off-center.
- **No transparency in fit mode.** Fit mode always fills the border with your chosen color. In fill mode with PNG, transparent areas of a transparent PNG stay transparent, but YouTube may show them against a background of its choosing.
- **The circle preview is a guide.** YouTube decides how the image is cropped and displayed on each device.
- **Specs can change.** The sizes here match the tool's presets and YouTube's guidance at the time of writing.

## Frequently asked questions
### What size should a YouTube profile picture be?
A YouTube profile picture should be a square 800×800 pixels. YouTube shows it as a circle, at roughly 98×98 on the channel page at the time of writing, so keep the important part in the center and avoid small text.

### What size is a YouTube banner?
Upload the banner at 2560×1440 pixels and under 6 MB. Only the central area of about 1546×423 is visible on every device, so keep your text and logo inside that strip.

### What size is a YouTube thumbnail?
A YouTube thumbnail should be 1280×720 pixels (16:9), at least 640 pixels wide and under 2 MB, at the time of writing. If a PNG is too big, download it again as JPG.

### Is my image uploaded anywhere?
No. The resizing happens in your browser on your device, and the image is never sent to our servers. Close the tab and it's gone.

### Can I use this to get someone else's YouTube profile picture?
No, this tool only resizes images you already have. If you need to look up a channel's public profile picture for reference, the [YouTube PFP downloader](/tools/youtube-pfp-downloader) does that; the images belong to the channel owner, so use them for research or with permission, never to impersonate anyone.

### Why does YouTube reject my thumbnail?
The usual reasons are a file over 2 MB, a channel that isn't verified yet, or an image under 640 pixels wide, at the time of writing. Resize to 1280×720, download as JPG, and verify your channel in YouTube Studio if custom thumbnails aren't available.

### Does the resizer keep transparency?
Yes, in fill mode with PNG output: transparent parts of a transparent PNG stay transparent. Fit mode always fills the border with your chosen color, and JPG never supports transparency.

### Does the resizer work on a phone?
Yes. It works in mobile browsers: tap the upload area, choose a photo from your library, pick a preset and tap download.
