## What the LinkedIn Comment Generator Does

![LinkedIn Comment Generator: 12 Free Ideas in 5 Tones, Edit & Post. Type what the post is about, pick a tone, and copy a comment idea to edit in your own words before you post it on LinkedIn.](/images/tools/linkedin-comment-generator-hero-1200x675.webp)

This free LinkedIn comment generator gives you 12 comment ideas for a post, in the tone you choose. Type the post's topic in a few words, pick supportive, curious question, funny, professional or hype, and copy the idea that's closest to what you want to say. Then edit it so it's yours, and post it on LinkedIn yourself.

![What the LinkedIn Comment Generator Does, in 2 groups: How it works and What it never does.](/images/tools/linkedin-comment-generator-what-the-linkedin-comment-1200x675.webp)

It's built for the moment everyone knows: a colleague shares a promotion, a client posts a long reflection, a peer writes about something you have opinions on, and you stare at the comment box. A starting point gets you past the blank box, and you add the part only you can: a detail, an example or your actual opinion.

### How it works
The ideas come from hand-written templates for each tone, with your topic filled in and the order shuffled. On LinkedIn, the pool also includes extra templates written for professional conversations, such as "How did you measure the impact of…" and "Useful framing of… One thing I would add…". There's no AI model and no network call: everything runs in your browser, and nothing you type is stored. Press **More ideas** for a new set.

### What it never does
The tool never posts, likes or comments on your behalf, and it doesn't connect to your LinkedIn account. It shows text you can copy. That's deliberate: comments that sound pasted do more harm than no comment, and automated commenting breaks LinkedIn's rules.

## LinkedIn Comment Types You Can Generate

![LinkedIn Comment Types You Can Generate, in 3 groups: Supportive comments, Curious question comments and Professional comments.](/images/tools/linkedin-comment-generator-linkedin-comment-types-you-1200x675.webp)

Below, each tone shows real output from the generator for a sample topic, so you know what to expect. Your results use your own topic.

### Supportive comments
Thank the author and say what helped. Good for posts by colleagues, mentors and clients. For "customer interviews":
- This is such a helpful take on customer interviews. Thank you for sharing it.
- Thanks for sharing this. The point about customer interviews matches what we have seen on our team.
- Clear, practical take on customer interviews. Bookmarking it for our next planning session.

The last two are LinkedIn-specific templates. They work best when you follow them with one sentence on what you actually saw on your team.

### Curious question comments
Questions keep a conversation going and give the author a reason to reply. For "customer interviews":
- What tools do you use for customer interviews?
- How long did it take you to get comfortable with customer interviews?
- How did you measure the impact of customer interviews? Curious what changed first.

### Professional comments
Measured and specific, for industry posts and thought leadership. For "pricing experiments":
- Useful framing of pricing experiments. One thing I would add: start small and document what works.
- Appreciate the specifics here. Pricing experiments advice is rarely this concrete.
- Agree on pricing experiments. The hardest part is usually getting buy-in early, not the execution.

The "One thing I would add" template is a prompt: replace the suggestion with your own point.

### Funny comments
Light humor for posts that already have a playful tone. For "four-day work weeks":
- Me, pretending I understood four-day work weeks before this post.
- Okay but who gave you permission to be this good at four-day work weeks?
- This four-day work weeks post lives in my head rent-free.

Use these sparingly on LinkedIn. A joke under a layoff post or a serious announcement reads badly, however well meant.

### Hype comments
Big, enthusiastic congratulations, for launches, wins and milestones. For "the launch":
- Absolutely nailed it. Best the launch content I've seen this week.
- This deserves way more attention. The launch at its best.

As the second example shows, hype templates are short and blunt, and they read best with a short noun as the topic. They're also the tone that most needs a specific detail added, such as the feature you liked or the result you noticed.

## LinkedIn Comment Best Practices the Generator Bakes In

![LinkedIn Comment Best Practices the Generator Bakes In: do one clear intent per comment, short enough to read in the feed, questions that invite a reply and professional language by default.](/images/tools/linkedin-comment-generator-best-practices-1200x675.webp)

### Comments are starting points, not finished text
The tool's own note says it: edit each one so it's genuinely yours, and never post the same comment in bulk. Every idea is short on purpose, leaving room for your specific detail.

### One clear intent per comment
Each tone does one job: thank, ask, add, joke or celebrate. Mixing all of them into one comment makes it long and vague. If you want to thank the author and ask something, pick the question and add a short "thanks" yourself.

### Short enough to read in the feed
LinkedIn shows only the start of longer comments before a "see more". The ideas are one or two sentences, which shows in full on most screens and leaves room for you to add a line of your own.

### Questions that invite a reply
The curious question tone uses open questions, such as "what", "how" and "would you", rather than yes-or-no questions, so the author has something to answer.

### Professional language by default
LinkedIn-specific templates avoid slang and emoji, and use phrases that fit a work context, such as "on our team" and "our next planning session". The generator doesn't add hashtags or emoji to comments.

## How to Use the LinkedIn Comment Generator

![How to Use the LinkedIn Comment Generator, in 4 steps: describe the post in a few words, pick a tone, read the 12 ideas and copy the closest one.](/images/tools/linkedin-comment-generator-how-to-1200x675.webp)

1. **Describe the post in a few words.** Type what it's about in the box, up to 80 characters. A short noun phrase works best: "customer interviews", "remote onboarding", "the launch". Full sentences such as "hiring your first salesperson" can produce awkward grammar in some templates.
2. **Pick a tone.** Supportive, curious question, funny, professional or hype.
3. **Read the 12 ideas.** Each one has a copy button. If none fits, press **More ideas**.
4. **Copy the closest one.** Tap the copy button.
5. **Edit it in LinkedIn.** Paste it into the comment box and change it: add a name, a number, an example or your own view. Delete anything you wouldn't say out loud.
6. **Post it yourself.** Read it once more, then post.

### Turning an idea into a real comment
| Generated idea | Edited comment |
|---|---|
| What tools do you use for customer interviews? | What tools do you use for customer interviews? We record ours on Zoom but tagging the notes is still a mess. |
| Agree on pricing experiments. The hardest part is usually getting buy-in early, not the execution. | Agree. When we tested annual pricing, getting finance on board took longer than the test itself. |
| Thanks for sharing this. The point about remote onboarding matches what we have seen on our team. | Thanks for sharing, Priya. The buddy system point matches what we saw: new starters asked more questions in week one. |

The edited versions keep the shape of the idea and add one specific thing. That's usually all it takes.

## Who the LinkedIn Comment Generator Is For
### Professionals building a network
Commenting regularly on peers' posts is one of the simplest ways to be remembered. The generator helps when you want to show up but the words aren't coming.

### Founders and small business owners
You want to support customers, partners and other founders without spending half an hour per comment. Pick professional or supportive, add one line about your experience, and move on.

### Job seekers
Thoughtful comments on posts from people at companies you'd like to join get noticed. Use the curious question tone and ask something you genuinely want to know.

### Sales and partnership teams
A good question under a prospect's post is a far better first touch than a cold message. Keep it about their post, not your product.

### Recruiters and hiring managers
Congratulate candidates and connections on new roles and milestones, then make each one personal with their name and the detail you noticed.

### Non-native English speakers
If you write LinkedIn comments in your second language, the ideas give you natural phrasing to adapt. Change the details and keep the structure.

## How to Get the Most Out of Your Comments

![LinkedIn Comment Generator: how to Get the Most Out of Your Comments, in 4 steps: add one specific detail, never paste the same comment twice, match the post's mood and comment where you have something to add.](/images/tools/linkedin-comment-generator-how-to-2-1200x675.webp)

### Add one specific detail
A name, a number, a short example or a disagreement turns a template into a comment people reply to. Without it, generic praise looks automated.

### Never paste the same comment twice
Posting identical comments across many posts looks like spam to people and to LinkedIn. Use the generator for ideas, not for copies.

### Match the post's mood
Read the post properly first. Hype under a sensitive post, or a joke under bad news, does more damage than staying quiet.

### Comment where you have something to add
Ten thoughtful comments a week beat fifty forgettable ones. Pick posts in your field where your experience is relevant.

### Reply when the author answers
A comment is the start of a conversation. If the author replies to your question, answer back.

### Keep your own posts in shape too
If people click through from your comment, they'll see your profile and recent posts. The [LinkedIn text formatter](/tools/linkedin-text-formatter) helps with bold text and spacing, and our guide to the [LinkedIn post character limit](/blog/linkedin-post-character-limit) covers how long posts can be.

### Know the tool's limits
It generates comments from templates in English, from a topic you type. It doesn't read the post, so it can't refer to what the post actually says. That's your part.

## Frequently asked questions
### Does the LinkedIn comment generator post comments for me?
No. It only shows comment ideas you can copy. It doesn't connect to LinkedIn, and you edit and post every comment yourself.

### Is this an AI comment generator?
No. It uses hand-written templates with your topic filled in, shuffled in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server, and you should always edit the result so it says what you mean.

### Is the LinkedIn comment generator free?
Yes. It's free, needs no account and works in any browser, on desktop or mobile. Press **More ideas** as often as you like.

### Is it OK to use generated comments on LinkedIn?
Yes, as long as you edit them and post them yourself. Copying the same comment onto many posts, or using tools that comment automatically, looks spammy and goes against LinkedIn's rules.

### What should I type in the topic box?
Type a short noun phrase that describes the post, such as "remote onboarding" or "the launch". Short phrases fit the templates better than full sentences.

### Which tone should I choose?
Choose curious question if you want a reply, supportive or professional for most work posts, hype for launches and milestones, and funny only when the post is already light-hearted.

### How long should a LinkedIn comment be?
One to three sentences is plenty for most comments. Lead with your point, since longer comments are cut off behind "see more".
