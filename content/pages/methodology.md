## Best time to post: three sources, in order {#best-time}

Each best-time page shows the best source available for its platform and country, and says which one it is under the heatmap.

### 1. Posts scheduled through Schedule For You

Where enough posts have gone out through Schedule For You, each one is counted in the weekday and hour it was published, in the account's own time zone. Slots whose posts earned more link clicks score a little higher. We only keep counts per hour, never the posts or account details. A platform needs at least 150 posts in a region, or 400 worldwide, before this data replaces the sources below.

### 2. Google Trends, weighted by when people are online

Until then, the pages use Google Trends. In the countries we measure directly, we read hourly search interest in each platform over the past 7 days and the weekday pattern over the past 90 days. We then weight each hour by how many people in that country are online, using hourly traffic to the country's local-language Wikipedia over the past 28 days (Wikimedia, CC0).

In countries where Google Trends can't be read reliably, the page is an **estimate**: that country's own Wikipedia traffic, combined with the platform's hourly pattern from the measured countries. The source line on those pages says so.

### 3. A starting baseline

A few countries have neither yet. Their pages show a starting baseline: weekday mornings and lunchtime strongest, evenings next, weekends later and flatter. **The baseline is an estimate, not measured data.** Treat its times as a first test, not a finding.

### What these pages can't tell you

They show when people are active on a platform, not when *your* followers engage with *your* posts. Engagement studies often point to different hours because they also reflect how crowded the feed is. Use our times as a starting point, test two or three slots for three or four weeks, and keep what your own insights show.

## Platform sizes and limits {#specs}

The size, length and character-limit pages are checked by hand against each platform's help pages and against the limits Schedule For You enforces when it publishes. When a platform allows more in its own app than through its API, which is what scheduling tools use, we show both numbers. Instagram carousels and TikTok captions are examples.

Each page shows the date it was last verified and links to a way to report a change. Platforms change limits without notice, so we re-check whenever one announces a change, and at least every few months.

## Does it notify {#does-it-notify}

Each yes, no or partly answer is checked against the platform's help center and how the app behaves. An answer only changes when we have a source for the change. Every page shows the date it was last checked and links to the platform's help center.

## Guides on the blog {#guides}

Guides are written and reviewed by the Schedule For You team. Menu labels and limits change between app versions, so guides say when a step depends on your version. Where a guide compares tools or prices, it says when the prices were checked.

## Corrections

If you spot a number that's wrong or out of date, email [support@scheduleforyou.com](mailto:support@scheduleforyou.com) with the page and what you saw. We fix confirmed errors and update the page's date.
