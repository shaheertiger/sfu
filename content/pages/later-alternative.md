## Who looks for a Later alternative, and why
Later built its reputation on Instagram. Its visual planner lets you drag posts into place and preview how your grid will look, and its link-in-bio page gives Instagram and TikTok profiles a clickable landing page. For brands whose main channel is Instagram, those are genuinely useful tools.

People start looking elsewhere for a few common reasons:

- **Their channel mix has changed.** A creator who started on Instagram may now post as much to LinkedIn, Threads, Bluesky or YouTube, and wants one place that treats every network equally.
- **They want text-first posting.** Some networks, like X, Threads, Bluesky and LinkedIn, work fine without images. A tool designed around a visual grid can feel like the wrong shape for text posts.
- **They manage clients.** Agencies and freelancers need workspaces per client and an easy way for clients to approve posts.
- **They want to automate.** Developers and operations-minded marketers may want an API, webhooks or an RSS feed that posts for them.
- **Plan structure.** Some want simple pricing with unlimited posts.

None of these mean Later is a bad tool. They mean the job has changed.

## What to look for in a Later alternative
Write down the networks you post to and how you work, then check each candidate against this list.

### Platform coverage
Confirm the tool publishes directly to every network you use, and check the details for each one. "Supports Instagram" can mean feed posts only, or feed posts plus Stories. "Supports Facebook" usually means Pages, not personal profiles or Groups.

### Per-platform editing
Look for a way to write one post and change the wording for each network separately, with character-limit checks for each. Otherwise you end up with a caption that's too long for X or too thin for LinkedIn.

### Scheduling workflow
Do you prefer picking exact times, or adding posts to a queue that fills preset time slots? Is there a calendar you can drag posts around in? Can you bulk upload a month of posts?

### Collaboration
If others are involved, check for team members, approvals and a way for clients to review posts without paying for a seat.

### Reliability signals
Find out what happens when a post fails. A good scheduler tells you immediately and makes it easy to reconnect and retry.

### Pricing and trial terms
Check how many accounts each plan includes, whether posts are capped, and whether the trial needs a card.

## Where Later may suit you better
Later may be the better choice if:

- **You plan Instagram visually.** If previewing your grid before posting is central to how you work, Schedule For You doesn't offer a grid planner. (For one-off grid layouts, our free [Instagram grid maker](/tools/instagram-grid-maker) splits an image into tiles, but it isn't a planner.)
- **You depend on a link-in-bio page.** Schedule For You doesn't provide one.
- **You schedule Instagram Stories or first comments.** Schedule For You publishes feed photos, Reels and carousels, not Stories, and doesn't post first comments.
- **You want analytics in the same tool.** Schedule For You focuses on planning and publishing and doesn't include analytics.
- **You run influencer campaigns.** Later also offers influencer marketing products; Schedule For You doesn't.

If most of your work is Instagram-first and visual, staying with Later may be the right call.

## How to switch from Later
The goal is to avoid losing or double-posting anything:

1. **List what's scheduled.** Note every post scheduled in Later for the next few weeks, including captions, media and times.
2. **Download your media.** Save the images and videos you plan to reuse so you can upload them to the Schedule For You media library.
3. **Start your trial and connect accounts.** Sign up, then connect each network from the Accounts screen. Instagram needs a Business or Creator account linked to a Facebook Page.
4. **Set your time slots.** Recreate your usual posting times as queue time slots.
5. **Rebuild the schedule.** Add upcoming posts one by one or, for a long list, prepare a CSV with captions and media and use bulk scheduling.
6. **Prepare Instagram images.** Schedule For You doesn't crop images, so make sure feed images fall between 4:5 and 1.91:1. The free [Instagram photo resizer](/tools/instagram-photo-resizer) helps.
7. **Remove the duplicates in Later.** Delete or pause each post in Later once it's scheduled in Schedule For You, so nothing goes out twice.
8. **Decide on link-in-bio.** If you used Later's link-in-bio page, keep it or choose a replacement before you cancel, so your profile link keeps working.
9. **Watch the first week.** Check posts as they go out and respond to any failed-post alerts by reconnecting the account.

## Frequently asked questions
### Is there a free Later alternative?
Each platform's own tools, like Meta Business Suite for Facebook and Instagram, are free but separate. Schedule For You isn't free: it has a 7-day free trial (card required) and paid plans from $19 a month. ### Can Schedule For You post Instagram Stories like Later?
No. It publishes Instagram feed photos, Reels and carousels of up to 10 items, but not Stories, and it doesn't post a first comment.

### Does Schedule For You have a link-in-bio page?
No. If you rely on a link-in-bio page, keep your existing one or choose a dedicated tool before you move.

### Which platforms does Schedule For You support that matter for Later users?
All 10: Instagram, Facebook Pages, X, LinkedIn, Threads, Bluesky, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest and Google Business Profile. Check Later's site for its current list if you need to compare network by network.

Yes. Client preview links let clients approve or comment on posts in the browser without signing up.
