## How to choose the best rust converter

### 1. Make sure a converter is the right tool

There are three kinds of rust products, and they solve different problems:

- **Converters** (Corroseal, Rust Reformer, VHT, Rustex, Ospho) react with rust and turn it into a stable compound you can paint. Use them on tight surface rust on steel you cannot easily take to bare metal.
- **Removers** (Evapo-Rust is the best-known example) dissolve rust and leave clean, bare metal. Use them on parts you can soak, such as tools, hardware and small car parts. You then prime the bare metal.
- **Encapsulators** (Eastwood Rust Encapsulator, POR-15) seal rust under a tough film. Use them on heavier or pitted rust in places you cannot fully clean.

If you are on the fence between the first two, our [rust converter vs. rust remover](https://www.thehonestreviewers.com/rust-converter-vs-rust-remover/) breakdown goes deeper.

### 2. Prep for tight rust, not bare metal

Wrong prep is the most common reason converters fail. Wire brush or scrape off anything flaking or loose, but leave firmly bonded rust, because the converter needs iron oxide to react with. Degrease, rinse and let it dry. Spots ground to shiny metal will not convert; prime those with a regular metal primer.

### 3. Tannic vs. phosphoric acid

- **Tannic acid** products react with rust to form a dark blue-black layer, and they usually come in a polymer primer base. They are easier to handle and often double as a primer.
- **Phosphoric acid** products form iron phosphate. They are thin, soak into seams and are cheap by the gallon, but they are strong acids, need goggles and gloves, and usually want a primer on top.

### 4. Spray vs. brush-on

A 10-12 oz aerosol covers roughly 8-12 sq ft. A gallon of brush-on can cover around 200 sq ft at the recommended film thickness. Rule of thumb: sprays for anything you can carry, brush-on for fences, gates, trailers and railings. An inexpensive synthetic brush you can throw away is fine for acidic treatments; for the primer and paint coats, see our [best paint brushes](https://www.thehonestreviewers.com/best-paint-brushes/) guide.

### 5. Temperature, humidity and cure

Most converters want roughly 50-95°F and moderate humidity (VHT: 60-95°F, under 60%; Rust-Oleum: 50-90°F, under 65%). Skip it if rain or heavy dew is likely within 24 hours, and avoid metal hot from direct sun. Plan on about 24 hours before topcoating, longer in cool weather.

### 6. Plan the topcoat

A converted layer is thin and sunlight breaks it down. Outdoors, follow it with a compatible primer and paint, usually an oil-based or alkyd enamel. Check the label for topcoat limits: some tannic products bleed through water-based topcoats, and some need days before lacquer. After painting a fence or railing, seal gaps where water sits, such as where posts meet concrete, with a good [exterior caulk](https://www.thehonestreviewers.com/best-exterior-caulk/).

### 7. Know the limits

Converters are not rated for high heat, so skip them on grills, exhaust parts and stoves. They are generally not for aluminum or stainless, and Corroseal and Rust-Oleum both warn against using their products on galvanized steel. And they do nothing for rust-through: if you can push a screwdriver through the metal, it needs patching or replacement.

### Mistakes to avoid

- Painting converter over loose scale, which flakes off and takes the coating with it
- Grinding to bare metal and expecting the converter to protect it
- Thinning the product or laying it on too thin to change color
- Leaving converted metal outdoors without a topcoat
- Skipping eye protection with phosphoric acid products

## FAQ

### What is rust converter?

A rust converter is a liquid, usually based on tannic or phosphoric acid, that chemically reacts with iron oxide and turns it into a stable, dark compound (iron tannate or iron phosphate). The converted layer stops the rust from spreading and gives you a paintable surface without grinding to bare metal. It is a pre-paint treatment, not a final coat.

### Do I need a primer over rust converter?

It depends on the product. Primer-type converters like Corroseal are designed to be topcoated directly, though the maker recommends an oil-based primer if you add one. Phosphoric acid treatments like Ospho and Rustex add little film, so a primer before paint is the safer choice, especially in harsh weather.

### How long does rust converter last without paint?

Not long outdoors. UV light and weather break down the converted layer, so treat bare converter as a short-term holding coat and paint it within days or weeks.

### Is rust converter good for cars?

Yes, for surface rust on frames, floor pans and brackets you cannot strip to bare metal. It is not a repair for rot, holes or rust bubbling under paint from behind; that metal needs to be cut out and replaced. Topcoat anything exposed with a chassis paint.

### What is the best rust converter for a trailer?

Brush-on beats aerosol on a trailer frame. Ospho or Rustex penetrates seams and rivet lines under an oil-based primer and enamel, while Corroseal is the simpler one-step option. For heavily pitted spots, use an encapsulator.
